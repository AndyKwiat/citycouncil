3	9. Delegations
3	9.1 Randy Vaine, resident appeared before Council in support of regulating
3	Short Term Rentals in Centre Wellington. He indicated the lack of hotels
3	and affordable accommodations in the community support the need for
3	short term rentals. He supported licensing short term rentals provided
3	there are tools available to address noise, litter and garbage, and
3	parking concerns.
3	9.2 Darcy Saunders, resident shared his experience living adjacent to a
3	short term rental in Centre Wellington. He explained incidents of
3	drunken disorderly behaviour, noise at all hours, parking issues,
3	invasion of privacy, etc.
3	9.3 Rohan Sovig, Malone Given Parsons Ltd addressed Council regarding
3	the Greenfield Density and Intensification Target Preferred Scenario. He
3	noted Malone Given Parsons Ltd., is the planning consultant for Elora
3	Sands Development, the owner of the property at 7581 Sideroad 15,
3	Nichol in Centre Wellington. He indicated they continue to be pleased
3	with the direction the Township, the County and Watson & Associates
3	took with the Lands Needs Assessment (LNA), however, they believe
3	the Township should still rely on the original analysis conducted by
3	Waston & Associates as part of the County LNA. R. Sovig suggested
3	they are concerned the higher intensification rates and density targets
3	outlined in the preferred scenario appear to be unrealistic and
3	unachievable.
3	9.4 Rob Stovel, RSR Inc. appeared before Council to speak to the
3	Greenfield Density and Intensification Target Preferred Scenario. He
3	explained his ownership of a small local company, who hires local
3	contractors and strives to provide attainable housing. He explained he
3	has an interest in property at 996 Scotland Street in Fergus and requires
3	approximately 12 acres of land to be brought into the urban boundary to
4	provide attainable housing.
4	9.5 Janet Harrop, Wellington Federation of Agriculture appeared before
4	Council regarding the Greenfield Density and Intensification Target
4	Preferred Scenario. She provided statistics indicating over the past 20
4	years Ontario has lost farmland area that is equivalent to eleven times
4	the size of Toronto, or roughly the entire size of the Greater Toronto
4	Area (1,741,286 acres). Since 2000, we have lost 238 acres of farmland
4	a day and over the last five years, this has increased to 319 acres a day.
4	She suggested at 319 acres of farmland loss per day by 2051 there will
4	be half the farmland in Ontario than there is today. J. Harrop indicated
4	the Federation recognizes the work completed by Centre Wellington and
4	the County of Wellington and is supportive of scenario 4 which
4	preserves the most agricultural land.

6	11.2 Greenfield Density and Intensification Target Preferred Scenario
6	Report from B. Salmon, Managing Director of Planning and
6	Development
6	Presentation from Jamie Cook, Watson & Associates Economists Ltd.
6	B. Salmon, Managing Director of Planning and Development introduced
6	Jamie Cook, Watson & Associates Economists Ltd. J. Cook provided an
6	overview of the exercise, indicating the purpose is to prepare a local
6	assessment of land needs that will prioritize the quantity of land required
6	for servicing to the year 2051. He indicated four (4) scenarios have been
6	developed based on density and intensification levels, and the preferred
6	scenario selected by Council will be utilized for local planning, phasing
6	and servicing plans.
6	He reviewed the various scenarios and provided highlights on Scenario
6	3, as follows; Increase to Designated Greenfield Area (DGA) Density,
7	No Change to Residential Intensification Rate, 20% intensification rate,
7	52 people and jobs/ha in DGA Community Area and Land Need of 132
7	ha for growth to 2051.
7	Brett Salmon, Managing Director of Planning and Development
7	indicated staff is supportive of the recommended scenario 3 and believe
7	the scenario remains realistic and achievable while accomplishing the
7	goal of mitigating the need for urban expansion into prime agricultural
7	areas.
7	The consultant and staff responded to questions regarding consideration
7	given to existing vacant lands and brownfields, review of historical
7	patterns and data, and the possible timing for a review.
7	Moved by Councillor Jennifer Adams, Seconded by Councillor
7	Kimberley Jefferson.
7	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington endorse
7	Scenario 3 as the Preferred Scenario for land need associated with
7	Settlement Area Boundary Expansion for the Fergus and Elora-Salem
7	Urban Centres;
7	AND THAT Township staff be directed to utilize the Preferred Scenario
7	when presenting revised urban centre boundaries for Council
7	consideration.
7	CARRIED

8	11.4 Official Plan Amendment OP01/23 and Zoning By-law Amendment
8	RZ01/23 - Decision Report (22 Park Road, Elora)
8	Report from M. Iglesias, Manager of Planning Services
8	Mariana Iglesias, Manager of Planning Services provided an overview of
8	the report, indicating the property is a 0.7 hectare site, on the east side
8	of Wellington Rd 7, west side of Park Road, in the Elora Salem Urban
8	Centre. She noted the property is designated “Industrial” in the Official
8	Plan and adjacent to “Highway Commercial” designation to the west and
8	south, with other Industrial lands to the north and east. M. Iglesias noted
8	the owner is seeking an Official Plan and Zoning By-law Amendment to
8	change the property from Industrial to Highway Commercial to facilitate
8	a commercial redevelopment of the parcel. She indicated the proposal is
8	consistent with the County and Provincial decision to redesignate the
8	land from Employment Land and the redesignation is compatible with
8	the surroundings, is accessible from a major arterial roadway (County
8	Road 7) and services the needs of the travelling public.
8	Moved by Councillor Barbara Lustgarten-Evoy, Seconded by Councillor
8	Kimberley Jefferson.
8	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington approves
8	applications OPA01/23 and RZ01/23;
9	AND THAT proposed Official Plan Amendment Number 16 be adopted
9	by the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington and submitted to
9	the County of Wellington for approval;
9	AND THAT the proposed Zoning By-law Amendment be given three
9	readings and passed.
9	CARRIED
