2	5. Presentations and Announcements
2	5.1 Courtney O'Neill, Centre Wellington Community Foundation
2	Re: Smart Cities Report
2	C. O’Neill, Nutritious Food Coordinator outlined her role to increasing
2	community food access, with a focus on bringing multi-sector
2	stakeholders together to shape how our community can best respond to
2	the increased food needs through knowledge sharing, collective learning
2	and system thinking. She explained household food insecurity,
2	indicating 24% of Ontario households experienced food insecurity in
2	2023 and 30% of children lived in a food-insecure household in Ontario
2	in 2023.
2	C. O’Neill outlined the community impact various programs such as
2	summer student nutrition support, community food outreach program,
2	free safe food handler certification and multi-partner projects and
2	resource sharing have on the community. She outlined the next steps to
2	increased food access are to secure funding, measure the impact and
2	establish new pathways for distribution and program delivery.
2	C. O’Neill responded to questions regarding healthy food vending
2	machines and innovative ways to distribute healthy and affordable food.
2	5.2 County Council Report - Councillor Mary Lloyd, County Ward 5
2	Councillor Mary Lloyd provided highlights of the OPP annual report,
2	Housing services and activity report, mental health training in the
2	daycare centres, the volunteer appreciation video, and the various
2	ongoing activities at the museum. She outlined ongoing activities at the
2	various library branches, shared comments submitted by the County
2	regarding Bill 185, informed Council of a public meeting regarding the
2	reallocation of employment lands upcoming in June and spoke about the
2	increase to the cost of garbage bags and waste diversion measures.

3	6.1 Pierre Chauvin, MHBC Planning re: item 8.1 - Official Plan and Zoning
3	Amendment (OP02/23 and RZ05/23) - Status Update, 19/19A East Mill
3	Street, Elora
3	P. Chauvin indicated the revised design now proposes a 4-storey mixed
3	use building, extending along the East Mill Street frontage. He
3	highlighted the details of Building A, including:
3	• 3 ground floor commercial units (252m²)
3	• 18 accessible dwelling units
3	• 22 residential parking spaces provided in a basement parking garage
3	• 4-storey building height (14.5m) in order to maintain 18 units above
3	flood elevation
3	and the Building B to the rear of the property:
3	• 2 storey stone building will be converted from a casket manufacturing
3	facility to a restaurant and/or retail
3	• Addition is proposed along with outdoor patio area
3	• 11 outdoor parking spaces to serve all commercial use on the property,
3	including buildings A and B
3	P, Chauvin Design highlighted the design changes and rationale,
3	presented various artist renderings and noted the heritage mitigation &
3	conservation efforts. He responded to questions of clarification.
