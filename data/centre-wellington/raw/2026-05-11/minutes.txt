4	8.2 Tova Davidson, Executive Director on behalf of Sustainable Waterloo
4	Region (SWR) provided an update for 2025, it was a year of scaling,
4	expanding into new geographies and supporting 40 communities.
4	She noted SWR started as a student project almost 18 years ago and has
4	continued to grow into several programs including the Climate action
4	program whole community action plan, designing how we move forward
4	together to create low carbon futures that drive towards equity, inclusion,
4	resiliency and prosperity. In addition to Waterloo Region, SWR now
4	serves Guelph-Wellington, Perth, Oxford and Dufferin Counties and
4	scaled across the county by creation of another non-profit “Green
4	Economy Canada” supporting other non-profits across Canada to create
4	the Impact network Program.
4	T. Davidson provided a brief overview of the programs run by SWR, the
4	organics program has been operating in Centre Wellington for some time
4	formally run by the circular innovation council, taking the organic waste
4	and bringing it to an anaerobic digester making it into clean energy, also
4	offer food rescue as part of this program, the impact network program
4	which Centre Wellington is a member, helps organizations track their
4	carbon footprint and assists with tracking the carbon footprint for events.
4	She commented on evolve1 Canada’s first net positive energy office
4	building build by The Cora Group. Last year SWR launched the
4	Frameworks program, Ontario’s only building innovation exchange
4	designed to support the building and development sector with building
4	retrofits and in preparation for the projected harmonized code in 2030.
4	SWR launched a “microforest” program, native species trees in urban
4	spaces, planted in school yards parks and other areas benefitting the
4	community. The microforest program is projected to have planted 75
4	forests by the end of 2026.
4	T. Davidson responded to questions and comments from Council
4	regarding requirements for development of microforests, the associated
4	costs and the opportunity to attend SWR’s pretty spaces, geothermal
4	tours and SWR’s position on advocating for Provincial policy.

5	9. Delegations
5	9.1 Randy Vaine spoke on behalf of Royal Canadian Legion - Fergus and
5	Elora Branches on the proposed cross walk policy.
5	R. Vaine stated that in 2025, the Royal Canadian Legion, Fergus and
5	Elora Branches requested that the Township install veterans’ crosswalks
5	in Elora and Fergus. There are currently over 110 veterans’ crosswalks
5	across Canada. These crosswalks are typically installed near cenotaphs
5	and Royal Canadian Legion Branches to honour veterans and active
5	service members, and they act as a permanent, year-round community
5	tribute.
6	He noted that Veterans should not be lumped into a group as part of a
6	policy. These men and women fought for Canada and for freedom.
6	Thousands of Canadian veterans have died or been injured while fighting
6	for the freedoms and the lifestyle that Canadians enjoy today.
6	9.2 Don Cubbidge, President of the Royal Canadian Legion Branch 229 in
6	Elora, spoke in opposition to the Township staff’s recommendation not to
6	install veterans’ crosswalks. Provided a brief overview of the Act of
6	Remembrance which happens across the Township and the Country
6	every November 11th, where the local branches organize and conduct
6	Remembrance Day services at their respective cenotaphs, it’s a solemn
6	occasion and an opportunity for the community to pay their respect to
6	those who made the ultimate sacrifice. Branch 229 has been conducting
6	these services since 1932.
6	He noted that the veterans’ crosswalks are not the same, as they are
6	meant to honour veterans every day, not just November 11th. They
6	recognize both past and current veterans, these veterans are neighbours,
6	coworkers, and friends in the community.
6	D. Cubbidge provided a brief presentation and commented that he
6	provided a mock up of what the cross walks could look like at a DEI
6	meeting in 2025, the mock-up is in line with the standards of the Royal
6	Canadian Legion.
6	9.3 Randy Graham, President Royal Canadian Legion Branch 275 Fergus,
6	spoke on behalf of the members and executive, echoing the thoughts of
6	D. Cubbidge, the staff recommendation to not allow veterans’ crosswalks
6	is not the right path to take in light of the debt owed to those who paid the
6	ultimate sacrifice for the rights and freedoms Canadians have today.
6	He stated that today we have annual remembrance services in the form
6	of cenotaph services, dinners, parades and student literary competitions
6	and stressed that remembrance is every day. Veterans’ crosswalks
6	provide a daily reminder of what men and women in the service do for us
6	every day. Communities all around Centre Wellington have already
6	approved and installed veterans’ crosswalks, our community is proud,
6	progressive, and thankful for service, and we need to continually
6	demonstrate this to our citizens and veterans.
6	R. Graham thanked Council and commented that this is the 100th
6	Anniversary of the Royal Canadian Legion and respectfully requested that
6	Council see this as timely and move forward.

8	WHEREAS generations of Canadians have served our country and the
8	world during times of war, military conflict, and peace. Through their
8	courage and sacrifice, these men and women have helped to ensure that
8	we live in freedom and peace, while also fostering freedom and peace
8	around the world; and
9	WHEREAS every year on November 11, Canadians pause in a silent
9	moment of remembrance for the men and women who have served, and
9	continue to serve our country during times of war, conflict, and peace; and
9	WHEREAS the Royal Canadian Legion’s mission to promote
9	remembrance is a yearlong commitment, they endeavor to promote it
9	through programs, services, and resources; and
9	WHEREAS the Royal Canadian Legion Branch 's in Elora and Fergus has
9	locally promoted the objectives of the Royal Canadian Legion’s mission
9	since 1952; and
9	WHEREAS the Township of Centre Wellington has previously partnered
9	with the Royal Canadian Legion which displays memorial banners,
9	wreaths, and flags to honour veterans, past and present, that served to
9	protect our peace and freedom; and
9	WHEREAS municipalities such as Town of Bradford West Gwillimbury,
9	City of Calgary, City of Belleville and Municipality of Chatham-Kent,
9	Wellington North, New Hamburg, Guelph, Halton Hills have installed
9	Veterans’ Crosswalks to provide a year-round reminder of the sacrifices
9	made by veterans and their families to keep our communities safe.
9	THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that Staff be directed to report back to
9	Council with costs and initiate the crosswalk installed before
9	Remembrance Day November 11, 2026.
9	THEREFORE, BE IT FINALLY RESOLVED that the Staff Report includes
9	information on the feasibility of the Veterans’ Crosswalk being located in
9	Elora, and Fergus in the Municipality of Centre Wellington and Wellington
9	County.
