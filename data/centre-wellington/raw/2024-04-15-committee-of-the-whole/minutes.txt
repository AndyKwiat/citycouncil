2	South Fergus Secondary Plan
2	Report from B. Salmon, Managing Director of Planning and
2	Development
2	B. Salmon, Managing Director of Planning and Development provided
2	background and introduced Dave Aston, Partner, MHBC.
2	D. Aston provided an overview of the secondary plan framework,
2	outlined other examples of secondary plans and indicated the proposed
2	secondary plan addresses the mix, arrangement and density of land
2	use, the collector road network, the size and location of parks and
2	school sites and the location of major services. He noted the secondary
2	plan implementation is accomplished through future zoning by-law
2	amendments, plans of subdivision and site plan applications.
2	D. Aston outlined the key components undertaken with respect to the
2	implementation of South Fergus Secondary Plan as well as the
2	development of the Community Design Guidelines, including the design
2	vision and principles. He responded to questions regarding policies to
2	ensure compatibility between existing uses and new uses, reducing
2	height of buildings and increasing density within low density areas, and
2	the estimated community park size and location.
2	Brett Salmon, Managing Director of Planning and Development outlined
2	the regulations regarding park dedication and the location of the various
2	parks, open spaces and sports fields within the secondary plan. He also
2	outlined the necessity for a mix of housing to mitigate the impact of
2	development on prime agricultural lands and the use of low impact
2	development such as rain and bioretention gardens, pervious pavement,
2	green roofs and infiltration.
2	Colin Baker, Managing Director of Infrastructure Services indicated staff
2	has initiated discussions with the Ministry of Transportation and has
2	been invited to participate in the development of a highway access
2	management plan that will review adjacent land uses, future traffic
2	patterns and determine the future design of Highway 6.
3	D. Aston and staff responded to additional questions regarding planning
3	for future cemeteries, stepping back levels on high density buildings,
3	future health care needs, and potential incentives to encourage low
3	impact development.

3	6.2 Amendments to the Procedure By-law 2021-31
3	Report from K. O’Kane, Manager of Legislative Services & Municipal
3	Clerk
3	Kerri O’Kane, Manager of Legislative Services & Municipal Clerk
3	indicated consideration of the draft procedural by-law was deferred from
3	the March 25th meeting. Staff is suggesting the following amendments
3	to improve meeting efficiencies:
3	• updates to the electronic meetings provision and associated definitions
3	to include hybrid participation;
3	• provisions regarding use of a consent agenda;
3	• changes to the Order of Business (inclusion of land acknowledgement,
3	consent agenda);
3	• adjustment to the notice provision for advisory committees to align with
3	current practices;
3	• reduction in time limit and restrictions on the number of opportunities a
3	delegate may appear;
3	• adjustment to the meeting curfew;
3	Councillor Adams expressed concerns with the structure of two Council
3	meetings per month and provided suggestions such as frequent
3	information reports prior to major decisions, consideration for additional
3	time to review the agendas and an awareness of upcoming projects.
3	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor
3	Jennifer Adams.
3	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington receive Report
3	COR2024-14, Amendments to the Procedure By-law 2021-31, dated
3	April 15, 2024 for information.
