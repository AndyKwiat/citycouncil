1	Background Study and By-law
1	5.1 Mayor Watters welcomed everyone to the public meeting of Council
1	under Section 12 of the Development Charges Act, 1997, as amended.
2	Mayor Watters indicated the purpose of this public meeting is to give the
2	public an opportunity to ask questions, provide comments, and make
2	representations on the Development Charges Background Study and
2	proposed by-law. Council will not be taking any action on this report or
2	by-law at this time.
2	Mayor Watters stated the DC Background Study and By-law were
2	posted on the Township website on December 19, 2025. A media
2	release and social media posts were released on the same day.
2	Mayor Watters advised notice was given in the Wellington Advertiser, a
2	local newspaper, on January 8, 2026, and a developer virtual meeting
2	occurred on January 21, 2026.
2	Mayor Watters called on Daryl Abbs, Partner, Watson & Associates
2	Economists Ltd., to do a brief presentation regarding the study and the
2	draft by-law.
2	Daryl Abbs, Partner, Watson & Associates Economists Ltd., provided a
2	development charges (DC) overview, outlined recent changes to the
2	Development Charges Act, the DC implementation policy, the growth
2	forecast summary and the proposed rates and policies. He outlined the
2	next steps, including the adoption of the by-law at the March 23rd
2	Council meeting.
2	Mayor Watters indicated written comments received to date are
2	attached to Report CAO2026-02 dated February 9, 2026.
2	Mayor Watters asked if anyone present wishes to speak at this time. He
2	asked that delegations to provide their name and adhere to the 5 minute
2	presentation rule.
2	DELEGATIONS
2	Joe Harris, Grand Highlands Home Builders Association requested
2	Council prioritize projects and expenditures that support the core
2	infrastructure necessary to enable new housing and growth –
2	specifically roads, wastewater, and water. He indicated now is not the
2	right time to increase development charges, an increase in DCs would
2	further impact project viability, delay or cancel planned developments in
2	the Township, and ultimately slow the delivery of much-needed housing.
2	He respectfully requested Council consider deferring a decision to afford
2	the industry additional time to review options.
3	Joseph Puopolo, Polocorp Inc., supported the comments of Mr. Harris
3	related to the housing crisis and housing costs and expressed concerns
3	with the DC background study and by-law related to the urban boundary
3	expansions. He did not want to see a two-tiered system of lands that
3	may be within or added to the urban boundary resulting in development
3	charges and perhaps a second class of lands coming in through another
3	mechanism.
3	Marcus Gagliardi, Senior Land Development Manager, indicated there
3	are lands owned by Cachet Homes inside and outside the current urban
3	affected by the DC projects pushed out to 2034 and 2035 that doesn’t
3	fall in line with the servicing program to bring those lands on-line. He
3	requested those projects to be brought back to the 20231 time-line to
3	align with the need for services and development. He noted housing is
3	expensive and all demographics are struggling to afford to own a home
3	and he reiterated the absorption of the DC increase cannot solely land
3	on the developer.
3	Staff and the consultant responded to questions regarding the rates
3	related to apartment buildings and muti-residential units, affordable
3	versus attainable housing, deferral options and the cost and the
3	responsibility for any updates to Township studies.
3	Mayor Watters thanked everyone for attending and declared the public
3	meeting adjourned.
3	Mayor Watters called for a 5 minute recess.

5	9. Consideration of Reports
5	9.1 Confederation Park Revitalization Plan
5	Report from L. Rogerson, Manager of Parks
5	L. Rogerson, Manager of Parks provided an overview of the report,
5	indicating the goals for Confederation Park included a) lead a
5	community design process, b) develop a revitalization plan design and
5	guiding document and c) inform the Township and Council of potential
5	design options and adaptations.
5	Kara Singbeil and Mandy Cadger, GSP provided an overview of the
5	community engagement undertaken and the guiding principles
5	developed. They outlined the revitalization plan and implementation
5	strategy, including the community priorities, costing & funding and the
5	flexible timelines. They highlighted the various park improvements, the
5	suggested materials to use and the budget and phasing outline.
5	Lucas Rogerson, Manage of Parks outlined the next steps, including
5	activation of the phase 1 recommendations within the $150,000 budget.
5	Staff and the consultants responded to questions regarding the removal
5	and replacement of the invasive species, trimming trees to preserve the
5	look out and sightline enhancement, other funding sources and potential
5	community partners, improved signage at the boat launch, lighting, and
5	the redesign of the play structure to include all types of play.
5	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor
5	Jennifer Adams.
5	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington receive for
5	information Confederation Park Revitalization Plan Report Number
5	CS2026-07;
6	AND THAT Council endorses the Confederation Park Revitalization Plan
6	recommendations outlined in Report Number CS2026-07.
6	CARRIED
