2	5.1 Mayor's County Council Report
2	Mayor Watters indicated the County Budget was passed with a 3.7%
2	increase. He indicated the increase will mean an additional $27 per year
2	for every $100,000 worth of assessed residential property value. Mayor
2	Watters also noted the County of Wellington is pursuing funding
2	opportunities in order to support the climate change adaption plan.
2	Mayor Watters also responded to a question regarding the involvement
2	of the area municipalities in climate change initiatives.
2	5.2 Councillor's Committee Updates
2	Members of Council provided updates and highlights of the recent
2	activities of the advisory committees, including the Fergus BIA and Farm
2	Safety.

2	7. Consent Items
2	Consent Agenda Items are considered routine in nature and are voted on
2	collectively. However, any Member of Council may request one or more items
2	be removed from the Consent Agenda for separate discussion.
2	Councillor Wilton requested a staff overview of item 7.6.
2	Ian Duncan Water Conservation Coordinator provided an update on the
2	2024 water conservation program, ongoing conservation efforts and
2	2025 program goals and future conservation program initiatives. He
2	outlined the branding initiative, the education and outreach program, the
2	various conservation programs including the water audit program, toilet
2	rebate program, water efficiency building guidelines, etc., and the
2	updated outdoor water use by-law.
2	Ian Duncan responded to questions regarding the current uptake of the
2	toilet rebate program and potential expansion of the program.
2	Mayor Watters asked if there where any further questions.
2	Councillor Adams posed a question to the Municipal By-law
2	Enforcement Officer regarding the number of complains with respect to
2	long grass and weeds.
2	Councillor Jefferson asked for a brief overview of the Annual Drinking
2	Water Report which was provided by Dino Masiero, Manager of Water
2	Services and Environmental Sustainability.

4	8. Consideration of Reports
4	8.1 Bridge and Transportation Network: Preliminary Preferred Alternative for
4	Bridges 2-WG, 3-E, 5-E and 7-E
4	Report from A. Dickieson, Engineering Services Coordinator
4	Adam Dickieson, Engineering Technologist outlined the project scope
4	and study area. He reviewed the impacts of the closures to
4	transportation and the various options to repair, to remove or to replace.
4	He reviewed the public and area resident consultation process
4	undertaken.
4	Adam Dickieson responded to questions regarding the differences in the
4	community’s response, the timing of the replacement, impact to the
4	farming practices, additional signage installed and the lack of impact to
4	the emergency response time.
4	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor
4	Barbara Lustgarten-Evoy.
5	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington endorse
5	Alternative No. 5 – Replacement of Bridges 2-WG, 5-E and 7-E and the
5	removal without replacement of Bridge 3-E as outlined in Report
5	IS2025-03, dated February 10, 2025;
5	AND THAT Council directs staff to provide the public with a Notice of
5	Project Completion for the Bridge and Transportation Network Study of
5	Bridges 2-WG, 3-E, 5-E and 7-E.
