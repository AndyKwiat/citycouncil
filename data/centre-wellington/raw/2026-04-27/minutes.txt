7	11.4 Community Planning Permit System (CPPS) Initiation
7	Report from M. Iglesias, Manager of Planning Services
7	M. Iglesias, Manager of Planning Services indicated a Community
7	Planning Permit System (CPPS) is a land use planning tool available to
7	municipalities in Ontario as authorized under Section 70.2 of the
7	Planning Act. It is intended as an alternative tool to help plan and shape
7	the future of a community based on its own vision and objectives. She
7	noted the process combines several typical planning application types,
7	such as zoning by-law amendments, minor variances, and site plan
7	approval into a single streamlined application and approval process with
7	a 45 day turnaround instead of 180 days common with other application
7	processes.
7	M. Iglesias outlined the key differences and benefits between the current
7	system and the CPPS, the lessons learned from other municipalities, the
7	intended scope of the request for proposal (RFP), timing of the project
7	short term and long term, proceeding with a feasibility study initially,
7	Discussion ensued and Members of Council discussed a phasing
8	approach, additional committee of the whole meetings or workshops and
8	revising the scope of the draft RFP.
8	Moved by Councillor Jennifer Adams, Seconded by Councillor
8	Bronwynne Wilton.
8	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington receives staff
8	Report PLN2026-19 regarding initiation of a Community Planning Permit
8	System (CPPS) for information;
8	CARRIED

8	11.5 Update on Ontario's "Bring-Your-Own" at Outdoor Festivals and Events
8	Framework
8	Report from A. Gilmore, Managing Director of Community Services
8	K. Bettiol, Manager of Community Development indicated the Province
8	of Ontario introduced a framework, titled “Bring-Your-Own” Alcoholic
8	Beverages at Outdoor Public Events (herein referred to as the "BYO
8	Framework"), allowing municipalities to opt-in to permitting attendees at
8	designated outdoor festivals and events to bring their own alcohol for
8	personal consumption within approved event areas. She noted
8	participation in the BYO Framework is voluntary, requiring municipalities
8	to formally opt-in by April 30, 2026 through passage of a participation
8	by-law.
8	K. Bettiol indicated staff have reviewed the BYO Framework and
8	considered its potential impacts on municipal operations, event
8	management, public safety, and community standards and are
8	recommending Centre Wellington not opt-in to the BYO Framework and
8	staff will continue to monitor the implementation of this program in other
8	municipalities and report back to Council should conditions or guidance
8	change.
8	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor Denis
9	Craddock.
9	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington receives Report
9	CS2026-18 for information;
9	AND THAT Council endorses the staff recommendation to not opt-in to
9	the Province of Ontario’s "Bring-Your-Own" at Outdoor Festivals and
9	Events Framework at this time;
9	AND THAT Council directs staff to continue to monitor the
9	implementation of this program in other municipalities and report back to
9	Council should conditions or guidance change.
9	CARRIED

9	Establishments Licensing update
9	Report from A. McNabb, Managing Director of Corporate Services &
9	Treasurer
9	Mayor Watters indicated in the interest of time and efficiency, the short-
9	term rental licensing by-law and the associated proposed zoning by-law
9	amendment will be combined and considered together.
9	Adam McNabb, Managing Director of Corporate Services & Treasurer
9	provided an overview of the report, indicating the purpose of the report
9	is to summarize and provide staff responses to the feedback received
9	from ConnectCW, provide an update regarding the proposed
9	amendments to the Township's zoning By-law, and obtain Council
9	direction on next steps as it pertains to the licencing of Short-Term
9	Rental Accommodations and Bed and Breakfast Establishments in
9	Centre Wellington.
9	B. Salmon, Managing Director of Planning and Development provided
9	background on the development of the draft zoning bylaw amendment,
9	indicating the proposed zoning definitions for amendment added a new
9	definition of "short-term rental accommodation" consistent with the draft
9	licensing by-law, and also modified existing zoning hotel and motel. He
9	explained Council heard from operators of existing short-term rental
9	accommodations in commercial areas, raising a concern that the
9	proposed by-law provisions would make their short-term rental use
9	illegal and expressing a very clear desire that existing short-term rentals
9	be permitted to continue as such and be protected from zoning
9	regulations that would interfere with their continued use.
9	Staff and Paul Dray, Dray & Associates responded to questions
9	regarding the change to the different definition of hotel and the inclusion
10	of a general provision indicating where a residential dwelling unit is a
10	permitted use as of April 27, 2026, an STR and Bed and Breakfast
10	establishment per permitted uses and shall be deemed to comply with
10	the provisions of the zoning bylaw so long as it continues to be used for
10	that purpose.
10	Staff responded to additional questions regarding use of the zoning to
10	assist with enforcement, uses associated with weddings, parties and
10	events on properties with STRs or Bed and Breakfasts, compliance with
10	zoning and parking provisions if establishments existed prior to the
10	passing of the by-laws, enforcement approach, staff resources, and
10	steps to address life safety concerns.
10	Moved by Councillor Denis Craddock, Seconded by Councillor Jennifer
10	Adams.

10	Accommodation and Bed and Breakfast Establishments Licensing for
