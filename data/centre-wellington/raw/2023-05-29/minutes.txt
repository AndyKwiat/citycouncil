2	5. Presentations and Announcements
2	5.1 Stephanie Toohill from the Save Middlebrook Bridge group provided an
2	overview of the history of the bridge, noting its construction in 1910 and
2	its relocation to its current position in 1946. She indicated the bridge has
2	significant heritage value as a rare structural example of a pin-connected
2	steel truss camelback truss bridge.
2	Mark Walker from the Save Middlebrook Bridge group noted the
2	significance of the bridge, indicating it is a safe connector route for Active
2	Transportation to trails and to many low traffic rural roads. He also noted
2	it is an access to a nature point of the Grand River, pedestrian access to
2	the Grand Valley Trailway, a visiting place of natural beauty for wildlife
2	observation and a popular brown trout fishing area.
2	Mark Walker indicated the group is advocating for the rehabilitation of the
2	bridge as:
2	1. A safe connector route for Active Transportation to the G2G, Cottontail
2	Road Trail, Elora-Guelph trail, Grand Valley hiking trail, Trans-Canada
2	Trail and to many low traffic rural roads away from dangerous vehicular
2	roads;
2	2. An “Access to Nature Point” to the Grand River heritage watershed;
2	3. Pedestrian access to the Grand Valley Trailway;
2	4. A rare structural example of a pin-connected steel truss bridge. One of
2	only four surviving truss bridges in Waterloo Region as noted in the
2	Cultural Heritage Impact Statement;
2	5. An important and well known historic landmark via river or land that
2	offers connection to our heritage, rural landscape and sense of place as
2	part of the Grand Watershed Trails Network;
2	6. A visiting place of natural beauty for wildlife observation and popular
2	brown trout fishing point.
2	He also noted the group has investigated potential sources for funding.
2	The Municipal Clerk provided procedural advice on how to bring forward
2	a motion for reconsideration.
2	Stephanie Toohill and Mark Walker responded to questions regarding
2	fundraising, consultation with the Mennonite community, discussions with
2	the Township of Woolwich and they provided clarification on their desire
2	to maintain the historic bridge for pedestrian traffic only. They indicated
2	they are requesting Council reconsider the previous decision to remove
2	the bridge and hold an additional public meeting.
3	5.2 Janet Harrop, President Wellington Federation of Agriculture provided
3	background and current statistics on agricultural land and activity. She
3	outlined the impact of Bill 97 and provincial policy statement changes
3	affecting Wellington County. She noted the Federation is not supportive
3	of the proposed lot creation policies which will lead to the loss of farmland,
3	and also impact farms in many ways ranging from impacts on land values,
3	to trespass, to road safety, to concerns over odour, dust, noise, etc. J.
3	Harrop noted the new lot creation policy will ultimately reduce
3	opportunities to create and expand livestock in Ontario.

3	Seconded by Councillor Wilton
3	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington support the
3	Wellington Federation of Agriculture by providing municipal support in the
3	form of comments through the Environmental Registry;
3	AND staff be directed to prepare a resolution supporting the concerns of
3	the Federation to share and circulate with the area municipalities.
3	After a brief discussion, Council Members withdrew the motion with a view
3	to consider the request of the Wellington Federation of Agriculture to
3	strongly oppose the proposed lot creation policies after the staff

3	7.1 2023 to 2026 Strategic Plan
3	Report from D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer
3	Presentation by Dave Augustyn & Jordan Mammoliti from Augustyn
3	Enterprises
3	D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer provided an overview of the report
3	presenting the 2023 to 2026 comprehensive strategic plan. He indicated
3	following the presentation from Dave Augustyn & Jordan Mammoliti from
4	Augustyn Enterprises, additional information will be provided regarding
4	business planning and advisory committees.
4	Dave Augustyn & Jordan Mammoliti from Augustyn Enterprises
4	introduced the 2023 to 2026 strategic plan as a living document to help
4	guide Council and staff.
4	Jordan Mammoliti outlined the project launch which included a full
4	document review and environmental scan of local Municipalities, a SWOT
4	(strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats) analysis and feedback
4	from the senior management team. She noted the extensive public
4	consultation process and the numerous Council and staff workshops to
4	develop the Strategic Plan Framework, including the Vision, Mission, and
4	Values.
4	Dave Augustyn provided an overview of the process followed to establish
4	the vision, mission and values in the strategic plan. Jordan Mammoliti
4	outlined the goals and priorities established.
4	D. Wilson reiterated the importance of the strategic plan in assisting
4	Council and staff to set the priorities each year. He noted the corporate
4	business plan is a key component linking the annual budget to the
4	strategic plan. D. Wilson outlined the process to develop the corporate
4	business plan and associated report card.
4	D. Wilson outlined the proposed advisory committee structure directly
4	connected to the strategic plan. This structure includes existing
4	committees such as Heritage Centre Wellington and the Diversity, Equity
4	& Inclusion Advisory Committee and proposes two additional advisory
4	committees, Economic Prosperity and Growth Advisory Committee and
4	the Healthy Communities Advisory Committee, including two working
4	groups the Activity, Health and Wellness working group and the Active
4	Transportation and Environment working group.
4	D. Wilson outlined he next steps, including the development of the terms
4	of reference for each committee, resident nominations and Council
4	appointments.
4	Staff responded to questions regarding ensuring a youth representative
4	or voice, benchmarking success or the report card, and highlighting and
4	recognizing volunteers.
4	Councillor Wilton noted the plan was a result of collaboration between
4	Council and staff, and expressed concerns that there is no mention of
5	business attraction and retention, the significant or importance of water
5	and attainable or affordable housing.
5	Dave Augustyn noted because the strategic plan is a living document,
5	new goals and initiatives can be added through the corporate business
5	plan process.
5	Moved by Councillor MacDonald
5	Seconded by Councillor Adams
5	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington approve the 2023
5	to 2026 Strategic Plan;
5	AND THAT Township Staff be directed to prepare an annual Corporate
5	Business Plan, starting with the 2024 Budget process;
5	AND THAT the Council Advisory Committee structure be approved as
5	outlined in report CAO2023-08 dated May 29, 2023;
5	Councillor Wilton proposed the following amendment to be added to the
5	end of the resolution:
5	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor
5	Barbara Lustgarten-Evoy.
5	THAT the Council and staff conduct an internal review and refresh of the
5	Strategic Plan prior to the 2025 budget process.
