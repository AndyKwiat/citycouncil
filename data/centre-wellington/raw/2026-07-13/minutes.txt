4	12.2 Naturalized Gardens and Vegetation Maintenance
4	Report from Satnam Chauhan, Supervisor of Bylaw and Licensing
4	Satnam Chauhan, Supervisor of Bylaw and Licensing provided a report
4	recommending amendments the Township’s Clean Yards By-law. These
4	proposed changes would recognize and permit naturalized gardens under
4	defined maintenance standards, improve clarity and enforceability of the
4	by-law, and would assist with the overlapping yard maintenance
4	provisions from the Property Standards By-law.
4	He noted the proposed amendments are intended to preserve the
4	Township’s ability to address unmanaged and safety nuisance concerns
4	at a property. It would also assist with vegetation definitions by
4	distinguishing intentional and maintained naturalized landscaping from
4	neglected property conditions.
4	S. Chauhan responded to comments and questions from Council about
4	the percentage of By-law complaints relating to neglected property
4	conditions, the Clean Yards By-law relating to vacant building lots, public
4	notice of changes and overall enforcement of the By-law and the
4	complaints process.
4	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor Denis
4	Craddock.
4	THAT Council receives Staff Report COR2026-31 "Naturalized Gardens
4	and Vegetation Maintenance"; and
5	THAT Council endorses the amending by-laws described in this report to
5	amend the current Clean Yards By-law and Property Standards By-law.
5	CARRIED

5	Report from Kristen Bettiol, Manager of Community Development
5	Kristen Bettiol, Manager of Community Development gave a presentation

6	12.6 Asset Management - 2026 Update Report
6	Report from Adam McNabb, Chief Financial Officer & Treasurer
6	Adam McNabb, Chief Financial Officer & Treasurer provided an update
6	on the 2025 Asset Management Plan, endorsed by Council in June 2025.
6	He provided an overview of the progress, asset conditions, financial
6	update, the levels of service, other areas of focus for 2026/2027, the
6	overall asset management strategy, financing strategy and doubling of
6	funding of the Ontario Community Infrastructure Fund (OCIF).
7	A. McNabb responded to comments and questions from Council
7	regarding designation of vehicles, recreational equipment, other sources
7	of funding, target levels of service, modelling future levels of service,
7	assessment of condition and life cycle management.
7	A. Gilmore responded to questions from Council regarding bike lanes.
7	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor
7	Barbara Lustgarten-Evoy.
7	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington receive report
7	FIN2026-15; and
7	THAT Council direct staff to communicate the support resolution to the
7	Acting Minister of Infrastructure and MPP Joseph Racinsky; and
7	WHEREAS recent municipal infrastructure funding programs by the
7	Government of Canada and Province of Ontario have tended to prioritize
7	growth-related or housing-enabling infrastructure projects over
7	infrastructure renewal needs;
7	AND WHEREAS the Government of Ontario has regulated municipal
7	infrastructure asset management through O. Reg 588/17 (Asset
7	Management Planning for Municipal Infrastructure) under the
7	Infrastructure for Jobs and Prosperity Act, 2015;
7	AND WHEREAS municipal asset management plans and the Financial
7	Accountability Office of Ontario have identified that municipalities have
7	significant state-of-good-repair backlogs and renewal needs for
7	infrastructure;
7	AND WHEREAS the Government of Ontario doubled the Ontario
7	Community Infrastructure Fund (OCIF) from $200M/year to $400M/year
7	for 2021-2026 but has not publicly committed to maintaining this level of
7	funding beyond 2026;
7	AND WHEREAS the OCIF has become a vital and indispensable
7	resource for municipalities, assisting them with improving their asset
7	management programs and meeting community preserving infrastructure
7	renewal needs;
7	AND WHEREAS increased geopolitical uncertainty is threatening the
7	economic prosperity of Canada, Ontario, and every municipality;
8	AND WHEREAS municipal infrastructure is a key enabler and preserver
8	of economic prosperity;
8	AND WHEREAS the Municipal Finance Officers’ Association of Ontario
8	(MFOA) has heard from its members and their municipalities concerns
8	regarding the potential erosion of existing municipal infrastructure and
8	thus service levels in light of the ongoing focus on housing enabling
8	infrastructure by grant funding programs;
8	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that The Township of Centre
8	Wellington supports MFOA’s letter to the Ministry of Infrastructure
8	requesting that the doubling of the Ontario Community Infrastructure
8	Fund be made permanent in advance of the allocations for the 2027
8	calendar year, helping municipalities to continue building housing-
8	enabling infrastructure while preserving the economic value provided by
8	existing infrastructure.
8	CARRIED
8	12.7 Water and Wastewater Public Corporation Model
8	Report from Dan Wilson, CAO
8	Dan Wilson, CAO provided a report related to the establishment of a new
8	model for water and wastewater service delivery through municipal
8	service corporations, the Province of Ontario is advancing a legislative
8	and regulatory framework to enable the creation of Water and Wastewater
8	Public Corporations.
8	He indicated the model is not framed as a voluntary tool for municipalities
8	and that the province has established a list of municipalities who will be
8	required to deliver water and wastewater services through the publicly
8	owned Water and Wastewater Corporation.
8	D. Wilson responded to comments and questions from Council regarding
8	water wastewater billing, debt and funding, AMO’s involvement,
8	Provincial intent, timelines, staff time and expression of potential interest.
8	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor Lisa
8	MacDonald.
8	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington directs staff to
8	respond to the Province’s Expression of Interest, confirming potential
8	interest in implementing a Water and Wastewater Public Corporation.
9	CARRIED
