1	3. Short Term Rentals Workshop
1	3.1 Introduction and Brief Overview (Presentation to be Added)
1	Adam McNabb, Managing Director of Corporate Services & Treasurer
1	introduced Paul Dray, Dray & Associates and outlined the agenda for
1	the workshop. He outlined the journey taken to date regarding the
1	development of a draft short term rental (STR) licensing by-law and the
1	benefits of licensing these establishments.
1	Adam McNabb outlined the common components of a licensing by-law
2	as well as other mechanisms used to regulate STRs. He indicated
2	municipalities have a legal right to implement programs to regulate and
2	license STR operations and outlined the limitations of licensing and land
2	use tools also available.
2	Paul Dray, Dray & Associates outlined the various approaches other
2	municipalities have taken regarding the regulation and licensing of short
2	term rentals. He noted zoning provisions are used to provide definitions
2	and to regulate land use and licensing is used to regulate the activities
2	conducted on the land.
2	P. Dray explained the benefits of licensing and using an administrative
2	monetary penalty system. He responded to questions regarding
2	enforcement, parking restrictions and the penalties for not following the
2	licensing rules.
2	3.4 Areas for Discussion
2	Adam McNabb outlined the areas of concerns and matters for
2	discussion, including:
2	 Responsible Person
2	 Additional Residential Units used as STRs
2	 Inspections
2	 Distance Between STRs
2	 Number of STR operating in Centre Wellington
2	 Maximum Occupancy of STRs
2	 Treatment of Existing vs. New STRs
2	 License Validity Period
2	 License Cost
2	 Parking
2	 Enforcement
2	Responsible Person
2	Definition of Responsible Person
2	Current By-law
2	o Owner of property or a person 25 years of Age or older
2	duly appointed by the Owner
2	o Responsible Person shall not have a principal residence
2	that is outside the Township
2	o Must be able to respond to an enquiry within 30 minutes of
2	a request from an Officer.
2	Other options for consideration
2	o Alternate age
2	o Remove age requirement and add 24/7 availability and
3	response time within 1 hour of contact
3	o Alternate time to respond or proximity limit
3	o Require Responsible Person to live on-site
3	Staff and Prosecutor Recommendation
3	 Remove age requirement and add 24/7 availability and response
3	time within 1 hour of contact
3	Council Direction/Consensus
3	 Owner of property or a person 25 years of Age or older duly
3	appointed by the Owner
3	 Must be able to respond to an enquiry within 30 minutes of a
3	request from an Officer.
3	Discussion ensued regarding grandfathering, legal non-complying
3	or non-complying status and potentially limiting the number of
3	STRs to 1 per property.
3	Council Direction/Consensus
3	 Limit the number of STRs permitted to 1 per property.
3	Inspections
3	Current By-law
3	o Physical Inspections prior to issuance of license (Fire
3	Chief, Chief Building Official, Supervisor of By-law and
3	licensing)
3	o Compliance with Fire Code, Property Standards, Clean
3	Yards By-law
3	o Declaration that electrical is in good working order
3	o Septic inspection report (adequately sized, current
3	standards, good working order) where applicable
3	o Annual Inspection of chimney, flue pipes (inspected,
3	cleaned and safe to use)
3	Other options for consideration:
3	o Self Declaration signed with every license application
3	indemnifying the Township
3	o Proof of inspection in the last XX years
3	o Proof of insurance
3	Staff and Prosecutor Recommendation
3	 Inspections to demonstrate compliance with applicable Laws
3	including but not limited to:
3	o STR By-law
3	o Fire Protection and Prevention Act
3	o The Electricity Act
4	o The Township’s Property Standards By-Law as amended
4	or superseded
4	o Any other applicable laws that apply to the Licensed
4	Premises
4	Council Direction/Consensus
4	 Agree with Staff/Prosecutor Recommendation
4	Distance between STRs
4	Establishes the minimum distance between operating STRs
4	Current By-law
4	o Minimum distance between operating STRs must be 100
4	metres outside of the historic area and no minimum within
4	the historic area
4	Options for Consideration
4	Minimum distance between STRs of 100 metres everywhere
4	o Existing STRs are exempt from minimum distance
4	requirements; however, no new STRs will be licensed if
4	within XX metres of another operating STR within the
4	historic area
4	o Impose minimum distance separations outside of the
4	historic area
4	Discussion ensued regarding zoning regulations required to
4	establish a minimum distance between operating STRs.
4	Council Direction/Consensus
4	 Revisit minimum distance between STRs in the future through
4	zoning
4	Limiting the Number of STRs Operating in Centre Wellington
4	Current By-law
4	o No limit on number of STRs
4	Options for consideration
4	o Impose limit on number of licenses issued
4	o Definition of licensed STR – is this a suite or a property
4	(how is a property with multiple suites licensed)? – Will this
4	influence the maximum number of licenses?
4	o Different license types with individual maximums (Guest
4	room, Principal Residence, ARU, etc.)
4	Staff and Prosecutor Recommendation
4	 Limit the number of STRs operating within Centre Wellington –
5	establish a cap and waitlist
5	 Allow issuance of one license per property
5	Discussion ensued regarding the current number of STRs in
5	Centre Wellington, enforcement tools required to monitor any
5	capping restrictions
5	Council Direction/Consensus
5	 Limit the number of STRs that can operate within Centre
5	Wellington – establish a cap and waitlist
5	 Allow issuance of one license per property
5	 Staff to update the inventory list of existing STRs to provide for a
5	realistic cap.
5	Establish maximum occupancy numbers on operating STRs
5	Current By-law
5	 In the urban area, the maximum is the lower of:
5	o 2 per bedroom
5	o Maximum of 8
5	 In the rural area, the maximum is 2 people per available parking
5	space
5	Staff and Prosecutor Recommendation
5	 Two per bedroom identified in the application to a maximum of 8
5	persons
5	Council Direction/Consensus
5	 Agree with Staff/Prosecutor Recommendation
5	Treatment of Existing vs New STRs
5	Are operating STRs exempt from all restrictions imposed through
5	By-law?
5	Current By-law
5	o Yes.Currently operating STRs are exempt from restrictions
5	such as location, proximity, parking, etc.
5	Other options for consideration
5	o No exemptions granted – all operating STRs must strictly
5	adhere to By-law restrictions
5	o Exemptions only granted in historical area for currently
5	operating STRs
5	Council Direction/Consensus
6	 Recognize zoning provisions would have to be introduced to
6	impose restrictions such as location, proximity, parking, etc.
6	License Validity Period
6	The duration of time a license issued by the Township will remain
6	valid
6	Current By-law
6	o Non Transferable
6	o 3 years
6	Other options for consideration
6	o Any alternate duration
6	o With or without caveat that any license issued by the
6	Township expires upon the sale or transfer of the property
6	without refund (prorated or otherwise)
6	Council Direction/Consensus
6	o Non -Transferable
6	o 3 years
6	License Cost
6	How much should the license fee be?
6	Current By-law
6	 Not defined, but staff recommendation is that this fee should be
6	sufficient to recover costs associated with administering and
6	enforcing the program
6	Other options for consideration
6	 Operate at a loss
6	Council Direction/Consensus
6	 Fee to be adequate to recover costs associated with
6	administering and enforcing the program.
6	Parking

6	Current By-law
6	o Inside the historic area – none defined
6	o Outside of the historic area – based on maximum
6	occupancy – one space for every 2 renters aged 16 and

6	Other options for consideration
7	o A consistent application regardless of location
