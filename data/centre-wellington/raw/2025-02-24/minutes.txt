2	8.1 RZ08/24 - 7120 Sixth Line, Belwood
3	Mayor Watters indicated the public meeting is to present and receive
3	public input regarding a proposed zoning by-law amendment on the
3	subject land known as 7120 Sixth Line in the former Township of West
3	Garafraxa.
3	He noted the purpose of the proposed zoning by-law amendment is to
3	change the zoning from “A - Agricultural” to “A - Agricultural Exception”
3	on the retained parcel to restrict residential uses. The amendment also
3	seeks to permit the maximum floor area for all accessory buildings to be
3	6.2% of the total lot area on the severed parcel.
3	The effect of the proposed zoning by-law amendment is to implement
3	zoning regulations necessary to facilitate a farm dwelling surplus
3	severance and allow an increased maximum floor area on the severed
3	parcel. The zoning amendment is a condition of approval of the County
3	of Wellington Land Division Committee Application B54-24.
3	He indicated if a person or public body would otherwise have an ability
3	to appeal the decision of the Township of Centre Wellington to the
3	Ontario Land Tribunal (OLT) but the person or public body does not
3	make oral submissions at a public meeting or make written submissions
3	to the Township of Centre Wellington before the by-law is passed, the
3	person or public body is not entitled to appeal the decision.
3	Mayor Watters stated if a person or public body does not make oral
3	submission at the public meeting, or make written submissions to the
3	Township of Centre Wellington before the by-law is passed, the person
3	or public body may not be added as the party to the hearing of an
3	appeal before the OLT unless, in the opinion of the Tribunal, there are
3	reasonable grounds to do so.
3	Mayor Watters advised if anyone wishes to be notified of the adoption of
3	the proposed zoning by-law amendment, a written request must be filed
3	with the Municipal Clerk, Township of Centre Wellington, 1 MacDonald
3	Square, Elora, ON, N0B 1S0.
3	The Municipal Clerk advised notice was mailed to prescribed persons,
3	agencies and public bodies on or before January 23, 2025. A copy was
3	also placed in the Wellington Advertiser on January 23, 2025. The
3	Municipal Clerk confirmed the requirement for giving notice was
3	completed on January 23, 2025.
3	Mayor Watters asked Deanna Maiden, Senior Development Planner, to
3	introduce the application.
4	Deanna Maiden provided an overview of the application, indicating the
4	purpose is to change the zoning to restrict residential uses on the
4	retained parcel as well as permission for a maximum floor areas for all
4	accessory buildings to be 6.2% of the total lot area on the severed
4	parcel. She indicated the zoning bylaw amendment is to facilitate a farm
4	dwelling surplus severance and allow an increased maximum floor area
4	on the severed parcel.
4	Jeff Buisman, agent for the applicant, indicated he is available to
4	respond to any questions.
4	Mayor Watters indicated there are no delegations registered and asked
4	if there was anyone present who wished to speak to the application.
4	There being none, Mayor Watters asked for questions from Council
4	There being no questions from Members of Council, Mayor Watters
4	thanked everyone for attending and indicated Council will be considering
4	the by-law at a future Council meeting.
4	He declared this public meeting adjourned.

7	12.1 Home Energy Efficiency Transition Program
7	Report from D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer
7	Dan Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer indicated the Home Energy
7	Efficiency Transition (HEET) program initiated by the County of
7	Wellington plans to provide financial assistance to homeowners in
7	Wellington County who wish o make energy-efficient upgrades to their
7	homes. He introduced Karen Chisholme, Climate Change and
7	Sustainability Manager, County of Wellington to outline the program.
7	Karen Chisholme indicated the purpose of the program is to accelerate
7	the adoption of low-emissions and energy conservation home retrofits
7	and address energy poverty. She outlined the program features,
7	including financing and incentives, concierge, centralized web platform,
7	climate-ready pathway plan and the promotion of low-carbon resilience
7	and the terms of the loans. K. Chisholme noted the various roles and
7	responsibilities of municipal staff and outlined the next steps in the
7	process.
7	K. Chisholme responded to questions regarding incentives for multi-
7	project initiatives, and coordination with other provincial and federal
7	programs.
7	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor Lisa
7	MacDonald.
7	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington supports the
7	implementation of the Home Energy Efficiency Transition Program
7	within the Township of Centre Wellington;
7	AND THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington direct staff
7	to prepare the necessary Local Improvement Charge bylaw for the
7	implementation of the Home Energy Efficiency Transition Program to be
7	presented to Council for consideration at a future meeting;
7	AND THAT the Mayor be authorized to provide a letter of support to the
7	Federation of Canadian Municipalities (FCM) on behalf of Council, in
8	support of the County of Wellington's application for grant funding to
8	assist with the implementation of the Home Energy Efficiency Transition
8	Program.
8	CARRIED
8	12.2 Ontario Heritage Act Alternative Notice Policy
8	Report from M. Iglesias, Manager of Planning Services
8	Mariana Iglesias, Manager of Planning Services provided an overview of
8	the report, indicating the Ontario Heritage Act requires notices to be
8	published in a newspaper having general circulation in a municipality.
8	She explained the Ontario Heritage Act and the Municipal Act allow
8	municipalities to adopt policies to publish notices in alternative media
8	sources, such as a website. M. Iglesias indicated staff is recommending
8	Council adopts the proposed draft Ontario Heritage Act Alternative
8	Notice Policy allowing the Township to publish notices required by the
8	Ontario Heritage Act on the Township’s website.
8	M. Iglesias responded to questions regarding the volume of notices
8	currently placed in the newspaper, the estimated cost and time
8	efficiency savings, no impact to right of appeal, and consolidation of
8	notices.
8	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor
8	Kimberley Jefferson.
8	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington adopts the
8	Ontario Heritage Act Alternative Notice Policy, as outlined in staff report
8	PLN2025-12 dated February 24, 2025, and enacts the corresponding
8	by-law.
8	CARRIED
