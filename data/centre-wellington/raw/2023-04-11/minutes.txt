1	5.1 Community Improvement Plan (CIP)
2	Luciano Piccioi, President, RCI Consulting, indicated he is currently
2	working with both the Township and the County to coordinate efforts in
2	the Community Improvement Plan (CIP). He updated Council on the CIP
2	Basics, purpose of the review, current CIP and incentive programs, key
2	findings to date and presented the draft CIP goals and incentive
2	programs. He indicated there will be further opportunity for Council to
2	provide input at the Statutory Public Meeting.
2	The consultant responded to questions regarding the façade and property
2	improvement grants, the next steps in the process, and long term rental /
2	affordable housing incentives.
2	5.2 James Scott re Disc Golf
2	James Scott indicated he is an advocate for disc golf in Centre Wellington.
2	His is requesting Parks and Recreation staff consider creating a pocket
2	sized 9 hole disc golf course at the Centre Wellington Community
2	Sportsplex. He indicated it is accessible, equitable and has a positive
2	impact with disc golf tourism. He indicated the estimated cost is $10 000.
2	6. Delegations
2	6.1 Linda Williamson spoke to item 7.3 2023 Parking Strategy. She indicated
2	she is a resident of Elora with concerns with proper parking and noise
2	enforcement in the downtown. She believes the needs and wishes of
2	residents and merchants need to all be considered equally and the
2	business community is divided. She would like to see the parking return
2	to pre-covid ways if a solution can not be found to accommodate
2	everyone.
2	6.2 Cathi Bastien addressed Council regarding Item 7.3 2023 Parking
2	Strategy. She indicated she is a resident and business owner and has
2	concerns with extended patios and the lack of fairness for local
2	businesses. She believes some are no longer feeling welcome in the
2	downtown and the inequities in the number of seats allocated to each
2	restaurant, the size of the extended business space and the use of
2	sidewalks for businesses. She would like to see all businesses treated
2	equally.
2	6.3 Wayne Bridgman spoke to Council regarding Item 7.3 2023 Parking
2	Strategy. He indicated the patio season is too long, believes it creates an
2	unsafe walking environment, has concerns with accessibility and safety
2	and the ability for quick pick up at all downtown businesses. He believes
2	the Township should support all businesses.
2	6.4 Staci Barron addressed the Strategic Plan and requested a time
2	extension to allow residents more time to complete the survey. She spoke
2	to her concerns regarding patios and the policy content. She indicated
3	patios should be a privilege not a right and violations should effect the
3	issuance of permits in future years.
3	6.5 Mary Anne Neville addressed Item 7.3 2023 Parking Strategy indicated
3	she agrees with previous delegations; however, she is not a business
3	owner, but a downtown resident. She raised safety concerns and parking
3	concerns for residents.
3	6.6 Maclean Hann spoke to Item 7.3 2023 Parking Strategy, indicating he is
3	representing owners and operators of patios on Township property. He
3	stated the economic recovery is far from over and they are happy to work
3	with Council to address the complaints; however, he is in support of the
3	policy. He explained the costs to business owner cost of building a patio
3	and outlined how they provide more employment and service area for
3	individuals.

3	7.2 Pierpoint Research Project Update
3	Report from M. Iglesias, Senior Planner
3	M. Iglesias, Senior Planner outlined the history of project and introduced
3	the consultant.
3	Annie Veilleux, Archaeological Services Inc. gave a presentation
3	regarding the Pierpoint Settlement Research Project. She indicated the
3	settlement is the earliest known non-indigenous settlement in Centre
3	Wellington with ties to black Canadians. She further explained Richard
3	Pierpoint was designated a national historic person by Parks Canada in
3	2020. She outlined what a Cultural Heritage Landscape is and how they
3	work and reviewed the recommendations for the Pierpoint Settlement
3	area.
4	M. Iglesias, Senior Planner reviewed the recommendations before council
4	including the request that the flyfishing reserve be added as the 19th
4	Cultural Heritage Landscape, be included in the Official Plan amendment
4	project and that Council pursue designation of the subject property.
4	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor Denis
4	Craddock.
4	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington receives for
4	information the "Pierpoint Settlement Research Project: Interpretation
4	Framework" report prepared by Archaeological Services Inc., provided as
4	Attachment 1 to staff report PLN2023-17; and
4	THAT the Pierpoint Fly Fishing Nature Reserve be added to the Centre
4	Wellington Cultural Heritage Landscape Inventory and the Cultural
4	Heritage Landscapes Official Plan Amendment Project as an identified
4	significant cultural heritage landscape; and
4	THAT staff be directed to bring forward a report concerning heritage
4	designation of the Pierpoint Fly Fishing Nature Reserve as a significant
4	cultural heritage landscape under Part IV of the Ontario Heritage Act;
4	AND THAT staff be directed to report back on the development of a
4	Pierpoint Community Working Group to advance work associated with the
4	Pierpoint Research Project.
4	CARRIED
4	7.3 2023 Parking Strategy
4	Report from D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer
4	D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer reviewed the report, he indicated
4	a long term parking strategy is planned for 2024. He highlighted staff are
4	not recommending street closures in Elora due to the closure of East Mill
4	for construction; however in Fergus it is recommended to continue with
4	the closure of Provost Lane. Further, staff recommends continuation of
4	parking enforcement with Alpha for another year at the same service level
4	of services as 2022. In addition, staff is recommending continuation of an
4	improved shuttle service between Fergus and Elora.
4	Staff responded to questions regarding accessible parking spots, shuttle
4	bus ridership, the long-term parking strategy including a parking permit
4	system and the patio by-law.
4	Moved by Councillor Denis Craddock, Seconded by Councillor
4	Bronwynne Wilton.
5	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington approve the 2023
5	parking strategy outlined in Report CAO2023-05 dated April 11, 2023.
5	AND THAT the Managing Director of Corporate Services & Treasurer be
5	authorized to sign:
5	a. A 2023 service agreement with Alpha Technology Systems Inc.
5	b. An 2023 Access and Use Agreement with the owner of the
5	parking lot located at 739 St Andrew St W, Fergus.
5	c. 2023 Service Agreements for the use of a bus and taxi as a
5	shuttle.

5	Annual Speed Limit and Traffic Calming Review and Recommendations
5	Report from A. Gilmore, Manager of Engineering
5	Adam Gilmore, Manager of Engineering reviewed the report, indicating
5	this review and recommendation is a requirement of the Speed Limit
5	Policy approved in 2021 which includes the monitoring and review of
5	speed limits and traffic calming measures.
5	Staff responded to questions regarding the reason for the increased
5	funding and the methodology behind the recommended changes.
5	Richard Wright, delegation, addressed speed concerns on the Second
5	Line in West Garafraxa. He is in support of the four way stop on the
5	Second Line and Sideroad 20 due to visibility and raised concerns with
5	the speed reduction on Second Line, a lack of enforcement and ongoing
5	construction during the speed observation. He believes a solution is
5	additional signage and enforcement as opposed to speed reduction.
5	Staff responded to further questions regarding public consultation and
5	circulation of the proposed changes to the public and follow up on the
5	effectiveness of the change.
5	Moved by Councillor Kimberley Jefferson, Seconded by Councillor
5	Bronwynne Wilton.
5	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington endorses
5	recommendations outlined in Report No. IS2023-09 related to proposed
5	speed limit reductions on Township roads, to be implemented through an
5	upcoming amendment to the Township's Consolidated Speed Bylaw,
5	AND THAT Council directs staff to proceed with implementing speed
5	reduction measures as outlined in Report No. IS2023-09,
6	AND THAT Council approves increasing the budget for capital project
6	2023-042: Traffic Calming Measures 2023 from $125,000 to $250,000.

6	7.5 Request for Tender #06-23: Reconstruction of Beatty Line
6	Report from S. Wiles, Purchasing & Risk Supervisor
6	C. Baker, Managing Director of Infrastructure Services indicated this
6	project is a full reconstruction of Beatty Line from Hill Street to North of
6	Colborne, which includes the addition of storm sewers, a new roundabout,
6	traffic calming (narrow lanes), streetlights, landscaping and additional
6	servicing.
6	D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer reviewed the request for tender,
6	indicated there were 37 plan takers and 9 submitted proposals received.
6	He reviewed the project funding, indicated the project is under budget and
6	noted staff is recommending awards to Cox Construction Limited.
6	Moved by Councillor Lisa MacDonald, Seconded by Councillor
6	Kimberley Jefferson.
6	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington award Request
6	For Tender #06-23 regarding the Reconstruction of Beatty Line, to Cox
6	Construction Limited at a contract price of $4,606,609.10, including
6	contingency and excluding HST.
6	AND THAT project funding be updated as shown in report COR2023-32
6	dated April 11, 2023.
