2	8.1 Housing Needs Assessment Report Findings
2	Report from B. Salmon, Managing Director of Planning and
3	Development
3	Erik Karvinen, Watson & Associates indicated the Housing Needs
3	Assessment provides an assessment of current and future housing
3	needs and strategic recommendations for the Township. He noted the
3	study analyzes economic, demographic and real estate trends to inform
3	future housing demand and policy for the next few decades.
3	E. Karvinen outlined the population and housing growth trends,
3	suggesting population growth in Centre Wellington has accelerated post
3	2021, growing by 7% to 34,300 in 2024. He also outlined the existing
3	and future housing needs and provided the study conclusions and
3	recommendations.
3	E. Karvinen and staff responded to questions regarding temporary
3	agriculture housing, additional residential lots on serviced lands, the
3	need to update the definition of affordable housing in accordance with
3	the new P.P.S., 2024 and update affordable housing targets to align with
3	affordable housing needs, and the next steps.
3	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor Lisa
3	MacDonald.
3	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington receive the
3	Housing Needs Assessment report prepared by Watson and Associates
3	Economists; and,
3	THAT staff be directed to report to Council on future housing related
3	initiatives based on the Housing Needs Assessment on or before
3	September 29, 2025.
3	CARRIED

4	8.4 Corporate Climate Action Plan Terms of Reference
4	Report from M. Tamlyn, Climate Change Coordinator
4	M. Tamlyn, Climate Change Coordinator outlined the primary objectives
4	of the corporate climate action plan, including:
4	- Establishing a baseline of current GHG emissions
4	- Conducting a climate risk assessment for key infrastructure and
4	services
4	- Identifying priorities, pathways, and timelines for implementing
4	effective mitigation and adaptation strategies
4	- Setting ambitious but achievable targets
4	- Integrating climate considerations into municipal business processes;
4	and
4	- Identifying and secure climate budget allocation and funding sources
4	He outlined the pathways, priorities and targets, the deliverables and the
4	expected project completion by Q2 of 2026.

5	8.5 Operations Centre - Solar Panel Update
5	Report from M. Tamlyn, Climate Change Coordinator
5	M. Tamlyn, Climate Change Coordinator provided an overview of the
5	report, including the environmental and financial benefits of solar. He
5	indicated after review, a net-metered rooftop system is considered the
5	preferred choice. He suggested this design utilizes existing
5	infrastructure, can accommodate energy needs throughout the year, and
5	provides the most significant net return and GHG reduction.
5	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor Lisa
5	MacDonald.
5	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington endorses the
5	recommended solar panel system at the new Operations Centre,
5	described as Option 1 and outlined in Report No. IS2025-26.
5	AND THAT Council authorizes Township staff to receive competitive
5	bids, through the General Contractor for the project, for the inclusion of
5	a solar panel system to an upset limit of $1.2 million as outlined in
5	Report No. IS2025-15.

5	Report from A. Gilmore, Manager of Engineering
5	Adam Gilmore, Manager of Engineering provided an overview of the
5	report, indicating the Township's Active Transportation and Mobility Plan
5	has identified needs for safety and crossing improvements at several
5	intersections, noting that intersections located along school routes
5	should be prioritized.
5	The four locations noted as high priority for improvement are:
5	1. The intersection of Beatty Line, Sideroad 18, and Farley Road in
5	Fergus;

6	in Fergus;
6	3. The intersection of Gartshore Street, Middleton Avenue, and Forfar
6	Street East in Fergus; and,
6	4. The intersection of David Street and Irvine Street in Elora.
6	Moved by Councillor Denis Craddock, Seconded by Councillor Barbara
6	Lustgarten-Evoy.
6	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington endorses
6	intersection improvement recommendations as outlined in Report No.
6	IS2025-24;
6	AND THAT Council approves an expenditure of $17,500 to hire four new
6	crossing guards starting in September 2025 through to December 2025

6	AND THAT Council directs Township staff to bring forward operating

9	8.15 Parks and Recreation Plan - Final Approval
9	Report from P. Newson, Managing Director of Community Services
9	P. Newson, Managing Director of Community Services provided an
9	overview of the report, indicating the final report includes the following:
9	- Engagement Report: A summary of community input received,
9	highlighting themes and key concerns raised by participants.
9	- Needs Assessment: An extensive review identifying the existing
9	opportunities and needs regarding parks and recreation across the
9	Township, projecting future requirements.
10	- Parks Plan: A framework categorizing parks and providing a roadmap
10	for future parkland dedication and development, including a draft
10	Parkland Dedication Bylaw for Council’s future consideration.
10	- Parks and Recreation Plan: Defined themes with actionable
10	recommendations focusing on partnerships, facility development,
10	programming, and outdoor spaces.
10	Staff responded to questions regarding the next steps and budget
10	implications.
10	Moved by Councillor Lisa MacDonald, Seconded by Councillor Jennifer
10	Adams.
10	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington approve the
10	2025 Parks and Recreation Plan, inclusive of the Parks Plan, and Needs
10	Assessment.
10	CARRIED
