2	6.1 2026 Preliminary Draft Capital Budget Report
2	Report from A. McNabb, Managing Director of Corporate Services &
2	Treasurer
2	Adam McNabb, Managing Director of Corporate Services & Treasurer
2	introduced the 2026 preliminary draft capital budget. He indicated staff is
2	seeking direction from Council to include the 2026 Preliminary Draft
2	Capital Budget in the overall 2026 Staff Draft Budget. He outlined the
2	topics to discuss including the 10-year Bridge and Major Culvert Plan,
2	the 2027 to 2035 Capital Forecast (if complete), a projection of debt
2	requirements, a calculation of debt capacity and repayment limits, the
2	results of the Public Engagement Survey, and any other budget related
2	matters ready for Council’s consideration.
2	Adam McNabb and other members of staff spoke to the 2026 proposed
2	capital budget through the storyboard, including the New Housing Action
2	Plan, the Sportsplex Grounds Plan and Lands Site Development study,
2	Active Transportation intersection improvements and
2	sidewalk/wayfinding and signage improvements, various bridge, road
2	and culvert works, the Fergus water and wastewater treatment plant
2	works, the water meter modernization, the Belwood Hall accessibility
3	design and renovation project, the corporate operations facility, and the
3	Sportsplex roof
3	Mark Bradey, Manager of Finance & Deputy Treasurer provided an
3	overview of the 2026 draft capital budget, noting the new IP column,
3	explaining the various funding sources and responding to questions.
3	Staff responded to questions regarding the ERP System, in particular
3	the HR and Health & Safety module, the plans and studies to be
3	completed in 2026 to re-zone the site for recreational use and develop a
3	high-level layout for future facilities, the scope of the Cameron Street
3	project, connecting the wayfinding signage and parking, the timing of the
3	29WG bridge replacement and the replacement of the watermain on
3	Douglas Crescent project.
3	Gerri Atkinson, Manager of Wastewater Services provided an overview
3	of the Fergus WWTP Aeration Basin Rehabilitation project and
3	responded to questions regarding the Fergus WWTP headworks.
3	Dino Masiero, Manager of Water Services and Environmental
3	Sustainability provided an overview of the water meter modernization
3	project, noting the water meters were originally installed in 2003 and are
3	at the end of their life expectancy. He indicated there are approximately
3	5,000 meters scheduled for replacement.
3	Staff continued to respond to questions regarding the water supply
3	projects and the expected disruptions in the various neighbourhoods,
3	the various funding allotments/opportunities for the Belwood Hall
3	Accessibility Design and Renovation, the communication strategy to
3	share the progress of the Corporate Operations Facility, the inclusion of
3	a new vehicle for By-law Enforcement, the purpose of the Rugby
3	Building Future Use Strategy, the fencing around the Elora Cemetery,
3	and the extent of the Provost Lane and Fergus Curling Club parking lot
3	improvements.
3	Mayor Watters called for a 6 minute recess, the meeting resumed at
3	10:40 am.
3	Pat Newson, Managing Director of Community Services highlighted
3	several of the projects and equipment contained within the budget,
3	including outdoor basketball courts at Victoria Park, the utility vehicle
3	and trail groomer, the Webster Park upgrades and the disc golf course

5	11.3 Battery Energy Storage Community Benefit Contribution Agreement
5	Report from D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer
5	D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer provided an overview of the
5	report, indicating the agreement is typical between BESS providers and
5	the municipalities they are a part of. It outlines an annual contribution
5	that the BESS provider provides to the municipality (i.e. dollar amount
5	per Megawatt or MW of project contract capacity). Based on research
5	conducted and discussions with other municipalities, the typical
5	contribution amount per MW is in the range of $500 to $1,100 per MW
5	annually.
5	D. Wilson responded to questions regarding a regular reporting
5	structure, future reapportionment and discussions regarding the
6	agreement.
6	Moved by Councillor Denis Craddock, Seconded by Councillor
6	Kimberley Jefferson.
6	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington authorize the
6	Mayor and Clerk to execute a Community Benefit Contribution
6	Agreement with Elora BESS LP as outlined in Report CAO2025-16;
6	AND THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington approve
6	the BESS Community Benefit Allocation Policy.
6	CARRIED

7	11.1 Multi Use Recreation Facility Studies
7	Report from P. Newson, Managing Director of Community Services
7	Tracey McQueen and Terry Allyman explained the purpose of the study
7	and business plan is to determine whether a proposed capital
7	construction project is practical, sustainable and aligned with community
7	needs. They highlighted the community engagement and data collection
7	practices as well as the amenities and users. T. Allyman noted the
7	preferred location at the community sportsplex either as an extension or
7	a stand-alone facility. They outlined the proposed funding formula and
7	the community benefit, the comparable municipal facilities and rental
7	rates, the staffing plan and the projected annual operating revenues and
7	expenditures.
7	Councillor Wilton suggested a friendly amendment to paragraph 3, to
7	add “the Economic Prosperity and Growth Advisory Committee”.
7	Moved by Councillor Jennifer Adams, Seconded by Councillor Denis
7	Craddock.

7	Indoor Multi-Use Recreation Facility Feasibility Study and the Indoor
7	Multi-Use Recreation Facility Business Plan for information;
7	AND THAT Council direct staff to share the studies on ConnectCW and
7	circulate to the Healthy Communities Advisory Committee, the Diversity,
7	Equity and Inclusion Advisory Committee and the Economic Prosperity
7	and Growth Advisory Committee.

7	11.4 Priority Setting for Indoor Recreation Facilities
7	Report from P. Newson, Managing Director of Community Services
7	P. Newson, Managing Director of Community Services provided an
7	overview of the report, indicating both the proposed new Ice Pad and
7	MultiUse Recreation Facility (MURF) are vital components of the
7	community's recreational landscape. She outlined the various options
7	and benefits for consideration.

7	Treasurer responded to questions related to debt servicing,
7	development charge and grant funding.

7	amendment” to the staff recommendation:
8	Moved by L. MacDonald
8	That Council direct staff to include a Multi-Use Recreation Facility in the
8	2026 Capital Ten-Year Plan; and further, that staff be directed to
8	prioritize the design and development of the Multi-Use Recreation
8	Facility at the Fergus Sportsplex, while evaluating the feasibility of
