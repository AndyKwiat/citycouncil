4	Plans and Studies
4	Active Transportation & Mobility Master Plan
4	The Master plan aims at establishing an active transportation network
4	for the Township that considers the varied mobility needs of all residents
4	and visitors. The Plan will take the Township’s existing network of
4	sidewalks, trails, multi-use paths, and roads into consideration, and it
4	will make recommendations for future improvements to enhance safety
4	and connectivity. The Study will be completed in accordance with the
4	Municipal Class Environmental Assessment process, and it will include
4	public engagement and consultation.
4	Economic Development and Tourism strategic plan
4	A joint Economic Development and Tourism strategic plan will create a
4	20-year visionary plan to be able to create an intentional redirection of
4	tourism strategies as sustainable drivers of economic development and
4	build on existing and emerging economic development opportunities.
4	Parks and Recreation Master Plan and Parkland Strategy
4	The Parks and Recreation Master Plan identifies needs and priorities for
4	the Township of Centre Wellington in the areas of parks, recreation
4	facilities and recreation services. The current Plan was completed in
4	2018 and identified needs to the year 2028. Centre Wellington is facing
4	unprecedented growth creating challenges to identify what will be
4	needed to sustain current levels of service and meet the needs of a
4	growing and diverse population. An update is needed to consider new
4	challenges and opportunities. The update to the Plan would look at
4	future growth targets forecasted 20-30 years into the future, and
4	consider facilities, services and land recommendations to meet the
4	community needs. The current challenge is how to strategically locate
4	new facilities, parks and services as the community changes. This study
4	will consider future needs and priorities, land and space needs,
4	operational needs, growth targets, diversity, accessibility and provide
4	the Township with a path forward. The methodology involves extensive
4	research, consultation, and assessment efforts. Community and partner
5	engagement will be part of the assessment and information gathering for
5	the study.
5	Development Planning Policy
5	The Provincial government added land to the Fergus and Elora-Salem
5	Urban Centre boundaries in its decision to approve County of Wellington
5	OPA 119. Other lands may be added through the County Municipal
5	Comprehensive Review (MCR) process and a number of property
5	owners have requested the inclusion of their land. In order to ensure the
5	logical and orderly progression of development, and the availability of
5	municipal and community infrastructure, the Township needs to update
5	its development policies and create a servicing allocation policy. This
5	review will include an overview of municipal servicing, a review of the
5	legislative authority for servicing allocation by-laws, leading to
5	recommendations for the prioritization of development reflecting our
5	local capacity constraints.
5	Jefferson Elora Community Centre (JECC) Asphalt Parking Lot
5	The parking lot asphalt is at end of life and needs to be resurfaced.
5	Infrastructure will be coordinating this project as part of their asphalt
5	program. This was intended as part of the JECC renovation project,
5	however timing of the project requires performing this as a 2024 project,
5	so it was excluded from the construction project. The work will be
5	performed in August when sports are in the off season to allow for
5	minimal interruption.
5	Jefferson Elora Community Centre Digital Sign
5	The current sign at the Community Centre is a manual sign that staff
5	need to access with a ladder to change the messaging. This is a health
5	and safety risk, time consuming, and antiquated for messaging. A new
5	digital sign would improve messaging to promote Township programs
5	and events. The project will take place in August at the same time as the
5	parking lot paving so the power and fibre wire can be buried under the
5	parking lot. The Township’s digital signs can all be hosted on the same
5	software and updated from within the office.
5	Millburn Playground and Washroom
5	Millburn Park requires a new playground and washroom to augment the
5	existing splashpad. Currently, there are portable washrooms onsite
5	which are not ideal for small children at the splash pad. The addition of a
5	washroom similar to the building at O’Brien Splash pad and a
5	playground will enhance the variety and usability of the park. Utility
5	servicing to the building site will be required to accommodate the
5	addition of a washroom. This is a multi-year project and planning work is
5	reflected in the 2024 capital budget.
6	Urban Forestry
6	The Township of Centre Wellington manages thousands of trees on
6	streets, in parks, and in natural areas. The Centre Wellington Public
6	Forest Policy captures the operation and management practices,
6	procedures, policies, by-laws, and standards employed to protect and
6	enhance the Township’s public forest. Centre Wellington aims to ensure
6	healthy, vibrant, and sustainable practices to manage and maintain the
6	public forest for generations to come.
6	Ainley Development – Required Offsite Improvements
6	To accommodate the Ainley development, offsite improvements are
6	required to Gerrie Rd, Colborne St as well as a sanitary sewer extension
6	through Drimmie Park. Work includes removal and replacement of
6	approximately 825m of the existing road base/surface, hydro pole/hydro
6	infrastructure relocations, installation of a new 300mm watermain and
6	upgrading the west side to an urban standard including storm sewers,
6	curb and gutter, multi-use path, street trees and streetlights. The east
6	side will remain a rural standard.
6	Colborne St Reconstruction Design - John Street to Wilson Crescent
6	Design work for the removal and replacement of approximately 415m of
6	the existing road base/surface and the removal and
6	replacement/upsizing of approximately 220m of sanitary sewer to
6	accommodate the projected flows being conveyed from the Ainley
6	development. The remaining sanitary sewer along this section of
6	Colborne St will also be removed and replaced.
6	East Mill Street Reconstruction
6	The East Mill Street reconstruction project is driven by the need to
6	replace aging infrastructure in this area. The project also provides the
6	opportunity to improve the aesthetics and function of the street and
6	enhance access to downtown Elora. Phase 1 of the project is being
6	carried out in 2023, and it includes the construction of most underground
6	utilities. Phase 2 of the project will be completed in 2024, and this phase
6	of work will focus on surface finishes such sidewalks and boulevards,
6	decorative streetlights, street trees, seating, decorative features, final
6	asphalt surface paving, and line painting.
6	Eighth Line W - Sideroad 11 to Wellington Rd 17
6	This segment of the Eighth Line W will be repaved to improve current
6	road conditions. The project will also include drainage improvements
6	such as ditching and culvert replacements. This segment of paved road
6	was identified as a high-priority for replacement in the Township’s Asset
6	Management Plan.
7	Fifth Line Bridge 4-E
7	Located on the Fifth Line between Wellington Road 22 and Sideroad 30
7	in former Eramosa Township, Bridge 4-E is key link between Centre
7	Wellington and communities to the south. This project includes
7	rehabilitation to the existing bridge deck and railing to improve safety
7	and extend the life of the bridge.
7	First Line West Bridge 26-P
7	To improve safety, a guiderail system will be installed at Bridge 26-P,
7	located on the First Line West just south of Sideroad 4 in former
7	Pilkington Township. This project with also include drainage
7	improvements and roadway erosion control measures.
7	Municipal Class EA for Closed Bridges 2-WG, 30-WG, 3-E, 5-E & 7-E
7	The Township’s Bridge Program includes plans to review closed
7	bridges, and where possible, re-open bridges to improve connectivity in
7	rural areas. A key part of this process is the completion of Municipal
7	Class Environmental Assessment studies to evaluate bridge options
7	with public consultation and input. Options to be considered will include
7	bridge removal without replacement, bridge rehabilitation and bridge
7	replacement. Bridges 2-WG, 3-E, 5-E, and 7-E will be grouped into one
7	study, and a separate study will be completed for Bridge 30-WG.
7	St David St N – St Andrew St to Edinburgh Ave
7	St David Street North is an important regional and local mobility corridor,
7	economic hub for local business, and a gateway into Centre
7	Wellington’s cultural and architectural heritage. As outlined in the
7	Township’s Asset Management Plan, the road and buried infrastructure
7	in this area is in urgent need of replacement. The Township has been
7	selected for funding through the Ontario Ministry of Transportation's
7	Connecting Links Program, with a contribution of $2,849,176 towards
7	this project. The first phase of construction in 2024 will take place in the
7	block from just north of the St. Andrew Street intersection to just north of
7	the Garafraxa Street intersection. A second phase of construction will be
7	completed in 2025, focusing on the block from Garafraxa St to
7	Edinburgh Ave.
7	Bridge St Water Tower Exterior Overcoating
7	The interior and exterior coating is original and approximately 21 years
7	old. In August 2022, a third-party assessment was completed on both
7	the interior and exterior coatings. The results indicate that both interior
7	and exterior coating are in fair condition, however an overcoat can
7	increase the life cycle of the existing coating 10 to 12 years. If left as is,
7	a recoat will be required in 2025. A full recoat would be well over
8	$1,000,000. The exterior has substantial chalking and weathering of the
8	lower cone, and the roof paint has completely deteriorated. The interior
8	inspection indicates that there are number of corrosion spots where
8	previous repairs have been made. This project will include an overcoat
8	application to the exterior. Interior tank to be drained, cleaned and a
8	touch-up coating to all corroded areas. Completing both external and
8	internal projects brings efficiency to operations as well as cost savings.
8	The tower will also be updated with current Township branding.
8	Chalmers St Watermain Replacement
8	This project includes the replacement of approximately 145m of aged
8	cast iron watermain with new PVC watermain on Chalmers St. from Moir
8	St. to Colborne St, and the resurfacing of the road with new asphalt
8	pavement. Road grades will also be reviewed to improve storm drainage
8	where possible.
8	Fergus Waste Water Treatment Plant (WWTP) Biosolids Storage
8	Assessment & Rehabilitation
8	The Fergus WWTP Biosolids Storage Assessment & Rehabilitation is
8	required due to solids increasing and current storage is being
8	maximized, sludge storage #3 roof has failed remediation steps and
8	needs to be assessed. Primary digester roof showing signs of
8	degradation.
8	Fergus Waste Water Treatment Plant (WWTP) UV System
8	Replacement
8	The WWTP requires an upgrade to the UV system due to increasing
8	operation and maintenance costs. Existing equipment is 20 plus years
8	old and components are no longer available or supported. UV light
8	provides rapid, effective inactivation of microorganisms through a
8	physical process. When microorganisms are exposed to germicidal
8	wavelengths of UV light, they are instantaneously rendered incapable of
8	reproducing and infecting. The process adds nothing to the water and
8	UV light has no impact on the chemical composition or the dissolved
8	oxygen content and does not create by-products.
8	Groundwater Model Updates, Wellhead Protection and Municipal Class
8	EA Studies
8	The Township relies on municipal groundwater wells to supply water to
8	residents and businesses. As outlined in the Township's 2019 Water
8	Supply Master Plan, more water supply is needed for growth. This
8	additional supply will come from a combination of optimizing production
8	from existing water supply wells, and developing new municipal supply
8	wells. An existing well located in Fergus (F2R), and two new well areas
8	located in the rural areas surrounding Fergus and Elora (Area 7 and
8	Area 3) are being studied to determine if they can be used for additional
9	water supply. Part of the study and approval process includes using
9	computer groundwater models to assess impacts caused by pumping
9	from these locations. To ensure groundwater from these wells will be
9	protected from drinking water threats, it is also necessary to delineate
9	areas referred to as “Wellhead Protection Areas”.
9	Well F5 Treatment
9	Well F5 produces raw water which contains a high concentration of
9	suspended solids (referred to as "turbidity"), caused by silt and sand
9	particles in the water. The turbidity observed in raw water increases at
9	higher pumping rates, so Well F5 is generally not pumped to the
9	capacity permitted in the Township's Permit to Take Water. To increase
9	the water supplied from Well F5 and improve treatment at this location,
9	the Township will install a raw water filtration system capable of
9	removing turbidity from raw water. This project includes the design,
9	supply, installation, and commissioning of a turbidity treatment system at
9	Well F5R.
9	Facilities
9	Corporate Operations Facility
9	The Township is in process of developing a new multi-department
9	Operations Centre to be located at 965 Gartshore Street, Fergus. This
9	facility will enable the Township to continue to effectively deliver
9	services to meet the increasing needs of our growing community. The
9	2024 budget for this project will be used to fund earthworks and the
9	installation of services at the development site. These works will pave
9	the way for construction of the new facility, which is planned to begin in
9	2025.
9	Elora Tourism Washrooms
9	Redesigning the Elora Tourism Pocket Park will allow for seasonal
9	washrooms to better serve the community and tourists in the core of
9	Elora. The tourism peak season will often draw over 1000 visitors a day
9	to the current indoor washrooms and Tourism Information desk. Tourism
9	in Elora has grown to a point where it is necessary to include the pocket
9	park in the plan to better serve visitors. Redesigning this space will give
9	opportunities to serve after hours, reduce congestion in the Tourism
9	Information Centre and utilize the pocket park as an extended space.
