2	8. Public Meeting to Consider A Zoning Amendment
2	8.1 Mayor Watters welcomed everyone and asked Council if there was any
2	disclosure of Pecuniary Interest under the Municipal Conflict of Interest
2	Act.
2	Mayor Watters stated the purpose of the public meeting is specific to the
2	zoning amendment and is not intended to involve any information or
2	discussions related to the licensing of Short Term Rental
2	Accommodations.
2	He noted the purpose and effect of the proposed zoning by-law
2	amendment is to modify the by-law provisions related to overnight
2	accommodation, including hotels, motels, short-term rental
2	accommodation, and bed and breakfast establishments.
2	He indicated if a person or public body would otherwise have an ability
2	to appeal the decision of the Township of Centre Wellington to the
2	Ontario Land Tribunal (OLT) but the person or public body does not
3	make oral submissions at a public meeting or make written submissions
3	to the Township of Centre Wellington before the by-law is passed, the
3	person or public body is not entitled to appeal the decision.
3	Mayor Watters stated if a person or public body does not make oral
3	submission at the public meeting, or make written submissions to the
3	Township of Centre Wellington before the by-law is passed, the person
3	or public body may not be added as the party to the hearing of an
3	appeal before the OLT unless, in the opinion of the Tribunal, there are
3	reasonable grounds to do so.
3	Mayor Watters advised if anyone wished to be notified of the adoption of
3	the proposed zoning by-law amendment, a written request must be filed
3	with the Municipal Clerk, Township of Centre Wellington, 1 MacDonald
3	Square, Elora, ON, N0B 1S0.
3	The Municipal Clerk advised notice was provided to the prescribed
3	persons, agencies and public bodies on or before February 26th, 2026.
3	A copy was also advertised in the Wellington Advertiser on February
3	26th, 2026. She further confirmed the requirement for giving notice was
3	completed on February 26th, 2026.
3	Mayor Watters asked Mariana Iglesias, Manager of Planning Services,
3	to introduce the application.
3	M. Iglesias stated the public meeting is regarding the zoning by-law
3	amendment for short term rental accommodations and for Bed &
3	Breakfast establishments; staff was directed by Council to draft a zoning
3	by-law amendment as a supporting tool for the proposed licensing by-
3	law for Short Term Rental Accommodations and Bed & Breakfasts.
3	She indicated the proposed zoning by-law concerns land use and how
3	that land use is regulated. The draft zoning by-law is intended to provide
3	clarity, outline the proposed changes to the regulations and provide an
3	enforcement tool to assist with the implementation of a short-term rental
3	licencing by-law.
3	Anthea Fisher, a resident of Elora residing in the C1 Zone, spoke in
3	opposition to the proposed elimination of short-term rental units above
3	commercial uses in the C1 Zone.
3	Ms. Fisher raised concerns regarding the parking requirements for
3	short-term rentals, specifically the requirement that parking be located
3	on an owned lot within 300 metres of the property. She noted this
3	requirement is costly and difficult for property owners to achieve,
3	particularly in the downtown core. As a result, the regulation would
4	significantly reduce the number of short-term rental units and may
4	incentivize an increase in hotel development in the downtown area.
4	She expressed concern with the proposed cap on short-term rentals,
4	particularly, changes to the definition of “short-term rental” and the
4	revised definition of “hotel.” She stated redefining hotels could result in a
4	loss of long-term rental housing, as units may be converted to a hotel
4	use while still falling outside the short-term rental cap.
4	Ms. Fisher encouraged Council to consider alternative approaches to
4	securing parking, rather than imposing strict ownership-based
4	requirements. She also expressed concern regarding a lack of
4	transparency in the process, noting the implications of the proposed
4	changes were not clearly communicated to affected property owners.
4	Darcy Saunders, resident, raised concerns regarding clarity and
4	enforcement. He questioned whether the definitions and regulations
4	would be applied and enforced consistently, the By-law defines a Bed &
4	Breakfast as a property where the owner resides and requires one
4	parking space per guest.
4	He expressed concern regarding consistency and fairness, noting that
4	while one property is currently operating, another property is required to
4	meet a higher threshold and emphasized the importance of regulations
4	being applied consistently, transparently, and equitably across the
4	Township.
4	Erika Montero, resident stated she supports the regulation of short-term
4	rentals; however, she expressed opposition to the proposed zoning by-
4	law amendment. She advised the proposed definition of “hotel,”
4	described as a building or group of buildings, is overly broad and would
4	be ineffective as an enforcement tool. She indicated the proposed
4	definition could enable developers to avoid permitting requirements by
4	converting short-term rental units into hotel-style accommodations.
4	She expressed concerns regarding the potential impact on the housing
4	supply, noting zoning by-laws should protect residential housing and the
4	proposed amendment would remove dwellings from the housing stock.
4	She further identified public safety concerns, stating hotels permitted
4	through the zoning amendment would not be subject to the same safety
4	regulations as short-term rentals and recommended restoring and
4	strengthening the definition of “hotel” to ensure housing protection and
4	public safety, emphasizing the importance of fairness and transparency
4	in zoning regulations.
4	Sophie Hogan – did not attend.
4	Mayor Watters asked if anyone if anyone is present wished to speak to
5	the application.
5	Glen Rumble, resident, expressed concerns about the proposed cap of
5	265 short-term rental units, stating it would reduce income opportunities
5	for local homeowners. He commented the proposed zoning framework
5	would heavily regulate short-term rentals under the new amendment.
5	He noted the most significant impacts of the proposed changes relate to
5	the licensing constraints, which he indicated would be the most relevant
5	and restrictive aspect of the by-law for property owners operating Bed &
5	Breakfasts.
5	Mayor Watters asked Mariana Iglesias to respond to
5	comments/questions from council.
5	M. Iglesias addressed the issue of legal non-compliance and the change
5	to the hotel definition. She noted when a unit is converted to an
5	overnight accommodation for stays of less than 28 days, it is considered
5	a hotel, as it constitutes a commercial use. As a hotel, the use had
5	already undergone zoning and parking review at the time it was
5	established. Therefore, additional parking requirements do not apply,
5	since parking was addressed when the commercial use was originally
5	reviewed.
5	M. Iglesias responded to questions from Council regarding the demerit
5	point system and clarified that the demerit point system does not apply
5	to hotels. Hotels are inspected for compliance with the Building Code at
5	the time they are established. She further noted short term rentals are
5	subject to parking requirements, which differ from those applicable to
5	hotels.
5	B. Salmon clarified the purpose of the hotel definition, noting in the draft
5	licencing by-law, the direction given by Council was to permit one (1)
5	licence per property which impacted the properties located in the
5	downtown core that have more than one unit. He explained if the hotel
5	definition applied, they would not be subject to that limitation, if kept as
5	STR’s the licencing would render a number of existing units
5	noncompliant.
5	M. Iglesias responded to questions from Council regarding legal
5	nonconforming status for those establishments currently operating as a
5	B&B, explaining, under the new licencing by-law if the owner resides on-
5	site, it remains a B&B, however, if the owner resides outside the main
5	dwelling, they could apply for a minor variance but would still be
5	required to reside on the main property.
5	M. Iglesias indicated staff will take the feedback and comments received
5	from the public and Members of Council and bring back a revised draft
5	amendment together with the STR licensing by-law for Council’s
6	consideration.
6	Mayor Watters thanked everyone for attending and advised Council will
6	be considering the by-law at a future Council meeting and declared the
6	meeting adjourned.

13	legislative changes. He outlined addendums 1 and 2 resulting in a
13	decrease in calculated DC rates, the local service policy and the DC
13	deferral policy.

13	including the productive changes. He outlined the options for Council’s
13	consideration including a) partial DC exemption for medium and high
13	density intensification, b) a 6 month DC rate freeze and c) a 2-stage DC
13	rate increase (50% of the total rate increase effective October 1, 2026,
13	50% if the rate increase effective April 1, 2027) or a 1-stage DC rate
13	increase.

13	to questions regarding partial DC exemption for high density and noted
13	four plex units are considered medium density.
13	D. Wilson responded to comments and questions regarding partial
13	exemptions based on growth projections, tracking building permits and
13	development applications to identify if the 6 month freeze accelerates
13	applications.
13	Discussion ensued regarding housing intensification and consideration
13	of a partial exemption for housing other than medium and high density,
13	the impact of the partial exemptions, and the cost of growth.
13	In light of discussions and additional questions, D. Wilson indicated if it
13	is Council’s intent to keep the 5 dwelling unit rule and remove reference
13	to medium and high density in the draft by-law, Section 3.8 m (iii) and n

13	medium and high density in Section 3.8 of the by-law to include
13	exemptions to all types of housing and responded to questions

13	In response to a question regarding amendments to the intensification
13	exemptions, Daryl Abbs, Partner, Watson & Associates Economists Ltd.,
13	indicated The Development Charges Act permits future amendments to
13	the by-law to lower the charges or expand the exemptions.
