13	12.9 Item 12.10 - Review of Township Road Salt Management Programs
13	Report from Colin Baker, Managing Director of Infrastructure
13	Colin Baker, Managing Director of Infrastructure provided an overview of
13	the Salt Management Programs, noting Environment and Climate
13	Change Canada’s mandate to manage road salt use while ensuring
13	effective winter maintenance to protect public safety, in accordance with
13	provincial legislation, with an emphasis on minimizing the adverse
13	environmental impacts associated with road salt.
13	Kyle Davis, Risk Management Official for Wellington Source Water
13	Protection gave a presentation outlining the risk management plans
13	required under the Clean Water Act to protect municipal drinking water
13	sources. He also noted the importance of implementing voluntary salt
13	management plans in areas subject to Planning Act approvals, along
13	with ongoing public education initiatives.
13	K. Davis responded to comments and questions from Council regarding
13	requirements risk management plans for private properties.
13	C. Baker responded to questions from Council regarding snow removal
13	in municipally owned properties and training.
13	Moved by Councillor Lisa MacDonald, Seconded by Councillor
13	Bronwynne Witlon.
13	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington receive the 2026
13	Township of Centre Wellington Salt Management Plan for information
13	AND;
14	Whereas road salt is a known toxic substance designated under the
14	Canadian Environmental Protection Act because of tangible threats of
14	serious or irreversible environmental and health damage from road salt;
14	and
14	Whereas salt levels in Ontario’s groundwater aquifers, creeks, rivers, and
14	lakes have increasingly worsened since the 1970s, seriously affecting
14	municipal drinking water sources and aquatic life; and
14	Whereas the Township of Centre Wellington takes actions to minimize
14	salt impacts, local salt concerns and economic damages from salt, etc.,
14	and
14	Whereas the Ontario and Canadian governments have taken many
14	actions over the past 25 years including setting water quality guidelines,
14	developing voluntary codes of practice, signing the Canada-Ontario Great
14	Lakes Agreement, and holding workshops, yet still the salt problem
14	continues to grow; and
14	Whereas numerous situation analyses have recommended salt solutions
14	liability protection, contractor certification, government-approved Best
14	Management Practices (BMPs) and salt management plans; and
14	Whereas increased numbers of slips and falls claims, and other
14	injury/collision claims related to snow and ice, are resulting in salt
14	applicators overusing salt beyond levels considered best practices; and
14	Whereas unlimited contractor liability is making it difficult or expensive for
14	snow and ice management contractors to obtain insurance coverage,
14	resulting in contractors leaving the business, thereby making it difficult for
14	municipalities and private owners to find contractors; and
14	Whereas the Snow and Ice Management Sector (SMS) of Landscape
14	Ontario is working with the Ontario government to institute a limited
14	liability regime for snow and ice management, including enforceable
14	contractor training/certification and government-approved BMPs for salt
14	application; and
14	Whereas many Ontario municipalities have Salt Management Plans, but
14	these often require updating in light of improved science and better salt
14	management practices now available; and
14	Whereas environmentally friendly and pet friendly alternatives to road salt
14	and sidewalk have been widely available for decades and are used
14	successfully world wide;
15	Therefore Be It Resolved That:
15	1. The Township of Centre Wellington urges the Province of Ontario to
15	work urgently with key stakeholders to develop limited liability legislation,
15	including enforceable contractor training and a single set of provincially-
15	endorsed standard Best Management Practices (BMPs) for snow and ice
15	management including incorporating the enforcement and BMPs into Risk
15	Management Plans and Source Protection Plans pursuant to the Clean
15	Water Act where applicable; and
15	2. The Township of Centre Wellington urges the province of Ontario to
15	create and fund an expert stakeholder advisory committee to advise the
15	province and municipalities on the best courses of action to protect
15	freshwater ecosystems and drinking water from the impacts of salt
15	pollution; and
15	3. The Township of Centre Wellington investigate and implement, where
15	feasible and cost-effective, measures to reduce the use and impacts of
15	winter maintenance materials on the environment, infrastructure, pets,
15	and the community, while maintaining safe and accessible roads,
15	sidewalks, and parking lots; and
15	4. This resolution be sent to all municipalities within the County of
15	Wellington, the City of Guelph, the Regional Municipality of Waterloo, the
15	Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO), local MPPs, Conservation
15	Ontario, Minister Todd McCarthy (MECP), Attorney General Doug
15	Downey, and Premier Doug Ford.
15	CARRIED
15	12.10 Item 12.11 - Development Charge Reduction Program
15	Report from Adam McNabb, Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer
15	Adam McNabb, Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer provided an
15	overview of the report outlining the Ontario Development Charge
15	Reduction Program (DCRP). The program is a one-time, competitive
15	intake that provides up to 90% funding for eligible housing-enabling
15	municipal infrastructure projects, conditional upon the municipality
15	reducing residential development charges (DCs) by at least 30% and
15	maintaining the reduction for three years.
15	He stated that, for an application to be considered, projects must support
15	the creation of new housing units, be included in the Township’s most
15	recent Development Charges Background Study, align with the capital
16	plan, commence no later than July 31, 2030, and be completed by
16	October 31, 2035.
16	Dan Wilson, CAO advised that staff are proposing new calculations
16	based on additional information received from the Ministry regarding the
16	Development Charge Reduction Program (DCRP). Noting that, based on
16	Ministry guidelines, eligible costs are limited to growth-related
16	construction expenses.
16	He advised that some projects are already underway and that soft costs
16	are ineligible toward the Township’s required 10% contribution, placing
16	additional pressure on municipal funding for growth-related costs. He
16	further noted that the post-period benefit component of the wastewater
16	treatment plant is ineligible and identified significant growth-related
16	capital project that directly supports new housing construction.
16	D. Wilson responded to comments and questions from Council regarding
16	DC rates beyond the 3 year reduction period, funding and 30% reduction
16	in DC rates.
