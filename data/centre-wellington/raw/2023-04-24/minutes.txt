2	Toni Ellis provided an overview of the work undertaken by
2	Neighbourwoods, in particular, the Tree Trust Program. She explained
2	the Tree Trust Program raises money through donations to hire arborists
2	skilled in conservation arboriculture to preserve legacy trees.
2	T. Ellis recognized the work the Township has accomplished over the past
2	several years with respect to the importance of trees and offered to work
2	more closely with staff to explore opportunities for remedial work rather
2	than removal, and a foster tree program.
2	She responded to questions regarding funding sources, and collaboration
2	opportunities.

2	7. Delegations
2	7.1 Ian MacRae spoke in favour of Council adopting a proactive termite
2	management system aimed at a significant reduction in the termite
2	population. He supports an all encompassing, coordinated, block by block
2	approach to shrink or eradicate the termite population.
3	Tammy Rutherford expressed support for Option 2 outlined in the staff
3	report, noting homeowners have invested thousands of dollars trying to
3	control termites. She is supportive of undertaking a similar program that
3	the City of Guelph has had success with and a proactive termite
3	management program.
3	Kirk McElwain spoke in favour of Council adopting a termite management
3	program and noted a product called Sentricon has been approved by
3	Health Canada for use in Canada. He suggested a long-term approach is
3	not necessarily the only option with new technology and chemicals
3	available.
3	8. Consideration of Reports
3	8.1 Health Care Professional Recruitment Update - Phase 1
3	Report from D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer
3	Dan Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer provided an overview of the
3	report, outlining the stakeholder group’s recommendation for a phased in
3	recruitment approach.
3	Angela Stanley, President and CEO Wellington Health Care Alliance
3	noted accommodation is the most pressing issue, hosting medical
3	students, residents and physician locums for short periods throughout the
3	year is a crucial tactic for recruiting new medical staff. She indicated
3	finding affordable and stable housing for the short-term medical
3	professionals continues to be challenging.
3	Dan Wilson indicated the staff recommendation is to use the stone
3	farmhouse on land purchased for the Operations Facility to house
3	healthcare professionals while the operations facility is being designed.
3	Moved by Councillor Denis Craddock, Seconded by Councillor Lisa
3	MacDonald.
3	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington approve the use
3	of the Township owned farmhouse located at 965 Gartshore Street,
3	Fergus as accommodation space for health care professionals as outlined
3	in report CAO2023-07 dated April 24, 2023.
3	CARRIED

4	8.3 Termite Management Direction Report
4	Report from D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer
4	D. Wilson, Chief Administrative Officer provided an overview of the report,
4	including a chronology of events related to termite management over the
4	past several years. D. Wilson outlined the two Options set forth, to either
4	continue with a passive approach to termites or direct staff to release a
4	negotiated request for proposal to initiate a proactive termite
4	management strategy.
4	D. Wilson responded to questions regarding monies expended to date,
4	encouraging interest in termite management, and proactive enforcement
4	methods.
4	Moved by Councillor Bronwynne Wilton, Seconded by Councillor
4	Barbara Lustgarten-Evoy.
4	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Wellington direct staff to
4	release a negotiated Request for Proposal to initiate a proactive termite
4	management program, subject to the successful award of the Request for
4	Proposal.
4	CARRIED
