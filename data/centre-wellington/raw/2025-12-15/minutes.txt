2	8.2 Karen Rathwell, Member Water Watchers and the Ontario Salt Pollution
2	Coalition appeared before Council to outline the harmful effects of road
2	salt, a toxic substance because of tangible threats of serious or
2	irreversible environmental damage. She asked Council to urge the
2	Provincial government to take the following action:
2	1. Establish an expert stakeholder advisory committee to advise the best
2	courses of action to protect freshwater’
2	2. Revamp limited liability legislation, including enforceable contractor
2	training and a single set of provincially-endorsed standard Best
2	Management Practices;
3	3. Commit to the reduction of the use of road salt as much as possible
3	while maintaining safety on roads, parking lots and sidewalks; and
3	4. Forward accepted resolution to all municipalities within the County,
3	the Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO), local MPPs,
3	Conservation Ontario, the Ontario Municipal Water Association, Minister
3	Todd McCarthy (MECP), Attorney General Doug Downey, and Premier
3	Doug Ford.
3	8.3 Ernest Kalwa, resident re Use of sidewalk and road salt appeared
3	before Council suggesting the Township consider reducing the use of
3	road salt and consider more environmentally and pet friendly
3	alternatives that have been available for decades. He indicated the
3	Township needs to recognize and immediately address the excessive
3	use of road salt and sidewalk salt that is causing irreversible
3	environmental damage to our rivers, the great lakes, and concrete
3	infrastructure (bridges, buildings, steps, sidewalks, etc.). Mr. Kalwa
3	requested Council adopt the draft resolution provided in the material
3	submitted in order to support the need for Provincial action on salt
3	pollution from road salt.
3	Councillor MacDonald read the following:
3	Whereas road salt is a known toxic substance designated under the
3	Canadian Environmental Protection Act because of tangible threats of
3	serious or irreversible environmental and health damage from road salt;
3	and
3	Whereas salt levels in Ontario’s groundwater aquifers, creeks, rivers,
3	and lakes have increasingly worsened since the 1970s, seriously
3	affecting municipal drinking water sources and aquatic life; and
3	Whereas the Township of Centre Wellington takes actions to minimize
3	salt impacts, local salt concerns and economic damages from salt, etc.,
3	and
3	Whereas the Ontario and Canadian governments have taken many
3	actions over the past 25 years including setting water quality guidelines,
3	developing voluntary codes of practice, signing the Canada-Ontario
3	Great Lakes Agreement, and holding workshops, yet still the salt
3	problem continues to grow; and
3	Whereas numerous situation analyses have recommended salt
3	solutions involving liability protection, contractor certification,
3	government-approved Best Management Practices (BMPs) and salt
3	management plans; and
4	Whereas increased numbers of slips and falls claims, and other
4	injury/collision claims related to snow and ice, are resulting in salt
4	applicators overusing salt beyond levels considered best practices; and
4	Whereas unlimited contractor liability is making it difficult or expensive
4	for snow and ice management contractors to obtain insurance coverage,
4	resulting in contractors leaving the business, thereby making it difficult
4	for municipalities and private owners to find contractors; and
4	Whereas the Snow and Ice Management Sector (SMS) of Landscape
4	Ontario is working with the Ontario government to institute a limited
4	liability regime for snow and ice management, including enforceable
4	contractor training/certification and government-approved BMPs for salt
4	application; and
4	Whereas many Ontario municipalities have Salt Management Plans, but
4	these often require updating in light of improved science and better salt
4	management practices now available;
4	Whereas environmentally friendly and pet friendly alternatives to road
4	salt and sidewalk have been widely available for decades and are used
4	successfully world wide;
4	Therefore Be It Resolved That:
4	1. The Township of Centre Wellington urges the province of Ontario to
4	work urgently with key stakeholders to develop limited liability
4	legislation, including enforceable contractor training and a single set of
4	provincially-endorsed standard BMPs for snow and ice management;
4	and
4	2. The Township of Centre Wellington urges the province of Ontario to
4	create and fund an expert stakeholder advisory committee to advise the
4	province and municipalities on the best courses of action to protect
4	freshwater ecosystems and drinking water from the impacts of salt
4	pollution; and
4	3. The Township of Centre Wellington commits to the reduction of the
4	use of road salt as much as possible while maintaining safety on roads,
4	parking lots and sidewalks by immediately incorporating environmentally
4	friendly and pet friendly alternatives to road salt and sidewalk salt; and
4	4. This resolution be sent to all municipalities within the County of
4	Wellington, the Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO), local
4	MPPs, Conservation Ontario, Minister Andrea Khanjin (MECP), Interim
4	Minister Todd McCarthy (MECP), Attorney General Doug Downey, and
5	Premier Doug Ford.
5	Councillor MacDonald suggested this matter and the proposed
5	resolution be referred to staff for a report.
5	Moved by Councillor Lisa MacDonald, Seconded by Councillor
5	Bronwynne Wilton.
5	THAT the Council of the Township of Centre Welligton refer the matter
5	of salt pollution brought forward by Water Watchers and the Ontario Salt
5	Pollution Coalition and Ernest Kalwa to staff for a report.
