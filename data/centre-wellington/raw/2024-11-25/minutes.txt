2	8. Public Meetings to Consider Zoning Amendments
2	8.1 RZ05/24 Public Meeting Information Report - 6235 Guelph Street (Aypa
2	Power Battery Energy Storage System Facility).
2	Mayor Watters indicated the purpose of this public meeting is to present
2	and receive public input regarding a proposed zoning by-law
2	amendment on the subject land known as 6235 Guelph Street, former
2	Nichol Township.
2	He noted the purpose of the proposed zoning by-law amendment is to
2	change the zoning from ‘A.19.3’ (Agricultural Exception) and ‘EP’
3	(Environmental Protection) to ‘A.19.X’ (Agricultural Exception) and ‘EP’
3	(Environmental Protection). The effect of the proposed zoning by-law
3	amendment is to permit a battery energy storage facility on a portion of
3	the property (subject land).
3	He indicated the purpose of the proposed County of Wellington Official
3	Plan Amendment is to amend Schedule B1 (Centre Wellington) by
3	adding a site specific policy on a portion of the land described as Lot 11,
3	Concession 3, in the Township of Centre Wellington from “Prime
3	Agricultural” to “Prime Agricultural Special” to permit a battery energy
3	storage facility.
3	He indicated if a person or public body would otherwise have an ability
3	to appeal the decision of the Township of Centre Wellington to the
3	Ontario Land Tribunal (OLT) but the person or public body does not
3	make oral submissions at a public meeting or make written submissions
3	to the Township of Centre Wellington before the by-law is passed, the
3	person or public body is not entitled to appeal the decision.
3	Mayor Watters stated if a person or public body does not make oral
3	submissions at a public meeting, or make written submissions to the
3	Township of Centre Wellington before the by-law is passed, the person
3	or public body may not be added as a party to the hearing of an appeal
3	before the Ontario Land Tribunal (OLT) unless, in the opinion of the
3	Tribunal, there are reasonable grounds to do so.
3	Mayor Watters stated if a person or public body does not make oral
3	submissions at a public meeting, if one is held, or make written
3	submissions to The Corporation of the County of Wellington in respect
3	of the proposed official plan amendment before the approval authority
3	gives or refuses to give approval to the proposed official plan
3	amendment, or if a person or public body would otherwise have an
3	ability to appeal the decision of The Corporation of the County of
3	Wellington to the Ontario Land Tribunal (OLT) but the person or public
3	body does not make oral submissions at a public meeting or make
3	written submissions to The Corporation of the County of Wellington
3	before the proposed official plan amendment is adopted, the person or
3	public body is not entitled to appeal the decision of The Corporation of
3	the County of Wellington to the OLT.
3	Mayor Watters advised if a person or public body does not make oral
3	submissions at a public meeting, if one is held, or make written
3	submissions to The Corporation of the County of Wellington in respect
3	of the proposed official plan amendment before the approval authority
3	gives or refuses to give approval to the official plan amendment, or if a
3	person or public body does not make oral submissions to The
4	Corporation of the County of Wellington before the proposed official plan
4	amendment is adopted, the person or public body may not be added as
4	a party to the hearing of an appeal before the Ontario Land Tribunal
4	unless, in the opinion of the Tribunal, there are reasonable grounds to
4	do so.
4	Mayor Watters stated if anyone wishes to be notified of the decision of
4	the Township of Centre Wellington in respect of the proposed zoning by-
4	law amendment, a written request must be filed with the Clerk of the
4	Township of Centre Wellington, 1 MacDonald Square, Elora, Ontario,
4	N0B 1S0.
4	Any person or public body may appeal a decision of The County of
4	Wellington not later than 20 days after the day that the giving or written
4	notice had been completed. If anyone wishes to be notified of the
4	decision of The Corporation of the County of Wellington in respect of
4	this proposed County Official Plan amendment, a written request must
4	be filed with the Director of Planning and Development, Corporation of
4	the County of Wellington, 74 Woolwich Street, Guelph, Ontario, N1H
4	3T9
4	The Municipal Clerk advised notice was mailed to prescribed persons,
4	agencies and public bodies on or before October 31, 2024. A copy was
4	also placed in the Wellington Advertiser on October 31, 2024. She
4	confirmed the requirement for giving notice was completed on October
4	31, 2024.
4	Mayor Watters asked Mariana Iglesias, Manager of Planning Services to
4	introduce the application.
4	M. Iglesias indicated the property is the subject of a County Official Plan
4	Amendment application (OP 2024-04) and Township Zoning By-law
4	Amendment application (RZ05/24) to facilitate the installation of an
4	independently operated Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) on a
4	portion of the property. She explained the entire property is
4	approximately 43 ha and is located at the southeast corner of Guelph
4	Street and Second Line. It is surrounded by future development lands
4	related to the South Fergus Secondary Plan Area, a landscaping
4	business to the east, and agricultural lands to the west and south. The
4	property is actively farmed and contains two dwellings and farm
4	buildings accessed from Guelph Street. M. Iglesias further explained
4	only a portion of the property, approximately 6 hectares would be leased
4	to the applicant and would be subject to the amendments. The applicant
4	is seeking to add site specific provisions to the property to allow the
4	BESS, but the site would remain Prime Agricultural.
5	She indicated the site plan approval process would address design
5	details such as lighting, landscaping, grading/servicing, fire safety and
5	agricultural restoration.
5	Mayor Watters asked Pierre Chauvin, MHBC on behalf of Aypa Power
5	to make a presentation. The consultant team consisted of the following:
5	Ondrej Benjik, Director, Business Development, Leslie Greener,
5	Stantec, Chief Brian Fink, Fire & Risk Alliance and Nadia Pabst, Aypa
5	Power, Vice President, Government & Regulatory Affairs.
5	P. Chauvin indicated the Independent Electricity System Operator
5	(IESO) is a Crown Corporation that manages Ontario’s power system
5	and plans for future capacity that is procuring solutions to support the
5	grid reliability, including storage facilities through Long Term
5	Procurements. The Elora BESS is part of a Long-Term Procurement
5	project, planning to connect to the existing transmission line that bisects
5	the subject property. They will receive electricity from the power grid and
5	store it, re-injecting the energy when needed.
5	He provided site context, outlined the scope of the development, the key
5	benefits to the Township and the various studies undertaken and
5	completed. P. Chauvin outlined the land stewardship and site restoration
5	goals, the design to incorporate vegetation and screening and the fire
5	safety strategy.
5	Delegations:
5	a) Anthea Millikin, resident, expressed concerns with respect to fire
5	safety, and the impact of the development on the groundwater and soil
5	in the event of a fire. She questioned if a fire safety and evacuation plan
5	has been developed and suggested the municipality collect monetary
5	security in the event of a fire.
5	b) Donna McCaw, on behalf of Save Our Water, emphasized the
5	large scale of the proposal and the potential dangers, including fire,
5	pollution, and contamination. D. McCaw suggested Council consider
5	either reducing the size of the facility or indicate Centre Wellington is not
5	a willing host.
5	c) Janet Harrop, on behalf of Wellington Federation of Agriculture,
5	provided an overview of the membership and the advocacy role to
5	support the protection and preservation of agricultural land for the
5	purpose of growing, harvesting and producing food. She indicated the
5	Federation recognizes the Provincial Policy Statement permits energy
5	storage systems as on-farm diversified uses (OFDU) in prime
6	agricultural areas, limited to 2% or 1 hectare to provide additional
6	revenue streams for farmers and remain a secondary use on the site. J.
6	Harrop expressed concerns regarding the impact the proposal will have
6	on existing municipal drains, water, disruption of the soil, and the
6	restoration of the site, given it is prime agricultural land. She indicated
6	the Federation opposes the rezoning application and encouraged further
6	investigation to find a more suitable site.
6	Mayor Watters asked if there was anyone in the audience wishing to
6	speak to the application. There being none, Mayor Watters offered the
6	applicant or staff an opportunity to respond to comments/questions
6	raised by the delegations.
6	There being none, Mayor Watters offered Council an opportunity to ask
6	any questions of the applicant or staff.
6	Ondrej Benjik, Director, Business Development, Leslie Greener,
6	Stantec, Chief Brian Fink, Fire & Risk Alliance and Nadia Pabst, Aypa
6	Power, Vice President, Government & Regulatory Affairs responded to
6	questions related to the benefits of the proposal for Centre Wellington,
6	noise impact on future residential development, potential for fires and
6	the emergency response preparedness, air pollution monitoring,
6	restoration strategy and the rationale for the 400 mega watt size of the
6	facility.
6	Fire Chief Tom Mulvey responded to questions regarding Centre
6	Wellington’s training and ability to respond to an emergency.
6	Colin Baker, Managing Director of Infrastructure Services responded to
6	questions regarding future reconstruction of 2nd Line to accommodate
6	residential development.
