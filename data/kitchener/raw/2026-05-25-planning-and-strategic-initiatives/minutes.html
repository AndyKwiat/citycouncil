5.
Public Hearing Matters under the Planning Act (advertised)

 

This is a formal public meeting to consider applications under the Planning Act. In accordance with the Ontario Planning Act, only the applicant or owner of land affected by the planning applications, a specified person, or a public body, and or the Minister may appeal most decisions.  If you do not make a verbal submission to the Committee or Council, or make a written submission prior to City Council making a decision on the proposal, you may not be entitled to appeal the decision of the City of Kitchener to the Ontario Land Tribunal (OLT), and may not be added as a party to the hearing of an appeal before the OLT. To understand your right of appeal, if any, or for further clarification regarding appeals, please see the Ontario Land Tribunal website.

Councillor S. Stretch entered the meeting at this time.

The Committee considered Development Services Department report DSD-2026-184, dated May 12, 2026, recommending approval of Zoning By-law Amendment Application ZBA26/003/K/AA, for the properties municipally addressed as 450-470 King Street East. It was noted that a written submission was received from S. Nabi.

D. McGolderick provided opening remarks. A. Alzoor, Planner provided an overview of the report. K. Anderl, Project Manager, Planning, T. Malone-Wright, Manager, Development Approvals, and G. Stevenson, Director, Development and Housing Approvals, were also in attendance to respond to questions from the Committee.

R. Kelly-Ruetz, GSP Group, N. Cipriani, McCallumSather, and E. Alani, Kitchener Housing Inc., attended in support of the application and responded to questions from the Committee.

In response to questions, R. Kelly-Ruetz and E. Alani provided clarification regarding the increase in building height to 19 storeys, the affordability of units, and parking considerations. It was noted that the development is proposed as a no-parking building, with no designated parking spaces for residents or visitors, and that lay-by spaces are intended for short-term drop-offs only. It was further noted that the site’s proximity to the LRT, Grand River Transit services, established cycling infrastructure, and a nearby municipal underground parking facility is expected to mitigate parking demand. Bicycle parking will also be provided on site. Additional information was provided regarding the unit mix, indicating approximately 65 percent one-bedroom units, 33 percent two-bedroom units, and the remainder three-bedroom units. E. Alani advised that the primary need within the housing portfolio is for one-bedroom units and confirmed that all units within the development will be affordable and barrier-free.

Councillor A. Owodunni left the meeting at this time.

Councillor A. Owodunni entered the meeting at this time.

Councillors S. Davey, M. Johnston, and D. Schneider left the meeting at this time.

B. Wales and Q. Dupont addressed the Committee in opposition to the application, requesting that the residential component of the project be removed and that the land be used for a public park instead.

Councillor S. Davey, M. Johnston, and D. Schneider entered the meeting at this time.

D. Fine addressed the Committee in opposition, expressing concerns regarding the proposed density and the capacity of existing community services and infrastructure to accommodate the development.

T. Hanusiak also addressed the Committee, raising concerns regarding the anticipated construction timeline and the timely delivery of housing resulting from the revised design.

In response to questions from the Committee regarding the potential use of the lands for park purposes, Staff advised that the initial parcel had been acquired for a fire station and that additional lands were subsequently acquired in alignment with the Housing Accelerator Fund and Building Faster Fund initiatives, both of which require that funding be directed toward housing development. Staff noted that these funding requirements limit the ability to repurpose the lands entirely for park use; however, opportunities may exist to refine the design to incorporate green space or amenity areas.

Councillor P. Singh left the meeting at this time, and Councillor S. Davey assumed the Chair.

Councillor P. Singh re-entered the meeting and resumed the Chair.

It was noted that any recommendation arising from this matter will be considered at a Special Council Meeting on June 1, 2026.

  • On motion byCouncillor M. Johnston

    it was resolved:

    "That Zoning By-law Amendment Application ZBA26/003/K/AA for The Corporation of the City of Kitchener, for 450-470 King Street East, be APPROVED in the form shown in the ‘Proposed By-law’, and ‘Map No. 1’ attached to Development Services Department report, DSD-2026-184 as Attachments ‘A1’ and ‘A2’ and further;

    That the Urban Design Report, prepared by GSP Group Inc., dated May 2026, attached as Attachment ‘G’ to Development Services Department report, DSD-2026-184, be endorsed, and that staff be directed to implement the Urban Design Report through a future Site Plan Approval process."

    Carried

The Committee considered Development Services Department report DSD-2026-205, dated May 14, 2026, recommending approval of City Initiated Official Plan Amendment OPA24/009/COK/CM, for the purpose of implementing the Dundee Secondary Plan. It was noted written submissions were received by J. Alati and P. Chauvin.

C. Musselman, Senior Environmental Planner and G. Khandelwal, Senior Planner, provided an overview of the report. N. Goss, Manager, Policy and Research, R. Bustamante, Director of Planning and Housing Policy, J. Schmitter and T. Mahood, City Consultants, were also in attendance to respond to questions from the Committee.

P. Chauvin, MHBC Planning, was in attendance on behalf of Activia Holdings and 1000682913 Ontario Inc., and provided comments regarding parkland dedication, noting concerns with the exclusion of trails from eligible parkland credit.

J. Alati, Davies Howe LLP, was in attendance on behalf of Schlegel Urban Developments and provided comments including but not limited to natural heritage and environmental policies. Concerns were also raised that Map B, Policy 9.22, and Map D continue to identify 1878 New Dundee Road as a cultural heritage resource, despite the property not being designated or listed under the Ontario Heritage Act, which may impede future development.

In response to questions from the Committee, N. Goss provided clarification regarding the provision of institutional uses within the community, including places of worship and event spaces. G. Khandelwal advised that staff have heard similar concerns regarding the need for institutional spaces and indicated that there remains an opportunity to incorporate such uses through the planning process. Staff have engaged with faith-based communities to better understand their land requirements and have facilitated connections with landowners. It was noted that the City continues to enable permissions across a range of locations and zones to ensure such uses can be accommodated wherever feasible.

The Committee also inquired about transit service within the area. J. Zunic advised that engagement has occurred with Regional Transportation Planning Staff and Grand River Transit, and that the mobility plan identifies potential future transit routes and a transit hub. Staff noted that enabling early transit service has been a key objective from the outset of planning for the community, and that frequent transit should be supported as development progresses.

Further information was requested regarding the heritage status of the property located at 1878 New Dundee Road. Staff confirmed that the property is not currently designated under the Ontario Heritage Act and advised that its identification through policy mapping does not prevent development but may require further review as part of future planning applications.

The Committee also inquired about the removal of trails from parkland dedication calculations. Staff advised that the intent is to prioritize programmable park space that can better serve active recreational needs, while recognizing that trails function as part of a broader connectivity network and can complement park spaces rather than replace them.

Councilor J. Deneault brought forward a motion to refer the recommendation outlined in Development Services Department report, DSD -2026-205, to the June 29, 2026, Council Meeting, to allow staff the opportunity to further discuss the concerns raised by the delegations in attendance on this date.

  • On motion byCouncillor A. Owodunni

    it was resolved:

    "That the following recommendation be referred to the June 29, 2026 Council Meeting to permit staff to further engage with the developers and delegations in attendance regarding concerns:

    That City Initiated Official Plan Amendment OPA24/009/COK/CM, for the purpose of implementing the Dundee Secondary Plan, including amendments to mapping and text amendments, be approved, in the form shown in the Official Plan Amendment attached to Development Services Department report, DSD-2026-205 as Attachment ‘A’; and,

    That the Dundee Secondary Plan Class Environmental Assessment (Draft Final), prepared by Jacobs, dated April 28, 2026, and attached to Development Services Department report, DSD-2026-205, as Attachment ‘B’, be received; and, further

    That the Dundee Secondary Plan Class Environmental Assessment Report attached to Development Services Department report, DSD-2026-205 as Attachment ‘B’, be filed with the Ministry of the Environment Conservation and Parks for the mandatory thirty (30) day review period as required by the Environmental Assessment Act."

    Carried
