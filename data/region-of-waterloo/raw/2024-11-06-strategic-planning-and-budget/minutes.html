4.
Grand River Conservation Authority 2025 Draft Budget Presentation

 
1.DOCS 4818117-GRCA 2025 Draft Budget - Region of Waterloo PPTX.pdf

Kayleigh Keighan, Manager of Finance at the Grand River Conservation Authority (GRCA), presented the draft 2025 budget. A copy of the presentation is appended to the original agenda. Her presentation covered the GRCA’s strategic priorities, challenges anticipated in the 2025 budget, a comparison between the 2024 and draft 2025 budgets, and the proposed municipal funding increases.

In response to a questions from the Committee, K. Keighan explained that if the province introduces changes in the area of planning, it would align with adjustments made earlier in the year. These changes restricted conservation authorities to making decisions solely on ecological and heritage aspects. She further responded that they have contracts with the school boards to provide education sessions for students.

In response to questions, Samantha Lawson, CAO of the Grand River Conservation Authority (GRCA), clarified the implications of the full cost recovery program. She noted that while the program is being phased in to achieve 100% cost recovery, some support will continue to be provided under the levy portion. However, the transition primarily affects the application process itself.

In response to a question regarding the municipal funding portion, Jennifer Rose, Commissioner of Engineering & Environmental Services, clarified that this funding is drawn from water and wastewater rates, not from the levy.

The presentation was received for information.

5.
2025 Plan and Budget Presentation

 
1.November 6 2024 Plan and Budget.pdf

Mathieu Goetzke, Acting Chief Administrative Officer, introduced the 2025 Plan and Budget presentation. A copy of the presentation is appended to the original agenda.

M. Goetzke began by explaining that the purpose of the presentation was to provide an overview of key elements, allowing Council to offer strategic direction on the 2025 budget, its impact on services, and the pace of strategic plan implementation.

Wayne Steffler, Commissioner of Corporate Services and Chief Financial Officer, continued the presentation, highlighting that the base budget increase stands at $58.1 million, equating to a 9.3 percent tax impact. He outlined proposed service expansions at $14 million, resulting in a potential combined 12 percent tax impact. W. Steffler emphasized that these figures are preliminary and that Council will work through the planning and budgeting process to bring the tax impact to a manageable level. He also noted updated assessment growth data, indicating a growth rate of 1.81 percent.

In response to a Committee question, Chris Wilson, Manager of Corporate Budgets, stated that the overall tax rate in 2024 for the Region of Waterloo, including police services, was 6.94 percent.

Peter Sweeney, Commissioner of Community Services, continued the presentation and highlighted that 8% of the population in Waterloo Region lives below the poverty line, with employment being a major barrier, especially for newcomers. He further spoke to housing affordability and that it remains a challenge. He indicated that as of September 30, 572 individuals were chronically homeless, with Indigenous people disproportionately affected. He noted that for 2025, the Region aims to invest in affordable housing and expand economic opportunities. Key initiatives include meeting affordable housing targets through programs like Building Better Futures and increased rent supplements, expanding housing services, and collaborating with the community to address chronic homelessness. Employment services will also be enhanced, including the expansion of the Grand Valley Construction Path to Employment program and partnerships with industries in technology, health, and manufacturing. The Region continues to foster economic growth through the Waterloo Airport, which supports over 1,300 jobs and generates $390 million in economic output.

J. Rose continued the presentation, noting that as the community grows, there is an increasing need for the expansion of water, sewer, and utility services. To address this, the Region plans to invest over $470 million over the next ten years. Key projects highlighted included the roundabout construction at the Fountain/Maple Grove intersection, which is the first step in expanding capacity on Fountain Street and Maple Grove Road. Additionally, work is underway to widen Fischer-Hallman and Bleams Roads, with the addition of roundabouts to meet the needs of southwest Kitchener. Moving forward, the extension of McQueen Shaver Boulevard and the construction of East Boundary Road will provide an important transportation link between Highway 24 and Highway 401. These projects are aligned with the Homes for All initiative, providing necessary infrastructure to support growth and improve capacity for all modes of travel.

P. Sweeney continued the presentation, highlighting the significant capital investment in Housing Services, particularly the WRH Masterplan (also referred to as the Revitalization Plan). This plan is a key component of the Building Better Futures framework, with a net contribution of 669 new affordable homes to be owned and operated regionally. Ongoing redevelopments include Kingscourt Drive in Waterloo, Wilson Avenue in Kitchener, and Langs Drive in Cambridge, with future construction planned for Mooregate, Courtland Shelley, and Weber. The 2025 budget also includes capital repairs and upgrades to existing WRH stock to ensure continued maintenance and renewal.

J. Rose discussed the airport's role as a key component of the Homes for All priority and a driver of employment in Waterloo Region. He highlighted the Region’s commitment to economic growth, with over $330 million allocated in the next ten years for airport development. Current initiatives at YKF include the order of two firetrucks, fully funded by the federal government. The airport’s Master Plan, expected to be completed in 2025, will guide its future growth. Starting in 2025, the Region will explore options for additional operational space, expand parking to accommodate passenger growth, and work on improving airport access to enhance the passenger experience. Future plans include terminal expansion (phase two), runway extensions and rehabilitations, and acquiring and servicing lands for development to support aerospace investments and further regional economic growth.

Members of the Committee enquired about safe and affordable housing, the housing master plan and future operating impacts. Responding to questions, Ryan Pettipiere, Director of Housing Services, discussed the housing and homelessness budget, noting that the Region has a strong understanding of community development through ongoing conversations with partner agencies. He emphasized that the Region of Waterloo is the largest provider of social and community housing, as well as supportive and emergency shelter solutions, by a significant margin.

Responding to questions, C. Wilson clarified that the total capital program for the Region is $6.8 billion, with $1.3 billion in tax-levy-supported debentures. Of that, $500 million is allocated to housing, while the remaining $800 million in debenture funding across the rest of the capital program is also levy-supported and will have future operating impacts.

Committee members discussed tax incremental grants, the cost of housing versus the cost of homelessness, and rent supplements. R. Pettipiere noted that rent supplements are a critical tool for housing individuals experiencing homelessness. In many cases, these supplements function as de facto supportive housing, providing both affordability components to help individuals pay market rent and support services to help them maintain stable housing.

Members of the Committee deliberated on the recommendations. 

B. Vrbanovic moved a motion to defer the staff recommendations to the final budget day. The committee discussed the deferral, and Regional Chair K. Redman amended the deferral to specify November 27, 2024.

Doug Spooner, Commissioner of Transportation Services, continued the presentation, focusing on the fourth area, Climate Aligned Growth. He highlighted the Region’s commitment to sustainable community development as part of the Growing with Care strategic plan. He highlighted that in 2022, the Region achieved a 12% reduction in greenhouse gas emissions from 2010 levels. He further noted that climate models predict warmer and wetter conditions for the Region over the next 50 years, with significant financial impacts if proactive measures are not taken. He pointed out that transportation remains the largest source of emissions, but regional services like Grand River Transit and MobilityPLUS are helping drive change. Additionally, the Region continues to make progress in waste management, with a high diversion rate and the use of landfill methane to generate energy. Spooner emphasized that continued focus will lead to further progress in addressing climate-related challenges.

J. Rose provided an overview of major projects underway, including the pedestrian bridge over Highway 7/8 and improvements at Conestoga College. The 2025 budget includes additional work at the college to enhance the terminal, improving passenger amenities and bus efficiency. The Cambridge Bus Garage is at capacity, and preliminary work will begin in 2025 to explore a new facility to support future growth. She also highlighted GRT's ongoing efforts to replace aging buses, with a focus on reducing emissions through hybrid buses and expanding charging infrastructure to support bus electrification. In waste management, the Region is planning a $330 million investment over the next ten years, with key projects including the construction of new landfill cells, upgrades to the Waterloo transfer station, and development of property adjacent to the Waterloo Landfill. Future projects will focus on expanding the Waterloo Landfill, upgrading infrastructure, and investing in the Cambridge Waste Transfer Station to ensure continued operational efficiency.

D. Spooner discussed fare increases, noting that monthly pass prices have been adjusted but remain 15-30% lower than comparable transit agencies in the GTHA. There were no increases to stored value or MobilityPLUS tickets to maintain affordability for occasional riders. The cash fare increase, which affects 7% of riders, is balanced by a 20% reduction in EasyGO fare card prices, encouraging savings for users who pay with the card, which can be loaded with as little as $0.05 per transaction.

Responding to questions, J. Rose noted that the 2024 wastewater collection use rate increase was 2.9%, which impacted residents in North Dumfries and Wellesley as a retail rate. While a 4.9% increase had initially been projected for 2025, a review of current data and needs showed that a 3.9% increase would be sufficient.

A question was raised about factoring potential revenue declines due to reduced student numbers, particularly international students, into the budget. D. Spooner explained that transit service planning begins in July and advances through the budget cycle in November, typically without the September and October ridership data. He noted that ridership decreases affect both revenue and potential provincial gas tax revenue, emphasizing the need to balance growth with sustainable ridership levels.

Responding to questions, D. Spooner addressed youth mobility and opportunities for a universal pass during the school year, highlighting the student pass and future strategies for engaging young riders.

10.1
Councillor D. Craig Notice of Motion

 

D. Craig introduced a motion requesting area MPPs to seek a new funding arrangement to support residents in the Region of Waterloo. He emphasized the need for MPPs to share the Region's goals and sensitivities regarding the impact on taxpayers and to work collaboratively to improve conditions for the community without necessarily increasing taxes.

Committee members discussed the motion, emphasizing the need for a stronger presence in advocating for a new deal to support residents. Members expressed support for the motion and reminded Council that the Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO) is actively engaged in similar efforts, encouraging members to review resources available on the AMO website. S.Foxton proposed adding a line to the notice of motion to ensure the resolution is shared with area municipalities.
