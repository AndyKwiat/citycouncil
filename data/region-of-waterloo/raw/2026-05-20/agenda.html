13.1
Councillor D. Craig

 
  • Recommended Motion:

    Whereas:

    The historic community off Blair Village is experiencing increasing traffic volumes, congestion ands safety concerns,

    And whereas, the broader corridor including Shantz Hill, Fountain Street and  King Street  is experiencing congestion, extended queuing and slower travel times,

    And whereas traffic conditions in these areas are interconnected with congestion delays that are having spillover effects and short cutting through adjacent neighbourhoods including Blair Village,

    And whereas ongoing residential and employment growth continues to place increasing pressure on this road network,

    And whereas concerns have been raised regarding road safety , pedestrian impacts, speeding and the impact on the heritage designated Blair Village,

    And whereas a coordinated system-wide analysis may be required to properly assess traffic operations, safety impacts and long term infrastructure needs across this corridor,

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED:

    • That Regional Council direct staff through the“Integrated Mobility Plan” to initiate a review of traffic and safety encompassing Blair Village , Fountain Street, Shantz Hill Road, Fountain Street  and the connections to King Street,
    • And that the review include past studies on traffic volumes, turning movements , safety analysis, signal coordination and growth impacts,
    • And that the review identify possible medium and long term solutions including operational improvements, roadway modifications and potential network changes,
    • And that the review comment on past studies of a by-pass for the Blair Village and the possibility of an alternative by-pass that respects all the environmental issues of the surrounding area.

14.1
Councillor N. Salonen

 
  • Recommended Motion:

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo waive notice of motion as per section 70 of By-law 26-017, A By-law to Provide Rules for Governing the Proceedings of Council and its Committees, and to repeal By-Law 22-051, as amended.

  • Recommended Motion:

    WHEREAS residents who rely on private wells must have access to a fair, transparent, evidence-based, and resident-centred process when they experience groundwater interference that may be connected to Regional water-taking activities;

    AND WHEREAS the Region’s current Policy and Procedures for Responding to Groundwater Interference Complaints, revised in April 2015, provides for Regional staff to investigate complaints and provides that appeals are heard by the Commissioner of Transportation and Environmental Services (now interim Commissioner of Water Services and Wastewater Operations);

    AND WHEREAS prior to the 2015 policy change, groundwater interference appeals in the Region of Waterloo were heard through a Council-appointed Well Interference Appeal Committee, formerly known as the Regional Water Liaison Committee;

    AND WHEREAS residents have raised concerns that the current process does not provide sufficient independence, transparency, or meaningful appeal from staff determinations, particularly where the Region is both the operator of the municipal wells and the body responsible for investigating and deciding complaints related to those wells;

    AND WHEREAS other Ontario municipalities, including the City of Guelph, maintain a Well Interference Committee model to hear appeals where a resident is dissatisfied with the municipality’s investigation, resolution, or compensation related to municipal groundwater taking;

    AND WHEREAS the City of Guelph’s Well Interference Committee model provides for a committee to review staff investigations, hear directly from affected well owners, consider municipal pumping data and well information, and recommend restoration or additional restoration where appropriate;

    AND WHEREAS the purpose of any well interference process should be to restore an adequate and reliable water supply to affected residents, rather than to limit the Region’s response to a fixed compensation amount that may not reflect the actual cost of restoration;

    AND WHEREAS residents in multiple areas of the Region have raised concerns regarding groundwater interference associated with municipal pumping activities;

    AND WHEREAS public confidence in groundwater management is strengthened through transparent, evidence-based, and arms-length review processes;

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT the Regional Municipality of Waterloo direct staff to bring forward a report and draft revised policy to the August 12, 2026 Special Council Water meeting to replace the current groundwater interference appeal process with a Council-appointed Well Interference Appeal Committee;

    AND THAT the revised policy and Terms of Reference be modelled on best practices from comparable municipal well interference appeal processes, including the City of Guelph model, and include the following:

    a) a mandate for the Committee to hear appeals from private well owners regarding groundwater interference complaints related to Regional water-taking activities;

    b) a process for the Committee to be convened where Regional staff determine that the Region is not responsible for a well interference complaint, where a resident is dissatisfied with the resolution offered, or where a resident is dissatisfied with the amount or form of compensation offered;

    c) a membership model that includes members of Regional Council and representatives from affected lower-tier municipalities, with consideration given to independent hydrogeological or technical expertise;

    d) a requirement that members hearing an appeal be independent from the staff responsible for the original investigation and recommendation;

    e) a process that allows affected residents to provide written materials, attend the hearing, present evidence, respond to staff findings, and answer questions from the Committee;

    f) a requirement that the Committee review all relevant information, including the location of the affected well relative to Regional wells, private well depth and water levels, municipal well details, municipal pumping data, water-level monitoring data, complaint history, investigation findings, and any mitigation measures taken to date;

    g) authority for the Committee to make recommendations to Regional Council respecting appropriate remedies, including temporary water supply, pump lowering, well deepening, new well drilling, connection to municipal water where feasible, abandonment of the old well where required, reimbursement of eligible costs, or other measures necessary to restore a reliable water supply;

    h) options to remove or revise the current $4,000 compensation limit where the evidence supports greater actual costs to restore an adequate water supply;

    i) options to ensure residents are not required to sign a full and final release of future claims before having access to a meaningful appeal process;

    j) enhanced public transparency respecting groundwater monitoring data, complaint tracking, investigation reporting, and Committee recommendations to Council, while protecting the personal information and privacy of affected residents;

    k) options for annual reporting to Council respecting the number, status, and resolution of groundwater interference complaints, trends, and mitigation measures undertaken by the Region;

    AND THAT staff include a transition provision allowing any resident who has submitted a groundwater interference complaint related to the K50, K51 and K52 wells since January 1, 2019, including residents who previously received a staff determination, compensation offer, denial, or release request, to request review through the new appeal process;

    AND THAT pending Council’s consideration of the revised policy, staff be directed not to require residents with active or unresolved complaints related to the K50, K51 and K52 wells to sign a full and final release as a condition of receiving temporary water support or emergency assistance;

    AND THAT staff report back with any legal, financial, staffing, procedural, privacy, and operational implications required to implement the revised process, including any amendments required to the current Policy and Procedures for Responding to Groundwater Interference Complaints or any related Regional policies.

  • Recommended Motion:

    Amendment

    WHEREAS residents who rely on private wells must have access to a fair, transparent, evidence-based, and resident-centred process when they experience groundwater or surface water interference that may be connected to Regional water-taking activities;

    AND WHEREAS the Region’s current Policy and Procedures for Responding to Groundwater Interference Complaints, revised in April 2015, provides for Regional staff to investigate complaints and provides that appeals are heard by the Commissioner of Transportation and Environmental Services (now interim Commissioner of Water Services and Wastewater Operations);

    AND WHEREAS prior to the 2015 policy change, groundwater interference appeals in the Region of Waterloo were heard through a Council-appointed Well Interference Appeal Committee, formerly known as the Regional Water Liaison Committee;

    AND WHEREAS residents have raised concerns that the current process does not provide sufficient independence, transparency, or meaningful appeal from staff determinations, particularly where the Region is both the operator of the municipal wells and the body responsible for investigating and deciding complaints related to those wells;

    AND WHEREAS other Ontario municipalities, including the City of Guelph, maintain a Well Interference Committee model to hear appeals where a resident is dissatisfied with the municipality’s investigation, resolution, or compensation related to municipal groundwater taking;

    AND WHEREAS the City of Guelph’s Well Interference Committee model provides for a committee to review staff investigations, hear directly from affected well owners, consider municipal pumping data and well information, and recommend restoration or additional restoration where appropriate;

    AND WHEREAS the purpose of any well interference process should be to restore an adequate and reliable water supply to affected residents, rather than to limit the Region’s response to a fixed compensation amount that may not reflect the actual cost of restoration;

    AND WHEREAS residents in the area of the K50, K51 and K52 wells have raised concerns regarding well interference and the impacts of increased or altered municipal pumping activity dating back to 2019;

    AND WHEREAS residents in multiple areas of the Region have raised concerns regarding groundwater interference associated with municipal pumping activities;

    AND WHEREAS public confidence in groundwater management is strengthened through transparent, evidence-based, and arms-length review processes;

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT the Regional Municipality of Waterloo direct staff to bring forward a report and draft revised policy to the July 8th, 2026 Special Council Water meeting to replace the current groundwater interference appeal process with a Council-appointed Well Interference Appeal Committee;

    AND THAT the revised policy and Terms of Reference be modelled on best practices from comparable municipal well interference appeal processes, including the City of Guelph model, and include the following:

    1.  a mandate for the Committee to hear appeals from private well owners regarding groundwater or surface water interference complaints related to Regional water-taking activities;
    2. a process for the Committee to be convened where Regional staff determine that the Region is not responsible for a well interference complaint, where a resident is dissatisfied with the resolution offered, or where a resident is dissatisfied with the amount or form of compensation offered;
    3. a membership model that includes members of Regional Council and representatives from affected lower-tier municipalities, with consideration given to independent hydrogeological or technical expertise;
    4. a requirement that members hearing an appeal be independent from the staff responsible for the original investigation and recommendation;
    5. a process that allows affected residents to provide written materials, attend the hearing, present evidence, respond to staff findings, and answer questions from the Committee;
    6. a requirement that the Committee review all relevant information, including the location of the affected well relative to Regional wells, private well depth and water levels, municipal well details, municipal pumping data, water-level monitoring data, complaint history, investigation findings, and any mitigation measures taken to date;
    7. authority for the Committee to make recommendations to Regional Council respecting appropriate remedies, including temporary water supply, pump lowering, well deepening, new well drilling, connection to municipal water where feasible, abandonment of the old well where required, reimbursement of eligible costs, or other measures necessary to restore a reliable water supply;
    8. options to remove or revise the current $4,000 compensation limit where the evidence supports greater actual costs to restore an adequate water supply;

    i) options to ensure residents are not required to sign a full and final release of future claims before having access to a meaningful appeal process;

    j) a transparent reporting process to Council, while protecting the personal information and privacy of affected residents;

    j) enhanced public transparency respecting groundwater monitoring data, complaint tracking, investigation reporting, and Committee recommendations to Council, while protecting the personal information and privacy of affected residents;

    k) options for annual reporting to Council respecting the number, status, and resolution of groundwater interference complaints, trends, and mitigation measures undertaken by the Region;

    AND THAT staff include a transition provision allowing any resident who has submitted a groundwater or surface water interference complaint related to the K50, K51 and K52 wells since January 1, 2019, including residents who previously received a staff determination, compensation offer, denial, or release request, to request review through the new appeal process;

    AND THAT pending Council’s consideration of the revised policy, staff be directed not to require residents with active or unresolved complaints related to the K50, K51 and K52 wells to sign a full and final release as a condition of receiving temporary water support or emergency assistance;

    AND THAT staff report back with any legal, financial, staffing, procedural, privacy, and operational implications required to implement the revised process, including any amendments required to the currentPolicy and Procedures for Responding to Groundwater Interference Complaints or any related Regional policies.
