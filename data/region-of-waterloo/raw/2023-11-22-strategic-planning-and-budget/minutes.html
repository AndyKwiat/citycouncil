Waterloo Regional Police Services - 2024 Budget Presentation

 
1.WRPS Supplemental Information Package for 2024 Approved Operating and Capital Estimates.pdf
2.WRPS 2024 Approved Operating and Capital Estimates Presentation - November 22, 2023.pdf

Ian McLean, Vice-Chair of the Waterloo Regional Police Services Board, presented key insights on the Waterloo Regional Police Services (WRPS) 2024 budget. The presentation encompassed an overview of the WRPS mandate, legislative requirements, the Police Services Act, the formulation process of the 2024 budget, findings from the strategic business plan community survey, WRPS staffing levels, historical officer expansion, and the proposed hiring strategy for the Police Services Board (PSB).

Police Chief Mark Crowell continued the presentation, addressing Regional support services, calls for service, total crime severity index, violent crime severity index, 2023 violent crime trends, 2023 major crimes, 2023 complex crime trends, growth of hate crimes in the Region, 2023 traffic safety trends, emergency response and public safety, investing in frontline technologies, alignment with community safety and wellbeing plan, community outreach and engagement, alternate mental health services, intimate partner violence, youth engagement strategy, 2024 budget drivers, annual cost for police services per resident, WRPS use of overtime, 2024 approved capital estimates, 2024 approved operating estimates, and budget investment areas.

In response to a question from the Committee, Chief Crowell indicated that, according to the latest report, the WRPS is on par and leading in the move towards total civilianization when compared to Ontario's Big 12 municipalities. More information will be provided to the Committee. 

In response to a question from the Committee, Chief Crowell stated that about 25% of the crime within the Region originates from outside the region, with factors such as highway access and shopping centers contributing to this dynamic. The WRPS is actively collaborating through integrated partnerships to address these challenges, and the police force is responding to and addressing these incidents to the best of their ability.

In response to a question, the Chief Crowell mentioned that WRPS is responding to schools more frequently than ever. The police force has maintained ongoing collaboration with education partners to establish the best possible protocols. Recognizing a growing concern across the spectrum, WRPS acknowledges and addresses the continuing issue of youth-related violence.

In response to a question from the Committee, Chief Crowell addressed the imperative need highlighted by municipal leaders for increased visibility and presence in communities. Despite the challenges of planning, WRPS is committed to maintaining a visible presence in downtown, uptown, and townships, responding to the ongoing request from the community. This budget reflects a commitment to frontline availability as a top priority, ensuring a variety of response modes such as on bike and foot patrols. Additionally, it emphasizes the importance of dedicating resources to build meaningful relationships with business owners, residents, and communities.

In response to a question from the Committee, Chief Crowell addressed concerns about the ability to increase the number of officers. He explained the success in onboarding new officers, with an average of approximately 48.6 officers per year, and highlighted the budgeted $2 million for this purpose. While minimum requirements are currently met through overtime, he emphasized the need to hire more officers for the well-being of the force, given the costliness of overtime. The overarching goal is to maintain a lean, efficient, and versatile approach to service delivery. Vice Chair McLean added that the issue of overtime is tied to the necessity for officers, stressing the importance of recruiting to ensure there is sufficient staff to effectively serve the community.

In response to a question from the committee, Chief Crowell addressed the demographics of the force. He emphasized that, in compliance with federal and provincial provisions, they are obligated to report on this aspect. Chief Crowell also discussed the demographics of the leadership structure within the team.

In response to a question from the Committee, Chief Crowell provided insights into the reserve and reserve funds, assuring the Committee that more detailed information would be shared. He emphasized that WRPS makes collaborative efforts to manage all finances with the utmost accountability, acting in full transparency to the Police Services Board and the community, and in accordance with the Board’s Reserve and Reserve Fund Management Policy.

9.
2024 Plan and Budget Presentation

 
1.2024 Plan and Budget - November 22 Budget Committee Presentation.pdf

Craig Dyer, Commissioner, Corporate Services, and Chief Financial Officer, presented the 2024 Plan and Budget. The presentation covered the current status of the 2024 operating budget, the proposed use of tax stabilization reserve in the preliminary 2024 budget, and the Regional funding for services that should be funded by Provincial revenues.

Continuing the presentation, Peter Sweeney, Commissioner, Community Services, brought attention to National Housing Day. He addressed the concerning growth of chronic homelessness in Waterloo Region, emphasizing the need to bridge the gap. P. Sweeney outlined four pillars crucial to closing this gap, which include responding to the current reality, transitioning to the future, creating a sustainable and equitable future, and maintaining existing solutions.

In response to inquiries from the Committee, P. Sweeney addressed the multifaceted challenges associated with closing the gap on chronic homelessness, emphasizing that the response needs to be equally comprehensive. He underscored the urgency of addressing immediate needs, cautioning that without intervention, the gap would only widen. P. Sweeney provided additional details on transitional housing, permanent housing, the necessity for a women's shelter, hybrid shelters, and the Lived Expertise Prototyping Project.

12.1
Councillor C. James

 

Councillor C. James addressed her motion, discussing the challenges associated with addressing systemic justice issues concerning youth, health, and collaborating with community organizations focused on addressing disparities.

In response to a question from the Committee, Connie MacDonald, Chief Communications & Strategy Officer, provided insight into the Community Capacity Building fund. She explained that it was established as part of the council-approved equity fund, representing a one-time allocation within the upstream fund process. As staff actively work on equity initiatives in the Region, the fund was created to support capacity building in community organizations. The opportunity was extended to the community to apply for this funding, with some organizations receiving both upstream and community capacity building funding. C. MacDonald clarified that upstream funding primarily focused on program and service delivery, while capacity building funding aimed to assist organizations in enhancing their capabilities to deliver services.

Regional Chair K. Redman proposed an amendment to the original motion, suggesting an inclusion of $1,000,000 in the 2024 Operating Budget.

B. Vrbanovic joined the meeting at 5:43 PM. ()
B. Vrbanovic left the meeting at 5:48 PM. ()
  • Moved byC. James
    Seconded byC. Huinink

    As amended:

    WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo is committed to advancing Truth and Reconciliation and Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion;

    AND WHEREAS First Nations, Inuit and Métis, African, Caribbean, Black identifying, racialized and historically under-recognized communities continue to face systemic barriers and discrimination;

    AND WHEREAS Organizations led by and serving these priority communities, know and understand the complex needs of their communities best, but have been traditionally under-resourced and under-funded to effectively respond;

    AND WHEREAS a Community Capacity Building Fund was created in response to this need and funding was distributed in 2023 alongside the Upstream Fund using budget capacity available in the Equity Fund;

    AND WHEREAS the Equity Fund is fully committed for a range of programs in 2024;

    AND WHEREAS the distribution of funding in 2023 has resulted in increased capacity for organizations led by priority-communities to support achievement of the Community Safety and Wellbeing Plan’s goals;

    NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED:

    THAT the Regional Municipality of Waterloo direct staff to include in the 2024 Operating Budget a provision of $1,000,000 to establish an annual Community Capacity Building Fund with funding from the property tax levy, and

    THAT staff report back in Q1 2024 with the proposed terms of reference for the Community Capacity Building Fund, incorporating the learnings from the Upstream Fund evaluation, and a timeline for application and award.

12.2
Councillor C. Huinink

 

Councillor C. Huinink spoke to her motion, expressing that her intent was to alleviate additional strain on individuals within the community. She articulated her hope that, in the coming year, the motion would lead to further reductions in barriers to transit.

Members of the Committee discussed cash payments, accessible transit, and youth fares. 

  • Moved byC. Huinink
    Seconded byR. Deutschmann

    WHEREAS an increase of the adult Grand River Transit monthly pass from the proposed $96 to $99 per month would generate as much revenue as the proposed cash fare increase from $3.75 to $4; and

    WHEREAS a corresponding increase of the discount rate to 52% for the Affordable Transit Program monthly pass would maintain its cost under $48 per month; and

    WHEREAS an increase of the Grand River Transit monthly pass to $99 per month amounts to less than the cost of one fare per day; and

    WHEREAS an increase of the Grand River Transit monthly pass to $99 per month is more in line with the cost of a bus pass in similar municipalities; and

    WHEREAS maintaining the Grand River Transit cash fare at $3.75 for the duration of 2024 will avoid putting greater financial strain on those who pay transit by cash; and

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT Grand River Transit cash fares be maintained at $3.75 for the duration of 2024 and that the Grand River Transit monthly pass cost be further increased to $99 effective July 1, 2024; and

    THAT the discount level on the monthly pass for customers eligible to the Affordable Transit Program be increased to 52%; and

    THAT the 2024 User Fee and Charge By-law be prepared accordingly.

    In Favor (5)S. Shantz, S. Foxton, P. Wolf, R. Deutschmann, and C. Huinink
    Opposed (7)J. Erb, K. Redman, M. Harris, D. Craig, C. James, N. Salonen, and K. Williams
    Defeated (5 to 7)
