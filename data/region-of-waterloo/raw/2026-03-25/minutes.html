Regional Council Remuneration, Benefits, and Support Review, Mungall Consulting Group Executive Summary

 
1.Report of Findings and Recommendations Council Remuneration Staffing Supports.pdf
2.Regional Council Remuneration Presentation March 2026.pdf

Andrea Friesen and Tammy Elliott of Mungall Consulting Group provided an overview of their consulting services and the scope of the remuneration review specifically their approach and methodology through a lens of both transparency and confidentiality. T. Elliott provided an explanation for the formula for their calculation. The formula considered remuneration for Councillors of City of Cambridge, City of Kitchener, and City of Waterloo, cost of living, and the findings of the previous Citizen Advisory Committee. T. Elliott explained that the analysis found that the remuneration for Councillors was found in alignment with comparable municipalities, and the Regional Chair remuneration is maintaining remuneration levels with comparators. Responding to Council, T. Elliott clarified that the scope of work directed that part-time comparators be used in the analysis and Council may choose to redefine Council roles to justify an adjustment in remuneration.

Responding to a question from Council, A. Friesen explained that additional compensation for a Standing Committee Chair is reported as a flat dollar amount in public records, and there is insufficient data to determine if a base salary increase of 15% is too low for the additional responsibilities. 

Responding to Council, T. Elliott spoke to the evolving risks of assuming the role of a Councillor. T. Elliott spoke of the personal risks and legal liabilities that have increased due to the rise of public intolerance.

12.4.1
CAO-EDI-26-002, Community Safety & Wellbeing Plan (CSWP) Update 2026

 
1.Community Safety and Well Being Plan - Plan Refresh - CAO-EDI-26-002.pdf
2.Apprendix A CSWP Refresh Report Final pdf.pdf
3.Appendix B Community Safety and Wellbeing Plan Refresh Presentation .pdf

Samina Sami, Executive Director, Community Safety and Wellbeing, provided a presentation on the 2026 update to the Community Safety & Wellbeing Plan (CSWP). A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda. She explained that the Community Safety and Policing Act (2019), requires all municipalities in Ontario to develop a CSWP in collaboration with community groups and systems partners, and to refresh these plans every four years. The current plan is set to expire in the Spring of 2026. She highlighted the consultation process, which engaged with over 90 community organizations and all area municipalities. The feedback was collected and incorporated into the plan to reflect community needs and goals.

Fauzia Baig, Director, Equity, Diversity and Inclusion, provided an overview of the four Priority Areas which include Intimate Partner Violence, Gender Based Violence, and Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls and 2SLGBTQIA+ Persons, Combatting Hate, Mental Health and Addictions, and Safe and Inclusive Spaces. She highlighted that the action table engaged Indigenous communities on how to establish Indigenous spaces that will encourage community building and healing.

Aaron Mathias, Inspector of Community Safety Partnerships, expanded on the four priorities by highlighting Intimate Partner Violence, Gender Based Violence, Missing and Murdered Idigenous Women and Girls and 2SLGBTQIA+ Persons as an epidemic. He further emphasized how mental health and addictions continue to drive high demand of resources across paramedic, police, healthcare and community services.

S. Sami highlighted that the focused priorities are wide reaching and intersectional. F. Baig further highlighted the forward-looking themes and priorities which includes clearer governance and administrative processes and deeper embedding of Truth and Reconciliation and Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion. 

 S. Sami concluded with next steps, which will include the public launch of the CSWP and community recognition events in May-April 2026. This will be followed by community engagement and communications, action planing, the Fall Symposium and the Winter Forum in later 2026 and early 2027. 

Council Members expressed concerns that the plan submission deadline did not permit Council and area municipal Councils to provide feedback and input. Council expressed further concerns the general population is not being represented as the community input they have received has been related to theft, senior fraud, and gun violence.

In response to Council, S. Sami explained that there has been expansive community consultation and shared details about decision-making and accountability. S. Sami explained that decisions are made by the steering committee, which is comprised of representatives including the Chief of Police, Chair, elected officials and community members. S. Sami stated they are committed to reviewing representation in governance of the CSWP. She further explained that Regional Council is responsible for approving the CSWP as per legislation. Connie MacDonald, Commissioner, Resident Experience, Strategy and Communications, explained that the CSWP is largely funded through grants, and that the Region and WRPS provides in-kind funding. 

Responding to Council, S. Sami clarified that the CSWP is a living document that signals areas for future development. The plan is to develop these priorities during the next four years to include diverse local issues. 

Council Members expressed concern that the CSWP does not address accountability to the community and should have more specific actions that will be taken to address general community concerns.

Responding to a question from Council, S. Sami explained the Region's plan is notable in how it is robustly rooted in equity, truth, and reconciliation. Many themes in our plan are shared accross other municipalities. 

In response to a question from Council regarding which groups are represented as contributors to the plan, S. Sami confirmed that the CSWP has identified that community input from certain diverse groups needs to be increase, particularly individuals with disabilities, seniors, and rural youth. 

A. Mathias explained that some of the other issues not reflected in the CSWP are included in the WRPS Crime Supression Plan.  

Responding to a question about the governance of the steering committee, S. Sami confirmed that the priorities were identified through collected data which focused the CSWP approach based on community indicators.

Council expressed support for the plan and that there have been opportunities for Council to provide individual feedback to the steering committee. 

Council expressed concerns over consultation and overlap with departments in area muncipalities. S. Sami confirmed that the CSWP emphasizes collaboration and synergy. She provided an example of how the CSWP worked with Immigration Partnership after identifying increasing hate towards newcomers and the correlation with the Compating Hate Campaign. C. MacDonald confirmed that the draft CSWP was circulated to all area municipal CAOs with request to circulate to all Council Members and relevant staff. She explained that the CSWP identified priorities based on evidence-based data with a focus on root-cause issues and that a future focus can include a deeper dialogue.

Council expressed concerns on the lack of attention on provincially and locally recognized issues such as speeding/road safety, housing security, the lack of youth spaces and chronic homelessness. Council recognized that this plan focuses on social and upstream focused priorities, but may be hyper focused on specific groups and underrepresents the broader community. 

S. Sami confirmed that housing and homelessness is imbedded in the CSWP and that rural youth community isolation was also identified as an area of concern. She further confirmed that during consultation, speeding and road safety was not identified in the collected feedback. S. Sami confirmed the CSWP will develop future pillars based on collected evidence-based data. 

Council noted the role of policy in combatting hate, and requested clarification on what success looks like. F. Baig highlighted that the metrics of success is looking back to see the progress that has been achieved during the last four years. A. Mathias further highlighted that the measure of success has been the trust established in the community and explained how established base lines and measurable outcomes are used to measure success. C. MacDonald identified that success has been measurable by seeing community partners collaboration. S. Sami explained that success is dependent on following through with plans and actions, and that success should be a tangible, measured outcome that reflects the community. 

S. Sami explained that yearly updates on the CSWP will be brought back to Council yearly, and that added the word 'prelimary' to the title of the plan may not be in line with the provincial mandate.

EES-WIM-26-003, Optimizing the Sustainable Use of Water Resources for Municipal Supply (1980 Policy on Water Taking - Wilmot Township)

 
1.EES-WIM-26-003, Sustainable Use of Water Resources for Municipal Supply (1980 Water Taking Policy, Wilmot Township).pdf
2.EES-WIM-26-003, Appendix A - Policy of Water Taking in Wilmot Township Apr 1, 1980.pdf
3.EES-WIM-26-003, Appendix B - Portion of Council Direction Apr 26, 1984.pdf
4.EES-WIM-26-003, Appendix C - Wilmot Centre Monitoring Program Map.pdf

The motion was deferred to the Special Council meeting on April 8, 2026.

  • Motion Referred

    1. That Council temporarily suspend the 1980 Council Wilmot Water Take Policy, rather than revoke it, until the Region’s new Water Supply Strategy is completed and presented to Council in 2027;
    2. That Council formally review the status of the 1980 policy upon receipt of the Water Supply Strategy in 2027, prior to making any permanent determination respecting revocation;
    3. That upon initiation of any increased draw from Wilmot wells, staff provide regular public reporting to Regional Council and the public, at a minimum quarterly, including but not limited to:
      • total volume of water being taken;
      • peak flow data;
      • well performance indicators;
      • aquifer health and drawdown monitoring data;
      • commentary on whether continued suspension remains appropriate based on observed conditions;
    4. That staff establish and implement a monitoring framework to collect and publicly report data regarding:
      • neighbouring private wells;
      • surface water systems;
      • agricultural water supply conditions;
      • and any observed correlations associated with increased regional draw;
    5. That staff provide Council and the public with a clear definition of “surplus water” as used in Regional staff's SID Feb. 10 report on the 1980 water take policy, including:
      • the modelling methodology applied;
      • growth and demand assumptions used;
      • safety factors and contingency thresholds;
      • and alignment with Provincial permitting requirements;
    6. That staff provide clarification regarding the basis of the current statement that Wilmot’s growth to 2051 will not be impacted, including:
      • the population and employment forecasts being used;
      • the land use assumptions underlying those forecasts;
      • the infrastructure modelling methodology applied;
      • and the treatment of post-2051 growth within long-term supply planning;
    7. That the forthcoming Water Supply Strategy clearly distinguish between water allocation assumptions for Wilmot’s planned residential and employment growth and any projected demand associated with the Region’s designated Mega Site, including:
      • the range of potential end-user water demand scenarios being modelled for the mega site;
      • the planning assumptions being used in the absence of a confirmed end user;
      • and the implications of those assumptions for long-term regional water allocation and that the mega site water use is independent of the guaranteed allocations for Wilmot's planned growth as per their 2026 Official Plan
    8. And that prior to implementation, Council be provided written confirmation that all applicable provincial permits and regulatory approvals required to support the increased water taking have been obtained
  • Moved byN. Salonen
    Seconded byC. James

16.1
Councillor J. Gowing

 

S. Shantz requested confirmation that private well owners would not be impacted by this motion. In response, F. McCrea confirmed approvals for residential wells will not be affected. 

Council requested confirmation that staff are considering aquafers that are shared with other muncipalities outside of the Region of Waterloo. Ken Brothers, Commissioner of Water and Wastewater, confirmed staff have extensively assessed aquafers both inside and outside the Region, and that staff will be available to answer questions in the April 8, 2026, Special Council Meeting. 

Council considered how the proposed motion would impact developers. N. Salonen requested clarification on the threshold for when the moratorium will no longer need to be in place. In response to Council, J. Gowing explained that the temporary moratorium would end when the Water Supply Strategy Update is provided. K. Brothers stated that a date for the expected Water Supply Strategy Update is currently unavailable, but can be provided by staff the following day. 

  • Moved byJ. Gowing
    Seconded byP. Wolf

    WHEREAS the Province of Ontario regulates Permits to Take Water (PTTW) under the Ontario Water Resources Act;

    AND WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo relies primarily on groundwater for its municipal drinking water supply;

    AND WHEREAS provincial data indicates there are currently 108 active PTTWs within the Region of Waterloo, authorizing a combined maximum taking of approximately 1.01 billion litres per day;

    AND WHEREAS within the Mannheim Service Area municipalities (City of Kitchener, City of Waterloo, Township of Woolwich, and Township of Wilmot ), there are 65 active permits authorizing approximately 740 million litres per day, of which 22 municipal permits account for approximately 472 million litres per day and 43 private permits account for approximately 268 million litres per day;

    AND WHEREAS recent groundwater capacity constraints identified within the Mannheim Service Area highlight the need for a precautionary and cumulative aquifer-level review of private permitted water takings to prevent the risk of interference to existing municipal and private water supplies, and to safeguard groundwater resources for both the natural environment and potential future use for municipal water supply;

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT:

     

    The Region of Waterloo formally request the Province of Ontario:

    1. implement a temporary moratorium on the issuance of new or expansion of existing private-sector Permits to Take Water within areas of the Region of Waterloo experiencing identified groundwater capacity constraints, including the Mannheim Service Area, until completion of the Region’s Water Supply Strategy Update;
    2. continue municipal engagement in the PTTW review process by providing formal notification to affected municipalities of new, amended, or renewed PTTW applications; a defined municipal commenting period; and a written provincial response addressing municipal comments prior to final permit decisions; and
    3. Whilethe Province currently publishes a quarterly Permit to Take Water dataset, require proactive notification and supporting documentation be provided directly to the Region of Waterloo at the time of application submission and final decision.
    In Favor (14)B. Vrbanovic, J. Erb, J. Nowak, K. Redman, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, J. Liggett, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, N. Salonen, C. Huinink, M. Rodrigues, and J. Gowing
    Carried (14 to 0)

C. James provided a description of her motion and confirmed that explicit mentions of specific hate symbols aids in the legal enforcement of hate-motivated crime. 

S. Foxton requested that the motion be shared with Senator Deacon. 

  • Moved byS. Foxton
    Seconded byC. Huinink

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo waive notice of motion as per section 70 of By-law 22-051, A By-law to Provide Rules for Governing the Proceedings of Council and its Committees, and to repeal By-Law 00-031, as amended.

    In Favor (14)B. Vrbanovic, J. Erb, J. Nowak, K. Redman, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, J. Liggett, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, N. Salonen, C. Huinink, M. Rodrigues, and J. Gowing

  • Moved byC. James
    Seconded byJ. Gowing

    Whereas the Region of Waterloo’s Community Safety and Well-Being Plan, developed under the requirements of the Community Safety and Policing Act, identifies systemic racism, hate, and discrimination as critical risk factors impacting community safety, belonging, and well-being;

    And whereas the display of a noose is widely recognized as a symbol of racial terror, violence, and intimidation, particularly against Black communities, rooted in the history of anti-Black racism;

    And whereas symbols of hate, when displayed publicly, contribute to fear, trauma, and exclusion, and undermine the Region’s commitments to equity, inclusion, and proactive prevention within its Community Safety and Well-Being Plan;

    And whereas the Government of Canada has introduced Bill C-9 to strengthen tools to address hate-motivated offences, including provisions related to the public display of certain hate symbols;

    And whereas the current draft of Bill C-9 does not explicitly include the noose as a prohibited symbol, despite its well-documented use as an instrument of racial intimidation and its direct relevance to community safety and well-being outcomes;

    And whereas addressing hate symbols through federal legislation complements municipal efforts by strengthening upstream prevention, reducing harm, and supporting safer, more inclusive communities;

    Therefore be it resolved that:

    1. The Regional Municipality of Waterloo formally request that the Government of Canada amend Bill C-9 to explicitly include the noose as a prohibited hate symbol within the legislation; 
    2. The Regional Chair be directed to send correspondence to the Hon. MP Sean Fraser, Minister of Justice and Attorney General of Canada, local Members of Parliament, and relevant federal committees conveying this request, including the Senate of Canada, noting its alignment with the Region’s Community Safety and Well-Being Plan priorities;
    3. This motion be circulated to all Ontario municipalities, the Federation of Canadian Municipalities (FCM), and the Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO) for endorsement and support as a measure that strengthens community safety and well-being across jurisdictions.
    In Favor (14)B. Vrbanovic, J. Erb, J. Nowak, K. Redman, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, J. Liggett, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, N. Salonen, C. Huinink, M. Rodrigues, and J. Gowing
    Carried (14 to 0)
