4.1
2024 Plan and Budget

 

Craig Dyer, Commissioner, Corporate Services/Chief Financial Officer introduced the presentation, speaking to Regional assessment growth, 2024 operating budget status, and the proposed use of the Tax Stabilization Reserve in the preliminary 2024 budget. 

John Riches, Director/Chief of Paramedic Services, continued the presentation, discussing the range of services and service levels offered by Paramedic Services and their contributions to the community. He emphasized that the dedicated staff delivers crucial services to around 190 patients daily. He underscored the significance of ensuring efficient services, highlighting the interdependence among the staff members, who act as critical support for one another. He emphasized the collaborative nature of their work, emphasizing how each staff member relies on others, creating a cohesive support system. Julie Wheeler, Director of Seniors' Services, proceeded with the presentation, underscoring the vital role played by Regional staff in keeping severity levels low during COVID-19 outbreaks at Sunnyside Homes facility. She emphasized the importance of well-staffed and well-resourced teams, crediting the successful mitigation of outbreaks in long-term care settings to the steadfast support provided by the Regional staff.

Doug Spooner, Director, Transit Services continued the presentation speaking to the services and current service levels of the Grand River Transit (GRT). He emphasized that GRT's ability to provide transit services to residents hinges on the critical support from staff across the organization.

Kim Bellissimo, Commissioner, Human Resources & Citizen Service, spoke to employee retention, increased diversity in leadership, municipal related Truth and Reconciliation calls to action, and service renovations. Cheryl Braan, Director, Corporate Finance continued the presentation speaking to the human resources and citizen services budget highlighting that the biggest expenditure in the forecast is the Human Experience Management Solution.

In response to questions from the Committee, C. Dyer emphasized that staff regularly assess opportunities to repurpose positions within the organization, and this evaluation occurs continuously, extending beyond the confines of the budget process. 

Responding to inquiries, Mathieu Goetzke, Commissioner of Transportation Services, discussed the potential outsourcing of functions related to the Automated Speed Enforcement (ASE). He clarified that while certain aspects, such as camera provision, are being outsourced, highly regulated functions such as those performed by provincial officers, cannot be outsourced due to strict regulations. Therefore, the decision to outsource functions depends on the nature of the specific position in question.

The Committee deliberated on exploring service expansions with the aim of postponing the start date of positions to mitigate costs and minimize the levy impact in 2024. Staff were given direction to provide this information and report back on December 13, 2023. 

Will Short, Director, Council & Administrative Services/Regional Clerk, spoke to the implementation of Lobbyist Registry Program. He outlined the objectives and proposed changes for the program, emphasizing its potential to enhance accountability in the interactions between lobbyists and Region of Waterloo public officials and staff. Furthermore, he stressed that the program aims to increase transparency for the public.

Jerry Biersteker, Director, Facilities & Fleet Management spoke to building a resilient and future ready organization in facilities management including Facilities Asset Management, objectives, and proposed changes. Mike Henderson, Director, Transportation continued the presentation speaking to the objectives and proposed changes for Transportation Services, highlighting improving service response times and diversifying data sources to support evidence-based decision making for road safety.

C. Dyer continued the presentation, speaking to the 2024 funding for external organizations. He highlighted that there is official notification from the Province of Ontario about the discontinuation of transition funding associated with Reaching Home funding, effective April 1, 2024. Staff is currently assessing the impact of this change and will provide a detailed report with more information on December 13, 2023, for further discussion.

4.2
2024-2033 Capital Program

 

C. Dyer introduced the 2024-2033 Capital Program presentation speaking to the review of the draft 2024-2033 capital program.

Jennifer Rose, Commissioner, Engineering & Environmental Services spoke to corporate asset management, strategic investments, levels of service, infrastructure interventions, growth, and reserves. C. Braan spoke to the 10 year capital program, existing assets (renewal) and new assets (growth). 

In response to a question from the Committee, Peter Sweeney, Commissioner, Community Services, stated that currently the capital investments are all related to the previously approved masterplan and the building better futures. 

Answering questions, C. Dyer explained that adjustments made to certain capital projects won't affect the levy in 2024. However, it is important to note that these changes will have an impact on future budgets.

D. Spooner proceeded with the presentation, providing an overview of the 2024-2033 capital investments for Transit. He highlighted the ongoing projects, those set to begin in 2024, and outlined future initiatives.

In response to a Committee question, D. Spooner discussed the eligibility criteria for the Affordable Transit Program (ATP), emphasizing its availability to all residents in the Waterloo Region with low income, encompassing children aged 17 and younger. M. Goetzke added that, as part of the ongoing business plan, staff are exploring diverse age categories and strategies to enhance the affordability of transit for youth.

J. Rose furthered the presentation speaking to the 2024-2033 capital investment overview for Water Supply. She highlighted new water supply to support growth, consolidation and optimization of water supplies, and the pressure zone upgrades to Region's transmission network. Jon Arsenault, Director, Waste Management continued the presentation speaking to the capital investment overview for Waste Management. He highlighted the Waterloo cell construction, the Kitchener decommission site environmental controls,  and vehicle and heavy equipment replacement.

M. Goetzke continued the presentation speaking to affordable homes and economic opportunity for all that are part of inclusive and environmental sustainable communities. Gord Ryan, Director, Rapid Transit continued the presentation speaking to rapid transit highlighting business cases for Stage 2 LRT, design/studies for Stage 2, and Stage 2 construction. M. Henderson made comments with regards to talked about road growth. road expansion and roads program.  

Members of the Committee discussed and enquired about road widening and road repairs/inspections.

P. Sweeney continued the presentation, speaking to the 2024-2033 capital investment overview for Housing Services. He outlined the ongoing projects, those set to begin in 2024, and outlined future initiatives, highlighting Waterloo Regional Housing (WRH) units in Waterloo, Kitchener, and Cambridge under construction.

In response to questions, P. Sweeney provided additional details about the community housing waitlist, mentioning that staff will present a comprehensive review of improvements to the waitlist program to an upcoming Council meeting.

Rod Regier, Commissioner, Planning, Development & Legislative Services continued, speaking to the capital investments in Airport Services. He highlighted the Southeast surface parking expansion, design and construction of airport terminal expansion phase 2, and airport vehicle replacement. 

The Committee members discussed and inquired about the airport expansion, operating costs, and environmental studies. R. Regier explained that the expected initiatives from 2024-2026 stem from the Masterplan, scheduled for presentation to Council in early 2025. The Masterplan will encompass environmental studies potentially required for relocating the terminal. This plan will address sustainable aviation fuels and other measures aimed at mitigating the environmental impacts, such as greenhouse gas emissions, associated with airport operations.

R. Regier furthered the presentation, speaking to the Tax Increment Grants (TIG).

J. Riches continued the presentation, speaking to the Paramedic Services capital investment overview, highlighting a planning expansion of Waterloo station, a construction of new station in Cambridge in 2025, and the construction of 3 new stations in Waterloo, Kitchener, and Cambridge between 2027-2032.

In response to a committee question about partnerships and the exploration of building on top of stations, especially for affordable housing, J. Riches mentioned that one of the ongoing projects indeed focuses on affordable housing. The current plan entails dedicating the first floor to a specific purpose, while the upper levels will accommodate affordable housing units.

R. Regier continued the presentation, speaking to the capital investment overview for Cultural Services. He spoke to the continuing work for Doon Village, the Schneider Museum development, and the expansion of capacity in the Curatorial centre. 

J. Biersteker furthered the presentation, speaking to the capital investments into Facilities and Fleet management. He highlighted the Corporate Space Optimization that is currently underway, for a more innovating and hybrid work environment to staff.
