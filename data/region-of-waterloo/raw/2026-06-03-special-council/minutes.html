7.1
Larry Masseo, Policy Advisor, Waterloo Region Home Builders’ Association

 

L. Masseo spoke on the financial harm the development industry is experiencing due to the water capacity constraints.

L. Masseo requested Council immediately adopt a maximum 10% initial resiliency target and publish a forward-looking allocation policy with defined criteria, timelines, and reporting. L. Masseo further asked Council to allow projects with full planning approvals to move forward, move to increase water conservation, and take leadership to a establish a unified allocation policy across the Area Municipalities.

In response to Council, L. Masseo explained that through engagement, many issues have been raised with Regional staff.

7.2
John Hartwick, CEO, Activa

 

J. Hartwick spoke in favour of the motion to reallocate additional water from Wilmot to the Mannheim Service Area. J. Hartwick asked Council to consider allocating this water immediately instead of waiting until September.

J. Hartwick emphasized the urgency to respond to the Federal and Provincial mandate to build new homes and take advantage of the GST/HST rebate program that will end on March 31, 2027. J. Hartwick asked Council to allow the transfer of water capacity from one approved project in the same community to another to allow building to progress without impacting the amount of water required.

Responding to a question from Committee, J. Hartwick confirmed that the project in question would use less water than what was previously approved by the Region. J. Hartwick also further explained that in order to be eligible for the enhanced HST rebate, a signed purchase agreement needs to be completed by March 2027, and the construction needs to be completed by the end of 2031.

7.3
Chris Gerrits, Reopen Waterloo Region

 

C. Gerrits spoke to Council regarding concerns over the interim risk management framework in report EES-WIM-26-012. C. Gerrits voiced concerns about the timing included in the interim risk management framework and provided suggestions for increasing water capacity sooner.

C. Gerrits asked Council to consider reallocating water from Wilmot immediately, prioritize the work scheduled for Q3 of 2030 for the Middleton Mannheim connection project, look at the E10 Well for additional capacity, move up work scheduled for the P18 Well, and investigate emergency interconnection with the Middleton wells for risk mitigation. C. Gerrits further asked Council to immediately lift the development freeze within the Region of Waterloo.

In response to Council, C. Gerrits confirmed that the ideas to improve water capacity have been brought forward to Regional staff. C. Gerrits expressed concern over inconsistencies in what is being shared by staff and what is coming forward to Council.

7.4
Melissa Durrell, Executive Officer, Build Urban

 

M. Durrell shared concerns that the proposed Interim Risk Management Framework in report EES-WIM-26-012 is too restrictive.

M. Durrell asked Council to adopt an interim operational resiliency target of 10%, publish a forward-facing framework, and allow projects which have received approval to proceed with conditional permits and phased construction.

M. Durrell further asked Council to adopt a unified interim allocation policy with Area Municipalities with a commitment to return to unconstrained approvals once capacity is restored and implement enhanced conservation and demand management measures until the full 20% resiliency target is reached.

In response to Council, M. Durrell stressed the need for clear and direct communications.

8.2
EES-WIM-26-012, Mannheim Service Area - Recommended Interim Risk Management Framework

 
1.EES-WIM-26-012, Mannheim Service Area - Recommended Interim Risk Management Framework.pdf
2.Appendix A Interim Risk Management Framework – Final Draft Report.pdf.pdf
3.Appendix B Mannheim Service Area - Recommended IRMF Presentation.pdf.pdf

K. Brothers introduced the report and gave a brief overview of the proposed Interim Risk Management Framework. A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda.

Nicole Sapeta, Senior Engineer, presented the Interim Risk Management Framework, which provides an approach for how staged development could proceed based on acceptable thresholds and evidence-based decisions. N. Sapeta summarized the risks of the framework, including risks to water supply, water operations. capital program delivery, human resources, the economic and financial standing of residents and the Region, and growth/investment confidence.

N. Sapeta outlined the three (3) risk scenarios for the Interim Risk Management Framework, including 1) optimized planning to reliably supply water, 2) deferral of planned shutdowns, and 3) temporary extended pumping of supply sources. N. Sapeta explained that scenarios two (2) and three (3) build off scenario one (1) to increase water capacity.

N. Sapeta stated that through risk scoring, scenario two (2) was identified as having the lowest overall risk. N. Sapeta explained that the framework was built to be flexible and can adapt to changes over time, with timing to be confirmed as key milestones are completed. The Interim Risk Management Framework determines how much water can be made available, which will be allocated through the Mannheim Allocation Policy.

K. Brothers explained the recommended staff motions to endorse scenario two of the Interim Risk Management Framework and to allocate an additional 15L/s from Wilmot Center on an interim basis.

Responding to a question from Council, P. Law explained that the resiliency target is not set as a concrete percentage, instead it is based on what is required to have a safe operation of the Region's water system. P. Law stated that setting a firm percentage for the resiliency target would remove the Region's ability to adjust the required amount of resiliency as needed.

P. Law explained that the Region is currently not at the minimum protection for emergencies, and this will need to be achieved before releasing any allocation for growth.

Council discussed concerns about residents outside the historical compensation zones being impacted by additional pumping, and the Region's ability to supply emergency water assistance.

Council discussed a motion to add safeguards to the recommendation to allocate an additional 15L/s from Wilmot Centre to the Mannheim Service Area.

Responding to a question from Council regarding the ability to transfer water capacity between unbuilt projects that will not add additional capacity, Amanda Kutler, Director of Regional Growth, Development, and Sustainability Services, explained that there are plans to bring a report back to Council investigating this as an option after receiving several requests. A. Kutler explained that there needs to be both the water capacity and administrative framework to move forward with this.

In response to Council, K. Brothers explained how the framework is based on a fluid resiliency target. K. Brothers further explained that by setting a 10% minimum resiliency target, the framework will not work and would put the Region at a higher measure of risk.

P. Law clarified that a 10% resiliency target would not allow for key wells to be shut down for critical maintenance or repair.

Responding to Council, P. Law stated the Region is currently sitting at 11% resiliency, which provides protection for both planned shutdowns and unplanned emergencies.

Amy Shaw, Director, Water and Wastewater Operations, explained that staff are unable to sign off on Form 1s while the Region is in a deficit.

In response to Council, F. McCrea explained that bringing a full framework back to Council for forward-facing servicing agreements before the June 17, 2026, Council meeting would be challenging, but that staff will try to bring an update.

Council discussed the need to balance the needs for a safe water supply with the needs of the economy.

Council debated amendments to the recommended staff motion, including a 10% interim resiliency target, providing clarity and signing off on Form 1s for projects that have already received full planning approval, and engaging the Ontario Clean Water Agency to conduct an independent review.

Chair K. Redman called a ten (10) minutes recess.

K. Brothers explained staffs concerns regarding an amendment to the motion to set a 10% resiliency target, stated it is incompatible with the Interim Risk Management Framework and would extend the period in which Form 1s cannot be signed. K. Brothers further explained this this would be counterproductive and increase the length of delay for developers.

Council challenged the Chair's ruling to call on M. Grenier to speak. The Chair's ruling was upheld.

M. Grenier emphasized that under Section 19 of the Safe Drinking Water Act, individuals with decision making authority over municipal drinking water systems are both personally and legally liable. M. Grenier further stated that bypassing staff recommendations indicates a willingness to accept liability as individuals for providing safe drinking water, should an emergency occur.

Responding to a question from Council seeking clarification on why a 10% maximum resiliency target cannot be established, P. Law explained that the Interim Risk Management Framework relies on a target that exceeds this threshold at times. She further explained that setting a maximum resiliency target of 10% would override the risk analysis done to ensure safe drinking water.

Responding to a question from Council, P. Law stated that the decision to set a fluid resiliency target below 20% was in response to feedback that the target should be phased in. P. Law further explained that the resiliency targets are intended to protect the minimum amount needed to allow planned works and prepare for emergencies.

Council provided unanimous consent to strike the motions on the floor and put forward the amendments as individual motions.

Council discussed the possibility of approving the risk management framework and making changes to it after Provincial review. A. Shaw explained that if the framework is approved and Council decides to adopt a 10% resiliency target at a later date, the framework would be null, and staff would need to redo the three-month consultation and development process with stakeholders.

    That Recommendation (b) be amended as follows:

    “That the Region of Waterloo, at Council’s direction, allocate an additional 15 L/s from the Wilmot Centre Well Supply on an interim basis solely as a bridge measure until the side stream capacity solution is commissioned and operational, at which point the interim 15 L/s allocation shall automatically terminate and the water supply allocation shall revert to the previously approved levels.

    Further, that any future consideration of additional water allocation from the Wilmot Supplies beyond the existing approved allocation levels be considered through the updated Water Supply Strategy and include:

    i) an assessment of impacts on surrounding private well properties;
    ii) costing and implementation options to connect impacted private well properties to the Integrated Servicing Utility (ISU) system; and

    iii) costing and implementation options for deep replacement wells or other measures that would provide a long-term, reliable and secure water supply for affected residents.

    Further, that as an interim measure, effective immediately and until such time as a new Well Interference Policy is adopted by Regional Council, the Region of Waterloo provide emergency water supply assistance to any Wilmot resident experiencing water loss or well interference, and that such assistance not be limited to properties located within the existing historic well interference compensation area.

    For greater certainty, the provision of emergency water supply assistance shall be considered a precautionary measure intended to protect residents’ access to water and shall not, in and of itself, be interpreted as an admission of responsibility, liability, or well interference by the Region of Waterloo.

    Further, that as an interim measure, effective immediately and until such time as a new Well Interference Policy is adopted by Regional Council, where a Wilmot resident reports water loss or well interference reasonably believed to be associated with Regional groundwater takings, the burden of demonstrating that Regional pumping is not the cause of the interference shall rest with the Region of Waterloo and not with the affected resident.

    Further, that these above interim measures be reviewed as part of the Well Interference Policy review currently being brought forward to Regional Council in August 2026, at which time Council may confirm, amend, or replace these requirements.
