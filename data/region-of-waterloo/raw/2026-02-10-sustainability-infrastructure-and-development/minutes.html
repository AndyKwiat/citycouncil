    1. That Council temporarily suspendthe 1980 Council Wilmot Water Take Policy, rather than revoke it, until the Region’s new Water Supply Strategy is completed and presented to Council in 2027;

    • well performance indicators;
    • aquifer health and drawdown monitoring data;
    • commentary on whether continued suspension remains appropriate based on observed conditions;
    1. That staff establish and implement a monitoring frameworkto collect and publicly report data regarding:
      • neighbouring private wells;
      • surface water systems;
      • agricultural water supply conditions;
      • and any observed correlations associated with increased regional draw;

      • the modelling methodology applied;
      • growth and demand assumptions used;
      • safety factors and contingency thresholds;
      • and alignment with Provincial permitting requirements;
    2. That staff provide clarification regarding the basis of the current statement that Wilmot’s growth to 2051 will not be impacted, including:
      • the population and employment forecasts being used;
      • the land use assumptions underlying those forecasts;
      • the infrastructure modelling methodology applied;
      • and the treatment of post-2051 growth within long-term supply planning;
    1. That the forthcoming Water Supply Strategy clearly distinguish between water allocation assumptions for Wilmot’s planned residential and employment growth and any projected demand associated with the Region’s designated Mega Site, including:
      • the range of potential end-user water demand scenarios being modelled for the mega site;
      • the planning assumptions being used in the absence of a confirmed end user;
      • and the implications of those assumptions for long-term regional water allocation and that the mega site water use is independent of the guaranteed allocations for Wilmot's planned growth as per their 2026 Official Plan

Councillor J. Gowing, Protection of Existing Groundwater Supply and Wetlands

 
  • Moved byJ. Gowing
    Seconded byC. Huinink

    That staff of the Region of Waterloo be directed to report back to Council alongside the Water Supply Strategy in 2027 or sooner on measures available to protect and preserve the Region’s existing groundwater supply and associated wetlands, recognizing current water capacity constraints, long-term asset management planning and recent provincial legislative changes, including:

    1. an assessment of the effectiveness of existing Regional policies, designations, and development controls in protecting groundwater recharge areas, source water protection zones, wetlands, and moraine lands that contribute to the Region’s current water supply, and identification of any gaps or vulnerabilities;
    2. an evaluation of how land-use change within recharge areas and wetlands may affect groundwater availability, long-term water security, and future infrastructure requirements under existing growth scenarios;
    3. identification of planning, policy, or regulatory tools currently available to the Region to strengthen protection of existing water supply sources within the constraints of Bill 23 and provincial housing and growth objectives;
    4. consideration of precautionary or interim risk-mitigation measures that could reduce further stress on existing groundwater systems while remaining compliant with provincial legislation;
    5. identification of opportunities for coordination with area municipalities and conservation authorities to improve alignment between source water protection science and land-use decision-making;
    6. options for the Region to advocate to the Provincial Government for a provincially enabled “Bluebelt” or similar legislative framework to provide permanent protection for critical groundwater recharge areas, wetlands, and moraine lands beyond municipal authority, including an assessment of potential implications for growth management and infrastructure planning.

    And that the report be presented for information and discussion purposes, without prejudice, to future policy direction of Council.

  • Moved byB. Vrbanovic
    Seconded byM. Rodrigues

    WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo has identified water supply capacity constraints within the Mannheim Service Area through its Water Supply Strategy update and associated third party technical review; and

     

    WHEREAS the Region’s existing methodology for determining remaining water supply capacity has been updated to reflect operational constraints, hydrological realities, and the need to maintain an operational resiliency buffer; and

     

    WHEREAS the Region has already established a risk-based approach to provide allocation of future unbuilt water and wastewater service capacity to enable growth (example: plans of subdivision) where the following criteria has been met:

    1. A capacity expansion project is imminent and included in the first five years of the Region’s 10year capital forecast,
    2. A sound technical basis exists for the anticipated capacity (supported by an Environmental Assessment, master plan, or equivalent engineering analysis), and
    3. Area Municipal recommendations guide approvals, with no more than 50% of projected new capacity allocated in advance of completion; and

    WHEREAS the Region has developed a preliminary list of initiatives to restore and increase capacity within the Mannheim Service Area; and

    WHEREAS municipal and industry partners have identified the urgent need for a clear, transparent, Regionwide allocation approach to ensure consistent interpretation of capacity constraints and development impacts across all area municipalities; and

    WHEREAS Waterloo Region continues to be in a housing supply crisis, particularly for lower income individuals within Waterloo Region; and

    WHEREAS according to the 2019 labour force survey 24,300 people are employed in construction which could be at risk.

     

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED:

    That Region staff be directed to work with Area Municipalities on a risk-based approach to advance the allocation of up to fifty per cent of planned and unbuilt capacity coming on-line within the next 5 years to support development approvals, in conjunction with restoring sustainable operating rates, and the minimum operational resiliency, consistent with the Region’s long-standing practice.

     

    That Regional staff work with Area Municipalities to develop and implement an enhanced water conservation program, including possible regulatory, pricing and incentive-based measures, to both mitigate peak system demands year-round and especially from May 1 – September 30 in order to enable restored and new water supply capacity to come online in parallel with continued development approvals as outlined in Recommendation 1.

    In Favor (12)K. Redman, D. Craig, S. Foxton, J. Gowing, M. Harris, C. Huinink, C. James, J. Liggett, D. McCabe, M. Rodrigues, N. Salonen, and B. Vrbanovic
