Delegations

 

8.1
Donald Shaver, Kitchener

 

D. Shaver appeared before Council to request Council support for planting wild fruit trees along trails and within flood plains. He highlighted that it would be accessible to low income members of the public and wildlife, with no ongoing costs to the Region.

S. Foxton noted that the Grand River Accessibility Advisory Committee looks at the accessibility of trails whenever a new trail is developed and stated that she can raise the issue of planting native fruit trees with the Grand River Conservation Authority.  

8.2
Hanibal Teklehaimanot 

 

H. Teklehaimanot stated that he has been in Canada for seven years and wanted to draw attention to some issues that don’t get a lot of attention. He encouraged people that are holding positions in power to give support for people that need financial support and young generations. 

8.3
Victor Labreche, Cambridge

 

V. Labreche appeared before Council on behalf of Nicole and Robert Dixon. He requested that Council waive a $7000 Official Plan Amendment Review Fee for his clients, noting that Cambridge Council has already waived their Official Plan Amendment application fee, the fee is redundant given that the recent ROPA #6 approved by the Province permits limited development based on conditions that have already been met, and the application has already been extensively reviewed by Regional staff.

8.4
Beatrice Henry, Basic Income Waterloo Region

 

B. Henry provided a presentation in support of a basic income guarantee. A copy of the presentation is appended to the original minutes. She explained what a basic income guarantee is and the results of the project that was piloted and the need for a basic income to address poverty.

8.5
Carol Stalker, Basic Income Waterloo Region

 

C. Stalker shared stories of individuals that participated in the basic income pilot project, noting that it lets people participate in their local economies and reduces poverty and homelessness. She stressed that inequality in Canada has increased dramatically and basic income pilots have decreased costs of legal, social and health services.

8.6
Rob Evans, Basic Income Waterloo Region

 

R. Evans noted that a basic income plan for Canada would cost between $40 billion and $50 billion. He highlighted opportunities where this money could be found including a wealth tax on the super rich or increasing corporate taxes.   

8.7
Nelson Chukwuma, President, Conestoga Students Inc.

 

N. Chukwuma appeared on behalf of Conestoga Students Inc. in support of the implementation of a Conestoga College Universal Transit Pass (U-Pass) program. He noted that student growth has benefited Grand River Transit (GRT) and that this expansion supports both Regional and student goals.

In response to a question from Council, Kevan Marshall, Project Manager, Climate & Energy Transition, confirmed that the recently improved transit in Cambridge provides service to the skilled trades campus, and if the U-Pass is approved there will additional service recommended through the 2024 budget process.

8.8
Craig Sloss, Waterloo

 

C. Sloss appeared in support of D. McCabe’s motion to reallocate 70 hectares from the urban boundary. He noted that areas such as parks and conservation areas would be good candidates and encouraged the Region to not fear the province’s response.

D. Craig asked for staff’s position on the request from V. Labreche on behalf of the Dicksons. Rod Regier, Commissioner, Planning, Development & Legislative Services, stated that the by-law does not allow for fee waivers the same way that Cambridge’s does. Council requested that staff bring a report back on the request.

12.2.1
TSD-TRP-23-011.1, Cambridge Truck Diversion Study (Staff Presentation)

 

Mike Henderson, Acting Director, Transportation, provided a presentation outlining further staff findings after direction from the Planning and Works Committee. A copy of the presentation is appended to the original minutes.

Kornel Mucsi, Manager, Transportation Planning, provided an overview of the plan presented to the Planning and Works Committee. He explained that staff reviewed the request related to banning trucks on Concession Street, and in order to accomplish this, there are additional downtown streets that would need to be added to the truck ban. If Council bans trucks from these additional streets it will result in an increase in traffic on McQueen Shaver Boulevard that warrants a noise wall throughout. K. Musci cautioned that this decision could result in push back from the truck industry and issues in similar locations in the Region.

Council requested that staff provide an update on compliance with the Cambridge truck diversion in the summer of 2024.

In response to a question from Council, Mathieu Goetzke, Commissioner, Transportation Services, stated that staff are not considering any changes to the design of McQueen Shaver Boulevard to slow speeds, noting that they want to encourage trucks to use this route and avoid smaller residential streets.

17.2
Councillor P. Wolf Notice of Motion

 

Council considered the motion and Councillors spoke in support of the benefits of a guaranteed basic income.

M. Harris left the meeting at 9:09 PM. ()
N. Salonen left the meeting at 9:10 PM. ()
  • Moved byP. Wolf
    Seconded byD. Craig

    WHEREAS, the Region of Waterloo has a strategic focus of creating Healthy, Safe Inclusive Communities for the well being and prosperity of all its residents, which includes access to basic needs and opportunities to improve health;

    WHEREAS, social assistance recipients are living in extreme poverty and food insecurity and are at great risk to develop associated health issues;

    WHEREAS, Ontario Works (OW) and Ontario Disability Support Program (ODSP) rates are below Canada’s Official Poverty Line and since social assistance rates were reduced by 21.6% in 1995, rate increases have been nominal, representing an approximate 10% increase over 26 years;

    WHEREAS, Ontario Works rates have not been increased since 2018 and are not indexed to inflation causing further income inequality, poverty, and housing unaffordability;

    WHEREAS, use of food banks in Waterloo Region has increased by 45% since June of 2022. Evidence continues to show that food-based programs do not resolve food insecurity because it is an inadequate income problem, much bigger than just food. Many of the residents accessing local food banks are employed;

    WHEREAS, a Basic Income program was tested in Hamilton, Brantford, and Brant County from 2017 to 2019 as part of the Ontario Basic Income Pilot project and research shows that all the recipients in the pilot project who received the basic income reported benefitting in some way. Many reported improvements in their physical and mental health, labour market participation, food security, housing stability, financial status, and social relationships;

    WHEREAS, the downstream effects of increasing poverty is putting unsustainable pressure on municipalities limited resources;

    WHEREAS, the provision of a Guaranteed Liveable Basic Income would benefit individuals, families, and communities and protect the most vulnerable in society, it would also support community resilience by facilitating the transition to a local economy that responds to the climate crisis and other major challenges.

    WHEREAS, evidence shows that a federally funded basic income is possible, as successful income programs already exist in Canada for seniors (Old Age Security and the Guaranteed Income Supplement) and for families with children (Canada Child Benefit CCB);

    WHEREAS, the Federal budget Office upon reviewing the concept of a national Guaranteed Basic Income program determined it could, if properly set out, be a major economic driver to the Canadian economy; and

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED

    1. That the Region of Waterloo asks the provincial government to increase Social Assistance rates for both OW and ODSP to reflect the costs of living and to tie these rates to inflation.
    2. That the Region of Waterloo supports the concept of a Guaranteed Liveable Basic Income to combat poverty, income inequality, and economic insecurity.  
    3. That the Region of Waterloo calls on the provincial and federal governments to collaborate to implement a national Guaranteed Liveable Basic Income program.
    4. That the Regional Council directs the Regional Chair to write a letter to the Prime Minister, local Members of Parliament, and the Senate, the Premier of Ontario, local members of the Legislative Assembly of Ontario, calling on these orders of government to work collaboratively towards implementing a National Guaranteed Liveable Basic Income to eradicate poverty and homelessness, and through their representatives ensure everyone in Canada  has sufficient income to meet their basic needs.
    5. That the Region of Waterloo encourages other cities and regions to advocate for a Guaranteed Liveable Basic Income to their MPs and MPPs and at both Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO) and the Federation of Canadian Municipalities (FCM).
    In Favor (12)B. Vrbanovic, J. Erb, J. Nowak, K. Redman, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, D. McCabe, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, R. Deutschmann, and K. Williams
    Carried (12 to 0)
