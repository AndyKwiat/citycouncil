A&F Summary - September 8, 2026

 
1.Administration and Finance Summary - September 8, 2026.pdf
  • Recommended Motion:

    The Administration and Finance Committee recommends as follows:

    1. That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo direct the Regional Clerk to issue public notice of its intention to, on October 14, 2026:
      1. repeal By-Law Number 26-017, as amended, effective November 14, 2026, of the Regional Municipality of Waterloo, a By-Law to Govern the Proceedings of the Council and its Committees; and
      2. approve the proposed Regional Procedural By-law, to be effective on November 15, 2026, as provided in Appendix A of Report PDL-CAS-26-015 dated September 8, 2026.
    2. That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo adopt the refreshed Grand River Accessibility Advisory Committee Terms of Reference as provided in Appendix A of Report CAO-EDI-26-003, dated September 8, 2026.
      And that staff report to Council on September 23 with proposed language to ensure at least one GRAAC member be appointed from the area townships.
    3. That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo defer the following motion to the 2027 Budget process as a proposed addition to the budget:
      WHEREAS Woolwich Community Health Centre (WCHC) is a long standing local community governed not-for-profit charity that provides comprehensive primary health care and community support to over 28,000 residents of Waterloo Region, and;
      WHEREAS the rural population has more than 8,000 residents with no family doctor, and;
      WHEREAS WCHC has attached over 2,400 patients across local municipalities to a primary care provider and is working to increase access for roughly 6,000 more through the current expansion, and;
      WHEREAS the Regional portion of the Development Charges on the expansion project have been assessed at $81,681.86, and the Township of Woolwich has provided a grant of $35, 925.06, equal to their portion of development charges;
      THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT the Region of Waterloo provide a grant to WCHC in the amount of $81,681.86 to cover the Regional costs associated with the expansion.

11.2.2
CHS Summary - September 8, 2026

 
1.CHS Summary - September 8, 2026.pdf
  • Recommended Motion:

    The Community and Health Services Committee recommends as follows:

    1. That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo approve the Response Time Performance Plan (RTPP) for 2027, outlined below, in accordance with the Ambulance Act, O. Reg. 257/00, under Part VIII, Response Time Standards, as amended by O. Reg. 251/25, s. 5, as outlined in PHP-PSV-26-005, dated September 8, 2026.
      1. Sudden Cardiac Arrest (SCA) – 6 minutes or less 70% of the time. (Note: Ministry of Health target is 50%.)
      2. CTAS 1 – 8 minutes or less 70% of the time.
      3. CTAS 2 – 10 minutes or less 80% of the time.
      4. CTAS 3 – 15 minutes or less 80% of the time.
      5. CTAS 4 – 20 minutes or less 80% of the time.
      6. CTAS 5 – 25 minutes or less 80% of the time.

    2. Whereas the Region of Waterloo is responsible for funding and coordinating homelessness services, including emergency shelters, supportive and transitional housing, and outreach programming; and

      Whereas Council has a responsibility to ensure that new investments in housing and homelessness services do not unintentionally reinforce geographic inequities but instead contribute to a more balanced and accessible regional system; and

      Whereas the Region's commitment to addressing homelessness must include, not only increasing overall capacity, but also ensuring equitable access to services across all municipalities, including a more equitable distribution of funding and services dedicated specifically to women; and

      Whereas there are only 20 emergency homeless shelter beds for women in Cambridge, 42 total throughout the entire Region and only 80 of all shelter beds dedicated to men are in Cambridge while the remaining 130 are in Kitchener-Waterloo; 120 all gender beds in Kitchener-Waterloo and

      Whereas the single women’s shelter in Cambridge has been forced to deny almost 300 requests for services due to consistently being at capacity since August 2025; and

      Whereas residents experiencing homelessness in Cambridge are frequently required to travel outside their community to access shelter and other homelessness supports services, creating additional barriers to safety, continuity of care, and connection to local supports; and

      Whereas Porchlight Counselling and Addiction Services’ winter warming centre for men operated at a minimum of 128% and maximum of 198% throughout its 2025-6 seasonal operations and their request to operate this centre 365 days a year was rejected by the Region, the reason given being a lack of funding; and

      Whereas Thrive HIV Prevention and Support, which operates the Multi Agency Community Space out of 150 Main in Cambridge - the single drop-in space for individuals experiencing homelessness in Cambridge - is only funded to operate during afternoons, despite an immense need for extended operating hours; and 

      Whereas YWCA Cambridge has requested that the Region fund a 365-day, low-barrier overnight drop-in for women in Cambridge and been repeatedly told there is not adequate funding to do so, despite the lack of any overnight spaces for women in Cambridge existing outside of the 20-bed shelter; and

      Whereas the Region recently granted Kitchener a 365-day, 22.5-hr-a-day shelter; and

      Whereas such recent funding decisions only further concentrate services in one city and predominantly on programs dedicated to men or which are co-ed, while neglecting to address the immense lack of services in Cambridge; and

      Whereas research consistently shows that women avoid co-ed spaces out of fear for their safety, effectively rendering co-ed spaces for men only, further exacerbating the discrepancy in service availability for men versus women;

      Therefore, Be It Resolved That Regional Council direct staff to bring a report to the November 2026 Community & Health Services Committee with recommendations related to:

    • the principle that major investments in homelessness and infrastructure be accompanied by a strategy to improve service access and capacity in underserved municipalities; and
    • future homelessness-related capital funding allocations exceeding 1 million include an assessment of geographic distribution impacts and a requirement to identify complementary investments in underserved municipalities where gaps exist; and
    • improving homelessness service capacity and access in underserved municipalities, including Cambridge, as part of future homelessness system planning and capital budget deliberations; and
    •  the application of Gender-Based Analysis Plus lens to all investments in homelessness and infrastructure to ensure a gender equitable distribution in funding and services across the Region of Waterloo.

    5. That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo take the following action with respect to the curbside Christmas tree collection program as set out in report EES-WMS-26-003, dated September 8, 2026:

      1. Direct staff to partner with Family and Children’s Services Foundation (FACS Foundation), a local organization, to develop, implement and communicate a curbside Christmas tree fundraiser collection service in Waterloo Region, effective January 2027.
      2. Direct staff to offer a free Christmas tree drop-off service at the Region’s Waste Management sites for two weeks in January each year.
      3. Discontinue the curbside collection of natural Christmas trees provided by the Region’s contractor, Halton Recycling Ltd. dba Emterra Environmental (Emterra), effective January 2027.
      4. Direct staff to amend the waste collection contract (P2023-33 Automated Cart-Based, and Manual Waste Collection, and Supply, Initial Distribution and On-Going Maintenance of Carts) with Emterra to remove the Christmas tree collection service from the contract.

    6. WHEREAS the Grand and Speed Rivers create significant natural barriers within Cambridge and concentrate vehicular, pedestrian and cycling movements on a limited number of crossings;

    AND WHEREAS continued population and employment growth will place additional demands on Cambridge's transportation network and existing river crossings;

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT as part of the Region of Waterloo's current review of the Regional road and transportation system, staff be directed to:

      1. Assess whether existing Grand and Speed River crossings in Cambridge will provide sufficient capacity and network resiliency to accommodate projected growth to 2051;
      2. Assess the need and opportunities for additional vehicular, pedestrian and cycling crossings, including their potential to improve traffic movement, emergency and alternate-route access, active transportation connectivity and overall network resiliency;
      3. Review previous studies and assumptions concerning additional river crossings to determine whether they remain appropriate in light of current growth projections, development patterns and transportation demands;
      4. Identify any potential future crossing corridors that should be protected through long-term transportation and land-use planning; and
      5. Report back to Regional Council with findings and recommendations for consideration in the Region’s long term transportation plans
