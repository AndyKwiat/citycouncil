Strategic Priority - Homes for All

 

7.1.1
COR-CFN-24-007, 2024 Property Class Tax Ratios

 
1.2024 Property Class Tax Ratios - COR-CFN-24-007.pdf
2.Appendix A - History of Region of Waterloo Tax Ratios.pdf
3.Appendix B - Comparison of 2023 Municipal Tax Ratios.pdf

Craig Dyer, Commissioner of Corporate Services/Chief Financial Officer, highlighted that Regional Council must annually approve the property class tax ratios. Later this year, staff will report back on the new multi-residential sub-class, recommending that ratios remain consistent with last year. Consultations with all municipal treasurers in the area have been conducted, all are in support.

In response to questions from the Committee regarding the landfill ratio and its potential impact on the Regional budget, C. Dyer confirmed that restrictions within the Municipal Act would prevent Council from increasing the ratio. He further clarified that changes in tax ratios do not affect the overall amount of taxation.

In response to an inquiry regarding taxation for buildings on a farm and farm industries compared to aggregate, C. Dyer confirmed that in a farmland situation, the house and one acre would be assessed and taxed at the residential rate, while the remaining farmland would be assessed and taxed at the farmland rate.

  • Moved byC. Huinink
    Seconded byD. McCabe

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo take the following action with respect to 2024 property tax policies and rates as set out in Report COR-CFN-24-007 dated April 9, 2024:

    1. Establish the following property class tax ratios for the 2024 property tax year:

    Residential and New Multi-residential 1.0000

    Multi-residential, Commercial and Industrial 1.9500

    Landfill 1.5400

    Pipeline 1.1613

    Farmland and Managed Forest 0.2500

    b. Direct staff to prepare the necessary by-laws to establish the 2024 property class tax ratios and the resulting 2024 Regional property tax rates for Regional Council’s consideration on April 24, 2024; and

    c. Notify the Area Municipalities within Waterloo Region accordingly.

    In Favor (13)J. Nowak, K. Redman, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, D. McCabe, J. Liggett, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, R. Deutschmann, N. Salonen, C. Huinink, and K. Williams
    Carried (13 to 0)

Connie MacDonald, Chief Communications and Strategy Officer, provided a presentation on the results of 2023 plan and year end financials. A copy of the presentation is appended to the original minutes. She provided and overview of the year-book that will be available to the public, and highlighted some of the success stories that it contains.

Cheryl Braan, Director, Corporate Finance, presented the financial outcomes for 2023, noting a slight surplus in both the tax-supported and user-rate budgets. She elaborated on the tax-supported sector, excluding police services, revealing a surplus of approximately 0.5%, equating to just under $2.5 million. Additionally, she underscored the substantial revenues detailed in Appendix B. It was emphasized that all these surpluses will be allocated in accordance with best practices and the surplus allocation policy.

Members of the Committee expressed questions related to items pertaining to the Q4 2023 WRPS Financial Variance Report. C. Dyer clarified that the WRPS have their own financial representatives who prepare financial statements for consideration.

Regional Chair K. Redman suggested that item A of the recommendation be taken separately.

9.
Other Business

 

In response to a question from the Committee about ongoing efforts to enhance voter engagement, C. MacDonald, Chief Communications & Strategy Officer, noted the collaboration among communication professionals from the Region and area municipalities. They are working together to provide voter information, suggesting the possibility of reaching out to this group to explore different initiatives promoting voter turnout. The Committee directed staff to provide further information on this matter.

Members of the Committee discussed the motion that declared intimate partner violence as an epidemic and the Region's actions to support this, C. MacDonald mentioned that this issue is one of the four priorities outlined in the community safety well-being plan. Funding calls related to this priority have been initiated, she further noted that staff would provide an update to Council once the funding is issued.

Councillor Foxton requested that staff review the current policy regarding election signs on regional roads.
