8.1
Strategic Priority - Homes for All

 

8.1.1
PDL-BWR-26-001, Charles Street Terminal Update

 
1.PDL-BWR-26-001, Charles Street Terminal Update .pdf
2.CST Council Pres Jan 2026.pdf

Rod Regier, Commissioner of Planning, Development, and Legislative Services, provided an introduction to the report.

Matthew Chandy, Director of Build Waterloo Region, provided a presentation that outlined the history of the subject site and what Regional staff have done so far which includes beginning an environmental assessment and public engagement strategy. Staff are seeking direction to proceed with the re-development in a phased manner in order to better respond to market conditions.

In response to questions from Committee M. Chandy confirmed the next steps in the project pipeline and when procurement would begin.

Staff confirmed that the next step would be to incorporate the priorities of both Council and the public and move forward with those priorities as the guiding principles. 

In response to concerns from Committee, R. Regier explained that staff can report back on the potential impacts of different ownership models for this site.

D. McCabe joined the meeting at 2:46 PM.
  • Moved byB. Vrbanovic
    Seconded byJ. Gowing

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo proceed with the multi-phased plan for developing Charles Street Terminal, as outlined in report PDL-BWR-26-001 dated March 10, 2026.

    And that Regional staff report to the Sustainability, Infrastructure, and Development Committee as part of a subsequent Report regarding the Charles Street Terminal procurement framework on:

    • Options to retain public land ownership of all or portions of the site, and;
    • A specific affordable and/or non-market housing framework for re-development across the site, consistent with Building Better Futures.
    In Favor (14)K. Redman, D. Craig, J. Erb, S. Foxton, J. Gowing, C. Huinink, C. James, J. Liggett, D. McCabe, J. Nowak, M. Rodrigues, S. Shantz, B. Vrbanovic, and P. Wolf
    Carried (14 to 0)
  • Amendment:
    Moved byM. Rodrigues
    Seconded byC. Huinink

    Amendment

    That Regional staff report to the Sustainability, Infrastructure, and Development Committee as part of a subsequent Report regarding the Charles Street Terminal procurement framework on:

    • Options to retain public land ownership of all or portions of the site, and;
    • A specific affordable and/or non-market housing framework for re-development across the site, consistent with Building Better Futures.
    In Favor (14)K. Redman, D. Craig, J. Erb, S. Foxton, J. Gowing, C. Huinink, C. James, J. Liggett, D. McCabe, J. Nowak, M. Rodrigues, S. Shantz, B. Vrbanovic, and P. Wolf

8.2
Strategic Priority - Climate Aligned Growth

 

8.2.1
PDL-GDS-26-004, Update on the Implementation of the Corporate Climate Action Plan

 
1.PDL-GDS-26-004, Update on Implementation of the Corporate Climate Action Plan.pdf
2.Appendix A - Summary of Corporate GHG Inventory Changes.pdf
3.Attachment B - Updated Inventory - 2010 to 2022.pdf
4.March 10 2026 - Climate Aligned Growth Updates.pdf

R. Regier provided an introduction to the report.

Meaghan Eastwood, Manager, Climate and Sustainability and Max Chute, Corporate Energy Analyst, provided a presentation on the corporate climate action plan as well as recommended construction standards to meet LEED certification. 

In response to questions from Committee, staff elaborated on the various strategies that staff are looking at to both reduce corporate emissions as well as emissions from the public.

Staff also confirmed that the largest source of corporate transmissions is from GRT although GRT transmissions reduce community emissions making it an overall net benefit, regional buildings are the next largest source of corporate emissions.

8.2.2
EES-FFM-26-001, Minimum Sustainable Construction Standard for Regional Facilities

 
1.EES-FFM-26-001, Minimum Sustainable Construction Standard for Regional Facilities.pdf
2.EES-FFM-26-001, Appendix A CaGBC Zero Carbon Building-Design Standard v4.pdf
3.EES-FFM-26-001, Appendix B Differences between the CaGBC Zero Carbon Building – Design Standard and LEED™ Silver Standard.pdf
4.EES-FFM-26-001, Appendix C High Performance Building Costing Studies Briefing Note.pdf

In response to questions from the committee M. Chute elaborated on the difference between LEED Silver and Platinum.

M. Chute also confirmed that if this policy is approved staff will look to include projects that have begun construction already in these standards.

8.2.3
PDL-GDS-26-006, Regional Forest Management Program Update

 
1.PDL-GDS-26-006, Regional Forest Management Program Update.pdf
2.Regional Forest Management Presentation V2 2026 March.pdf

R. Regier provided an introduction to the report.

Andrew Puchalski, Regional Forester, provided a presentation on highlights from 2025, what work has been done so far for 2026, and upcoming projects and priorities with regional forests. 

In response to questions from Committee, A. Puchalski confirmed that the regional forest network is currently open to the public, staff have worked to substantially update its web pages for pubic information.

Staff also confirmed that while they do have good relationships with area municipalities they primarily work on regional forest management.

Staff also confirmed that if Council expressed interest in it, staff would be supportive of expanding the regional forest network.
