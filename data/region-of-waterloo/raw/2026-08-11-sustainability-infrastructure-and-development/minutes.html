Tova Davidson, Executive Director, Sustainable Waterloo Region

M. Goetzke introduced the report and provided an overview of the need for improvements on Myers Road in Cambridge. M. Goetzke emphasized safer travel and road conditions, improved drainage, and better active transportation conditions.

M. Goetzke explained that a key public concern was surrounding school pick up and drop off in this area, with safety risks observed in areas where parking is not permitted. M. Goetzke stated that the recommendation is to replace existing shoulders with raised boulevards and separated multi-use paths with more defined crossings.

M. Goetzke explained that if approved, construction will proceed in stages starting in 2028 and completed in 2030.

In response to a question from the Committee, M. Goetzke confirmed that staff looked at several solutions before bringing forward the recommendation to the Committee. M. Goetzke stated that many kids who live in the area will be able to walk or cycle to school following the improvements, as the current quality Myers Road does not facilitate active transport. He explained that the school located on the street has included temporary residential parking and new, specific dedicated parking for 30-40 cars. M. Goetzke explained that combined, these options are expected to accommodate peak demands.

Responding to the Committee, M. Goetzke confirmed that proceeding with the recommended design concept would not prevent staff from continuing to work with the school board to enhance road conditions. M. Goetzke stated that staff will continue conversations with the school board on how to provide the best solution possible.

In response to a question from the Committee, M. Goetzke confirmed that while there are three (3) schools located along Myers Road, the main pressure point is at Moffat Creek Public School.

TSD-TRS-26-009, GRT Mid-Year Status Update 2026

 
1.TSD-TRS-26-009, GRT Mid-Year Status Update 2026.pdf
2.Appendix A - TSD-TRS-26-009 GRT 2026 Mid Year Update Dashboard.pdf

Doug Spooner, Director of Transit Services, provided the Committee with a mid-year update on GRT operations. D. Spooner discussed factors that have impacted transit, such as the COVID-19 pandemic and a decreasing number of international students in the Region.

D. Spooner stated that overall ridership is down 9.6%, and Mobility Plus ridership is up 7% year-over-year. D. Spooner explained that the rate of undelivered trips rose to 4.2%, which increased from the 1.5% rate achieved in 2025. D. Spooner noted that the target set by Council for undelivered trips was 2.5%.

D. Spooner discussed on time performance, and stated that 72% of GRT bus trips arrive within three (3) minutes of the scheduled time, and 84% within five (5) minutes. For LRT trips, 94% of trips arrive within three (3) minutes of the scheduled time, and 96% within five (5) minutes.

D. Spooner noted that in 2026, GRT provided more connections to GO transit, increased late night service, wider geographical coverage, and a longer time span without any additional funding. He noted that the overall cost recovery of the system stayed the same, despite ridership dropping nearly 10%.

D. Spooner updated the Committee on GRT infrastructure, which included a new route in Chicopee and a new bus facility located at Conestoga College in Doon. D. Spooner also noted that accessibility upgrades at bus stops are ongoing.

D. Spooner noted that 24-hour snow clearance at bus-stops will be priced into the budget, while also looking at incentives for priority clearance, as well as possible penalties.

D. Spooner provided an update on recent programs, stating that results from the GRT Pay app are promising, with users reporting it as a primary driver of increased use of transit. D. Spooner updated the Committee on the two-ride fare card pilot, stating 12,500 cards were distributed to the 70 agencies able to participate, 16 of which were new. D. Spooner noted this was an unfunded pilot and will result in a $25,000 loss of revenue for GRT.

D. Spooner discussed the upcoming older adults’ pilot, which launched on July 1, 2026. He noted that 700 residents have applied, and results will be brought back in Q1 of 2027.

D. Spooner provided an update on the Affordable Transit Program, which was anticipated to serve around 8,600 people and currently serves over 23,000 users.

D. Spooner summarized ongoing initiatives, which included the ridership campaign, "Get There with GRT", work with Lime Canada, and a transit program with local high schools.

D. Spooner spoke on the $3.4 million in funding provided by the Province of Ontario over the next four (4) years to support the township transit strategy.

In response to the Committee, D. Spooner confirmed that a wholesome analysis of the two-fare card pilot project before budget time.

In response to the Committee, D. Spooner explained that safety is a priority when considering all micromobility vehicles, such as e-scooters and enclosed mobility devices. D. Spooner explained that there were no reported collisions involving neuron scooters or bikes in 2025, and that staff have taken initiatives to ensure a safe micromobility program.

Responding to a question from the Committee, D. Spooner spoke on the work being done to improve contractor performance for snow clearance, which include exploring incentive programs and possible penalties. 

Responding to the Committee, D. Spooner explained that decreased ridership is expected to be structural rather than temporary due to decreased post-secondary ridership and downtown vacancy rates. D. Spooner highlighted this as a motivation for the ongoing reallocation of services, where instead of eliminating service in areas that have seen a decrease in ridership, these hours are being redeployed to underserved areas.

M. Goetzke cautioned the Committee around expectation for 24-hour snow clearance, stating that a consistent 48-hour clearance would be considered an achievement. M. Goetzke stated that an innovative contract design that will reward high achieving contractors is in progress.

Responding to the Committee, D. Spooner stated that staff are looking at ways to increase Mobility Plus service and reduce the waitlist for subscription trips, and that information relating to this can be brought back to Council.

    WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo's 2023–2027 Strategic Plan, Growing with Care, supports equitable access to services, climate-aligned growth, and innovative approaches to service delivery;

  • Moved byC. James
    Seconded byP. Wolf

    MAIN MOTION AS AMENDED

    WHEREAS data centres are an important and growing part of the digital economy and are being developed in communities across Ontario and Canada;

    AND WHEREAS data centres vary significantly in size, function, and resource requirements, including electricity, water, telecommunications infrastructure, and land;

    AND WHEREAS recent data centre growth is being driven in part by artificial intelligence, which can require significant electricity and water for cooling;

    AND WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo is growing and faces increasing demand on drinking water, wastewater, electricity, and other critical infrastructure;

    AND WHEREAS the Region relies primarily on groundwater for drinking water, making water-intensive development a matter of regional interest;

    AND WHEREAS Council should understand how data centre development may affect infrastructure capacity, environmental sustainability, and economic development;

    AND WHEREAS municipalities are beginning to assess the opportunities, risks, and policy issues related to data centres;

    AND WHEREAS Council would benefit from objective information before considering any future policy direction;

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT Regional staff prepare an information report on data centre development and its potential implications for the Region of Waterloo, including: 

    1. Economic opportunities, including investment, innovation, construction jobs, temporary and permanent jobs, and support for the digital economy.

    2. Infrastructure needs and impacts, including:

        1. cumulative electricity demand relative to available grid capacity;
        2. water consumption based on different cooling technologies;
        3. wastewater servicing;
        4. telecommunications infrastructure;
        5. emergency services planning.

    3. Environmental considerations and best practices, including water use, electricity demand, greenhouse gas emissions, energy efficiency, water conservation, sustainable cooling, and responsible operations, and potential impacts on groundwater, source water, stormwater, wastewater, air quality, noise, spills and hazardous materials, including cumulative impacts and applicable provincial environmental approvals and monitoring requirements.

    4. Fiscal considerations, including servicing needs, capital and operating costs, assessment impacts, and how upgrade costs are allocated among operators, utilities, are municipalities, and existing ratepayers.

    5. Transparency and accountability, including best practices for public reporting on energy use, water use, cooling systems, and impact on environment.

    6. A jurisdictional scan to identify best practices for balancing economic development, environmental protection, infrastructure capacity, and community interests.

    7. Potential advocacy to the Province of Ontario for a consistent regulatory framework for data centre development that:

        1.  protects drinking water resources, groundwater, and the environment;
        2. plans and manages electricity, water, wastewater, and other infrastructure impacts;
        3. sets clear environmental, resource stewardship, and transparency requirements;
        4. ensures municipalities and existing ratepayers do not face undue costs for servicing data centres;
        5. supports responsible economic development and investment;
        6. gives municipalities clear tools to assess and manage proposed developments.
    1. Indigenous perspectives, including potential impacts on traditional lands, water, the environment and culturally significant areas, and opportunities for appropriate engagement with local First Nations and Indigenous communities.

    BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED THAT staff report back to Council by Q1 2027.
