6.3
Joe Salemi, Executive Director, Landscape Ontario Horticultural Trades Association

 
1.BRIEFING NOTE - Landscape Ontario’s Snow and Ice Management Sector - Salt Pollution Motion.pdf

The delegate spoke in favour of a motion listed on the agenda which seeks to reduce salt pollution in the Region. He alluded to some of the issues associated with the market and contracting for road salting services, such as concerns related to insurance and liability. The delegate indicated that the road salt and snow removal industry is asking for regulations which identify hazards and which balances responsibilities for property owners and snow removal contractors.

In response to a question from the Committee, J. Salemi indicated his organization has been in discussion with the province to change the legislation to equalize liability for contractors and property owners. Through a private members bill, the statute of limitations has been altered to 60 days for slips and falls, though loopholes still exist.

Replying to a question from the Committee, J. Salemi answered that his organization does not look at windshield fluid and other pollution impacts, but that could be an area for exploration.

Responding to a question from the Committee, J. Salemi spoke to how salt management may affect accessibility, explaining that packing snow on walkways and passageways is not a best practice and that training is in place for the industry to avoid this occurrence, with data showing that correct amounts of salt are known to get clear pavement.

6.4
Claire Malcolmson, Executive Director, Rescue Lake Simcoe Coalition

 
1.Salt Coalition powerpoint Waterloo Region.pdf
2.Policy Brief Salts Municipal Resolution.pdf

The delegate appeared to provide information on the initiatives her organization is involved in, urging action be taken to protect water in the province from salt pollution. The delegate and her organization support the motion listed on the agenda regarding salt pollution management. She mentioned that with growing road networks, more salt could affect more waterways, which needs to be addressed. The delegate elaborated on the ecological impact of salt usage based on impact on aquatic species.

EES-FFM-25-001, Electrification of WRH Water Heaters

 
1.EES-FFM-25-001, Electrification of WRH Water Heaters.pdf

J. Rose introduced the report, explaining how the Region is working towards its climate goals, elaborating on how the electrification of water heaters at Waterloo Region Housing will impact the budget and tenants. 

Responding to questions from the Committee, J. Rose answered the Region owns the water heaters. She further indicated that the impact to the average household estimate is over a 10 year period, and based on assumptions on electricity and gas rates. Furthermore, she provided that staff are recommending a pilot project to allow the Region to determine whether financial assumptions are correct. Staff will report back on the results and gas lines will not be removed during the project.

In reply to a question from the Committee, J. Rose explained that the team in charge of the pilot will look at costs as a whole. Peter Sweeney, Commissioner, Community Services, added that Waterloo Regional Housing facilities need a consistent approach to heating, with the idea being to move through the first phase and ensure residents are made whole. The first phase is two years.

Responding to a question from the Committee, J. Rose answered that rebates would be available for tenants with higher electricity bills as a result of the electrification of water heaters. The housing team will look at utilities in general. J. Rose provided that the replacement is based on the life of the water heater itself.

Replying to a question from the Committee, Wayne Steffler, Commissioner, Corporate Services/Chief Financial Officer, indicated that the capital $17,400 difference is an estimate based on utility rates. Additionally, the rates can change significantly on a continual basis. J. Rose clarified that each year will be different due to different quantities being replaced.

10.1
Notice of Motion - Councillor D. McCabe

 

D. McCabe spoke to her motion as a solution for supporting small businesses who do road maintenance and clarified questions of liability implicated within her motion.

In reply to a question from the Committee, J. Rose indicated she has received positive feedback on the delegation provided by Regional staff at the Rural Ontario Municipalities Association (ROMA) conference, and that the creation of a working group would be something to potentially pursue.

  • Moved byD. McCabe
    Seconded byC. Huinink

    WHEREAS road salt is a known toxic substance designated under the Canadian Environmental Protection Act because of tangible threats of serious or irreversible environmental and health damage from road salt; and

    WHEREAS salt levels in Ontario’s groundwater aquifers, creeks, rivers, and lakes have increasingly worsened since the 1970s, seriously affecting municipal drinking water sources and aquatic life; and

    WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo, in collaboration with local municipalities, is actively attempting to mitigate salt pollution using existing policies, programs and site design measures that would be significantly more effective with supporting Provincial legislation; and

    WHEREAS the Ontario and Canadian governments have taken many actions over the past 25 years including setting water quality guidelines, developing voluntary codes of practice, signing the Canada-Ontario Great Lakes Agreement, and holding workshops, yet still the salt problem continues to grow; and

    WHEREAS numerous situation analyses have recommended salt solutions involving liability protection, contractor certification, government approved Best Management Practices (BMPs) and salt management plans; and

    WHEREAS increased numbers of slips and falls claims, and other injury/collision claims related to snow and ice, are resulting in salt applicators overusing salt beyond levels considered best practices; and

    WHEREAS unlimited contractor liability is making it difficult or expensive for snow and ice management contractors to obtain insurance coverage, resulting in contractors leaving the business, thereby making it difficult for municipalities and private owners to find contractors; and

    WHEREAS the Snow and Ice Management Sector (SMS) of Landscape Ontario is working with the Ontario government to institute a limited liability regime for snow and ice management, including enforceable contractor training/certification and government-approved BMPs for salt application; and

    WHEREAS many Ontario municipalities have Salt Management Plans, but these often require updating considering improved science and better salt management practices now available.

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT:

    1. The Region of Waterloo urges the province of Ontario to work on a priority basis with key stakeholders to develop limited liability legislation, including enforceable contractor training and a single set of provincially-endorsed standard BMPs for snow and ice management; and
    2. The Region of Waterloo urges the province of Ontario to create and fund an expert stakeholder advisory committee to advise the province and municipalities on the best courses of action to protect freshwater ecosystems and drinking water from the impacts of salt pollution; and
    3. This resolution be sent to all municipalities in the Regional Municipality of Waterloo, Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO), local MPPs, Mayors and Regional Chairs of Ontario (MARCO), Ontario Big City Mayors Caucus (OBCM), Landscape Ontario, Conservation Ontario, the Minister of the Environment Conservation and Parks (MECP), the Minister of Red Tape Reduction, the Attorney General, and Premier Doug Ford.
    In Favor (14)J. Erb, J. Nowak, K. Redman, M. Harris, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, D. McCabe, J. Liggett, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, N. Salonen, C. Huinink, and M. Rodrigues
    Carried (14 to 0)

Notice of Motion - Councillor M. Rodrigues

 

M. Rodrigues introduced his motion, explaining its intent to start a conversation around road maintenance for snow removal and efforts that can be taken to ensure important services are able to continue to run during major snow events.

  • Moved byM. Rodrigues
    Seconded byD. McCabe

    WHEREAS Waterloo Region experienced multiple significant weather events in Winter 2024/2025, resulting in the greatest annual snowfall accumulations since 2008;

    WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo coordinates winter maintenance of the public right-of-way across multiple operators;

    WHEREAS timely and appropriate winter maintenance of the public right-of-way is vital for supporting year-round accessibility for all residents and businesses -- particularly those who walk and roll, active transportation users, transit riders, and emergency vehicles;

    AND WHEREAS responsive and well-coordinated winter maintenance of the public right-of-way is a core municipal service susceptible to variable weather patterns and a changing climate; 

    THEREFORE be it resolved that the Commissioner of Transportation conduct a review of Winter 2024/2025 maintenance operations and prepare a Report to the Sustainability, Infrastructure, and Development Committee prior to the 2026 Plan and Budget process, which:

    1. Reviews existing winter maintenance operational standards and agreements to determine how to optimize existing available resources;
    2. Investigates and reports on specific issues and coordination opportunities for infrastructure under Regional jurisdiction including but not limited to coordination of snow clearing and overhead ice build-up along the ION LRT corridor and snow clearing of separated cycling tracks and lanes, sidewalks and multi-use trails along Regional Roads, and transit stops;
    3. Identifies potential improvements to the Region's winter maintenance programs, policies, and agreements for Council’s consideration; and
    4. Establishes a reporting process to the Sustainability, Infrastructure, and Development Committee prior to the Plan and Budget cycle, outlining performance of winter operations from the previous season and insights around operational efforts and future continuous improvements.
    In Favor (14)J. Erb, J. Nowak, K. Redman, M. Harris, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, D. McCabe, J. Liggett, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, N. Salonen, C. Huinink, and M. Rodrigues
