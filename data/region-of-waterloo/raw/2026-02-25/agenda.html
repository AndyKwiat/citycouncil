    The Administration and Finance Committee recommends as follows:

     

    1.  That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo direct Facilities staff to explore the development of a Facilities Strategic Plan focused on the following five components:

      1. A complete inventory of all corporate facilities, including current usage, functional capacity, and condition;
      2. An analysis of operating and maintenance requirements and associated costs across all corporate facilities;
      3. A five- to ten-year capital outlook identifying required investments, lifecycle needs, and projected financial impacts;
      4. A review of accommodation standards and policies to support facilities planning and a demand analysis to clarify space needs;

13.1
EES-WIM-26-003, Optimizing the Sustainable Use of Water Resources for Municipal Supply (1980 Policy on Water Taking - Wilmot Township)

 
1.EES-WIM-26-003, Sustainable Use of Water Resources for Municipal Supply (1980 Water Taking Policy, Wilmot Township).pdf
2.EES-WIM-26-003, Appendix A - Policy of Water Taking in Wilmot Township Apr 1, 1980.pdf
3.EES-WIM-26-003, Appendix B - Portion of Council Direction Apr 26, 1984.pdf
4.EES-WIM-26-003, Appendix C - Wilmot Centre Monitoring Program Map.pdf
  • Recommended Motion:

    Motion Referred

    1. That Council temporarily suspend the 1980 Council Wilmot Water Take Policy, rather than revoke it, until the Region’s new Water Supply Strategy is completed and presented to Council in 2027;
    2. That Council formally review the status of the 1980 policy upon receipt of the Water Supply Strategy in 2027, prior to making any permanent determination respecting revocation;
    3. That upon initiation of any increased draw from Wilmot wells, staff provide regular public reporting to Regional Council and the public, at a minimum quarterly, including but not limited to:
    • total volume of water being taken;
    • peak flow data;
    • well performance indicators;
    • aquifer health and drawdown monitoring data;
    • commentary on whether continued suspension remains appropriate based on observed conditions;
    1. That staff establish and implement a monitoring framework to collect and publicly report data regarding:
      • neighbouring private wells;
      • surface water systems;
      • agricultural water supply conditions;
      • and any observed correlations associated with increased regional draw;
    2. That staff provide Council and the public with a clear definition of “surplus water” as used in Regional staff's SID Feb. 10 report on the 1980 water take policy, including:
      • the modelling methodology applied;
      • growth and demand assumptions used;
      • safety factors and contingency thresholds;
      • and alignment with Provincial permitting requirements;
    3. That staff provide clarification regarding the basis of the current statement that Wilmot’s growth to 2051 will not be impacted, including:
      • the population and employment forecasts being used;
      • the land use assumptions underlying those forecasts;
      • the infrastructure modelling methodology applied;
      • and the treatment of post-2051 growth within long-term supply planning;
    4. That the forthcoming Water Supply Strategy clearly distinguish between water allocation assumptions for Wilmot’s planned residential and employment growth and any projected demand associated with the Region’s designated Mega Site, including:
      • the range of potential end-user water demand scenarios being modelled for the mega site;
      • the planning assumptions being used in the absence of a confirmed end user;
      • and the implications of those assumptions for long-term regional water allocation and that the mega site water use is independent of the guaranteed allocations for Wilmot's planned growth as per their 2026 Official Plan
  • Recommended Motion:

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo rescind the 1980 Policy on Water Taking- Wilmot Township (commonly referred to as the 1980 Policy) to allow the transfer of surplus water to the Mannheim Service Area, while continuing to protect long-term water supply for growth in Wilmot Township, as outlined in report EES-WIM-26-003, dated February 10, 2026.

Councillor D. Craig

 
  • Recommended Motion:

    Whereas the Region of Waterloo is experiencing increasing constraints and uncertainty related to municipal water supply and capacity; and

    Whereas residents and the development industry have raised concerns regarding water sustainability, growth pressures and the availability of clear and transparent public information related to long term water planning; and

    Whereas a previous council motion authorized only staff level discussions with representatives of the Waterloo Home Builders Association, Build Urban, and the Grand Valley Construction Association regarding water supply constraints in an initial technical exchange; and

    Whereas water supply planning is a fundamental core public interest issue that requires direct engagement by Regional Council to ensure transparency, accountability and informed decision making.

    Therefore, be it resolved:

    1. That Regional Council direct staff to convene a one day working session involving regional council, relevant regional staff and invited representatives of the development industry including the Waterloo Home Builders association, Build Urban and the Grand Valley Construction Association to provide a comprehensive briefing on the Region’s water supply; and
    2. The working session be educational and informational in nature inviting commentary from both council and the development industry on water sustainability, growth pressures and the possibility of new guidelines on the staging of development; and
    3. Staff report back to council following the working session with a summary of key information presented, suggestions from the development industry on the staging of approvals and further recommended next steps for council.
