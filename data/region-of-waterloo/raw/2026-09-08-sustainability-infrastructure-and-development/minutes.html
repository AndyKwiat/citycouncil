Rod Regier, Commissioner of Planning, Development, and Legislative Services introduced the report and gave an overview of building permit activity from January 1 to June 30, 2026.

R. Regier stated that residential activity has remained strong, with 81% of new developments consisting of apartment units. R. Regier further stated that industrial, commercial, and institutional activity has slowed. 

R. Regier stated that there was a drop in building permits between April and June, possibly due to water capacity constraints. R. Regier commented that this is expected to rebound following the recent water allocations, which will allow the Area Municipalities to advance new developments.

8.3.1
EES-WMS-26-003, Review of Curbside Christmas Tree Collection Program

 
1.EES-WMS-26-003, Review of Curbside Tree Collection Program.pdf
2.Appendix A__Annual Tonnage of Christmas Trees Collected Curbside (2014 to 2026).pdf
3.Appendix B_SID Committee Presentation re Tree Collection - Sept 2026.pdf

Darshpreet Bhatti, Chief Administrative Officer, introduced the report. A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda. 

Andrea Ivanovs, Manager, Waste Collection and Diversion, summarized the current Christmas tree collection service, which costs $540,000 annually. A. Ivanovs explained that there has been a decrease in program participation, prompting staff to look at alternatives. 

A. Ivanovs explained that the recommendation to transition the program to the Family and Children Services Foundation will encourage donations to a local organization and promote waste diversion, while allowing residents to still access the service. 

A. Ivanovs explained that pending Council decision, next steps would include developing, implementing, and communicating a curbside Christmas tree fundraiser collection service, effective in January 2027. 

In response to a question from the Committee, A. Ivanovs explained that there would not be any cost to amend the contract with Emterra. 

Responding to questions from the Committee, A. Ivanovs stated that staff can look into options for drop-off dates that are inclusive, as well as recommended donation amounts.  A. Ivanovs further emphasized that staff will be using all available tactics to communicate collection service changes to the community. 

A. Ivanovs stated that the recommended model for this tree collection program is based on the Trees for Tots in the City of Guelph. 

In response to the Committee, A. Ivanovs confirmed that the procurement approach will reflect that this is a trial program and leave open the opportunity to return to the current tree collection method if desired. 

In response to the Committee, D. Bhatti stated that logistics are still being discussed, and that more information can be brought to Council in October. 

In response to the Committee, D. Bhatti confirmed staff are confident that the Family and Children Services Foundation have the appropriate resources to handle the workload. 

The Committee discussed the precedent this recommendation would set for programs with low participation. 

In response to the Committee, D. Bhatti confirmed that residents will have three (3) options to dispose of natural Christmas trees. Residents can drop off trees at the Region's Waste Management sites, call the Family and Children Services Foundation to coordinate pick up, or put trees at the curb on bulk item day, which occurs once a month. 

In response to a question from the Committee, A. Ivanovs explained that the program cost is based on the service of having dedicated waste collection vehicles and staff, and is not influenced by the number of trees collected. 

  • Moved byJ. Gowing
    Seconded byC. Huinink

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo take the following action with respect to the curbside Christmas tree collection program as set out in report EES-WMS-26-003, dated September 8, 2026:

    1. Direct staff to partner with Family and Children’s Services Foundation (FACS Foundation), a local organization, to develop, implement and communicate a curbside Christmas tree fundraiser collection service in Waterloo Region, effective January 2027.
    2. Direct staff to offer a free Christmas tree drop-off service at the Region’s Waste Management sites for two weeks in January each year.
    3. Discontinue the curbside collection of natural Christmas trees provided by the Region’s contractor, Halton Recycling Ltd. dba Emterra Environmental (Emterra), effective January 2027.
    4. Direct staff to amend the waste collection contract (P2023-33 Automated Cart-Based, and Manual Waste Collection, and Supply, Initial Distribution and On-Going Maintenance of Carts) with Emterra to remove the Christmas tree collection service from the contract.

    In Favor (13)K. Redman, D. Craig, J. Erb, S. Foxton, J. Gowing, C. Huinink, C. James, J. Nowak, M. Rodrigues, N. Salonen, S. Shantz, B. Vrbanovic, and P. Wolf
    Opposed (1)J. Liggett
    Carried (13 to 1)

10.1
Councillor D. Craig Motion Re: River Crossings in Cambridge

 

D. Craig introduced his motion and commented on the current traffic issues in the City of Cambridge and the need to provide river crossings to accommodate projected growth. 

P. Wolf voiced her support for the motion and commented on the need for more pedestrian bridges in the City of Cambridge. 

M. Goetzke confirmed that a report on river crossings can come back as part of the Integrated Mobility Plan. 

S. Shantz left the meeting at 2:10 PM.
  • Moved byD. Craig
    Seconded byP. Wolf

    WHEREAS the Grand and Speed Rivers create significant natural barriers within Cambridge and concentrate vehicular, pedestrian and cycling movements on a limited number of crossings;
    AND WHEREAS continued population and employment growth will place additional demands on Cambridge's transportation network and existing river crossings;
    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT as part of the Region of Waterloo's current review of the Regional road and transportation system, staff be directed to:

    1. Assess whether existing Grand and Speed River crossings in Cambridge will provide sufficient capacity and network resiliency to accommodate projected growth to 2051;
    2. Assess the need and opportunities for additional vehicular, pedestrian and cycling crossings, including their potential to improve traffic movement, emergency and alternate-route access, active transportation connectivity and overall network resiliency;
    3. Review previous studies and assumptions concerning additional river crossings to determine whether they remain appropriate in light of current growth projections, development patterns and transportation demands;
    4. Identify any potential future crossing corridors that should be protected through long-term transportation and land-use planning; and
    5. Report back to Regional Council with findings and recommendations for consideration in the Region's long term transportation plans.
    In Favor (14)K. Redman, D. Craig, J. Erb, S. Foxton, J. Gowing, C. Huinink, C. James, J. Liggett, J. Nowak, M. Rodrigues, N. Salonen, S. Shantz, B. Vrbanovic, and P. Wolf
