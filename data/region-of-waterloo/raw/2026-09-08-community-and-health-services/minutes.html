5.1
Kim Decker, CEO, YWCA Cambridge

 

Kim Decker spoke in support of Councillor P. Wolf's notice of motion listed on the meeting agenda. She expressed support for Regional funding equity and applying a gender-based analysis+ lens to investments in homelessness and housing. She also noted service issues and barriers to housing, including geographic and gender disparities, that restrict access to transitional and supportive housing in Cambridge, especially for women. She highlighted that local, targeted investments help to address housing and homelessness, but that these investments need to be made in a way that is geographically equitable across the Region so that Cambridge needs can be adequately met.

In response to the Committee, K. Decker confirmed that the YWCA has had conversations with Regional staff in regards to the challenges the organization is facing in terms of assisting its clients to secure housing.

K. Decker also confirmed that the YWCA is part of the Community Safety and Wellbeing Plan, the Gender Based Violence Strategy, and works with the Family Violence Project, and that the YWCA brings issues of housing and homelessness forward with these organizations as well.

In response to the Committee, K. Decker noted that the YWCA would like to be able to offer a low barrier overnight shelter for women who may struggle with other more structured shelters in order to help connect these women with community services and healthcare, and eventually help these individuals move into other shelters.

6.1.1
PHP-PSV-26-005, Response Time Performance Plan 2027

 
1.PHP-PSV-26-005, Response Time Performance Plan 2027 .pdf
2.Appendix A - Full Year 2025, and mid-year 2026 Response Time Performance Plan Compliance Results.pdf

John Riches, Director and Chief, Paramedic Services, provided a presentation on the Paramedic Services' Response Time Performance Plan 2027 and Annual Key Performance Indicator (KPI) results. A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda.

J. Riches highlighted that in comparison to 2025, vehicle responses have increased, unit utilization has decreased, and response times have improved. He also noted that following a 2025 decrease in ambulance offload delays, offload delays have now plateaued in 2026, and there were no Code Zero events between January and June 2026. He remarked that overall, these statistics indicate that the Paramedic Services system is stable and that some of the observed change has likely been a result of the implementation of the Medical Priority Dispatch System (MPDS).

J. Riches highlighted that since the implementation of MPDS, there has been a decrease in the proportion of vehicle responses dispatched as critical and a corresponding decrease in the proportion of times that Paramedic Services ambulance crews use lights and sirens to get to calls, as well as a decrease in the proportion of calls resulting in transport to hospital.

He also noted that there has been increased vehicle responses compared to 2025 but an improvement in unit utilization, and that response times have improved in six out of seven area municipalities.

J. Riches provided an overview of mitigation strategies for response time delays, including working with hospital partners to maintain Ambulance Offload Time performance, utilizing provincial funding for the Designated Offload Nurse Program, continuing expansion of Patient Care Model Standards options, implementing Council-approved Paramedic Services Master Plan recommendations, further advancing the Community Paramedicine program to address high utilization 911 users, adjusting the Deployment Plan to stabilize response times for rural townships, and adding temporary post locations for Woolwich and Wellesley townships.

J. Riches noted that Paramedic Services is meeting all Ministry of Health compliance targets for 2026 except for Canadian Triage and Acuity Scale (CTAS) 2 cases. He indicated that failure to meet CTAS 2 targets has been common amongst municipalities after the implementation of MDPS, which may be attributed to the fact that call response priority is determined by the province, but that Paramedic Services' performance is judged based on the patient acuity assessment that paramedic crews perform after arriving on scene. He noted that this issue will continue to be investigated and discussed with the Ministry of Health.

J. Riches recommended that Council approve maintaining current response time targets in order to provide sustainable service delivery, based on best practices amongst other municipalities and taking into consideration that MPDS was implemented just six months ago. 

J. Riches also noted that external violence against paramedics is an ongoing challenge and that the 2026 Annual Business Plan included the implementation of Advanced Learning for Evaluating and Recognizing Threats (ALERT) training, which was completed by Paramedic Services staff in the spring of 2026.

In response to the Committee, J. Riches clarified that an Alternate Destination Strategy is already in place for palliative care patients, and that it is a goal to eventually be able to transport eligible patients to a mental health and addictions community-based clinic rather than to hospital. 

J. Riches noted that projects from the Paramedic Services Master Plan are already in the process of being implemented, including a review of IT systems, service level agreements with corporate partners, internal service expectation agreements, and superintendents re-assigned to work directly within the Cambridge Central Ambulance Communications Centre (CACC). He also noted that recommendations in the Master Plan can be adjusted as is necessary based on evaluations of available data.

In response to the Committee, J. Riches confirmed that the cost of days lost to ambulance offload delays includes staff wages and other necessary costs for ambulance operation.

J. Riches also confirmed that there is a public communications strategy in place to educate residents and set expectations for service delivery, as fewer calls are now being responded to with lights and sirens, but that this communications strategy is being re-examined and updated as needed. 

J. Riches also confirmed that there is communication and sharing of data between Paramedic Services and other emergency service partners to ensure that the right departments are responding to calls and to avoid unnecessary deployment of resources when possible. He noted that tiered response agreements with area municipality fire departments have been adjusted since the implementation of MPDS and can be updated again at any time if needed.

J. Riches clarified that a paramedic station is scheduled to be built in the future in Elmira, but that temporary post locations will be utilized until a permanent station is constructed. He also confirmed that the new Cambridge paramedic station is scheduled to open in the last quarter of 2027.

In response to the Committee, J. Riches clarified that Code Purples and Code Reds require a lights and sirens response, but that paramedics have some discretion in utilizing lights and sirens in other circumstances depending on the specific scenario and considerations for public safety.

  • Moved byC. Huinink
    Seconded byM. Rodrigues

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo approve the Response Time Performance Plan (RTPP) for 2027, outlined below, in accordance with the Ambulance Act, O. Reg. 257/00, under Part VIII, Response Time Standards, as amended by O. Reg. 251/25, s. 5, as outlined in PHP-PSV-26-005, dated September 8, 2026.

    1. Sudden Cardiac Arrest (SCA) – 6 minutes or less 70% of the time. (Note: Ministry of Health target is 50%.)
    2. CTAS 1 – 8 minutes or less 70% of the time.
    3. CTAS 2 – 10 minutes or less 80% of the time.
    4. CTAS 3 – 15 minutes or less 80% of the time.
    5. CTAS 4 – 20 minutes or less 80% of the time.
    6. CTAS 5 – 25 minutes or less 80% of the time.

CSD-IPP-26-001, Immigration Partnership Update

 
1.CSD-IPP-26-001, Immigration Partnership Update.pdf
2.Appendix A - Waterloo Region Immigration Partnership Annual Report 2025.pdf
3.Appendix B - Immigration Partnership Update 2026 Presentation to Regional Council.pdf

Tara Bedard, Executive Director, Immigration Partnership, provided a brief introduction to the presentation. A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda. 

Trish Weigel Green, Chair, Immigration Partnership Council, highlighted the organization's vision and mission and noted that the Immigration Partnership's work happens across the three pillars of settle, work, and belong. She outlined the 2025-2028 strategic priorities that guide the work of the Immigration Partnership and its community partner organizations; gather insights and evidence to develop a shared understanding of immigration, mobilize partners and community leadership for change, influence decision makers, and build organizational resilience. She also noted that the Immigration Partnership's work is aligned with the Region's strategic priority of Growing with Care.

Dauda Raji, Vice Chair, Immigration Partnership Council, noted that in 2025, a significant drop in national immigration targets happened, immigration caps were introduced, and federal funding for community-based settlement services for immigrants was reduced. He also provided an overview of the Immigration Partnership's 2025 highlights that included building a nation-leading local research and community planning program, strengthening relationships and collaboration amongst community groups and partners, coordinating community readiness for thousands of immigrants, helping to grow investment in regional newcomer infrastructure, coordinating the third Regional celebration of Welcoming Week, and continuing to develop a diversified funding strategy to sustain this work. He also highlighted next steps and action items for the year ahead, which included mobilizing immigrant survey findings, building front-line staff knowledge and service capacity, strengthening connections and capacity among employers and employment services, launching the fourth annual Welcoming Week celebration, and securing long-term investment and sustainability to enhance newcomer integration.

T. Weigel Green noted that this update will also be shared with area municipalities. 

In response to the Committee, T. Bedard noted that the Immigration Partnership's employment partnership with the Food Bank of Waterloo Region is currently paused due to lack of financial investment, but that this program has had positive outcomes for newcomers participating in the program and often leads to newcomers securing employment opportunities with other local employers. She noted that there is interest in expanding the employment partnership program across other sectors but that a lack of funding has caused a roadblock for any expansion.

T. Bedard confirmed that the Immigration Partnership works closely with area municipality partners and that shared strategies exist to help inform area municipality strategic planning, service planning, and decision-making. 

T. Bedard noted that the Immigration Partnership's work drives toward the pillar of belonging and that area municipalities directly engage with this pillar through service delivery of parks, recreation, and other services. She also noted that data from the last immigrant survey conducted in 2023 and an international student survey conducted in 2025 indicated that immigrants felt a fairly strong sense of belonging within the community.

In response to the Committee, T. Bedard clarified that the immigrant survey does not specifically ask about secondary migration to Waterloo Region but does ask respondents their reason for moving to the Region, noting that immigration to the Region is typically driven by education, employment, and other opportunities.

In response to the Committee, T. Weigel Green noted that representatives from the Immigration Partnership are involved in the Region's Community Safety and Wellbeing Plan and that collaborative work is necessary to mitigate negative community discourse on immigration.

T. Bedard confirmed that funding requests were made to all area municipalities last year as part of the Immigration Partnership's budget process.

In response to the Committee, T. Bedard noted that each year, the federal government conducts a survey and public consultation process on immigration levels and the Immigration Partnership responds to this survey each year. She further noted that the next federal three-year Immigration Levels Plan will be released in November, and that the Immigration Partnership is advocating for more direct engagement with municipalities in federal immigration planning.

Councillor P. Wolf Motion re: Housing and Homelessness

 

The Committee had no objections to considering a revised version of Councillor P. Wolf's motion that was brought forward. 

P. Wolf and D. Craig both spoke briefly to the motion.

M. Harris left the meeting at 10:29 am. 

M. Harris left the meeting at 10:29 AM.
  • Moved byP. Wolf
    Seconded byD. Craig

    Whereas the Region of Waterloo is responsible for funding and coordinating homelessness services, including emergency shelters, supportive and transitional housing, and outreach programming; and

    Whereas Council has a responsibility to ensure that new investments in housing and homelessness services do not unintentionally reinforce geographic inequities but instead contribute to a more balanced and accessible regional system; and

    Whereas the Region's commitment to addressing homelessness must include, not only increasing overall capacity, but also ensuring equitable access to services across all municipalities, including a more equitable distribution of funding and services dedicated specifically to women; and

    Whereas there are only 20 emergency homeless shelter beds for women in Cambridge, 42 total throughout the entire Region and only 80 of all shelter beds dedicated to men are in Cambridge while the remaining 130 are in Kitchener-Waterloo; 120 all gender beds in Kitchener-Waterloo and

    Whereas the single women’s shelter in Cambridge has been forced to deny almost 300 requests for services due to consistently being at capacity since August 2025; and

    Whereas residents experiencing homelessness in Cambridge are frequently required to travel outside their community to access shelter and other homelessness supports services, creating additional barriers to safety, continuity of care, and connection to local supports; and

    Whereas Porchlight Counselling and Addiction Services’ winter warming centre for men operated at a minimum of 128% and maximum of 198% throughout its 2025-6 seasonal operations and their request to operate this centre 365 days a year was rejected by the Region, the reason given being a lack of funding; and

    Whereas Thrive HIV Prevention and Support, which operates the Multi Agency Community Space out of 150 Main in Cambridge - the single drop-in space for individuals experiencing homelessness in Cambridge - is only funded to operate during afternoons, despite an immense need for extended operating hours; and 

    Whereas YWCA Cambridge has requested that the Region fund a 365-day, low-barrier overnight drop-in for women in Cambridge and been repeatedly told there is not adequate funding to do so, despite the lack of any overnight spaces for women in Cambridge existing outside of the 20-bed shelter; and

    Whereas the Region recently granted Kitchener a 365-day, 22.5-hr-a-day shelter; and

    Whereas such recent funding decisions only further concentrate services in one city and predominantly on programs dedicated to men or which are co-ed, while neglecting to address the immense lack of services in Cambridge; and

    Whereas research consistently shows that women avoid co-ed spaces out of fear for their safety, effectively rendering co-ed spaces for men only, further exacerbating the discrepancy in service availability for men versus women;

    Therefore, Be It Resolved That Regional Council direct staff to bring a report to the November 2026 Community & Health Services Committee with recommendations related to:

    •  the principle that major investments in homelessness and infrastructure be accompanied by a strategy to improve service access and capacity in underserved municipalities; and
    • future homelessness-related capital funding allocations exceeding 1 million include an assessment of geographic distribution impacts and a requirement to identify complementary investments in underserved municipalities where gaps exist; and
    • improving homelessness service capacity and access in underserved municipalities, including Cambridge, as part of future homelessness system planning and capital budget deliberations; and
    •  the application of Gender-Based Analysis Plus lens to all investments in homelessness and infrastructure to ensure a gender equitable distribution in funding and services across the Region of Waterloo.
    In Favor (15)K. Redman, D. Craig, J. Erb, S. Foxton, J. Gowing, C. Huinink, C. James, J. Liggett, D. McCabe, J. Nowak, M. Rodrigues, N. Salonen, S. Shantz, B. Vrbanovic, and P. Wolf
    Carried (15 to 0)
