4.1
Petition for safety lights at the roundabout at Beverley and Dundas

 
1.Petition - Safety lights at Beverley-Dundas (June 4).pdf

Doug Spooner, Acting Commissioner, Transportation Services, spoke to the roundabout at Beverley and Dundas described within the petition, which possesses a newer design and geometric features. He indicated that flashing lights may not be the appropriate technical solution but that a team is already conducting a study to provide upgrades to roundabouts around the region to improve pedestrian crossings overall. He said that the particular site output will be included in the 2025 capital plan, with 3 design changes.

Replying to a question from the Committee, D. Spooner indicated he will look at a number of solutions, including flashing lights, but that lights may not achieve what is desired.

5.
Delegations

 

5.1
Lindsay Markle, Kitchener

 

Lindsay Markle spoke to concerns surrounding the Highland Road noise study that he and the neighbours have. The main issues include that a field test was not conducted prior to construction, the speed parameters in the study do not reflect what’s really happening with noise, and if noise warnings are added to titles it will devalue properties. He further noted an increase in traffic with lots of acceleration at the traffic light, and questioned whether exhausts, mufflers, were captured in the study. L. Markle said that he is not sure if noise barrier will provide solution, but he and the neighbours ask that everything is looked at. He provided that an earth berm is already present along with a fence, and noise is still in excess of 65 decibels.

5.2
Kyra Chisholm, Plan Manager, ClimateActionWR and Tova Davidson, Patrick Gilbride of Sustainable Waterloo Region & Reep Green Solutions

 
1.5.2 Kyra Chisholm GHG Presentation.pdf

Kyra Chisholm and Tova Davidson of Sustainable Waterloo Region & Reep Green Solutions provided a presentation to the Committee regarding Greenhouse Gas Inventory in Waterloo Region and efforts to reduce emissions.

Responding to a question from the Committee, K. Chisholm answered that their organization works with staff who complete the inventory. They access data through utilities and local delivery corporations, as well as data from the Ministry of Transportation, Regional Landfill, and the Heads of Livestock Association, with rural data only being livestock heads, not including food.

In response to a question from the Committee, K. Chisholm indicated that its difficult to be on track as part of a larger carbon emitting system, and they need to be ambitious to be on track. T. Davidson said when looking at transportation as a major emitter, they are seeing a major shift in the purchase of electric vehicles, which will in turn be charged at buildings, which will create a data challenge.

5.3
Kai Reimer-Watts, Organizer, Faith Climate Justice Waterloo Region

 

Kai Reimer-Watts spoke in support of the 50% by 2030 Carbon emissions target, and ClimateActionWR and their work. His message to Council is that though the work is daunting, there exists a climate emergency, declared so by Council along with all area municipalities. They are seeing record-breaking temperatures, and there is public will for action. K. Reimer-Watts expressed his hope that the work and the target can continue to be taken seriously, and underscored the ability to get off natural gas.

5.4
Brandon Gorr, Kitchener

 

Brandon Gorr spoke to a previously presented noise study, and noted issues and concerns with the modelling within. He explained that staff have denied previous requests to record field data to observe what is actually happening. He concluded that residents are not convinced that the modelled sound levels are accurate.

In reply to a question from the Committee, B. Gorr explained the biggest concerns are that some of the questions have not been reasonably addressed by staff.

6.
Presentations

 

6.1
Staff Presentation - Climate Aligned Growth Update

 
1.Climate_Aligned_Growth_Collaborative_Presentation_June_4.pdf

Staff provided a presentation and spoke to several elements of staff work being done to align with the strategic priority of climate aligned growth. This was presented by Jennifer Rose, Commissioner, Engineering and Environmental Services; Rod Regier, Commissioner, Planning Development and Legislative Services; Danielle De Fields, Director, Community Planning; Katie Johnson, Director, Strategic Initiatives and Asset Management; and Aaron Moss, Director, Facilities and Fleet Management.

Responding to the Committee, J. Rose indicated that the integrated mobility plan will look at project lists through climate aligned growth, and that Council needs to decide their comfort level in moving forward with certain initiatives and projects. The pace at which Council wants to do projects is based on budget, and if Council wants be more aggressive, this could cost more money, and also be disruptive.

Replying to a question from the Committee, D. Spooner indicated that staff will continue awareness about ridership, trails, multi-use pathways and more for people’s ability to move around.

In response to a question from the Committee, J. Rose indicated that assessments have been done of all available roofs that solar panels can go on, and that this is ongoing work. Additionally, A. Moss indicated that staff will look for opportunities to make buildings more efficient with every project.

8.1.1
EES-DCS-24-004, Highland Road - Updated Noise Assessment

 
1.EES-DCS-24-004, Highland Road - Noise Assessment Update.pdf
2.Final_Highland_Road_Updated_Noise_Study_2024-04-16 - No Appendices.pdf

J. Rose provided a brief introduction to the noise study report, which has been an ongoing process based on other construction occurring on Highland Road. In accordance with the noise policy, the recommendation is that no noise wall is needed, but residents may request another study after 5 years.

In reply to a question from the Committee, J. Rose indicated that the first study is no longer relevant due to some errors, as it was conducted to the property line, and the next two studies were done to the outdoor living area. Frank Kosa, Director, Design & Construction, replied that there were not physical readings completed as the noise policy uses modelling for determining existing and future noise predictions.

Responding to a question from the Committee, Mike Henderson, Manager, Transportation Program Development, spoke to the parameters of the modelling used, and how this model tends to overpredict noise. He indicated that peak noises will not be impacted by noise walls even twice the size. To add, D. Spooner said that trees would not be as effective as a noise wall but would provide some mitigation.

Replying to a question from the Committee, M. Henderson said there is no risk with doing a live test, but it is not Regional policy to approach it that way and that other municipalities do not do it that way. In follow-up, D. Spooner indicated that they are unsure if they still have that equipment, and if the ask is to do a current noise level study, he suggests they follow current practice.

In reply to a question from the Committee, M. Henderson answered that from his experience, they do look at speeding in projects, and, if there is speeding, they look at mitigating measures. In follow-up, F. Kosa indicated the road redesign is taken into consideration on all projects, they try to take into account various geometric changes but that there are limitations to what can be done.

Replying to the Committee, M. Henderson indicated that the MTO and speed cameras are not current conversations taking place but that this can be investigated.

The Committee asked staff to look into the feasibility of conducting a live study in the area in question including the availability of airport noise monitoring equipment, and report back to Council on June 19th
