EES-WWO-26-001, 2025 Drinking Water Summary Report

 
1.EES-WWO-26-001, 2025 Drinking Water Summary Report.pdf
2.EES-WWO-26-001, Appendix A, 2025 Annual Summary Report.pdf

Ken Brothers, Interim Commissioner, Water and Wastewater, provided a brief introduction to the report, noting that the report is an annual regulatory obligation that provides a compliance summary of the Region's 19 drinking water systems.

Amy Shaw, Director, Water and Wastewater Operations, highlighted that 15 of 19 water systems inspected by the Ministry of the Environment, Conservation, and Parks (MECP) received a compliance score of 100% and the remaining 4 water systems had a total of nine non-compliances identified during the inspections, which were mainly administrative issues. She also emphasized that in light of the water capacity constraint in the Mannheim service area, the municipal drinking water supplied by the Region of Waterloo continues to meet the necessary requirements under the Safe Drinking Water Act. She also noted that the Region's annual top management review of the drinking water quality management system occurred on November 25, 2025.

In response to Council, A. Shaw confirmed that there were less compliance issues in 2025 than in the previous year, and noted that the Region's average compliance score is higher than the average score across Ontario.

5.2
EES-WIM-26-005, Water Capacity Constraint- Interim Risk Management Approach Update

 
1.EES-WIM-26-005, Water Capacity Constraint- Interim Risk Management Approach Update.pdf
2.EES-WIM-26-005, Appendix A - Interim risk management framework update Presentation.pdf
3.EES-WIM-26-005, Appendix B – Updated 2025 Mannheim Housing Supply Data.pdf

K. Brothers provided a brief introduction to the report, noting that the report is to provide an update further to the motion passed on January 28, 2026, on the development of an interim risk management framework that will identify necessary operational thresholds and risk-based targets that must be met to allow staged development to proceed.

Nicole Sapeta, Senior Engineer, Water, explained that a preliminary review of forecasted development and anticipated new water supply has taken place, and that this review allows for a preliminary comparison to determine when it is expected that sustainable operations and operational resiliency could be restored. She also noted that an interim risk management framework study to support decision-making with respect to water servicing for growth has been initiated in parallel with this preliminary review.

Ryan Snider, Manager, Water Operations and Maintenance, noted that forecasting considerations must take various social factors into account that can impact development growth. He stated that discussion has taken place with the Planning Focus Water Servicing Capacity Working Group and the Water Supply Developer Stakeholder Group to better understand growth over the next few years, noting that both groups identified concerns with assessing growth based on estimated timing of future developments. He also noted that staff anticipate developing a more refined forecast to accompany the finalized risk-based assessment, and that staff are working with area municipal partners to refine data.

N. Sapeta explained that new water supply solutions are needed to sustainably operate the water system, restore operational resiliency, and support growth. She outlined anticipated future water supply demands, noting that to operate sustainably, the available water supply capacity should be greater than the average day water demands. She noted that the preliminary review indicates that sustainable operations and operational resiliency will not be restored until 2027.

N. Sapeta noted that an interim risk management framework study has been initiated to review how staged development could potentially proceed under certain risk-based conditions prior to restoring sustainable operations and minimum operational resiliency capacity, also noting that this approach would manage risks while protecting the existing drinking water system. She explained that the project team for the interim risk management framework study is meeting weekly to progress this work and that necessary technical analyses have been initiated. She noted that the target for study completion is May 2026, also mentioning that this is an ambitious timeline and that additional time may be needed for completion of some technical components. 

In response to Council, R. Snider confirmed that additional population data and breakdowns can be included as part of the study.

N. Sapeta also noted that work is being done to assess how much water capacity could be made available to support staged development, as well as to determine how this capacity would be allocated to developments.

In response to Council, K. Brothers noted that staff are working to increase communication and share further technical details with the development and business communities through working groups. He also noted that Regional staff are working to achieve a balanced solution by understanding challenges on both the development side of the issue and the water protection side of the issue.

In response to Council, R. Snider confirmed that 7,000 building permits were issued before 2025 and that staff expect that these permits will proceed to occupancy-stage, although for some projects this may not be until 2028.  Alyssa Bridge, Manager, Regional Development Services, noted that area municipalities are responsible for issuing building permits, and that the Region is working closely with the area municipalities to ensure that they have the necessary information to process building permits. She also confirmed that the Region would not intervene in development within area municipalities unless a Regional Form 1 is required. A. Shaw confirmed that a Form 1 would be required if infrastructure is being upsized to increase the size of the pipe to an existing property or if a new pipeline is being connected to the service system.

In response to Council's questions regarding the feasibility of a pipeline, N. Sapeta noted that a range of water supply solutions are being reviewed, and that pipeline construction would take approximately 20 years to complete. Regarding the establishment of a pipeline reserve fund, Mathieu Goetzke, Commissioner, Transportation Services, noted that with appropriate infrastructure investments, the Region will have 15-20 years of capacity to support growth trends. Next year's water supply strategy update will inform whether the Region should start investing in a pipeline as a long-term project. 

N. Sapeta clarified that forecasted new water supply is not currently accounted for as part of long-term planning processes. Upon completion of testing to confirm sustainable amounts of water produced by wells, forecasted water supply would become available for development in the long-term.

In response to Council, N. Sapeta confirmed that current water initiatives in place complement each other to support short-term, medium-term, and long-term planning. She also confirmed that "committed capacity" accounts for development that has gone through a plan of subdivision process and that building permits issued for developments not yet occupied are not included in this number.

Rod Regier, Commissioner, Planning, Development, and Legislative Services, clarified that infrastructure expansion will be required to deliver water to the units in plans of subdivision and in the development forecast. He also noted that there are heavy water users in the employment sector that will need to have special consideration in the risk-based assessment.

In response to Council, N. Sapeta confirmed that a water connection to Lake Huron has been considered, but noted that a connection to Lake Erie would be recommended rather than a connection to Lake Huron to avoid inter-basin transfer.

A. Shaw noted that a report will be brought back to provide additional information on Wilmot's water capacity and confirmed that temporary water allocations cannot be considered to support growth.

A. Shaw also confirmed that Regional staff have been working collaboratively with provincial counterparts and have been provided with support from MECP staff.

In response to Council, R. Regier clarified that different water requirements exist during the development and construction process for fire protection and for final occupancy.

N. Sapeta confirmed that staff will report back on how considerations to mitigate impacts to the 2027 construction season can be implemented.

N. Sapeta also noted that the water supply strategy update has been progressing but that staff from this project have been leveraged to support work surrounding the Mannheim service area water constraint. She noted that while the schedule is still to be confirmed for the water supply strategy update, an update is expected to come in Fall 2026.

In response to Council, N. Sapeta clarified that the recommendation for a 20% operational resiliency target will not be revisited, but an interim operational resiliency will be considered as part of the interim risk management framework study.

A. Shaw confirmed that a working group has been established to examine water conservation methods and that potential by-law changes could be considered, noting that recommendations will be brought forward in the near future and that outdoor conservation will begin May 31.

5.3
PDL-GDS-26-009, Mannheim Service Area Water Capacity Constraint: Update on Work Underway

 
1.PDL-GDS-26-009, Update on the Current Work for the Mannheim Service Area Water Capacity Constraint.pdf
2.2026-03-11 - Update Report - Presentation.pdf

M. Goetzke provided a brief introduction to the report, noting that this report intends to provide the community with an overview and update on work underway related to the Mannheim service area water capacity constraint.

R. Regier noted that an interim framework is underway and that this will inform how to sustainably and responsibly support development, also noting that the Region is working directly with area municipalities and developers. 

He also noted that where area municipalities have determined it to be an appropriate tool to address water constraints, holding provisions can be utilized to allow the planning process to continue and can be lifted upon confirmation from the Region that there is available water supply to support the new demand. He also noted that this is a shift from the report brought forward to Committee on February 10, 2026.

K. Brothers confirmed that short-term solutions are underway, noting that Parkway Water Treatment Plant is anticipated to come back online by the end of March and Greenbrook Water Treatment Plant is anticipated to come back online by fall 2026. He also noted that a sidestream treatment project for Mannheim Water Treatment Plant is underway, with an ultra filtration container expected to arrive in spring 2026.

Connie MacDonald, Comissioner, Resident Experience, Strategy, and Communications, reviewed stakeholder engagement that is taking place, noting several internal and external key groups and organizations that the Region is consulting with and cross-sharing information with. She noted that regular updates will continue to be brought forward to both Council and the community. She also noted that an industry white paper is underway with input from Regional Council, area municipal staff, and the development stakeholder group.

Danielle De Fields, Director, Regional Growth, Development, and Sustainability Services, confirmed that the local school boards participating officially in the Planning Working Group can be explored further, but did note that conversations with school board staff to provide them with updates have already occurred.

A. Shaw clarified that the Parkway and Greenbrook Plants will not provide new capacity; when these plants come back online, this will simply be restoring capacity back to operational resiliency and will take pressures off of existing systems. She also noted that an upgrade is planned for Greenbrook later this year that will add new capacity.

Regional Chair K. Redman confirmed that the Region is applying to available funding opportunities related to this issue. 

D. De Fields confirmed that discussion with post-secondary institutions has occurred, as well as with the Water Institute at the University of Waterloo.

A. Shaw confirmed that she has been providing updates to the Grand River Conservation Authority (GRCA), and that the GRCA is also a member of the Conservation Working Group.

A. Shaw noted that the recruitment process for water staff is underway, with several positions already posted and receiving applications.

A. Shaw confirmed that 1,455 litres per second refers to the amount of supply when all systems are operational.

A. Shaw clarified that the water demand is currently higher than sustainable capacity, which creates a deficit of operational resiliency, reiterating that when Parkway and Greenbrook come back online, there will not be any net new capacity. 

In response to the Committee, R. Snider clarified that when calculating average water demand, approximately 355 people are served by 1 litre of water supply per second.

In response to questions from the Committee regarding 20% operational resiliency, K. Brothers noted that aquifer restrictions create more supply vulnerability, as opposed to source supply systems that can more freely increase their draw from lake supply. A. Shaw noted that the 20% operational resiliency recommendation is evidence-based and has been peer-reviewed, and this recommendation is what staff want to adopt in the long-term. She clarified that part of the risk-based framework will be to analyze whether an interim operational resiliency of less than 20% will be acceptable.

A. Shaw confirmed that an ultra filtration container will be arriving tentatively in June for the sidestream treatment project at Mannheim Water Treatment Plant, noting that this timeline will allow for preliminary infrastructure upgrades to take place first in order to accommodate the weight of the container.

A. Shaw also confirmed that the Region is bound by a permit to how much water it can draw from the Grand River for Mannheim Water Treatment Plant, but this limit is currently constrained by the capacity of the treatment plant. She noted that the sidestream project will increase the system to its full capacity for treatment. 

In response to Council, staff also confirmed that water taking from the Grand River is not dependent on river flow unless there is a temporary river upset, in which case water taking is paused, noting that there are limitations on how much water can be stored in the aquifer storage and recovery system at Mannheim but that that this limit is currently being investigated and studied.

A. Shaw confirmed that once Parkway and Greenbrook Treatment Plants are back online, this will alleviate well pumping from the Mannheim wellfields. She also confirmed that damaged plate settlers at Mannheim are a separate repair project from the Parkway and Greenbrook repairs.

6.1
Councillor D. Craig

 

D. Craig introduced the motion and emphasized that the intention of the motion is not to assign blame but to identify weaknesses in forecasting, monitoring, and reporting, so that future infrastructure challenges are identified earlier and managed more effectively. He noted that independent reviews have commonly been carried out in other municipalities and organizations following system failures, and that staff participation is usually limited and does not significantly impact staff workload.

C. James seconded the motion and expressed that an independent review can help strengthen governance and strengthen reporting systems, with a focus on learning and improving systems.

There was unanimous consent from Council to amend the motion from a 6 month to 9 to 12 month completion date.

M. Goetzke clarified that an independent review would focus on providing a process and reporting review, rather than on providing a technical review.

Wayne Steffler, Chief Financial Officer, clarified that costs for a consultant have not yet been confirmed and that project scope would need to be further investigated, noting that whatever terms of engagement or procurement process is decided upon, details could be brought to a closed Council meeting for review before signing off on a consultant.

Council voted to amend the motion to remove the 2021 to 2026 timeframe to ensure that the review could investigate any relevant time periods.

  • Moved byD. Craig
    Seconded byC. James

    Whereas, the Region of Waterloo is responsible for the long term planning, management and reporting of essential water infrastructure capacity,

    And whereas, recent reporting indicates a significant shortfall in water capacity that undermines previous projections and communications provided to Council and the public over the past five years,

    And whereas, the capacity issue has immediate and detrimental impacts on economic development and municipal planning,

    And whereas, an objective, transparent and comprehensive understanding as to why this occurred is important in restoring public trust and preventing future occurrences,

    Therefore be it resolved,

    That council directs the CAO to retain a qualified independent third-party firm to investigate the region’s water capacity reporting and communicating processes prior to 2026

    And that the review examine:

    1. when capacity concerns were first identified,
    2. what internal reporting occurred and at what levels,
    3. whether existing thresholds or triggers for escalation to council notification are adequate,
    4. whether governance, communication or resource gaps contributed to any delay,

    And that, the report back to Regional council include:

    A clear timeline of events and decision points with findings on accountability and process gaps,

    And recommendations to ensure early notification of future infrastructure risks,

    And any recommended policy, governance or procedural changes to strengthen transparency and Council oversight.

    AND FURTHER, that this report be brought forward within the earlier of the completion of the report and 9 to 12 months from this date

6.2
Councillor J. Gowing

 

J. Gowing introduced the motion and noted that its intention is to protect the long-term sustainability of groundwater, not to stop production. He also noted that this motion is simply an ask of the Province, rather than a demand.

In response to Council, A. Shaw clarified the role of the Region and the role of the Province in issuing Permits to Take Water, noting that the Region would still continue to provide technical advice to the Province when asked to inform decisions to issue permits, even if the Province did not accept this recommendation. She also noted the Region should be cautious of requesting a moratorium on Permits to Take Water, as the Region will be seeking new Permits to Take Water for upcoming projects.

In response to the Committee, F. McCrea noted that while this motion is only a request of the Province, it could potentially be problematic for the Province as it makes requests that are outside of its usual process. She also noted that while she is not aware of any other municipalities who have made similar requests, this would need to be researched further by staff.

Council voted to defer the motion to the March 25 Council meeting.

  • Moved byJ. Gowing
    Seconded byP. Wolf

    WHEREAS the Province of Ontario regulates Permits to Take Water (PTTW) under the Ontario Water Resources Act;

    AND WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo relies primarily on groundwater for its municipal drinking water supply;

    AND WHEREAS provincial data indicates there are currently 108 active PTTWs within the Region of Waterloo, authorizing a combined maximum taking of approximately 1.01 billion litres per day;

    AND WHEREAS within the Mannheim Service Area municipalities (City of Kitchener, City of Waterloo, Township of Woolwich, and Township of Wilmot ), there are 65 active permits authorizing approximately 740 million litres per day, of which 22 municipal permits account for approximately 472 million litres per day and 43 private permits account for approximately 268 million litres per day;

    AND WHEREAS recent groundwater capacity constraints identified within the Mannheim Service Area highlight the need for a precautionary and cumulative aquifer-level review of private permitted water takings to prevent the risk of interference to existing municipal and private water supplies, and to safeguard groundwater resources for both the natural environment and potential future use for municipal water supply;

    THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT:

    1. The Region of Waterloo formally request that the Province of Ontario implement a temporary moratorium on the issuance or expansion of new private-sector Permits to Take Water within areas experiencing identified groundwater capacity constraints, including the Mannheim Service Area, until completion of the Region’s Water Supply Strategy Update;
    2. The Province continue municipal engagement in the PTTW review process by requiring formal notification to affected municipalities of new, amended, or renewed PTTW applications; a defined municipal commenting period; and a written provincial response addressing municipal comments prior to final permit decisions;
    3. While the Province currently publishes a quarterly Permit to Take Water dataset, proactive notification and supporting documentation be provided directly to the Region at the time of application submission and final decision.
