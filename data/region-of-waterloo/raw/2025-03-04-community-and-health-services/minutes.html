8.1.1
CSD-HOU-25-003, Building Better Futures Update and Next Steps

 
1.CSD-HOU-25-003, Building Better Futures Update and Next Steps.pdf
2.Appendix A - Building Better Futures, 2025 to 2030.pdf
3.Appendix B - Breakdown of Units Created through Building Better Futures (2021 to 2024).pdf
4.Appendix C - Types of Eviction and Eviction Process.pdf
5.Appendix D - Building Better Futures Presentation.pdf

Ryan Pettipiere, Director of Housing Services, provided a presentation on the Building Better Futures framework. A copy of the presentation is attached to the original agenda. He provided an update on the framework and next steps. He highlighted that the Building Better Futures initiative is ongoing and in accordance with the housing initiatives of Plan to End Chronic Homelessness and Waterloo Region Housing Revitalization Plan. The Building Better Futures initiative was targeted to create 2,500 new affordable units in five years (2021-2024). The Region achieved the initial target and Staff have set a new target for 3,500 affordable homes created or preserved in 5 years (2025-2029) with 60% (2,100) dedicated for very-low-to-low-income households. He further highlighted that Staff continue to look for opportunities to increase affordable housing by incentivizing affordable housing developments, utilizing and preserving existing affordable assets, and create disincentives for the financialization of housing. 


In response to the Committee, R. Pettipiere explained that supportive and affordable housing are critical to support all the housing plans initiatives not solely the Plan to End Chronic Homelessness. There are approximately 20,000 individuals that are struggling to afford housing, and Staff are working on establishing more affordable housing options as well as providing rent supplements for those needing assistance.


Responding to the Committee, R. Pettipiere further explained that the Municipal right-of-first-refusal can only be authorize through provincial legislation. This legislation would enable municipalities to purchase properties at market-value to preserve and establish affordable housing. Staff are interested in the province enacting this type of legislation, however, currently there has been no communication on whether it will be enacted. R. Pettipiere confirmed that the rent geared to income housing continues to be desirable as income increases have not matched the increased cost of living specifically with the housing market. He confirmed that residents living in rent geared cannot be evicted due to an increase in salary, however, the operating policy permits Staff to offer incentives. He further confirmed that the cost for rent geared to income buildings can only be a maximum of 30% of the tenant's income.


D. McCabe noted that there are outstanding issues that need to be addressed for the state of current regional housing.

  • Moved byC. James
    Seconded byS. Foxton

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo approve the revised Building Better Futures Framework set out in Appendix A, as the Region of Waterloo’s direction for the continued creation and protection of affordable and non-market housing over the next five years (2025 to 2030), contingent upon federal and provincial funding, as outlined in report CSD-HOU-25-003, dated March 4, 2025.

    In Favor (15)B. Vrbanovic, J. Erb, J. Nowak, K. Redman, M. Harris, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, D. McCabe, J. Liggett, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, N. Salonen, C. Huinink, and K. Williams

CSD-HOU-25-002, CMHC Affordable Housing Funding for Waterloo Region Housing Revitalization Project 589 Langs Dr., Cambridge

 
1.CMHC Funding for Waterloo Region Housing Revitalization Project 589 Langs Dr., Cambridge - CSD-HOU-25-002.pdf

R. Pettipiere provided a brief overview of the report CSD-HOU-25-002 that recommends proceeding to enter into an agreement with the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation. He explained that by entering into this agreement it would allow the Region to secure favourable loan terms that only the Federal government can offer and would allow a loan to be secured against debentures since the Region cannot hold a mortgage.


In responding to the Committee, R. Pettipiere confirmed that tenants at 589 Langs Drive in Cambridge will be relocated during the reconstruction process and that a youth center will be built on site as well.


The Committee acknowledged the benefits of this partnership and thanked Staff for their ongoing efforts.

8.2.1
PHP-SPS-25-002, Update on Consumption and Treatment Services and Homelessness Addictions Recovery Treatment (HART) Hub

 
1.PHP-SPS-25-002, Update on Consumption and Treatment Services and Homelessness Addictions Recovery Treatment (HART) Hub.pdf
2.Appendix A - CTS Closure Impacts and Mitigation Strategies.pdf
3.Appendix B - Presentation.pdf

Dr. Hsiu-Li Wang, Commissioner Public Health & Paramedic Services/Medical Officer of Health, introduced the report and Katie McDonald, Manager of Health System Integration and Strategic Partnerships to provide the presentation. A copy of the presentation is attached to the original agenda. K. McDonald provided background information on the closure of consumption and treatment services that are located within 200 meters of a school or licensed childcare centre. Staff and community organizations are collaborating on mitigation strategies in anticipation of the consumption and treatment services site closures. A Harm Reduction Work Group has been formed to lead these mitigation strategies including continued support for housing shelters
and engaging emergency services.

Tara Groves Taylor, CEO of Community Healthcaring Kitchener-Waterloo, provided an overview of the services that will be available at the Waterloo Region HART Hub. The HART Hub’s overall purpose will be to provide caring and coordinated support to assist with housing, mental health, and substance use concerns. She noted that there is no singular location for the HART Hub, however, there is an anchor site at Community Healthcaring Kitchener-Waterloo at 44 Francis Street in Kitchener. On April 1, 2025, there will be Staff available online and certain Staff will be transferred from the CTS site at 150 Duke Street West in Kitchener.

Jennifer Scott, Housing Services Director at House of Friendship, highlighted the services that will be offered by the HART Hub including culturally safe care and immediate needs such as food, showers, laundry, and peer support. Staff will assist in navigating the health care system with resources and support as well as increased access to services through expanded hours. She further highlighted that $1.3 million of the funding has been dedicated to mental health and addiction supportive housing. There also will be a twelve-bed transitional housing program and rent supplements available to help make permanent housing more accessible. Personal support programs will be designed to best assist each individual based on their specific needs.

In response to the Committee, T. Groves Taylor explained that HART Hub is meant to assist individuals during their transition into recovery and provide rehabilitation supports. Dr. H. Wang further explained that the previous funding allocated to the closing CTS site will be redirected to the HART Hub and the province has announced that they will be providing funding to the HART Hub approximately three times the current funding provided to the CTS site.

Responding to a question, T. Groves Taylor confirmed that Staff will monitor usage of the HART hub and they do not estimate that there will need to be a waitlist. J. Scott further confirmed that the twelve supportive beds will be an intensive program that is in addition to other supportive housing services and partnerships such as House of Friendship.

The Committee expressed concerns about the HART Hub program limitations and the critical need of the community for consistent mental health and addiction support services. Dr. H. Wang agreed that there are many community members in need of certain addiction support services that were previously provided at the CTS site, however, the provincial funding is earmarked for the HART Hub.

Responding to the Committee, K. McDonald confirmed that Staff and community partners collect data on addiction indicators already, such as overdoses and emergency service usage. Staff are currently in discussions with the province about the required data to be collected for their evaluation process and T. Groves Taylor further confirmed that there will be an evaluation team to review the more rigorous data being collected provincially. Staff and community partners will continue to evaluate the data through a local lens to determine best practices for both costs and services.

Received for information.

8.2.2
PHP-PSV-25-002, Paramedic Services Annual Key Performance Measurement Indicators

 
1.PHP-PSV-25-002, Paramedic Services Annual Key Performance Measurement Indicators.pdf
2.Appendix A - Paramedic Services Performance Measurement Report Charts and Figures, January – December 2024.pdf
3.Appendix B - Paramedic Services Presentation.pdf

The Committee meeting resumed at 12:48 p.m.

John Riches, Director/Chief, Paramedic Services, provided a presentation on the reports PHP-PSV-25-002, Paramedic Services Annual Key Performance Measurement Indicators and PHP-PSV-25-001, Paramedic Services' 2024 Response Time Performance Plan Results. A copy of the presentation is attached to the original agenda. He highlighted the key performance indicators such as the significant improvement to vehicle responses and ambulance offload time from January 2024 to December 2024. He further highlighted that Staff are working with the province to authorize ambulances to offload individuals at HART Hub locations if appropriate. He explained that the only area municipality to not have an improved response time was the Township of North Dumfries as there is larger percentage impact from a singular high response time due to the low amount of emergency calls. He provided a brief overview of the new metrics "code zero", "critical", and "capacity". The compliance results for the 2024 Response Time Performance Plan will be submitted to the Ministry of Health prior to the March 31, 2025. In 2026, the new dispatch procedure will be implemented, and Staff will report the results in 2027. An ongoing area of concern is accurate dispatching which negatively impacts response times as the medical need determines the permitted response time.

In response to the Committee, J. Riches confirmed that the Region of Waterloo has seen significant improvements and Staff will circulate further information on comparison metrics with provincial performance metrics. He further confirmed that funding is cost-shared with the province primarily on a 50/50 basis, with some initiatives fully funded at 100%. 

The Committee acknowledged all the efforts to improve response times and offload delays.

J. Riches clarified that in 2024 there were approximately 54,000 unique calls and 74,000 vehicle responses. He further clarified that Staff have been reviewing the response agreements to ensure that paramedic services are dispatched when medically necessary and properly coordinated with other emergency services such as police and fire. Although offload delays have improved, he noted that the alterative destination model initiative has not progressed, however, Staff are looking positively at the HART Hub model to provide an alterative to hospitalization.

In response to the Committee, J. Riches confirmed that ambulances were ordered in July of 2024, however, there may be a capital impact due to the tariffs.

Received for information.
