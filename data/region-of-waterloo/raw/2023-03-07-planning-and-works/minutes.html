7.3
Strategic Focus - Sustainable Transportation

 

7.3.1
TSD-TRP-23-002, Lancaster Street and Highway 85 Ramp Closure Environmental Assessment Study – Recommendation

 

Mathieu Goetzke, Commissioner Transportation Services, introduced the report, explaining that from a Ministry of Transportation (MTO) standpoint, the Lancaster ramps are not performing well, mostly due to collisions. M. Goetzke also noted that the ramps have relatively low usage compared to others, and that the closure of these ramps would provide an opportunity for a critical cycling connection to be implemented. M. Goetzke provided an overview of the public engagement that has taken place, including roughly 700 survey responses.

In response to the Committee, M. Goetzke noted that the risk of deferring this decision could potentially be missing the window of opportunity with MTO. The Committee expressed concerns with the lack of engagement with Kitchener City Councillors. 

  • Moved byD. Craig
    Seconded byR. Deutschmann

    That the previous question be called.

    Carried
  • Moved byB. Vrbanovic
    Seconded byR. Deutschmann

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo defer the motion discussed under item 7.3.1. TSD-TRP-23-002, Lancaster Street and Highway 85 Ramp Closure Environmental Assessment Study to no later than our next cycle of Regional Committee meetings on April 4, 2023 and as early as Council on March 22, 2023.

    Carried

7.1
Strategic Focus – Healthy, Safe and Inclusive Communities

 

7.1.1
CAO-REC-23-001, Indigenous Space Needs Final Report (Staff Presentation)

 
1.Presentation - Indigenous Space Needs.pdf

Paula Whitlow, Director, Truth and Reconciliation provided a presentation on the report, which included an overview of the project goals and engagement process. P. Whitlow shared that common themes for space attributes included an accessible physical indoor space, cultural and sustaining opportunities on the natural land, and wrap-around services. P. Whitlow noted that staff are excited to continue to build momentum in the community and explore options for a way forward, working towards reconciliation and establishing authentic long term relationships.

A copy of the presentation is attached to the minutes. This report was received for information.

In response to the Committee, P. Whitlow shared that the needs are varied and that sharing a space with other groups is not off the table, although was not specifically mentioned during the engagement process. 

Responding to questions from the Committee, Kara McLean, Head, External Stakeholder Management, confirmed that the work was done on behalf of all area municipalities and that there was representation from all municipalities. K. McLean explained that the report looks at how as a community we can respond to the diverse needs, which includes looking to the private sector. 

7.2
Strategic Focus - Thriving Economy

 

7.2.1
PDL-ECD-23-003, Charles Street Terminal Visioning Process, Community Consultation and Environmental Findings Update (Staff Presentation)

 
1.Appendix B - PDL-ECD-23-003, Presentation Slide Deck, Planning and Works Committee March 7, 2023.pdf

Matthew Chandy, Director Innovation and Economic Development and Sarah Millar, Manager, Land Portfolio provided a presentation on the Charles Street terminal visioning process, which included an overview of the history of the land, the technical and engagement work completed, and next steps for 2023. S. Millar noted that 2023 will include more stakeholder engagement, including the creation of five community connector roles. M. Chandy stated that this site is an opportunity to advance four shared community opportunities, which are, affordable housing, climate action, equity, diversity and inclusion, and a thriving economy. 

A copy of the presentation is attached to the minutes. This report was received for information. 

In response to the Committee, Rod Regier, Commissioner Planning, Development and Legislative Services confirmed that staff will come back to Council with a proposed strategy for facilitating the development of the site. R. Regier noted that Council would have the opportunity to sell the terminal outright with no conditions, if desired, but that Council does have the right to determine what the scope would be, in collaboration with the City of Kitchener. 

Responding to questions from the Committee, M. Chandy explained that specific outreach for different community groups is planned for 2023, including Indigenous groups, and that staff will reach out to City of Kitchener staff to determine how they can specifically target groups, such as condo boards.

7.3.3
Verbal Update: ION LRT Winter Operations

 

M. Goetzke and Keolis staff provided a verbal update on the ION LRT winter operations, explaining that the Region owns the network and Keolis maintains and operates it.

Richard Dean from Keolis, noted that this particular winter has been very challenging for the team due to freezing rain and ice accumulation. R. Dean provided an overview of potential solutions, such as a scraping device, painting deicing fluid onto the wire, and shuttle buses, as well as their respective benefits and challenges. R. Dean stressed the importance of learning, and making adjustments and improvements as we learn and work on the process. 

This verbal update was received for information.

In response to the Committee, R. Dean noted that continuous service would help to keep ice off the line, however, maintenance needs must be factored in as well.
