PDL-AIR-26-002, Airport Growth Plan

 
1.PDL-AIR-26-002, Airport Growth Plan.pdf
2.Appendix A - Airport Growth Plan Presentation for Council.pdf
3.Appendix B - YKF 2026 Airport Growth Plan.pdf

Rod Regier, Commissioner of Planning, Development, and Legislative Services, introduced the report and provided an overview of the plan to support growth at the YFK Airport. A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda.

Chris Wood, Airport Director, presented the 20-year plan for growth at the YFK airport. C. Wood explained the principles behind the Airport Growth Plan, including YFK becoming a Mobility Hub for a growing community, just-in-time infrastructure strategy, sustainability and environmental consciousness, development in the aerospace sector, and managing competing land uses.

Hilary Abel, Manager of Communications and Community Engagement, explained the community engagement that included two (2) in depth consultation with stakeholders included the Area Municipalities, First Nations groups, airport tenants, industry experts, advisory groups, and residents. H. Abel stated strong support for the growth plan.

Maria Laguna, Manager of Infrastructure Financing, provided a financial overview of the Airport Growth Plan. M. Laguna explained that the overall objective of the plan is to support responsible airport growth while managing levy impacts and positioning the airport for long term growth.  M. Laguna stated that the while grant funding is a critical component of the financing strategy, the plan also relies on the gradual accumulation of reserve balances to support future capital needs. She explained this will be accomplished using "Just-in-Time" infrastructure strategy, meaning new investments in infrastructure only begin as key milestones are within reach.

M. Laguna also highlighted that the plan prioritizes debt reduction, which reduces the burden on taxpayers.

M. Laguna explained the challenges the Airport Growth Plan faces, including reliance on external funding, not being eligible for development charge funding, a highly competitive and volatile industry, and its use of Regional growth assumptions. M. Laguna stated that these risks are mitigated by the phased and flexible approach.

Responding to a question from Committee, M. Laguna stated there is approximately $120 million for the 10-year capital plan for the project.

Responding to Committee, M. Laguna explained that the 3% annual cap on the airport's funding requirement, not on the entire Region.

Committee acknowledged the progress done to date on the YFK Airport, and the work accomplished by staff.

EES-WMS-26-002, Long-Term Waste Management Strategy Update

 
1.EES-WMS-26-002, Long-Term Waste Management Strategy Update.pdf
2.EES-WMS-26-002, Appendix A - Region_of_Waterloo_LTWMS_Council Report - Engagement Summary.pdf
3.EES-WMS-26-002, Appendix B - Region_of_Waterloo_LTWMS_Council Presentation_SID_June-2-2026.pdf

Connie MacDonald, Commissioner of Resident Experience, introduced the report, highlighting the need for sustainable waste management.

Olivia Kwok, Director of Waste Management, explained that the Long-Term Waste Management Strategy assesses how the Region of Waterloo manages garbage and organics long term, funds programs and services sustainably, collaborates with Area Municipalities and keeps up with changing legislation.

Tracey Annett, Senior Engineer, Waste Management, provided an update on the project, stating that the existing waste diversion and operational improvements have helped extend the lifetime of the Waterloo Landfill, however around 20 years of capacity remains. T. Annett stated it takes around 15 years to plan for a landfill replacement, which is why this process is starting now.

T. Annett stated the mission is to adopt a waste management strategy that is environmentally, socially, and fiscally responsible while still meeting the needs of the Region.

T. Annett explained that the Region is currently at the final phase of a three (3) phase timeline. She stated the focus in phase one (1) and two (2) were on building the foundation and evaluating possibilities, and now staff will move onto creating a roadmap.

T. Annett explained that there are opportunities to continue diverting recyclable and organic waste in both, as there are still high amounts of these materials going to the landfill.

T. Annett shared the 20 shortlisted strategic priorities that were identified through consultation, highlighting new programs to divert and reuse more materials, a plan for construction and demolition waste, a program to reduce waste and save edible food, and a long-term plan for garbage disposal as options with the highest community support.

T. Annett shared the next steps of the Long-Term Waste Management Strategy, which include the final round of public consultation in June 2026, the development of an updated Strategy starting in Summer 2026, and the final Strategy to be presented to Council for endorsement in Spring 2027.

Received for information.

TSD-TRS-26-006, GRT Fares and Revenue Strategy Update

 
1.TSD-TRS-26-006, GRT Fares and Revenue Strategy Update.pdf
2.TSD-TRS-26-006 Appendix A Staff Presentation - GRT Fares and Revenue Strategy Update.pdf

D. Spooner introduced the report and gave an overview of GRT's fare strategy. A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda.

D. Spooner stated the gross budget for GRT is approximately $232 million, of which $14 million is supported through the provincial gas tax program, $8 million in regional development charges and $52 million in fare revenue. This leaves a net levy impact of $156 million in 2026.

D. Spooner summarized recent efforts to improve transit affordability, including extending the transfer window, increasing the number of taxi coupons, increasing the affordable transit program discount, and introducing pilots for nonprofits and older adults.

Maggie Kerr, Project Manager, stated that GRT's affordable transit program is now supporting 22,000 individuals. M. Kerr explained that rising costs continue to pressure operating budgets, and that service expansion requires further investment.

M. Kerr explained the focus areas for review, including fare programs, fare technology, institutional and partner programs, and cost-recovery and revenue opportunities. M. Kerr stated that next steps will be carried out throughout the rest of 2026 and 2027, and include engagement, modelling and analysis, strategy refinement, preliminary recommendations, and a final report to be brought back to Council.

Responding to a question from Committee, D. Spooner stated that discussions are ongoing to provide discounted transit options to high school students.

In response to Committee, D. Spooner explained that increasing ridership is one of the main priorities for GRT.
