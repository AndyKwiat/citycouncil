TSD-TRP-26-005, Ira Needles Blvd Resurfacing

 
1.TSD-TRP-26-005, Ira Needles Blvd Resurfacing .pdf

M. Goetzke introduced the report and explained how the roads on Ira Needles Boulevard have deteriorated faster than anticipated, highlighting the need for a long-term solution. He further explained that this project will extend the service life of the road by five (5) to ten (10) years and reduce the risk of more costly repairs, supporting the general asset management strategy. 

M. Goetzke explained that the cost of the Ira Needles Boulevard Resurfacing project will cost $4.5 million and be funded through the transportation capital reserve, subject to Council's approval to add it to the 2026 Capital Budget. M. Goetze emphasized that this project would not deplete the reserve, as it would take the place of projects that are delayed.

M. Goetzke explained that should Committee approve the recommendation, staff will move forward with construction and communication plans.
Responding to a question from Committee, M. Goetzke stated that an updated view of financials will be brought to Council at the end of Q2 in 2027. He explained that at this point, the tendering period will be complete, which will tell us which projects will proceed in 2026. M. Goetzke further explained that at this point, there will be a better idea of which projects will be pushed out, creating space in the budget for the Ira Needles Resurfacing Project.

Committee expressed concerns over not having a full picture of how this project will impact future plans.
Responding to Committee, M. Goetzke explained that it is more cost effective to do this roadwork now. Wayne Steffler, Commissioner of Corporate Services, further explained that projects get reprioritized every quarter based on needs.

Responding to Committee, M. Goetzke explained that staff are open to alternative options and will look for the lowest cost solution that meets the needs of the Region.

Committee expressed concerns over the impact of this project on reserve funds.

In response to Committee, M. Goetzke explained the main risk to deferring this project is continuous rises in road maintenance costs. 

M. Goetzke clarified that the actual cost of this project is unknown until it can be brought to tender. 

Responding to Committee, M. Goetzke confirmed the Region does not have its own minimum standard, and that we follow the direction of the Province.

Staff received direction to bring back a business case, including the Capital Plan, at May Council.

M. Goetzke confirmed that this project will need to be moved forward quickly in order to start within construction season.

In response to a question from Committee, M. Goetzke confirmed that best practice is to get Council approval before moving to tender. W. Steffler, further confirmed that approval is needed to proceed with any procedural documents.

    Seconded byC. Huinink

    That the Regional Municipality of Waterloo approve the addition of resurfacing Ira Needles Boulevard, between Yellow Birch Drive and West Hill Drive, to the 2026 capital budget at an estimated cost of $4.5 million to be funded from the Transportation Capital Reserve as outlined in report TSD-TRP-26-005 dated May 5, 2026.

PDL-LEG-26-023, Waterloo Regional Police Services CCTV Camera Installation on Regional Hydro Poles

 
1.PDL-LEG-26-023, Waterloo Regional Police Services CCTV Camera Installation on Regional Hydro Poles.pdf
2.Appendix A - Region of Waterloo - CCTV Partnership - May 5, 2026.pdf

Liam Legate, Corporate Solicitor, provided an overview of the WRPS CCTV program and requested approval of an agreement to install cameras on Regional hydro poles. A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda.  

Responding to a question from Committee, Jennifer Davis, Deputy Chief, explained that the Province extensively supports this project. J. Davis further explained that the locations of CCTV cameras were driven by evidence-based data, including information from the community.

Responding to Committee, J. Davis explained the role of artificial intelligence (AI) on the back end of the CCTV program, where trained staff use AI to conduct searches using keywords or descriptors. J. Davis emphasized this process always includes a human-in-the-loop. 
Responding to Committee, J. Davis clarified that at this time, WRPS is only seeking an agreement to use Regional hydro poles to install CCTV cameras. J. Davis emphasized that consultation with the Area Municipalities has been supportive, and that any changes to the use of CCTV cameras would be first brought back to the municipalities' legal teams and councils.

In response to a question from Committee, J. Davis emphasized that CCTV cameras would not be used in municipalities until there is agreement.

J. Davis provided examples of success stories using CCTV cameras, including the recovery of a weapon used in a violent offense in Chatham Kent, tracking a suspect of violent crime in Guelph, and a 6% crime reduction in York.

Responding to Committee, J. Davis explained the CAM Safe initiative which is a public registry of where members of the public can register if there have CCTV footage of their residence. J. Davis explained this is a program independent of WRPS, but that it is a tool that can allow officers to see if there might be CCTV footage available. J. Davis stated that approximately 160 different residents have registered for the program.

Responding to a question from Committee, J. Davis stated that there is no knowledge of the reduction of crime in areas with CCTV cameras installed leading to an increase in crime in unsurveilled, nearby communities. J. Davis further explained that should this become an issue, the cameras are easy to move and can be relocated as needed.

In response to Committee, J. Davis reiterated that approval from the Area Municipalities is required before the installation of CCTV cameras.

Responding to Committee, J. Davis explained that the Privacy Impact Assessment was drafted in consultation with the IPC. J. Davis elaborated on the safeguards for privacy, including a Board Policy with very detailed procedures.

Responding to Committee, L. Legate stated he does not have knowledge of the Region using cameras similar to the planned CCTV cameras WRPS will use.

In response to a question from Committee, J. Davis stated the Ministry provided $100,000 of grant funding, which was matched by WRPS. J. Davis further explained that 10-year forecasting for the project has been included in the WRPS budget. 
Committee voiced concerns from residents regarding privacy, as well as those who have been supportive of the initiative.

Responding to Committee, L. Legate stated the cost of this program will be very low for the Region.

M. Goetzke clarified the Region's use of cameras, and stated cameras are used on Regional busses and at facilities as part of the standard security package. M. Goetzke stated that there are very strict rules surrounding the use of footage for investigations.

EES-SAM-26-001, Annual Review of Asset Management Planning Progress

 
1.EES-SAM-26-001, Annual Review of Asset Management Planning Progress.pdf
2.EES-SAM-26-001, Appendix A - State of Local Infrastructure Graphs.pdf
3.EES-SAM-26-001, Appendix B - Infrastructure Funding Gap Outputs.pdf
4.EES-SAM-26-001, Appendix C - Annual Review of Asset Management Progress Presentation SID May 5.pdf

Connie MacDonald, Commissioner of Resident Experience, Strategy, and Communication, and Katie Johnson, Director of Strategic Initiatives and Asset Management, introduced the report. A copy of the presentation is attached to the agenda.

Tim Walton, Manager of Asset Management, provided Committee with an update on the implementation of the Region's Asset Management Plan. T. Walton stated that the Region's infrastructure asset portfolio has a replacement value of $11.3 billion, with 84% of assets in a state of good repair. He stated that this is a slight deterioration since the last report, and that the infrastructure funding gap has grown from $82.8 million to $87.6 million per year.

T. Walton explained that the factors impacting progress include limitations on funding, an aging asset portfolio, competition for funds, capital plan delivery changes, challenges meeting operational demands, and increasing costs.

T. Walton stated that the Region has implemented several strategies to address factors inhibiting the Asset Management Plan.

Rod Reiger, Commissioner of Planning, Development, and Legislative Services, summarized the impacts of the infrastructure funding gap on the YKF airport, including reduced capital renewal spending, leading to temporary or deferred renewal work that will have significant impacts on the airport within the next ten (10) years.

C. MacDonald discussed the impacts of the infrastructure funding gap on waste management, explaining that renewal work in needed to maintain aging waste infrastructure. C. Macdonald explained that without this work, there is a risk of infrastructure failures, operation impacts, decreasing user experience, health and safety impacts, liability for not maintaining known critical assets, difficultly obtaining approval for future work, and loss of revenue.

Ken Brothers, Commissioner of Water and Wastewater, summarized the impacts of the infrastructure funding gap on Water and Wastewater Services, including increased reactive responses, operational burden, risk of failure, and life cycle costs.

W. Steffler summarized the impacts of the infrastructure funding gap on Facilities and Fleet management, and explained that reduced capital spending increases operating and maintenance costs, which has negative impacts on the Region's buildings and facilities.

M. Goetze summarized the impacts of the infrastructure funding gap on Transportation and Transit Services and explained that the infrastructure funding gap has led to challenges for repairing and maintaining transportation assets. M. Goetzke highlighted the concerns regarding risks related to the renewal of bus fleets.

K. Johnson provided a summary of risk mitigation strategies, including ongoing planning, increased maintenance, increased inspections, increased workload, risk-based assignment of resources, strategic and coordinated planning and providing redundancy and operational resiliency.

Received for information.
