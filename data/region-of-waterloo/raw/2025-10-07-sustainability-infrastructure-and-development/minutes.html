8.1.2
EES-WMS-25-005, Blue Box Transition Update

 
1.EES-WMS-25-005, Blue Box Transition Update .pdf

In reply to a question from the Committee, O. Kwok and J. Rose indicated that the blue bins which have Regional logos can continue to be used with Circular Materials (CM). Furthermore, O. Kwok elaborated that waste management staff will continue to look at items which can and cannot be added to green bins. 

J. Rose spoke to the $300,000 cost included in the recommended motion, explaining how the service is no longer required at public drop offs, but that staff anticipate an impact to the budget. With approval of the recommended motion, Council will be able to discuss continuation of the service as part of budget deliberation.

Responding to a question from the Committee, O. Kwok answered that there has been communication with residents, with information being provided for residents on how to contact CM. She further provided that communications have been ongoing with area municipalities. J. Rose answered that this Fall, more information will be provided by CM for items that are acceptable in blue box.

In response to questions from the Committee, J. Rose clarified that the public drop-off recycling program was funded 50% by the producers, which will be removed January 1, 2026, and staff are recommending Council make-up the remaining amount. O. Kwok indicated under provincial regulation, continuation of the existing service is all that CM is required to provide. With regard to student access to blue boxes, O. Kwok spoke to recycling availability to residents of post-secondary institutions, with CM expected to provide more information.

Replying to a question from the Committee, J. Rose explained that the $300,000 is a result of new tender prices for the service, adding that, as part of budget process, staff will break that number down further. She indicated that residents and businesses will use the existing facilities, and that CM is speaking with staff about what they would prefer if they funded some of the total (i.e., recycling and transportation costs).

In response to a question from the Committee, J. Rose answered that staff have asked CM to delegate to Council but have not heard back yet if they are willing to do so. She expects that the delay is likely due in part to CM fielding lots of requests, and C. MacDonald added staff and CM have a meeting scheduled, so a better view should be available for October Council.

Replying to a question from the Committee, J. Rose explained that public drop off includes construction material. Furthermore, construction material is not part of recycling program, so it would be accepted and processed in Waterloo and Cambridge. J. Rose added that this discussion is part of the work being done by regional staff, with a long-term waste management strategy to be completed hopefully in early 2026.

In reply to a question from the Committee, J. Rose clarified that organic and waste will be picked up on the same day, but that the blue box schedule has not been established yet. Staff are hoping to receive this schedule as part of an education campaign expected in the Fall.

Replying to a question from the Committee, O. Kwok indicated only one green bin is available, which is larger, but this is something that can be assessed once the program has been implemented. She further clarified that purchasing more bins is not an option at the moment, but that could be looked at. Staff were asked to report on this once the program has been implemented.

Responding to a question from the Committee, with regard to waste management for condominiums, with all the pending changes, J. Rose indicated staff are looking at policies for managing park programs and waste management in general for condominiums. These are part of the long-term waste management strategy, dealing with multi-residential units. J. Rose added that engagement is happening with relevant groups, with a survey out right now for feedback.

8.1.3
EES-WMS-25-003, Curbside Waste Collection Changes Implementation Update

 
1.EES-WMS-25-003, Curbside Waste Collection Changes Implementation Update .pdf

In reply to a question from the Committee, O. Kwok explained that cart delivery has not happened, but that messaging will correspond to its implementation. Residents have been asked to hold onto carts as they cannot be used yet, as they are particular to new trucks. Furthermore, as part of education, there are stickers on the bins along with educational packages.

Responding to a question from the Committee J. Rose indicated that with the Canada Post strike it is not possible to send out yellow stickers for waste collection upon request, but they are available at facilities and outreach events. She added that residents are not to use green bins for yard waste until the transition in March 2026. O. Kwok further noted that double collection weeks are bag based, and thus are not included in the new program, but more information can be provided in 2026 budget process.

Replying to a question from the Committee, O. Kwok indicated that compostable, or paper bags will still be able to be put directly into cart for organics collection. She added that they are not mandatory, except for instances of pet waste. 

In response to a question from the Committee, O. Kwok indicated that cart waste is for bulky and organics material, but due to their size, including yard waste, this process will still be manual. She noted that carts will still be on display at various regional sites for residents' knowledge.

8.1.4
EES-WMS-25-004, Downtown Waste Collection and Sani-Cans Update

 
1.EES-WMS-25-004, Downtown Waste Collection and Sani-Cans Update .pdf
2.Appendix A - Downtown Waste Collection and Sani-Cans Update.pdf

Clarifying, J. Rose indicated there will be no changes to service as of January 1, 2026. She added that costs outlined in the report are to maintain status quo, not provide additional services. However, J. Rose provided that there is a need to have ongoing conversations due to different waste technology existing out there, which should be explored.

In response to questions, O. Kwok indicated staff are in contact with the City of Guelph regarding their waste pilot projects. J. Rose indicated there are constant conversations with BIAs, all municipalities, and staff, to ensure the program's success and be responsive to unique needs in areas such as the downtown corridors. Downtown programs will start in March 2026, with the specific details to still be determined. Council approved the 4-days a weel as the level of service, however, this is a concern with businesses, and staff are working with them to monitor the program as it goes forward to see what changes need to be made.

Replying to questions, J. Rose explained that area municipalities are continuing to collect at certain waste management locations as the services were not uploaded when the program was shifted from area municipalities to the Region. J. Rose indicated that staff have spoken to area municipalities about equity in service, with conversations to be continued that will potentially include uploading the collection at these locations.

Responding to a question from the Committee, J. Rose clarified that the Region is not required to pick up Sani-cans, which is usually up to area municipalities, but continues to do so as legacy work. She added that it is not required to pick up waste from businesses, but Councils have ability to pick what services to provide as they so choose.

10.1
Notice of Motion - Councillor Dorothy McCabe

 
  • Moved byD. McCabe
    Seconded byC. Huinink

    Whereas under Ontario Regulation 391/2, Blue Box producers are fully accountable and financially responsible for their products and packaging once they reach their end of life and are disposed of for ‘eligible’ sources only; and

    Whereas ‘ineligible’ sources, which producers are not responsible for, include business improvement areas, places of worship, daycares, camp grounds, public facing and internal areas of municipal-owned buildings, and not-for-profit  organizations, such as shelters and food banks; and

    Whereas the province amended Ontario Regulation 391/2 to not expand services for new public space recycling; and

    Whereas should a municipality continue to provide services to the ‘ineligible’ sources, the municipality will be required to oversee the collection, transportation, and processing of the recycling, assuming 100% of the costs; and

    Therefore, be it resolved that Region of Waterloo Council request that the province amend Ontario Regulation 391/21, Blue Box so that producers are responsible for the end-of-life management of recycling products from all sources as stated above; and

    Further that Council request the support of all Ontario Municipalities; and

    THAT this resolution be forwarded to the Honourable Doug Ford, Premier of Ontario, the Honourable Todd McCarthy, Minister of the Environment, Conservation, and Parks, Mike Harris, Minister of Natural Resources and Forestry, Minister of Affairs and Housing, Rob Flack, AMO President Robin Jones, Waterloo Region municipalities, all Ontario Municipalities and First Nation Councils.

    That Minister McCarthy be asked to provide more information on the estimated $171M in savings for municipalities through this program.

    In Favor (15)B. Vrbanovic, J. Erb, J. Nowak, K. Redman, S. Shantz, S. Foxton, D. McCabe, J. Liggett, D. Craig, P. Wolf, C. James, N. Salonen, C. Huinink, M. Rodrigues, and J. Gowing
    Carried (15 to 0)
