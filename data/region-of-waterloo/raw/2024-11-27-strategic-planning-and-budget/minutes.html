4.
Deferred Motions

 

Members of the Committee deliberated on the deferred motions and decided to vote on them individually.

Responding to questions, Doug Spooner, Commissioner of Transportation Services, clarified that Traffic Systems Management Analysts are based in the operations center rather than out on the road. Their role involves adjusting signals to optimize traffic conditions, enhance pedestrian crossings, and improve overall system efficiency. He also noted that they will collaborate with the new Miovision system.

Responding to a question from the Committee, Mathieu Goetzke, Acting Chief Administrative Officer, clarified that the deferred motions under discussion are all expansions that were deferred to this meeting. He noted that other items with offsetting costs would be addressed later in the day and that staff reductions would be discussed during the closed session. Will Short, Director of Council and Administrative Services/Regional Clerk, further explained that the general purpose of the discussion is to determine whether these items should be added or removed for final budget day, with the option for Councillors to revisit and propose changes at that time.

Members of the Committee discussed and enquired about the structure of the motions and about how things are presented, the potential for giving preference to qualified applicants when applying to work for the region that live in the region and also for qualified applicants coming from marginalized groups. 

The Strategic Planning and Budget Committee recessed for Closed Committee at 9:56 a.m.

The Strategic Planning and Budget Committee reconvene at 2:23 p.m.

Responding to questions, W. Short clarified that a positive vote ensures the item is sent to final budget day, while a negative vote means it will not be forwarded.

Responding to questions about the Transit – Core Network Improvements recommendation involving 8 FTEs and how it would work if only partial expansions were implemented, D. Spooner outlined the four key areas of transit operations. He highlighted operational adjustments, including on-time performance measurements, MobilityPLUS services, and the associated staffing needs, emphasizing the importance of support staff in ensuring these improvements.

Members of the Committee discussed issues related to crowding, the proposed service expansion, sought clarification on the timing of the transit service plan, and emphasized the importance of transit services.

An amendment to the Transit – Core Network Improvements 8 FTEs  (Respond to growth and reduce system overloads), was approved to hire 8 FTEs instead of 16.

An amendment to the Transit – Customer-Driven Operational Adjustments and 5 FTEs (Effective customer service and transit reliability) recommendation, was approved to be changed from 9 to 5 FTEs.

In response to an inquiry, D. Spooner, Commissioner, Transportation Services, explained that GRT is required to meet Ministry of Transportation (MTO) requirements, including employing certified bus technicians with up to date licenses. He noted that the required training hours have been reduced from 24 to 13, which is considered sufficient. An amendment was approved for Transit Service Support staff 13 FTEs (Safety/compliance of vehicles/licenses and customer service). 

Regarding the recommendation for a Transportation Communications & Resident Feedback Specialist (1 FTE) to improve communications and engagement, D. Spooner responded to a question about who is currently performing the duties of this position. The Committee discussed the necessity of this role and considered whether it could be deferred to the next year. D. Spooner emphasized that department staff identified this as a critical position, aiding in effective communication and information sharing. In response to a request for further explanation, D. Spooner explained that the position would not only improve response times but also support the implementation of the Integrated Mobility Plan. Additionally, this role would allow engineers to focus on technical work while a specialist manages public communication.

Regarding the recommendation for an Engineering Technologist for Corridor Management (1 FTE) to support growth and address the increasing number of Municipal Consent and Work Permit applications, clarification was requested about inspection visits and quality control issues in projects. D. Spooner explained that the work often involves greenfield developments or existing projects, with most work being carried out by third parties while the Region conducts quality control. He noted that complaints might arise from the development community, third-party contractors, or residents, particularly due to project delays. M. Henderson, Director of Transportation, further clarified that the role involves site visits on regional roads and handling development applications. In response to a question about whether this was related to the planning responsibilities downloaded through Bill 23, D. Spooner confirmed that the Region remains the road authority, retaining responsibility for the operation and safety of regional roads.


Regarding the Climate and Sustainability Program Manager (1 FTE) position to commence June 1, 2025, as amended, a question was raised about the focus of the team’s efforts. It was noted that while much of the team’s current work is corporate-focused, significant community-related efforts are required to meet climate goals. This position is viewed as essential to ensuring adequate support for community-focused climate action strategies, including the Retrofit WR program. D. DeFields, Director of Regional Growth, Development and Sustainability Services, highlighted the importance of the Retrofit WR program as a key area for advancing climate targets. M. Eastwood, Manager of Planning and Sustainability, provided an update on the status of the Federation of Canadian Municipalities grant funding for the program, which is expected in the coming weeks. M. Eastwood also emphasized that this position involves broader responsibilities, including developing a community adaptation plan to prepare for extreme weather events.  An amendment to move the start date of this position to June 1, 2025, was approved. The Committee deliberated on the amendment, with M. Eastwood clarifying that community-facing adaptation work is a significant aspect of this role.

The Committee recessed at 4:00 p.m. recessed, and reconvened at 4:12 p.m.

Regarding the Affordable Housing Resources (3 FTEs) recommendation to provide supports for households at risk of losing their tenancies, R. Pettipiere, Director of Housing Services, provided clarification. A question was raised about the Tenant Access Coordinator position. R. Pettipiere explained that the role focuses on addressing tenancy issues and providing support by acting as a bridge to other services, such as legal clinics. While the position does not involve legal or paralegal work, it assists with navigating tenant-related matters under the Tenant Act. He noted that the department currently has similar positions and that the proposed new position would expand the capacity to address the growing portfolio and the increasing complexity of tenant needs.

The Committee deliberated on the projections and the proposed model for this expanded support.


Regarding the recommendation for Accounts Assistant - Facilities (1 FTE) to meet service level targets for invoice processing, there were questions raised about the Construction Act. Jennifer Rose, Commissioner of Engineering and Environmental Services, provided clarification. She explained that when the Construction Act came into effect in 2022, it introduced a prompt payment clause and associated penalties. J. Rose noted that there are valid reasons for withholding payment, and as long as the relevant information is provided to the contractor within 30 days, payment is not required.

Regarding the recommendation for Legal Assistant 1 FTE (due to increased demand), the committee discussed the legal department's approach to AI. Rod Regier, Commissioner of Planning, Development & Legislative Services, explained that the previous Regional Solicitor initiated conversations about potential tools, and the department plans to analyze technology to increase efficiency in legal services, which will be brought forward in the next fiscal year. He confirmed that AI is on their radar. Fiona McCrea, Acting Regional Solicitor, responded that the increased workload has led to additional responsibilities for both assistants and lawyers. She clarified that AI would not eliminate the need for external legal help entirely, as specialized legal advice will still be required, but it would reduce associated costs. When asked about the possibility of a pilot, R. Regier mentioned the challenges of recruitment, noting that offering a temporary assistant to a seasoned solicitor is not ideal for a competitive market.

Councillor R. Deutschmann - Notice of Motion

 
N. Salonen joined the meeting at 9:44 PM. ()
  • Moved byR. Deutschmann
    Seconded byP. Wolf

    Whereas the Waterloo Region Police Service (WRPS) has significantly increased its authorized service complement in recent years, adding 120 sworn officers and 60 civilians from 2019 to 2024, compared to just one sworn officer added between 2013 and 2018;

    And whereas the service complement (officers, civilians, special constables) for WRPS is comparable with Durham, Halton and Niagara regions;

    And whereas the WRPS has consistently struggled to meet its authorized officer complement since 2019, with an average shortfall of 33 officers per quarter in 2023 and a shortfall of 46 officers as at January 1, 2024;

    And whereas the WRPS faces ongoing challenges in maintaining its officer complement due to retirements and resignations, averaging approximately 14 officers per quarter during 2022-2023;

    And whereas police services across the province are actively recruiting from a limited graduating class of officers, creating a highly competitive environment for hiring new officers;

    And whereas the WRPS has discontinued their long-established practice of providing quarterly personnel reports in 2024, reducing transparency in staffing levels;

    And whereas research from the criminology department at Wilfrid Laurier University demonstrates no correlation between staffing rates by population and clearance rates among Ontario's 12 largest municipal police services;

    And whereas the WRPS's request for 18 additional officers and 10 civilians in 2024 would result in a budget increase exceeding $4 million, despite ongoing difficulties in filling existing positions;

    And whereas fiscal responsibility requires careful consideration of budget allocations, especially when authorized positions remain unfilled;

    Therefore, be it resolved that:

    The Regional Municipality of Waterloo requests that the Waterloo Regional Police Services Board further deliberate on the Waterloo Regional Police Service 2025 Operating Budget Estimate to reduce the net levy impact by $4 million and present a net levy impact of no more than $248,468,301 for Regional Council’s approval.
