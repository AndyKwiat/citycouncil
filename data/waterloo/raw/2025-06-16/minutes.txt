3	6. WORKSHOP
3	a) Title: Development Charge By-law Update –
3	Progress Report and Council Workshop
3	Report No.: CORP2025-018
3	Prepared By: Michael Pugliese
3	Michael Pugliese offered introductory remarks, explaining how the workshop is going to
3	flow and introducing Stefan Krzeczunowicz.
3	Stefan Krzeczunowicz, Associate Partner, Hemson Consulting gave a presentation
3	outlining the background of development charges, the recent changes to the legislation,
3	some of the politics surrounding development charges, and how they’re used in the City
3	of Waterloo and within the Region.
3	Council meeting recessed: (Time: 4:25 p.m.)
3	Council meeting reconvened: (Time: 4:28 p.m.)
3	Michael Pugliese responded to questions of Council. Stefan Krzeczunowicz also
3	responded to questions of Council. Brad Witzel also responded to questions of Council.
3	Moved by Councillor Bodaly, Seconded by Councillor Hanmer:
3	1. That report CORP2025-018 be approved.
3	2. That Council direct staff to proceed with preparing a Development
3	Charge Background Study as part of the process required to update
3	the City’s Development Charge by-law, pursuant to section 10 of
3	the Development Charges Act, on the basis of the growth-related
3	capital program attached as Appendix “A” of this report.
3	3. That Council, after considering the use of area specific
3	Development Charges, approves the continued longstanding
3	practice of imposing Development Charges on a uniform, city-wide
3	basis.
3	4. That Council direct staff to proceed with updates to FC-023 –
3	Development Charge Interest Policy, specifically, the removal of
3	charging interest on deferrals and reducing the rate freeze interest,
3	as outlined in this report, along with any other changes to interest
3	calculations that may be subject to recent proposed legislative
3	changes.
3	5. That Council direct staff to investigate consideration of an industrial
3	specific Development Charge, and impact on the city-wide non-
3	residential rate.
4	6. That Council direct staff to investigate potential incentives for higher
4	density residential development and non-residential development
4	that could include temporary reductions or exemptions for some
4	types of fees, charges, or costs related to municipal approvals.
4	Carried Unanimously
