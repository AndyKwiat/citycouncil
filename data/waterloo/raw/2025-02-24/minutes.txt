7	Mayor Dorothy McCabe – US Proposed Tariff Response
8	Craig Sloss, Resident of Waterloo spoke about the need to expand the scope of the
8	motion to explore the use of other social media platforms and include restricting the use
8	of U.S. social media platforms, especially those controlled by U.S. government officials
8	and those whose moderation policies are inconsistent with the Ontario Human Rights
8	Code. Cari Van Niekerk responded to questions of Council. Craig Sloss also responded
8	to questions of Council.
8	Moved by Mayor McCabe, Seconded by Councillor Hanmer:
8	WHEREAS contrary to terms of the existing United States Mexico Canada
8	Agreement as well as to the long standing strong and productive economic
8	and political relationship between Canada and the United States, the US
8	President threatened to impose 25% tariffs on Canadian goods as of
8	February 1st and although he has subsequently delayed the tariffs by thirty
8	days they remain a real threat; and
8	WHEREAS tariffs could lead to a halt of the supply chain and create
8	economic uncertainty in municipalities of all sizes; and
8	WHEREAS municipalities are the foundation on which the Canadian
8	economy is built, and as such municipalities should offer our support
8	towards a united and strong Team Canada response; and
8	WHEREAS municipalities have significant purchasing power through our
8	capital and infrastructure programs, with the City of Waterloo’s capital
8	program for 2025 being $200 million alone, while the Association of
8	Municipalities of Ontario cite that infrastructure spending across Ontario in
8	the next 10 years is expected to exceed $250 billion; and
8	WHEREAS Federal and Provincial leaders are encouraging all Canadians
8	to Buy Canadian; and
8	WHEREAS City of Waterloo and the broader region is proud and
8	honoured to be home to numerous U.S. based international students,
8	academic professionals, and institutional thought leaders, along with
8	prominent USA companies whose presence has greatly contributed to our
8	quality of life, economic development, innovation, and employment
8	opportunities; and
8	WHEREAS longstanding partnerships highlight the strong, mutually
8	beneficial economic ties between Canada and the United States, fostering
8	collaboration in technology, manufacturing, and research
8	sectors. Maintaining these free-trade practices will promote continued
8	strong growth and shared prosperity on both sides of the border;
9	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT THE CITY OF
9	WATERLOO:
9	Stands with Team Canada on the message that tariffs are not in the best
9	interests of Canadian or American residents, organizations, businesses
9	and employees;
9	AND calls on the Federal and Provincial governments to work with
9	municipalities on measures to protect Canadians and businesses;
9	AND calls on the Waterloo Economic Development Corporation (WEDC),
9	through the Business Economic Support Team Waterloo Region (BEST
9	WR), to develop an action plan to support Waterloo Region businesses
9	and our collective economic interests;
9	AND calls on the Federal and Provincial governments to better enable
9	municipalities to explore procurement opportunities that exist within other
9	trade agreements such as the Comprehensive Economic Trade
9	Agreement;
9	AND calls on the Federal and Provincial government to work
9	collaboratively and urgently to remove trade barriers between provinces
9	and territories, understanding that language requirements should continue
9	to be respected for Quebec;
9	AND calls on City of Waterloo staff to explore options for limiting the
9	impact of the potential United States’ sanctioned tariffs on City Services
9	and review current procurement processes for opportunities to increase
9	purchases from Canadian sources;
9	AND further that a copy of this motion be forwarded to the Right
9	Honourable Justin Trudeau, Prime Minister of Canada, The Honourable
9	Doug Ford, Premier of Ontario, local and area MP’s, MPP’s and Heads of
9	Council, The Association of Municipalities of Ontario and the Federation of
9	Canadian Municipalities.
9	Carried Unanimously

9	11. QUESTIONS
10	Councillor Freeman wanted to thank IPPW and Parks staff for their work on snow
10	removal over the last few weeks. She had a question regarding the height of some of
10	the snow piles in the City, and how we could bring them down as people are having
10	trouble seeing around them. She also asked about the clearance of snow on catch
10	basins, as things are starting to melt and water is building up and then freezing. Lastly,
10	she asked who is responsible for snow removal at fire hydrants.
10	Bob Henderson responded that getting rid of snow banks and snow loading are other
10	exercises for snow plow drivers when they’re not in the plow. There is a priority list with
10	snow banks that are causing sightline issues, the LRT, school zones, regional roads
10	and more. Regarding catch basins, he has been in talks with Leigh McDermott about
10	going through the City and looking for spots where they can avoid flooding. Regarding
10	fire hydrants, they have been requesting help from residents to help clear them. While it
10	is not the responsibility of residents, it is very helpful when they do. Fire Services staff
10	are also working to help clear them.
10	Cari Van Niekerk also responded that they are trying to get messages out publicly
10	asking residents to help out. There was great coverage last week about Fire Services
10	crews digging out hydrants, but there are 2600 of them across the City so it’s a big
10	process. Catch basin messaging is queued up to run this week to ask residents for
10	some assistance there.
10	Leigh McDermott also mentioned that the storm crews are now on a high priority list of
10	response for the catch basins and grates across the City. Today, they were coordinating
10	with Fire Services with what they’re doing with the hydrants and what City Utilities is
10	doing. There are three different divisions – Transportation, City Utilities, and Fire
10	Services – all working together on this.
10	Mayor McCabe had a question regarding old newspaper and real estate boxes
10	throughout the community. She was wondering if these something the City is able to
10	remove and if there is a process to cut the chains. Bob Henderson responded that there
10	is a process and they try to contact the owners first to remove them.
