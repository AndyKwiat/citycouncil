2	a) FCM Award – The Ann MacLean Award for Outstanding Service by a
2	Woman in Municipal Politics
3	Councillor Vasic opened with some remarks:
3	Over the past decade, I’ve popped in and out of Seven Shores, to Peter’s “office”,
3	and I’ve come to know Lynne through Peter. Lynne, for those of you who don’t
3	know, is a former mayor of Waterloo. For me, Lynne has come to join my other
3	role models, like my great-grandmother through to my mom who integrated both
3	a deep love for her family and a commitment to her family, while also working
3	and contributing to her community. While I’ve never had the fortune of meeting
3	her myself, I and many of us have benefitted from her staunch advocacy. My
3	kids’ daycare remembers her and has invited Peter on her behalf to their
3	anniversary celebration in September, because she was instrumental in getting
3	them their space. She advanced environmental work at the City, which we
3	continue to build on today, and she was a champion for affordable housing,
3	before the time the situation reached its crisis today. I hope I will have a legacy
3	that will live on in those who never meet me as well, just as she has had on me,
3	many of you, and the community. It’s because of this legacy and the impact that I
3	nominated her for the Federation of Canadian Municipalities Ann MacLean
3	Award for Outstanding Service by a Woman or Non-binary Person in
3	Municipalities, and that’s what we’re here to celebrate today.
3	The award recognizes retired women and non-binary municipal politicians who
3	have shown exemplary service to their community and constituents and to
3	mentoring women and non-binary folks who want to run for elected office. It is
3	named in honour of FCM Past President, Ann MacLean, who led FCM’s
3	campaign to increase the number of women in municipal politics across Canada.
3	The recipient is chosen by the FCM Board of Directors and is presented with the
3	award at their Annual Conference.
3	Our former Mayor Lynne Woolstencroft was given this honour in 2021
3	posthumously; however, due to the pandemic the conference and award
3	presentation was not held.
3	I wanted to thank City staff who helped organize this event, and thank you to
3	those who wrote letters of support for the nomination.
3	Councillor Freeman offered further remarks on Lynne Woolstencroft’s legacy:
3	Lynne dedicated her life to public service, helping others and championing the
3	environment.
3	She served as a school board trustee from 1970-1972, 1974-1985 and was
3	elected chair from 1979-1982. She was elected councillor for the City of Waterloo
3	for three terms, from 1985–1988, 1988–1991, and 1997–2000 and in 2000 she
3	was elected as Mayor of the City of Waterloo.
4	During her time on Council, she was a champion of the Environment First Policy
4	at the City which meant that environmental impacts would be thought of at the
4	beginning of projects and initiatives. She was always interested in learning more
4	about how to use our precious land resources more efficiently whether that was
4	by promoting methane gas reclamation at the Region of Waterloo landfill sites or
4	through infill housing development.
4	On a personal note, Lynne is the reason I chose to run as a City of Waterloo
4	councillor in 2006. I first met Lynne when she was serving as Mayor of the City of
4	Waterloo in 2001. I delegated to the City Council in support of a staff
4	recommendation to allow a site-specific Official Plan Amendment to permit a
4	childcare centre to be constructed and operated on land designated for
4	employment use. At the time, a childcare centre was deemed a commercial use
4	in the City zoning by-law. I explained to Council how a childcare centre was a
4	compatible land use, because it’s important for children to be cared for close to
4	where parents work. It would reduce travel times between work and home, it
4	would encourage families to spend more time together during the day, it would
4	facilitate mothers continuing to nurse their children during working hours, and it
4	would allow for sick children to be picked up and cared for quickly.
4	What I faced during this delegation was an all-male Council, except for Lynne. It
4	was clear to me that my concerns as a professional working engineer and a
4	mother were not represented nor understood by the individuals tasked to make
4	the decision, except for Lynne. There was talk of a deferral, requesting more
4	information, it was a summer session of Council and they would not be meeting
4	for at least another month. Lynne did not let this childcare centre fall through the
4	cracks. She demanded an agreement from the members of Council to sit in an
4	emergency meeting to receive the additional information, at which time the staff
4	recommendation was approved.
4	It was during this delegation to Council that I realized that if I wanted different
4	voices to be heard, more women, more engineers, more non-binary people, more
4	parents, more individuals need to be decision-makers. And that if I thought I had
4	something to offer, I should step up and put these skills on the municipal ballot
4	through running. Lynne encouraged me, and so did Peter. You all supported me
4	from the start to the finish, and I am so thankful for all that you shared with me
4	and taught me.
4	Even though she passed away, Lynne’s legacy and work ethic continues to
4	inspire my work as an elected official. It is for this reason, and many more, that I
4	am thankful that Lynne Woolstencroft was the recipient of the Ann MacLean
4	award for outstanding service by a woman in municipal politics. She was most
4	deserving.
5	It is our honour to present this award to Lynne’s family this evening on behalf of the
5	FCM Board.
5	Peter Woolstencroft spoke about Lynne’s legacy and her passion for education.
