3	8. WORKSHOP
3	a) Title: Waterloo Park Plan Update and Funding
3	Release for 2026 Implementation
3	Report No.: COM2026-022
3	Prepared By: Anna lee Sangster
3	Anna lee Sangster gave a presentation outlining the general improvements made to the
3	park after the 2009 Park Plan, as well as changes that were made through the
3	implementation efforts.
3	Councillor Bodaly left the meeting. (Time: 3:17 p.m.)
3	Claire Basinski, Take Consulting Ltd. and Eliza Oprescu, MHBC gave a presentation
3	outlining the plan for the workshop and for the Waterloo Park Plan project, including the
3	timeline and themes for the planning phase.
3	Councillor Bodaly joined the meeting. (Time: 3:24 p.m.)
3	Councillor Vasic left the meeting. (Time: 3:29 p.m.)
3	They then facilitated the workshop, further outlining each of the themes and giving
3	Council an opportunity to provide input.
3	Councillor Wright highlighted that waste management and managing invasive species
3	should be included.
3	Councillor Roe mentioned that, as the READI Team liaison, accessible play
3	components are very important, especially thinking about where an accessible swing
3	would go and what it would look like.
3	Councillor Freeman talked about the difference between passive and recreational
3	activation of space, as well as keeping programmed space separate, such as park
3	rentals. On the programming piece, it is helpful to have an understanding of a needs
3	assessment for the programmed recreational fields, as it ebbs and flows in pressure.
3	Councillor Vasic joined the meeting. (Time: 3:35 p.m.)
4	Councillor Hanmer expanded on what the other comments made, especially as far as
4	recreational activation and programmed space, as it should be available as a drop in
4	space. Accessibility of the spaces is also a key theme, as equipment and wayfinding
4	may need to be updated. Planning to make spaces multi-use is also appreciated.
4	Councillor Bodaly started with thanking the Waterloo Park Advisory Committee for the
4	substantive work they did to supplement this process. Adding on to what the other
4	Councillors mentioned about recreational vs. programmed space, Councillor Bodaly
4	also contributed that there is a tension because the City is running out of space for
4	programs. It’s also important to make the spaces inviting and active for the general
4	public when they’re not programmed. He also didn’t see arts space mentioned and
4	would advocate for that to have its own theme.
4	Councillor Roach provided feedback that Waterloo Park should remain a city-wide park,
4	not just for the Uptown or to be used as an events space. The park has to work for
4	everyone, and he emphasized safety, maintenance, and practical year-round use as
4	key themes. He supports investment in what is essential and urgent, such as safe
4	paths, lighting, seating and accessible connections. He also highlighted the importance
4	of a welcoming space that’s not over-programmed.
4	Councillor Vasic described balancing fiscal responsibility with enhancements to invest
4	in, giving the example of separating cyclists and pedestrians as what works well. The
4	park should be a place of tourism, and she didn’t see that element mentioned, as it
4	brings people in for international tournaments and events like Lumen. She would like to
4	see Waterloo Park as a beautiful, fully invested space in the centre of town, similar to a
4	Central Park feel.
4	Mayor McCabe also highlighted arts spaces in the park, including planned events like
4	the Waterloo Concert Band and their needs as far as electricity and storage space. She
4	asked about pop-up or impromptu performances as well and how you make those work
4	in the space. She wondered about connections to and from the park, and how to
4	connect people across the City in ways that are easy and accessible to everyone
4	regardless of age or ability. Mayor McCabe also asked if there were better uses of the
4	Park Inn or Potters’ House.
4	Councillor Wright added that the project will likely be dollar constrained, so they should
4	think about partnerships and opportunities, especially with the post secondary partners
4	along the park.
4	Claire Basinski, Take Consulting Ltd. summarized the comments received during this
4	section of the workshop.
4	Mayor McCabe added that accessibility is an important piece, and echoed Councillor
4	Wright’s comments about the universities along the park, adding the importance of
4	connections with them.
5	Councillor Freeman also noted the importance of arts of culture with the notion of
5	balance. It would be important to see how commentary on the themes are being
5	balanced within the plan. She emphasised Councillor Roach’s comments around how
5	the park means a lot of things to a lot of people.
5	Councillor Vasic asked about next steps, as she won’t be on Council to see the plan
5	come forward, and since not everything can be accomplished for Waterloo Park, maybe
5	some features could be implemented when Bechtel Park comes forward for an update.
5	She also mentioned the Parkland Strategy which points to park safety, and that park
5	ambassadors are worth thinking about.
5	Eliza Oprescu, MHBC outlined the next steps for engagement and the park plan. Claire
5	Basinski, Take Consulting Ltd. then responded to questions of Council.

17	Councillor Bodaly – Bill 60 Motion
17	Motion tabled June 15, 2026 for Council consideration on June 22, 2026.
17	Moved by Councillor Bodaly, Seconded by Councillor Hanmer:
17	WHEREAS the Province of Ontario’s Bill 60, Fighting Delays, Building
17	Faster Act, 2025 was passed in November of 2025; and
17	WHEREAS Schedule 16 of Bill 60 enacts the Water and Wastewater
17	Public Corporations Act, 2025; and
17	WHEREAS the Act allows the Province to designate water corporations to
17	manage water and wastewater services; and
17	WHEREAS the Province has now enacted legislative amendments
17	through Bill 98 to reinforce 100 per cent public sector ownership of water
17	and wastewater public corporations, which reflects an important step in
17	addressing concerns regarding ownership structures; and while the City
17	recognizes this commitment, it remains important that the implementation
17	of any such model continues to support strong municipal oversight,
17	transparency, accountability, rate setting authority, asset stewardship, and
17	long term integrated planning; and
17	WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo has a lengthy history of managing our
17	water and wastewater system, governed within a robust regulatory
17	framework; and
18	WHEREAS the current water capacity challenges within the Region of
18	Waterloo underscore how foundational water security is to growth
18	management, housing, economic stability and climate resilience within the
18	Region; and
18	WHEREAS Regional decision making, rate setting and governance are
18	transparently governed by open meeting rules, allowing for complete
18	public engagement; and
18	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo is ultimately responsible for the local
18	distribution and delivery of water to end users.
18	THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the City of Waterloo affirms its
18	support for the continued public ownership and governance of drinking
18	water and wastewater services, recognizing water as essential to public
18	health, environmental protection, and long-term community well-being; and
18	BE IT RESOLVED that the City of Waterloo encourages the Province of
18	Ontario to review Schedule 16 of Bill 60 through a lens of public health and
18	system resilience, groundwater protection and long-term water security,
18	governance, accountability, and transparency financial sustainability, and
18	affordability, planning, growth management, and climate adaptation; and
18	BE IT RESOLVED that the City of Waterloo encourages the Province of
18	Ontario to enact and maintain explicit statutory protections requiring
18	public-sector ownership of water and wastewater corporations, including
18	prohibitions on private share ownership or transfer; and
18	BE IT RESOLVED that a copy of this resolution be sent to the Premier of
18	Ontario, the Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing, to local and area
18	MPP’s, the Association of Municipalities of Ontario, the Ontario Big City
18	Mayors, and to the Region of Waterloo and local area municipalities.
18	Carried Unanimously
