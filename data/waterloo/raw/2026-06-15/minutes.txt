1	1. TERRITORIAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
1	Councillor Freeman opened the meeting with the following Territorial Acknowledgement:
1	I offer this acknowledgement with respect and gratitude, recognizing that my life has
1	unfolded across lands governed by longstanding treaty relationships between
1	Indigenous Nations and the Crown. These treaties—historic, living, and often
1	unfulfilled—shape the places that have sustained me and continue to guide my
1	understanding of responsibility and reconciliation.
1	My life began in Toronto, on lands covered by Treaty 13, the Toronto Purchase of 1805,
1	made with the Mississaugas of the Credit.
1	I was raised in Woodstock, Ontario, within the territory governed by the Upper Canada
1	Treaties, specifically Treaty 3, the Between the Lakes Purchase of 1792. These
1	agreements involve the Haudenosaunee, Anishinaabeg, and Neutral (Attiwonderonk)
1	Peoples.
1	As I moved to Waterloo for university and early career opportunities, I lived and worked
1	within the lands encompassed by the Haldimand Tract, granted to the Six Nations in
1	1784 – six miles on either side of the Grand River. This region also falls within the
1	Between the Lakes Treaty territory of the Mississaugas of the Credit.
2	During my time as President of Professional Engineers Ontario, I travelled across many
2	treaty territories throughout Canada. These journeys deepened my awareness of the
2	diversity of treaty histories and the responsibilities they carry, and how these
2	agreements continue to shape the identities and governance of the provinces and
2	territories.
2	Since August of last year, I have been privileged to temporarily live and work in the
2	Northwest Territories, on lands governed by Treaty 8 and Treaty 11, within the
2	traditional territories of the Yellowknives Dene First Nation (Drygeese Territory) and the
2	North Slave Métis.
2	In preparing for engineering work at Ipperwash recently, I undertook a two-day
2	awareness program to learn about the history of the Chippewas of Kettle and Stony
2	Point First Nation, whose lands are shaped by complex and painful treaty relationships,
2	including the legacy of Treaty 29 and the broader Upper Canada Treaties. Through this
2	learning, I was reminded of the Seven Grandfather Teachings, which continue to
2	influence how I understand my responsibilities within treaty relationships.
2	Across each stage of my life, these treaties remind me that I am a guest on lands
2	whose histories long predate my own. I acknowledge these treaty relationships as
2	ongoing commitments – ones that call for learning, respect, and meaningful action.

10	7. NOTICE OF MOTION
10	Councillor Bodaly – Bill 60 Motion
10	Motion to be tabled June 15, 2026 for Council consideration June 22, 2026.
10	Motion:
10	WHEREAS the Province of Ontario’s Bill 60, Fighting Delays, Building
10	Faster Act, 2025 was passed in November of 2025; and
11	WHEREAS Schedule 16 of Bill 60 enacts the Water and Wastewater
11	Public Corporations Act, 2025; and
11	WHEREAS the Act allows the Province to designate water corporations to
11	manage water and wastewater services; and
11	WHEREAS the Province has now enacted legislative amendments
11	through Bill 98 to reinforce 100 per cent public sector ownership of water
11	and wastewater public corporations, which reflects an important step in
11	addressing concerns regarding ownership structures; and while the City
11	recognizes this commitment, it remains important that the implementation
11	of any such model continues to support strong municipal oversight,
11	transparency, accountability, rate setting authority, asset stewardship, and
11	long term integrated planning; and
11	WHEREAS the Region of Waterloo has a lengthy history of managing our
11	water and wastewater system, governed within a robust regulatory
11	framework; and
11	WHEREAS the current water capacity challenges within the Region of
11	Waterloo underscore how foundational water security is to growth
11	management, housing, economic stability and climate resilience within the
11	Region; and
11	WHEREAS Regional decision making, rate setting and governance are
11	transparently governed by open meeting rules, allowing for complete
11	public engagement; and
11	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo is ultimately responsible for the local
11	distribution and delivery of water to end users.
11	THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the City of Waterloo affirms its
11	support for the continued public ownership and governance of drinking
11	water and wastewater services, recognizing water as essential to public
11	health, environmental protection, and long-term community well-being; and
11	BE IT RESOLVED that the City of Waterloo encourages the Province of
11	Ontario to review Schedule 16 of Bill 60 through a lens of public health and
11	system resilience, groundwater protection and long-term water security,
11	governance, accountability, and transparency financial sustainability, and
11	affordability, planning, growth management, and climate adaptation; and
11	BE IT RESOLVED that the City of Waterloo encourages the Province of
11	Ontario to enact and maintain explicit statutory protections requiring
11	public-sector ownership of water and wastewater corporations, including
11	prohibitions on private share ownership or transfer; and
12	BE IT RESOLVED that a copy of this resolution be sent to the Premier of
12	Ontario, the Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing, to local and area
12	MPP’s, the Association of Municipalities of Ontario, the Ontario Big City
12	Mayors, and to the Region of Waterloo and local area municipalities.
