2	3. MOMENT OF REFLECTION continued
2	Mayor McCabe provided Council with a further moment of reflection.
2	a) The City of Waterloo is mourning the passing of former Mayor James
2	Bauer (Mayor of Waterloo 1960-1965). Bauer was first elected as an
2	alderman at age 25, and by age 31 he had become Waterloo’s youngest
2	ever mayor, a distinction which still holds true today. During his time as
2	mayor, Waterloo underwent a period of unprecedented growth and
2	prosperity.
2	In 1960, a shopping mall development began in the core of the city, and
2	Waterloo Square was one of Canada’s premier malls containing 45 stores
2	when it opened in 1961. At the time, the development was deemed as one
2	of the most courageous and ambitious ever undertaken by any North
2	American municipality. A second shopping plaza at Bridgeport Road and
2	Weber Street was also constructed.
2	Waterloo’s land area increased significantly with the annexation of
2	Waterloo Township land for the creation of new residential subdivisions
2	that included Lincoln Village, Lakeshore Village, Maple Hills Acres, and
2	Colonial Acres. Land for the establishment of the 86-acre Parkview
2	Cemetery and the Conestoga Parkway were also secured. A second
2	sewage disposal plant was constructed along with the new fire department
2	headquarters on Weber Street and a substation on Westmount Road
2	North. Waterloo’s first high school, Waterloo Collegiate Institute, welcomed
2	students in 1960 and a new library building opened its doors to the public
2	in 1965.
2	Bauer spent considerable time during his tenure as mayor promoting
2	improved planning for the future betterment of Waterloo. In a 1963 speech,
2	Mayor Bauer stated, “There are those, who as a solution to Waterloo’s
2	problems, would propose that we stop the growth of the city…this city
2	must be led not by visionaries, but by men of vision.”
3	In 2023, I had the opportunity to meet former Mayor Bauer, and he shared
3	some of his memories of the work he did on Council. In particular, he
3	talked about major changes that occurred in Uptown. I asked if he had
3	advice and he said: ‘Always think about the people.’ That's advice that
3	stands the test of time. We can all be grateful for the lasting impact he
3	made on our city. On behalf of my council colleagues, I offer my sincere
3	condolences to James Bauer’s family and friends.
3	At the beginning of this Council meeting, we pause to think about the needs of our
3	community. May we show wisdom and compassion in all our decisions.

6	10. NEW BUSINESS
6	Councillor Wright wanted to give a shoutout to all the City Staff who made Winterloo this
6	past weekend. It was great fun, and awesome to be skating on the public square. She
6	managed to get a video of the Olympia Ice Resurfacer in action. It was accompanied by
6	Uptown Waterloo Comfort Food Crawl, which was supported by a number of local
6	restaurants and bars.
6	Councillor Vasic participated in a webinar run by the Canadian Centre for Diversity and
6	Inclusion about creating Neuro-Inclusive and Accessible Workplaces. She wanted to
6	highlight some points that are worth considering as the City moves forward in
6	accessibility work. She mentioned that 20% of workers are neurodivergent, and that
6	many neurodivergent people mask, which means they hide traits related to their
7	neurodivergence. The workshop highlighted that differences are not deficits, and that
7	neurodivergent professionals in some roles can be 30% more productive than those
7	who are not neurodivergent. Lastly, they highlighted the difference between
7	accommodations and accessibility. With accommodations, the disability is seen as a
7	challenge and the onus is on individuals to ask for accommodations as needed.
7	Accessibility is a proactive process that looks at Universal Design and sees difference
7	as an opportunity.
7	Councillor Bodaly mentioned that the Laurelwood rink team used neighbourhood mini
7	grants on Sunday to host a skating party at Laurelwood park. There was pizza, donuts,
7	and coffee, as well as neighbours gathering together. He gave a shoutout to rinks’
7	teams across the city for the work that they do, and it’s a good year for creating
7	neighbourhood rinks.
7	Councillor Freeman offered some community updates. She pointed out how fortunate
7	we are as a community to have Chicopee Ski Hill. It’s great to see families out there and
7	kids learning to ski. On the military front, both the Elgins and the Royal Highland
7	Fusiliers are actively training on winter operations to be able to support the Canadian
7	Army. Many soldiers from the community left on January 1st on active deployment,
7	heading to various operations worldwide.
7	Councillor Roach talked about Jason Kipfer starting the Eastbridge Business
7	Connections Group seven years ago, which blossomed and started other Business
7	Connections Groups in the City and Region. There is an anniversary party tonight at
7	Morty’s, with over 100 people from businesses in Waterloo planning to attend.
7	Mayor McCabe attended the Waterloo Siskins 90th anniversary alumni game on the
7	weekend. There were memorabilia from across the decades, including pamphlets and
7	programs from every year. They are the winningest Junior B team in Ontario history.
7	Additionally, she and Councillor Roach are hosting Community Conversations on
7	February 8 at 10:00 a.m. at the Albert McCormick Community Centre.
