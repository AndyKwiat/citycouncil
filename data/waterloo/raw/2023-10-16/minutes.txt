2	Councillor Freeman provided Council with moments of reflection.
2	a) This month is an opportunity to celebrate and recognize Islamic Heritage,
2	specifically the contributions of Muslims in our community and across
2	Canada.
2	Sharing the stories of individual and community achievement helps to
2	ensure that members of the Muslim community see themselves
2	represented in the fabric of our community. It is also an important way to
2	interrupt harmful ideas that reinforce stereotypes and beliefs that lead to
2	exclusion, discrimination and violence.
2	No one should be targeted because of who they identify as or appear to
2	be.
3	As a Council, we recommit ourselves to confronting Islamophobia and
3	hate in all forms. This work is not easy and it requires a long-term,
3	continual effort. Working towards a community where all people,
3	regardless of their identity, feel safe and included is worth the effort.
3	b) We invite everyone to join in a moment of silence to acknowledge and
3	grieve the loss of innocent lives from the recent escalation of violence in
3	the Middle East. This is a very challenging time, and there are many in the
3	City of Waterloo, our friends and neighbours, who are feeling the impacts
3	of this humanitarian crisis and who need our support. May we all find ways
3	to express that support through empathy, understanding and a community-
3	wide desire for an end to conflict. We reaffirm our commitment to standing
3	against all instances of violence and hate. Shalom. Salaam. Peace.

8	10. NOTICE OF MOTION
8	a) Intimate Partner Violence Motion – Councillor Roe
8	Jennifer Hutton, Amy Hachborn, Christine Taylor, and Dejana Bjelogrlic of The Family
8	Violence Project gave a presentation on the background of their organization and
8	Intimate Partner Violence in the City. They then responded to questions of Council.
8	Moved by Councillor Roe, Seconded by Councillor Hanmer:
8	WHEREAS intimate partner violence, often referred to as domestic
8	violence, means any form of harm caused by a current or former intimate
8	partner or spouse in public or private spaces, as well as online, including
8	actual or threatened physical or sexual force, emotional and/ or
8	psychological abuse or harassing behaviour. Persons of any gender or sex
8	can be victims of intimate partner violence;
8	AND WHEREAS women are at greater risk of experiencing domestic
8	violence including severe outcomes and femicide;
8	AND WHEREAS on June 28, 2022, the jury in a Coroner's Inquest held on
8	the deaths of Carol Culleton, Anastasia Kuzyk and Nathalie Warmerdam,
8	who were murdered by a former intimate partner, issued 86
8	recommendations to the Province of Ontario aimed at acknowledging and
8	combatting increasing levels of Intimate Partner Violence;
8	AND WHEREAS every six days in Canada, a woman is killed by her
8	intimate partner;
8	AND WHEREAS in Ontario, 30 women were killed in a 30-week window
8	between 2022 and 2023;
8	AND WHEREAS intimate partner violence has been escalating in the City
8	of Waterloo over the last decade where Waterloo Region Police Service
8	have responded to 10,134 calls, or an average of 1013 calls per year,
9	laying 5102 charges involving intimate partner violence, or an average of
9	510 per year, an increase of 109 percent;
9	AND WHEREAS racialized women and Indigenous women experience
9	disproportionately high rates of intimate partner violence;
9	AND WHEREAS Indigenous women are approximately 3.5 times more
9	likely to experience some form of intimate partner violence than non-
9	Indigenous women and the femicide rate for Indigenous women and girls
9	is approximately six times higher than for non-Indigenous women and
9	girls, and Indigenous women are 12 times more likely to be murdered or
9	missing than any other women in Canada and 16 times more likely than
9	white women;
9	AND WHEREAS 30 municipalities across Ontario have declared Intimate
9	Partner Violence an epidemic;
9	AND WHEREAS prevention and intervention strategies have been shown
9	to help disrupt the cycle of intimate partner violence and protect victims
9	from its effects;
9	AND WHEREAS the City of Waterloo's Strategic Plan supports Intimate
9	Partner Violence awareness as a stepping stone to shared wellbeing
9	through the World Health Organization's Sustainable Development Goals,
9	including:
9	• Good Health and Wellbeing
9	• Gender Equality
9	• Reduced Inequalities;
9	NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED:
9	THAT the City of Waterloo declares that intimate partner violence is an
9	epidemic for all persons regardless of gender or sex, AND
9	THAT the Province of Ontario be requested to declare intimate partner
9	violence an epidemic; AND
9	THAT the Province of Ontario provide additional funding aimed at
9	preventing intimate partner violence; AND
9	THAT the City of Waterloo continues to support the Region of Waterloo's
9	Community Safety and Well-being Plan, AND
9	THAT this resolution will be circulated to Doug Ford, Premier of Ontario,
9	Charmaine A. Williams, Associate Minister of Women's Social and
10	Economic Opportunity of Ontario, Marci Ien, Minister for Women and
10	Gender Equality and Youth, local area MP's and MPP's, and all local
10	municipalities.
10	Carried Unanimously
10	b) Affordable Housing Commitment Motion – Councillor Bodaly
10	Motion to be tabled October 16, 2023 for Council consideration October
10	30, 2023.
10	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo population increased by 15.7% between
10	2016 and 2021, faster than the provincial average of 5.8% and the national
10	average of 5.2% during that same time period; and
10	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo’s population is forecasted to grow by 45%
10	to 185,000 people by 2051; and
10	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo has committed to supporting the creation
10	of 16,000 new housing units by 2031 as its Housing Pledge to the
10	Province; and
10	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo approved an Affordable Housing Strategy
10	in June 2023 with 81 actions, that, among other things, recommended
10	ways to increase housing supply and the diversity of housing options, with
10	an emphasis on areas near transit and transportation corridors.
10	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo currently permits up to three residential
10	units per property as-of-right on a parcel of urban residential land; and
10	WHEREAS the Government of Canada’s Housing Accelerator Fund (HAF)
10	grant program represents an opportunity for the City to review and
10	accelerate actions to achieve more housing supply faster; and
10	THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED:
10	1. That staff be directed to initiate an amendment to Zoning By-law
10	2018-050 to permit, as-of-right, up to four residential units and four
10	storeys on residential parcels within low density residential zones,
10	where appropriate (the “Amendment”) and report back to Council in
10	Q1 2024;
10	2. As part of the Amendment, that staff be directed to identify
10	appropriate zoning regulations and development criteria, considering
10	matters such as safe access and servicing capacity, for Council’s
10	consideration; and,
11	3. That Council confirm its support for the principle of intensification in
11	transit supportive areas and near post-secondary institutions, and
11	directs staff to identify opportunities to achieve the following planning
11	objectives through the Official Plan Review, as recommended in the
11	City’s Affordable Housing Strategy:
11	a. higher density housing options in areas supported by transit
11	and near post secondary institutions; and
11	b. increase the supply of missing middle housing, evaluating
11	opportunities for new missing middle corridors, modifications
11	to the existing nodes and corridors strategy using a missing
11	middle lens, and consideration of minimum height and density
11	targets in specified areas to facilitate missing middle housing;
11	4. That Council reaffirm its commitment to our Affordable Housing
11	Strategy, including the City of Waterloo’s goals to prioritize non-
11	market housing, optimize existing housing, increase the supply of
11	affordable housing, providing incentives to build new affordable
11	housing, and enable a greater supply and mix of housing within our
11	community.
11	5. That Council reaffirm its housing pledge of achieving the goal of
11	16,000 new homes by 2031, in partnership with the development
11	industry.
11	6. That this motion be forwarded to the Hon. Sean Fraser, Minister of
11	Housing, Infrastructure and Communities of Canada for
11	consideration as part of our Housing Accelerator Fund (HAF) grant
11	application.
