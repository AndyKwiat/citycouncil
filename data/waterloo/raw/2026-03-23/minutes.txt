2	3. TERRITORIAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
2	Councillor Freeman opened the meeting with the following Territorial Acknowledgement:
2	I would like to acknowledge that I was raised on the lands traditionally cared for by the
2	Haudenosaunee, Lanape, and Anishinaabek peoples, covered by the Upper Canada
2	Treaties. I was thankful to be accepted into Engineering at the University of Waterloo
2	and move to the City where I have chosen to live and raise my family. These are the
2	lands traditionally cared for by the Neutral, Anishinaabeg, and Haudenosaunee peoples,
2	specifically within the Haldimand Tract, which is land granted to the Six Nations of the
2	Grand River.
2	Earlier this month we celebrated International Women’s Day. As many of you know,
2	engineering has shaped my life in many ways and is an amazing and meaningful career
2	for women. As identified by Engineers Canada, Indigenous women are severely
2	underrepresented in Canadian engineering, comprising only a small fraction of the
2	profession. While initiatives exist to increase diversity, Indigenous women engineers
2	face barriers including systemic discrimination, isolation, and underrepresentation.
2	Organizations like Engineers Canada and dedicated, trail-blazing professionals are
2	working to improve access and representation.
2	In my land acknowledgement today, I would like to acknowledge the contribution to the
2	engineering profession by the Indigenous community.
2	Mary Golda Ross was a Cherokee and American aerospace engineer. She was the first
2	known Indigenous female engineer. She worked at Lockheed Corporation from 1942
2	until her retirement in 1973. She was one of the 40 founding engineers of the renowned
2	and highly secretive Skunk Works project.
2	In 2018, Chelsea Banally became the first Indigenous woman to earn a PhD in
2	engineering at the University of Alberta. Dr. Banally works on ways to treat tailing water,
2	a toxic byproduct in the process of extracting bitumen from oil sands.
3	As a woman engineer, I would like to acknowledge the contributions made to the
3	engineering profession from Indigenous engineering trailblazers like Mary Golda Ross
3	and Dr. Chelsea Banally.
