8	a) Mayor Dorothy McCabe – Resolution Regarding Legislative Changes
8	to the OMERS Act in Bill 68
8	Motion tabled February 9, 2026 for Council consideration February 9, 2026.
8	Moved by Mayor McCabe, Seconded by Councillor Hanmer:
8	WHEREAS the Ontario Municipal Employees Retirement System
8	(OMERS) Pension Fund serves over 1,000 employers and over half a
8	million employees and retirees from diverse groups including: municipal
8	governments, school boards, libraries, police and fire departments,
8	children’s aid societies, and electricity distribution companies; and,
8	WHEREAS the long-standing jointly-sponsored governance model with
8	two corporate boards has provided stability, accountability, and fairness for
8	both plan members and employers for more than two decades; and,
8	WHEREAS the Government of Ontario has passed legislative changes to
8	OMERS’ governance structure through Bill 68; and,
8	WHEREAS these changes would replace the current OMERS Sponsors
8	Corporation with a new Sponsors Council that would lose its corporate
8	status and independent resources; and,
8	WHEREAS the proposed model could allow pension decisions affecting
8	municipal employers and employees to be made without meaningful
8	municipal oversight, increasing financial risk for municipalities and local
8	taxpayers; and,
8	WHEREAS municipalities are already under significant financial strain and
8	cannot absorb additional pension costs without consequences for property
8	taxes or local services;
8	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED:
8	THAT the City of Waterloo does not support the legislative changes to the
8	OMERS Act contained in Bill 68 and requests that the Government of
8	Ontario reconsider the advisability of proceeding with these changes; and,
9	THAT the City of Waterloo Council supports the Association of
9	Municipalities of Ontario (AMO) in calling on the Government of Ontario to
9	maintain the current OMERS governance model, with two corporate
9	Boards, as the structure that would best deliver on the long-term interests
9	of municipalities, taxpayers, and employees;
9	AND THAT a copy of this motion be forwarded to The Honourable Doug
9	Ford, Premier of Ontario; The Honourable Rob Flack, Minister of Housing
9	and Municipal Affairs; The Honourable Peter Bethlenfalvy, Minister of
9	Finance; local and area MPPs; the Association of Municipalities of Ontario
9	(AMO); the Association of Municipal Managers, Clerks and Treasurers of
9	Ontario (AMCTO); and to the Region of Waterloo and local area
9	municipalities.
9	Carried Unanimously
9	Councillor Roe left the Chair. (Time: 8:12 p.m.)
9	Mayor McCabe in the Chair. (Time: 8:12 p.m.)
9	b) Mayor Dorothy McCabe – Opposition to the Consolidation of
9	Ontario’s Conservation Authorities
9	Motion tabled February 9, 2026 for Council consideration February 23, 2026.
9	Motion:
9	WHEREAS the Conservation Authorities Act, 1990 (the “Act”), originally
9	enacted in 1946, was established to allow municipalities to form
9	conservation authorities equipped to deliver local, watershed-based
9	conservation, restoration, and natural resource management programs on
9	behalf of both the province and municipalities; and,
9	WHEREAS Ontario’s thirty-six (36) conservation authorities are each
9	distinct, reflecting the unique environmental, geographic, and community
9	needs of their respective watersheds; and,
9	WHEREAS municipalities have long relied on their conservation
9	authorities to tailor programs to local watershed priorities while ensuring
9	fair and transparent costs for ratepayers; and,
9	WHEREAS municipalities are best positioned to understand the needs of
9	their local communities and environments, and the existing watershed-
9	based structure enables effective, collaborative approaches to water
9	conservation and resource management that cannot be replicated through
9	centralized oversight; and,
10	WHEREAS Bill 68 (Schedule 3) and ERO Posting 025-1257 propose
10	consolidating Ontario’s 36 conservation authorities into seven regional
10	authorities and creating the Ontario Provincial Conservation Agency, a
10	Crown agency that would assume oversight and possess the authority to
10	levy conservation authorities for its costs; and,
10	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED:
10	THAT the City of Waterloo formally state that it opposes the consolidation
10	of Ontario’s conservation authorities without knowing the full financial and
10	operational impact to municipalities and the conservation authorities; and,
10	THAT the City of Waterloo require that the province meaningfully consult
10	with municipalities prior to advancing any changes that could affect local
10	control and management of the floodplain areas watersheds and natural
10	resources which Conservation Authorities were established to manage;
10	AND THAT a copy of this motion be forwarded to The Honourable Doug
10	Ford, Premier of Ontario; The Honourable Todd McCarthy, Minister of
10	Environment, Conservation and Parks; local and area MPPs; the Grand
10	River Conservation Authority (GRCA); the Association of Municipalities of
10	Ontario (AMO); the Association of Municipal Managers, Clerks and
10	Treasurers of Ontario (AMCTO); and to the Region of Waterloo and local
10	area municipalities.
