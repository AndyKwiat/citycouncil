1	2. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
1	Mayor McCabe provided Council with moments of reflection.
1	a) Today, March 20, is Nowruz! Also known as Persian New Year, this is an
1	annual celebration that marks the arrival of spring, rebirth of nature and a
1	new year. Nowruz means a "new day" for more than 300 million people
1	around the world with origins in the Middle East, Central Asia, Bahá’í and
1	Ismaili communities.
1	This day is a reminder for us all to consider the culture of peace behind
1	this celebration through everyday dialogue and solidarity. It is an
1	opportunity to reaffirm our commitment to human rights and dignity,
2	respect and reconciliation, and live with consciousness of nature by
2	upholding environmental protections.
2	There are many ways that observers celebrate Nowruz, some complete a
2	19 day fast from sunrise to sunset with a focus on daily prayers and
2	reflections; and for some the day is a non-religious celebration that is a
2	time to celebrate new beginnings. As family, friends and neighbours
2	gather, dress up and celebrate with prayers, food, dancing and music we
2	recognize the rich cultural heritage and diversity.
2	The hope for a more peaceful, sustainable world and compassion for more
2	inclusivity among all humans are values also shared by Ramadan
2	traditions.
2	b) Today, we also take this time acknowledge and honour the members of
2	our community who are preparing to observe the auspicious month of
2	Ramadan. Beginning this Wednesday, March 22, Muslims around the
2	world will observe this month-long time of reflection, contemplation, and
2	celebration.
2	Ramadan is one of the holiest months of the year for those who follow the
2	Islamic Faith; it is a special time of spiritual connection for Muslims through
2	prayer and fasting from dawn to dusk. Many attend mosque services and
2	there is a great deal of emphasis on charity and outreach during this time.
2	It is also a time for families to come together to share a pre-dawn meal
2	and the evening meal together.
2	We join together in support of our Muslim neighbours by being mindful of
2	the needs of those who are fasting and spending additional time in prayer.
2	May this month of Ramadan offer many opportunities for reflection,
2	showing gratitude and time spent with loved ones.

9	9. WORKSHOP
9	a) Title: 2023-2026 Strategic Plan – Council
9	Workshop #3
9	Report No.: CAO2023-014
9	Prepared By: Sandy Little
9	Sandy Little gave a brief introduction to the Strategic Planning Workshop, and the
9	overall Strategic Planning process.
9	Paul Bien, Partner, Deloitte discussed the plan for the session and helped facilitate the
9	workshop.
9	Himay Antani, Manager, Deloitte facilitated the workshop and gave a presentation on
9	what Council has discussed in the previous workshops, the difference between
9	“mission” and “vision”, and some examples of vision statements. He also responded to
9	questions of Council.
9	Paul Bien called out a few of the themes Council had put on the board, which Mayor
9	McCabe spoke to. She spoke about the importance of climate proofing and learning to
9	live with the current climate, as well as trying to be more sustainable. She mentioned
9	that it is important to have a sense of community and have Waterloo be a fun and
9	creative place to live for her kids and other people. Mayor McCabe also spoke about
9	how to be leaders and that being aspiring leaders means sometimes taking risks.
9	Himay Antani asked Council to come up with their own vision statements. He read out
9	some of the key themes that showed up in different Council visions, including
9	community, being climate-ready, and inclusivity. Council anonymously selected vision
9	statements that resonated with them.
9	Councillor Roe spoke about the statements that stood out to her, being statements that
9	used active words. “Actively creating an equitable and sustainable future,” and “building
9	a vibrant community” jumped out at her because they’re ideas they can work toward.
9	Councillor Wright echoed some of Councillor Roe’s comments around the use of active
9	language. She mentioned “embracing change,” and “learning and discovery,” as themes
9	with momentum.
10	Councillor Hanmer also liked the use of active language, and mentioned that it should
10	be a 10-second elevator speech that is easy to remember. She liked visions that
10	mentioned inclusion, to remember that they’re talking about the community as a whole.
10	Councillor Vasic spoke about how she doesn’t like the word “innovative”, but rather
10	themes of leadership, risk taking, and curiosity that underpin innovation.
10	Himay Antani explained Guiding Principles, and displayed the City’s current Guiding
10	Principles. He then led Council through creating new Guiding Principles.
10	Councillor Wright spoke about being climate ready and the news out of the UN today
10	about how climate targets need to be moved up by 10 years. To do that, Waterloo
10	needs to be bold and ambitious, because business as usual is not going to work.
10	Councillor Bodaly wanted to highlight his point of economic justice, being fiscally
10	responsible while still creating livability for everybody in the community.
10	Councillor Vasic wanted to highlight the principle of solidarity, meaning to be with
10	people wherever they’re coming from. This can be applied to social justice movements
10	and climate action, but as a councillor, everyone wants you to understand where they’re
10	coming from. What does it mean to be with people when you’re trying to serve them?
10	Paul Bien called attention to the theme of being inter-generational, which Councillor
10	Vasic spoke to. She explained the theme in terms of people living inter-generationally,
10	being able to age in place, how different generations connect and how we’re connected
10	to future generations.
10	Paul Bien also brought up the theme of being everyday leaders, prompting Councillor
10	Hanmer to speak to it. She believes each and every person is a leader in their own
10	right, and the theme encompasses everything from empowering support workers,
10	volunteers, and everyone up to the CAO to do certain things. It’s not by title that people
10	are leaders, but that people take the initiative and show imagination.
10	Paul Bien lastly brought up the theme of responsiveness and being democratically
10	engaged, which Councillor Wright spoke to. She said it’s about transparency and
10	accountability, and the responsibility to the community members to get things right. Part
10	of that is doing democratic engagement to the fullest extent at the municipal level.
10	Councillor Wright also spoke about being prudent, and working smarter not harder.
10	When you have big constraints, you need to be strategic.
10	Mayor McCabe also spoke about prudence, and the need to be bold and ambitious,
10	while keeping in mind the community at large.
10	Sandy Little discussed the next steps for Strategic Planning, which include commencing
10	staff engagement, and finishing up engagement with the community. April 1st is the free
10	public skate at RIM Park with a focus on youth engagement, while the scan report up to
11	the end of February will be presented to Council on April 3rd. The draft Strategic Plan
11	will be delivered on May 8th, with the final Strategic Plan being delivered on June 26th.
11	She answered questions of Council.
