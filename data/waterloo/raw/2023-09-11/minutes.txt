8	14. NOTICE OF MOTION
8	Guaranteed Livable Basic Income Motion – Councillor Vasic
8	Motion to be tabled September 11, 2023 for Council consideration September 18,
8	2023.
8	WHEREAS a Guaranteed Livable Basic Income (GLBI) has the potential
8	to prevent people from falling into poverty by providing an income-tested
8	income top-up for all who need it regardless of employment status;
8	AND WHEREAS the 2021 census data demonstrated an income
8	inequality challenge in the City of Waterloo with a higher percentage of
8	individuals making more than $100,000 per year (15.1%) and a higher
8	portion of individuals making less than $30,000 per year (37.3%) as
9	compared to the national average (10.8% and 36% respectively) and the
9	Region of Waterloo (11.3% and 34.8% respectively);
9	AND WHEREAS the Government of Canada’s report Building
9	Understanding: The first report of the national advisory council on poverty
9	from 2020 used 2016 census data to highlight how poverty
9	disproportionately impacts racialized individuals at twice the rate as
9	compared to non-racialized individuals in Canada, at 20.6% and 10.6%
9	respectively.
9	AND WHEREAS the pandemic, rising cost of living, and surges in food
9	and housing prices has worsened income inequality and created scenarios
9	where more people find it difficult to meet their basic needs;
9	AND WHEREAS social assistance recipients are living in extreme poverty
9	and food insecurity and are at great risk to develop associated health
9	issues;
9	AND WHEREAS Ontario Works (OW) and Ontario Disability Support
9	Program (ODSP) rates are below Canada’s Official Poverty Line and since
9	social assistance rates were reduced by 21.6% in 1995, rate increases
9	have been nominal, representing an approximate 10% increase over 26
9	years;
9	AND WHEREAS Ontario Works rates have not been increased since 2018
9	and are not indexed to inflation causing further income inequality, poverty,
9	and housing unaffordability;
9	AND WHEREAS there would be costs of implementing a GLBI, its
9	provision would save indirect costs we continue to experience by helping
9	alleviate the mounting financial and social pressures municipalities face
9	and would yield significant benefits, including reducing homelessness,
9	aiding the affordability of housing, reducing petty crime, improving mental
9	health, supporting efforts to effectively treat addictions, supporting
9	neighbourhood cohesion, and boosting local and regional economic
9	development;
9	AND WHEREAS the Province of Ontario undertook a Basic Income Pilot
9	program over 15 months starting in 2018 to test the view that a basic
9	income could provide a way to reduce poverty in a sustainable way;
9	AND WHEREAS that pilot project ceased earlier than intended and
9	without a full government report, published research out of McMaster
10	University indicated that pilot participants involved in the study reported
10	improvements in the areas of physical and mental health, access to health
10	services, enhanced food security, improved housing conditions, and
10	financial and social well-being:
10	AND WHEREAS in lieu of a provincial or federal anti-poverty strategy, the
10	City of Waterloo has developed programs to alleviate the impacts of
10	poverty and income inequality, and to make it possible for constituents to
10	participate in programs that provide a better quality of life and which can
10	positively impact mental and physical well-being, programs such as:
10	• Fee assistance services for participation in recreation, leisure, arts
10	and culture programs and fee assistance for groups for in-kind
10	services or rental discounts/rebates
10	• Implementation of a living wage strategy and living wage champion
10	designation for city employees and contractors
10	• Neighbourhood Matching Grants to support neighbourhood focused
10	initiatives
10	• Community Cash Grants and other corporate grants that support
10	programs and services aimed at providing cultural, sport and
10	community activities
10	• Participation in tax deferral and rebate programs
10	• Sidewalk winter control strategy that includes assisted supports and
10	priority clearing zones
10	• Providing funding and resources for programs and services
10	supporting the unhoused, and supporting The Food Bank and other
10	food distribution networks
10	• Support for shelter operations in Waterloo such as the House of
10	Friendship ShelterCare model and The Working Centre
10	• Affordable Housing Grant Program and Strategy to help address the
10	fast-growing problem of affordable housing availability
10	• Housing Pledge to help meet the provincial goal of building 1.5
10	million homes in Ontario in 10 years;
10	AND WHEREAS all of these programs and services and the myriad
10	additional programs and services offered by the regional government, and
10	community-based services and programs such as those that offer food,
10	housing, addiction, and neighbourhood support services, amount to a
10	federally and provincially downloaded responsibility to respond to and treat
10	needs created by the lack of a GLBI;
11	AND WHEREAS addressing a GLBI has been shown to reduce the need
11	for individuals to apply for multiple support programs in order to maintain a
11	healthy and full quality of life;
11	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that:
11	1) the City of Waterloo supports, in principle, advocates such as Basic
11	Income Waterloo Region who are pushing for the implementation of
11	a GLBI;
11	2) the City requests the Government of Canada and the Province of
11	Ontario establish an income-tested GLBI to combat poverty,
11	income inequality, and economic insecurity within our
11	community,
11	3) the City asks that as the GLBI is phased-in over time that existing
11	health and social service supports continue to operate and be
11	funded in order to firstly provide continuity to support recipients and
11	secondly so that long-term data can be collected to investigate the
11	effectiveness of the GLBI independent of the removal of any other
11	programs,
11	4) Until these programs are found to be redundant and a GLBI is fully
11	adopted should it demonstrate continued success, the City of
11	Waterloo asks the provincial government to increase Social
11	Assistance rates for both OW and ODSP to reflect the costs of living
11	and tie these rates to inflation, and
11	5) a copy of this resolution be sent to the Prime Minister of Canada,
11	the Premier of Ontario, local and area MP’s, MPP’s, local and area
11	councils, the Federation of Canadian Municipalities, and the
11	Association of Municipalities of Ontario for further consideration.

11	Mayor McCabe made a statement about the Strong Mayor legislation and her stance on
11	it. She formally spoke about her desire to not use these powers, and how authority was
11	delegated back to the appropriate parties in July. She further explained that she doesn’t
11	believe these powers support or advance democracy, and that, in Waterloo, the powers
11	aren’t needed to further the building of homes.
11	Councillor Vasic had a few events she wanted to draw attention to. She attended the
11	Canadian Clay and Glass Gallery on Saturday, where they celebrated their 30th
11	anniversary with some daytime activities and a gala. The Button Factory is having their
11	annual tulip fundraiser, with 24 bulbs for $15. BookFest was in Uptown Waterloo on the
11	weekend, and it was very well attended. As well, KW Famous hosted ArtHop all across
11	Kitchener and Waterloo. Ward 5 resident Betty Anne Keller also had an arts sale at her
11	house with a friend that makes jewelry.
12	Councillor Roe followed up with more information about the Canadian Clay and Glass
12	Gallery’s daytime activities, which included craft time and was great for families. It was
12	also a great summer in Ward 6 with the Beechwood Produce Stand every Wednesday.

12	local charities. Through their Summer Camp program they developed permanent art for
