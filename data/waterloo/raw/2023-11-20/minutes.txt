2	4. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
2	Councillor Freeman provided Council with a moment of reflection.
2	At the beginning of this Council meeting, we pause to think about the needs of our
2	community. May we show wisdom and compassion in all our decisions.
2	Today, we pause to remember individuals and communities affected by gender-based
2	violence. The emergence of three distinct observances to collectively grieve and call for
2	change is a critical reminder of the increasing prevalence of this violence.
2	Transgender Day of Remembrance is observed each year on November 20th to
2	acknowledge the people who have died as a result of acts of anti-trans violence. The
2	National Day of Remembrance and Action on Violence Against Women honours the
2	memory of the 14 young women murdered at the Ecole Polytechnique Montréal on
2	December 6, 1989. Linking these two days are the United Nations’ 16 Days of Activism
2	Against Gender-based Violence, from November 25th to December 10.
2	The threats of violence feel particularly close and prevalent this year:
2	• Incidents of femicide are increasing in Ontario at a rate of the death of one
2	woman per week.
2	• The knife attack at the University of Waterloo brought into vivid focus the local
2	hatred felt toward those who challenge traditional gender norms.
2	• The racist aggression towards a Muslim woman in a local Drive Test office
2	surrounded by countless bystanders.
2	• The regularity of demonstrations that denounce the rights of members of the
2	2SLGBTQ+ community.
2	• The backlash against demands to search a Winnipeg landfill for the remains of
2	three murdered women is one of many tragic examples of our society’s collective
2	indifference to the vulnerability of Indigenous women, girls and two-spirit people
2	in this country.
2	There are glimmers of hope in the midst of these threats to life and safety:
2	• More than 45 Ontario municipalities, including Waterloo, have declared intimate
2	partner violence an epidemic.
2	• Communities across the country have rallied in support of 2SLGBTQ+ youth.
3	• Local organizations are working hard to provide places where women, girls, two-
3	spirit, trans and gender-diverse people can find places of refuge when needed,
3	as well as safe spaces in which they can work, study and play.
3	We know we must do more. Addressing the harm caused by centuries of patriarchy and
3	colonialism will require collective systemic change – to improve our responses to
3	violence and how our institutions operate as well as an unlearning of biases and long-
3	held beliefs.
3	As a City, we re-commit ourselves to taking concrete steps toward stamping out gender-
3	based violence in all its forms. We further commit to acting and speaking with boldness
3	and conviction to create a safer community for women, girls, two-spirit, trans and
3	gender-diverse people.
