2	4. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
2	Mayor McCabe provided Council with a moment of reflection.
2	Today, we pause to reflect on the ongoing harmful impacts of gender-based
2	violence in our own community and around the world.
2	We especially remember trans people all over the world whose lives have been
2	lost due to anti-trans violence. November 20 was Transgender Day of
2	Remembrance – a day of mourning and solidarity with trans people in our
2	community whose lives are deeply affected by widespread violence. The recent
2	Club Q murders in Colorado were a stark reminder of deadly violence affecting
2	far too many in 2SLGBTQ+ communities.
2	The 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence, observed worldwide
2	from November 25 to December 10, is time dedicated to changing this reality.
2	Violence happens on a continuum, therefore our responses must reflect this –
2	we can all take action. As a council and as a community, we must respect the
2	right for self-determination. Upholding welcoming work places and community
2	spaces – where we use peoples’ preferred pronouns, and support people in
2	using any washroom of their choice are two simple ways we can actualize
2	support. We must commit to including trans women, trans men, non-binary, Two
2	Spirit people, gender-fluid, gender non-conforming and gender-diverse folks in
2	conversations about women’s rights, feminism and gender equality. Ideologies
2	that refuse to recognize trans women are harmful and further oppress trans and
2	gender diverse people.
2	Let’s use this time to re-commit ourselves to a world that challenges traditionally
2	rigid gender norms so that trans and gender diverse people can live
2	authentically and with safety that everyone deserves.
2	Mayor McCabe read a second Council Statement in recognition and remembrance of
2	the National Day of Remembrance and Action on Violence Against Women.
3	During the 16 Days of Activism, Canadians reflect on the presence of gender
3	based violence in our own country. Tomorrow, on December 6, many in our
3	community and across Canada will pause to remember the 14 young women
3	murdered at Polytechnique Montreal and all women who have suffered gender-
3	based violence.
3	These days of remembrance and activism push us to consider the links the
3	between sexism, transphobia and homophobia, and the greater risks faced by
3	women, trans people, non-binary people and gender-diverse people. The
3	additional impact of racism and colonialism put Indigenous women, girls and
3	Two Spirit people at even greater risk, similarly the impact of ableism on women
3	with disabilities.
3	As a City, we recognize the importance of taking action on the devastating
3	impacts of gender-based violence. We are also mindful that challenging sexist
3	and transphobic systems must not be limited to a few observances throughout
3	the year but an ongoing action.
3	I would now invite Councillor Freeman to make a few personal remarks
3	regarding this day.
3	Councillor Freeman also shared personal remarks about the National Day of
3	Remembrance and Action on Violence Against Women.
3	December 6th is a really profound date for me as a woman engineer. I was born
3	in 1967 and I would just like to name the women who were gunned down and
3	their ages and the academic pursuits that they were following:
3	• Geneviève Bergeron, born 1968, civil engineering student
3	• Hélène Colgan, born 1966, mechanical engineering student
3	• Nathalie Croteau, born 1966, mechanical engineering student
3	• Barbara Daigneault, born 1967, mechanical engineering student
3	• Anne-Marie Edward, born 1968, chemical engineering student
3	• Maud Haviernick, born 1960, materials engineering student
3	• Maryse Laganière, born 1964, budget clerk in the École Polytechnique's
3	finance department
3	• Maryse Leclair, born 1966, materials engineering student
3	• Anne-Marie Lemay, born 1967, mechanical engineering student
3	• Sonia Pelletier, born 1961, mechanical engineering student
3	• Michèle Richard, born 1968, materials engineering student
3	• Annie St-Arneault, born 1966, mechanical engineering student
3	• Annie Turcotte, born 1969, materials engineering student
3	• Barbara Klucznik-Widajewicz, born 1958, nursing student
4	On December 6th, I was working on a construction site as a Civil Engineering
4	student, the same age as these women, and it was a very difficult day. I hadn’t
4	heard the news. The phone in the construction trailer where I was working rang
4	incessantly all day long with people asking if I was okay, because there was
4	worry, more broadly, for women engineering students across the country that
4	there could be others who would feel similarly compelled as Mr. Lépine in his
4	desire to remove women from non-traditional careers.
4	I think that one of the things about this day too is it profoundly affected women
4	choosing to enter careers in Mathematics, Sciences, and Engineering, because
4	they felt that this could happen again. Engineering had been on a steady
4	increase with women entering the profession in undergraduates and was near
4	22%, and after this shooting it dropped back to almost 10%. Here we are all
4	these many years later and we’re only at 20%. So the recovery of women
4	choosing careers in engineering has been profoundly impacted in the events of
4	this day. And that’s just one of many professions where women strive to work in
4	non-traditional careers.
4	This day, December 6th, is one of those days where we should pause and
4	reflect about that struggle and how we can help to make a difference.

8	10. QUESTIONS
8	Councillor Bodaly asked a question about the financial implications of Bill 23 as part of
8	the next budget process, specifically about the lost revenue from Development
8	Charges. Tim Anderson responded to Councillor Bodaly, noting that Bill 23 is really
8	impactful to municipalities across Ontario, and that the City did submit comments to the
8	province by the November 24th deadline. He noted that the legislation was approved,
8	and that the City needs to adapt going forward, commenting that a report would be
8	coming out next week detailing that adaptation plan. Filipa Reynolds also responded,
8	noting the loss of revenue from Development Charges range from $23-$31 million
8	dollars over the first five years, and that it doesn’t take into account operating impacts.
8	The capital dollars are to build infrastructure. They have paused the work on the DC
8	Background Study and by-law.
8	Councillor Freeman also asked about the reserve funding, and the timing of getting a
8	message to the province before the provincial government goes on break. Mayor
9	McCabe offered some background on what the City is doing, as well as the Ontario Big
9	City Mayors. Tim Anderson responded regarding the reserve fund report coming next
9	week.
