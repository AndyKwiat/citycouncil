2	3. TERRITORIAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
2	Councillor Freeman opened the meeting with the following Territorial Acknowledgement:
2	As we recognize the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation on Wednesday,
2	September 30, I acknowledge the Indigenous Peoples who have cared for these lands
2	since time immemorial. I honour the Survivors of residential schools, the children who
2	never returned home, and their families and communities. I recognize that reconciliation
2	requires more than words; it calls for listening, learning, reflection, and meaningful
2	action. I remain on a learning journey and am committed to deepening my
2	understanding, confronting the ongoing impacts of colonialism, and carrying what I learn
2	into my actions and relationships.
2	My own connection to the land has been shaped by living in Woodstock, Kitchener, and
2	now Waterloo. I recognize Woodstock as being on the traditional territories of the
2	Haudenosaunee, Lenape, and Anishinaabeg and within lands covered by the Upper
2	Canada Treaties. I also recognize Kitchener and Waterloo as being on the Haldimand
2	Tract, within the traditional territory of the Chonnonton, Anishinaabeg, and
2	Haudenosaunee Peoples. As I reflect on the places I have called home, I am grateful for
2	the land and waters that have sustained me, and I acknowledge my responsibility to
2	learn about their histories, honour the enduring presence and contributions of
2	Indigenous Peoples, and deepen my relationship with the land through respectful
2	action.
2	We encourage everyone to take time to deepen their understanding of the residential
2	school system and its lasting impacts. Reconciliation is not a single day or action. It is
2	an ongoing commitment to building respectful relationships, advancing understanding
2	and taking meaningful action. There are a number of events and activities that will take
2	place in our community, and we encourage everyone to find the time to participate in
2	some way. City services will remain open, but staff will spend time reflecting and
2	learning.
2	As a City, we reaffirm our commitment to Reconciliation work, and to supporting a
2	community where future generations can thrive.
2	4. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
3	Councillor Freeman provided Council with a moment of reflection.
3	The City of Waterloo extends its condolences to the family, friends and many
3	colleagues of Dr. Ron Schlegel, whose remarkable contributions as an entrepreneur,
3	educator, philanthropist and community builder have left a lasting impact in our
3	community.
3	As a professor at the University of Waterloo, Ron played an important role in the
3	development of health studies and gerontology, helping to strengthen the connection
3	between academic research and the well-being of our community. His dedication to
3	improving the lives of older adults continued through his work with Schlegel Villages and
3	the Schlegel-UW Research Institute for Aging.
3	His entrepreneurial spirit, commitment to learning and remarkable generosity extended
3	across many sectors, from agriculture and urban development to health care, including
3	mental health and addiction care through Homewood Health Centre. He will be
3	remembered as a community builder who helped make Waterloo Region a stronger and
3	more connected place.
3	Ron was a true barnraiser in Waterloo Region, and his legacy is one of innovation,
3	compassion and a commitment to community, his contributions to urban development,
3	mental health and healthy aging continue to have an impact in Waterloo.
3	On behalf of the City of Waterloo, we extend our heartfelt condolences to Ron’s sons,
3	their families, and all those who had the privilege of knowing and working with him.
