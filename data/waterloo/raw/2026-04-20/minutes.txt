2	3. TERRITORIAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
2	Councillor Freeman opened the meeting with the following Territorial Acknowledgement:
2	Today the agenda includes a recognition associated with City support to the Canadian
2	armed forces through a forward-thinking human resources policy that facilitates the
2	ability for City Staff who are also reservists to answer the call to serve Canadians
2	domestically and abroad.
2	I would like to acknowledge the important role that Indigenous community members are
2	serving and have served in the Canadian Armed Forces.
2	As outlined on the Indigenous Veterans website, “the story of Indigenous service is a
2	proud one. While exact numbers are elusive, it has been estimated that more than
2	12,000 First Nations, Métis and Inuit people served in the conflicts of the 20th century,
2	with at least 500 of them sadly losing their lives.”
2	These determined individuals were often forced to overcome many challenges to serve
2	in uniform, from learning a new language and adapting to cultural differences, to having
2	to travel great distances from their remote communities just to enlist. The challenges
2	they faced often extended to their post-service life. Many Indigenous war Veterans did
2	not receive equal treatment compared to other Canadian Veterans.
3	Many Indigenous men brought valuable skills including: patience, stealth and
3	marksmanship from well-honed traits for those who had come from communities where
3	hunting was a cornerstone of daily life. These attributes helped many of these soldiers
3	become successful snipers and reconnaissance scouts.
3	During the second world war Indigenous soldiers took on a unique role of "code talker”
3	where sensitive radio messages were translated into Cree so they could not be
3	understood if they were intercepted by the enemy.
3	Indigenous men and women have continued to proudly serve in uniform and have been
3	deployed wherever they have been needed—from NATO duties in Europe during the
3	Cold War to service with United Nations and other multinational peace support
3	operations in dozens of countries around the world. In more recent years, many
3	Indigenous Canadian Armed Forces members saw hazardous duty in Afghanistan.
3	Closer to home, Indigenous military personnel have filled a wide variety of roles,
3	including serving with the Canadian Rangers. This group of army reservists is active
3	predominantly in the North, as well as on remote stretches of our east and west coasts.
3	The Rangers use their intimate knowledge of the land there to help maintain a national
3	military presence in these difficult-to-reach areas, monitoring the coastlines and
3	assisting in local rescue operations.
3	This long tradition of military service is also commemorated with the National Aboriginal
3	Veterans Monument in Ottawa. This deeply symbolic memorial features a large bronze
3	eagle at its top, with four men and women from different Indigenous groups from across
3	Canada immediately below. A wolf, bear, bison and caribou—powerful animals that
3	represent "spiritual guides" which have long been seen by Indigenous cultures as
3	important to military success—look out from each corner. Remembrance ceremonies
3	are held at this special monument, including on Indigenous Veterans Day which is
3	observed each year on November 8.
3	As an Honourary Lieutenant Colonel with 31 Combat Engineers, “the Elgins” I would like
3	to acknowledge the contributions made to the Canadian Armed Forces by First Nations,
3	Inuit and Metis community members.

7	11. NOTICE OF MOTION
7	a) Councillor Wright – Supporting a Provincial Deposit-Return Program
7	for Non-Alcoholic Beverage Containers
7	Motion tabled April 20, 2026 for Council consideration April 20, 2026.
7	Moved by Councillor Wright, Seconded by Councillor Roach:
7	WHEREAS Ontario is one of Canada’s only provinces without a deposit-
7	return program for non-alcoholic beverages;
7	AND WHEREAS Ontario has the lowest beverage container recovery rate
7	in Canada – at roughly 50%;
7	AND WHEREAS an average of 80% of all empties return to Beer Stores
7	each year, and the deposit return provides a consumer incentive to
7	consciously recycle;
7	AND WHEREAS the Ontario government proposed expanding the
7	program to include non-alcoholic containers in 2023.
7	THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED THAT Council call on the Province of
7	Ontario to immediately seek to implement a deposit-return program for
7	non-alcoholic beverage containers;
8	AND FURTHER THAT a copy of this motion be sent to the Hon. Doug
8	Ford, Premier of Ontario; the Hon. Todd McCarthy, Minister of the
8	Environment, Conservation, and Parks; MPP Peter Tabuns (NDP); MPP
8	Mary-Margaret McMahon (Liberal); MPP Aislinn Clancy (Green Party); the
8	Association of Municipalities of Ontario, and to local and area MPPs and
8	heads of council.
8	Carried Unanimously
8	b) Councillor Roe – Heritage Helping Housing Motion
8	Motion tabled April 20, 2026 for Council consideration April 27, 2026.
8	Motion:
8	WHEREAS Architectural Conservancy Ontario (ACO) is the province’s
8	leading non-governmental organization dedicated to the conservation and
8	reuse of Ontario’s built and cultural heritage; through education and
8	advocacy ACO encourages the prevention and adaptive reuse of buildings
8	and landscapes of architectural, historic and cultural significance;
8	WHEREAS ACO has emphasized that strong heritage protections are
8	compatible with and supportive of efforts to address Ontario’s housing
8	crisis, particularly through the retention, rehabilitation and repurposing of
8	older building for new residential uses;
8	WHEREAS heritage buildings can help solve Ontario’s housing crisis by
8	offering solutions that are affordable, equitable, sustainable and uniquely
8	Canadian; including the repair and reuse of existing buildings which
8	provides faster, cheaper and a more sustainable path to creating
8	affordable housing and by enabling the delivery of homes faster than new
8	construction while also supporting a stronger jobs market;
8	WHEREAS older and heritage buildings represent an important and often
8	underutilized opportunity to create additional housing units, provided that
8	appropriate financial incentives exist to support renovation and code-
8	compliance upgrades;
8	WHEREAS ACO has proposed that the Province of Ontario implement a
8	new housing-focused “Heritage Helping Housing Building Grant” of $10
8	million per year to encourage property owners and developers to
8	rehabilitate and make adaptive reuse of heritage buildings that creates
8	new housing units;
9	WHEREAS such a provincial grant program would support municipal
9	housing objectives, help revitalize main streets, encourage private
9	investment, reduce demolition waste, and strengthen the economic and
9	cultural vitality of Ontario communities;
9	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that this Council request the
9	Mayor send a letter to the Hon. Doug Ford, Premier of Ontario, with copies
9	to the Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing, The Hon. Rob Flack, the
9	Minister of Finance, the Hon. Peter Bethlenfalvy, the Minister of
9	Citizenship and Multiculturalism, the Hon. Graham McGregor and John
9	Ecker, Chair, Ontario Heritage Trust, requesting that the Province of
9	Ontario establish the Heritage Helping Housing Building Grant as
9	proposed by the ACO, and that a copy of this resolution be circulated to
9	local and area MPPs and heads of council.
