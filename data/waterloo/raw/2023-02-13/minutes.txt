3	7. OPENING REMARKS
3	a) Opening Remarks, Mayor McCabe
3	Mayor McCabe brought opening remarks. Mayor thanked Councillor Freeman for taking
3	on the role of the Finance Chair and acknowledged her experience on Council and
3	knowledge of the community. Mayor also thanked Filipa Reynolds, Commissioner of
3	Corporate Services, for leading the budget process. Speaking of the budget process,
3	Mayor McCabe indicated that 2023 budget reflected the priorities of the City and the
3	community throughout the year. Mayor stressed that the current budget was prepared in
3	challenging times when, nonetheless, a demand for City services and programs remained
3	high. Mayor McCabe emphasized that the City worked on gathering information that
3	helped Council and City staff understand the priorities of the community; and that this
3	work would continue to create the new strategic plan. Mayor finished her remarks by
3	thanking staff and Council for all the hard work that had gone into 2023 budget.
3	b) Introductory Remarks, Filipa Reynolds, Commissioner,
3	Corporate Services and Chief Financial Officer
3	Filipa Reynolds began her introductory remarks with a video highlighting some of 2022
3	landmarks that the City was proud to celebrate. Filipa Reynolds then spoke of 2023
3	operating and capital budget and their impact on an average household. Commissioner
3	Reynolds emphasised that $1.3 million in efficiencies and improvements helped to reduce
3	the tax impact. Filipa Reynold then spoke of tools that had been used to increase public
3	engagement in shaping 2023 budget. Commissioner Reynolds concluded by thanking the
3	Budget Committee members, Financial Planning team, the Communications team, as
3	well as City leadership and staff who had played a critical role in the budget preparation
3	process.
3	c) Budget Day Overview, Councillor Diane Freeman, Finance Liaison
3	In her introductory remarks, Councillor Freeman, Finance Liaison, highlighted the fact that
3	2023 budget was brought forward in challenging times. She stressed the importance of
3	adopting a budget that had a proposed property tax increase that was below the current
3	rate of inflation. Councillor Freeman indicated that over the years, the City has kept tax
3	rate increases competitive with other area municipalities by finding efficiencies. Councillor
3	Freeman spoke of the proposed budget impact on Waterloo residents and offered some
3	2023 capital and operating budget highlights. She indicated that the next budget, covering
3	2024-2026, would be prepared to execute on the new strategic plan that was being
3	worked on. She then introduced the process for considering the Budget items. She
3	concluded by thanking Council members, Budget Committee, City staff, as well as
3	members of the community for their contribution to this operating and capital budget
3	process over the past few months.

7	1) Mayor Dorothy McCabe - Snow Clearing
7	At this time, Mayor McCabe brought forward a motion to include directions to staff.
7	Moved by Mayor McCabe, Seconded by Councillor Vasic:
7	That Council direct staff to review and report back prior to June 2023 on
7	possible sidewalk snow clearing improvements for consideration as part of
7	the 2024-2026 budget process. Considerations to be reviewed will align with
7	the City’s current hybrid model where the city and the adjacent property
7	owner clear snow from sidewalks. The focus will be on a review of sidewalk
7	clearing at key nodes and corridors taking into consideration accessibility
7	needs, proximity and connectivity to public transit, schools, hospitals, health
7	services and other essential facilities in alignment with active transportation
7	and transit routes. An environmental scan of other similar communities and
7	public engagement will inform options along with Regional responsibilities
7	and the current road maintenance agreement. Opportunities for
7	improvement may include snow clearing at intersection corners, along curb
7	faced sidewalks, enhanced compliance, enhanced support and volunteer
7	services and improved communications with a potential phased-in
7	implementation as part of the upcoming 3 year budget process.
7	Carried Unanimously
