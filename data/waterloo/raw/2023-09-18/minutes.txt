2	3. TERRITORIAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
2	Councillor Freeman opened the meeting with the following Territorial Acknowledgement:
2	We would like to begin by acknowledging that the land on which many of us are
2	gathered today is the land traditionally cared for by the Haudenosaunee, Anishnaabe
2	and Neutral peoples. We also acknowledge the enduring presence and deep traditional
2	knowledge and philosophies of the Indigenous people with whom we share this land
2	today.
2	We offer this acknowledgement, a small act of reconciliation, at a time when as a
2	community and as a nation, we are turning our attention to learning and taking action for
2	change. September 30 marks the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, a day to
2	honour the children who never returned home and the Survivors of residential schools
2	as well as their families and communities. October 4 is Sisters in Spirit Day, a day to
2	raise awareness about missing and murdered Indigenous women, girls and two-spirit
2	people.
2	The proximity of these days to each other emphasizes the profound impact of
2	colonialism and racism on the lives of Indigenous people. The violence and injustice
2	experienced by Indigenous children and their families at the hands of residential schools
2	continues to be present in the form of disproportionate levels of violence against
2	Indigenous women, girls and two-spirit people in the face of a system that does not
2	deliver answers or justice.
2	The truth is difficult. But we cannot skip it on the way to reconciliation. We encourage all
2	residents of our city and region to stop, reflect, learn and act. Explore the many learning
2	resources curated by our local libraries. Unlearn biases and reject negative stereotypes
2	about Indigenous people. Wear orange. Attend upcoming events to remember and
2	honour Survivors, as well as events that celebrate Indigenous culture and contributions.
2	Donate to local Indigenous-led organizations.
2	As a City, we re-commit ourselves to continued learning about and grappling with the
2	truth during this time of reflection and remembrance. We further commit to the year-
2	round and long-term work of reconciliation with the goal of creating a better community
2	for the seven generations yet to come.
3	In preparation for this year’s National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, the City READI
3	Team is pleased to provide to Mayor McCabe and City Council, Orange Shirts that feature
3	the unique design from Alanah Astehtsi Otsistohkwa (Morningstar) Jewell of Morningstar
3	Designs, from the exclusive collection developed in partnership with the University of
3	Waterloo’s Office of Indigenous Relations. The artwork is a representation of the wildlife
3	and eco-system of the Grand River. The design depicts herons taking flight, drawing
3	energy from the Haudenosaunee Great Tree of Peace and florals growing beside the
3	riverbed, flowing up towards the moon, which controls the waters and has a deep
3	connection to the Grand River. The elements come together to signify the cyclical and
3	reciprocal relationships between the wildlife, the moon and the river.
3	A limited number of shirts will be available to staff for purchase as well but additional
3	shirts can be purchased at the W Store and online at wstore.ca at any time, while
3	quantities last. A significant portion of the proceeds from the sale of these Orange Shirts
3	will be directed to support the Indigenous Student Success Fund on campus,
3	established to provide financial assistance to those Indigenous students who are most
3	at risk of abandoning their education due to financial hardship. Another local resource
3	for Orange Shirts is Healing of the Seven Generations.

7	10. CONSIDERATION OF NOTICE OF MOTION GIVEN AT PREVIOUS MEETING
7	a) Councillor Vasic – Guaranteed Livable Basic Income Motion for
7	consideration September 18, 2023
7	Mayor McCabe left the meeting. (Time: 5:00 p.m.)
7	Mayor McCabe joined the meeting. (Time: 5:02 p.m.)
7	Bea Henry, Resident of Waterloo gave a brief presentation on what a basic income is,
7	why a basic income is necessary, and statistics surrounding poverty.
7	Lorna Aberdein, Resident of Waterloo gave a brief presentation on why a basic income
7	is necessary and essential, including the decrease in costs for legal, health, and social
7	services.
7	Colleen Cooper, Resident of Waterloo gave a brief presentation on the six strategies
7	that make basic income affordable.
7	Moved by Councillor Vasic, seconded by Councillor Bodaly:
7	WHEREAS a Guaranteed Livable Basic Income (GLBI) has the potential
7	to prevent people from falling into poverty by providing an income-tested
7	income top-up for all who need it regardless of employment status;
7	AND WHEREAS the 2021 census data demonstrated an income
7	inequality challenge in the City of Waterloo with a higher percentage of
7	individuals making more than $100,000 per year (15.1%) and a higher
7	portion of individuals making less than $30,000 per year (37.3%) as
7	compared to the national average (10.8% and 36% respectively) and the
7	Region of Waterloo (11.3% and 34.8% respectively);
7	AND WHEREAS the Government of Canada’s report Building
7	Understanding: The first report of the national advisory council on poverty
7	from 2020 used 2016 census data to highlight how poverty
7	disproportionately impacts racialized individuals at twice the rate as
7	compared to non-racialized individuals in Canada, at 20.6% and 10.6%
7	respectively.
7	AND WHEREAS the pandemic, rising cost of living, and surges in food
7	and housing prices has worsened income inequality and created scenarios
7	where more people find it difficult to meet their basic needs;
8	AND WHEREAS social assistance recipients are living in extreme poverty
8	and food insecurity and are at great risk to develop associated health
8	issues;
8	AND WHEREAS Ontario Works (OW) and Ontario Disability Support
8	Program (ODSP) rates are below Canada’s Official Poverty Line and since
8	social assistance rates were reduced by 21.6% in 1995, rate increases
8	have been nominal, representing an approximate 10% increase over 26
8	years;
8	AND WHEREAS Ontario Works rates have not been increased since 2018
8	and are not indexed to inflation causing further income inequality, poverty,
8	and housing unaffordability;
8	AND WHEREAS there would be costs of implementing a GLBI, its
8	provision would save indirect costs we continue to experience by helping
8	alleviate the mounting financial and social pressures municipalities face
8	and would yield significant benefits, including reducing homelessness,
8	aiding the affordability of housing, reducing petty crime, improving mental
8	health, supporting efforts to effectively treat addictions, supporting
8	neighbourhood cohesion, and boosting local and regional economic
8	development;
8	AND WHEREAS the Province of Ontario undertook a Basic Income Pilot
8	program over 15 months starting in 2018 to test the view that a basic
8	income could provide a way to reduce poverty in a sustainable way;
8	AND WHEREAS that pilot project ceased earlier than intended and
8	without a full government report, published research out of McMaster
8	University indicated that pilot participants involved in the study reported
8	improvements in the areas of physical and mental health, access to health
8	services, enhanced food security, improved housing conditions, and
8	financial and social well-being:
8	AND WHEREAS in lieu of a provincial or federal anti-poverty strategy, the
8	City of Waterloo has developed programs to alleviate the impacts of
8	poverty and income inequality, and to make it possible for constituents to
8	participate in programs that provide a better quality of life and which can
8	positively impact mental and physical well-being, programs such as:
8	• Fee assistance services for participation in recreation, leisure, arts
8	and culture programs and fee assistance for groups for in-kind
8	services or rental discounts/rebates
9	• Implementation of a living wage strategy and living wage champion
9	designation for city employees and contractors
9	• Neighbourhood Matching Grants to support neighbourhood focused
9	initiatives
9	• Community Cash Grants and other corporate grants that support
9	programs and services aimed at providing cultural, sport and
9	community activities
9	• Participation in tax deferral and rebate programs
9	• Sidewalk winter control strategy that includes assisted supports and
9	priority clearing zones
9	• Providing funding and resources for programs and services
9	supporting the unhoused, and supporting The Food Bank and other
9	food distribution networks
9	• Support for shelter operations in Waterloo such as the House of
9	Friendship ShelterCare model and The Working Centre
9	• Affordable Housing Grant Program and Strategy to help address the
9	fast-growing problem of affordable housing availability
9	• Housing Pledge to help meet the provincial goal of building 1.5
9	million homes in Ontario in 10 years;
9	AND WHEREAS all of these programs and services and the myriad
9	additional programs and services offered by the regional government, and
9	community-based services and programs such as those that offer food,
9	housing, addiction, and neighbourhood support services, amount to a
9	federally and provincially downloaded responsibility to respond to and treat
9	needs created by the lack of a GLBI;
9	AND WHEREAS addressing a GLBI has been shown to reduce the need
9	for individuals to apply for multiple support programs in order to maintain a
9	healthy and full quality of life;
9	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that:
9	1) the City of Waterloo supports, in principle, advocates such as Basic
9	Income Waterloo Region who are pushing for the implementation of
9	a GLBI;
9	2) the City requests the Government of Canada and the Province of
9	Ontario establish an income-tested GLBI to combat poverty,
9	income inequality, and economic insecurity within our
9	community,
9	3) the City asks that as the GLBI is phased-in over time that existing
9	health and social service supports continue to operate and be
9	funded in order to firstly provide continuity to support recipients and
10	secondly so that long-term data can be collected to investigate the
10	effectiveness of the GLBI independent of the removal of any other
10	programs,
10	4) Until these programs are found to be redundant and a GLBI is fully
10	adopted should it demonstrate continued success, the City of
10	Waterloo asks the provincial government to increase Social
10	Assistance rates for both OW and ODSP to reflect the costs of living
10	and tie these rates to inflation, and
10	5) a copy of this resolution be sent to the Prime Minister of Canada,
10	the Premier of Ontario, local and area MP’s, MPP’s, local and area
10	councils, the Federation of Canadian Municipalities, and the
10	Association of Municipalities of Ontario for further consideration.
10	Carried Unanimously
