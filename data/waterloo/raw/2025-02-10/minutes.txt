2	4. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
2	Councillor Freeman provided Council with moments of reflection.
2	Black History Month has been recognized in Canada since 1978 when advocates
2	from the Ontario Black History Society emphasized the importance of this
2	celebration for Canadians of all races. Almost fifty years of celebration of Black
2	history and Black excellence are still underlined by the struggles that come with
2	the Black experience.
2	Black communities are still underrepresented in workplaces and decision-making
2	structures, more negatively affected in health outcomes, lacking appropriate
2	ongoing investments for entrepreneurs and more. In communities across Canada
2	and locally, we have also seen that racialized individuals face extreme backlash
2	when they speak out against injustices.
2	We all have a role in creating a city that brings an end to racial discrimination,
2	and to build a more inclusive community. This starts with the basics of how we
2	relate to each other as humans -- we must be aware of our individual prejudice
3	and learn to treat each other with dignity, respect and compassion. Learning
3	about the experiences of others is an essential part of understanding how we can
3	effectively join in the fight against all forms of racism and discrimination, including
3	anti-Black racism.
3	Throughout February, we encourage you to support local Black-owned
3	businesses, read the work of Black writers and connect with your community at
3	one of the many events being hosted by local organizations.
3	As a Council, we reaffirm our acknowledgement to addressing anti-Black racism
3	and rededicate ourselves to making the City of Waterloo a place where the
3	contributions of Black community members are recognized and celebrated.
3	At the beginning of this Council meeting, we pause to think about the needs of our
3	community. May we show wisdom and compassion in all our decisions.

5	9. NOTICE OF MOTION
5	Mayor Dorothy McCabe – US Proposed Tariff Response
5	Motion to be tabled February 10, 2025 for Council consideration February 24,
5	2025.
5	WHEREAS contrary to terms of the existing United States Mexico Canada
5	Agreement as well as to the long standing strong and productive economic
5	and political relationship between Canada and the United States, the US
5	President threatened to impose 25% tariffs on Canadian goods as of
5	February 1st and although he has subsequently delayed the tariffs by thirty
5	days they remain a real threat; and
5	WHEREAS tariffs could lead to a halt of the supply chain and create
5	economic uncertainty in municipalities of all sizes; and
5	WHEREAS municipalities are the foundation on which the Canadian
5	economy is built, and as such municipalities should offer our support
5	towards a united and strong Team Canada response; and
6	WHEREAS municipalities have significant purchasing power through our
6	capital and infrastructure programs, with the City of Waterloo’s capital
6	program for 2025 being $200 million alone, while the Association of
6	Municipalities of Ontario cite that infrastructure spending across Ontario in
6	the next 10 years is expected to exceed $250 billion; and
6	WHEREAS Federal and Provincial leaders are encouraging all Canadians
6	to Buy Canadian; and
6	WHEREAS City of Waterloo and the broader region is proud and
6	honoured to be home to numerous U.S. based international students,
6	academic professionals, and institutional thought leaders, along with
6	prominent USA companies whose presence has greatly contributed to our
6	quality of life, economic development, innovation, and employment
6	opportunities; and
6	WHEREAS longstanding partnerships highlight the strong, mutually
6	beneficial economic ties between Canada and the United States, fostering
6	collaboration in technology, manufacturing, and research
6	sectors. Maintaining these free-trade practices will promote continued
6	strong growth and shared prosperity on both sides of the border;
6	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT THE CITY OF
6	WATERLOO:
6	Stands with Team Canada on the message that tariffs are not in the best
6	interests of Canadian or American residents, organizations, businesses
6	and employees;
6	AND calls on the Federal and Provincial governments to work with
6	municipalities on measures to protect Canadians and businesses;
6	AND calls on the Waterloo Economic Development Corporation (WEDC),
6	through the Business Economic Support Team Waterloo Region (BEST
6	WR), to develop an action plan to support Waterloo Region businesses
6	and our collective economic interests;
6	AND calls on the Federal and Provincial governments to better enable
6	municipalities to explore procurement opportunities that exist within other
6	trade agreements such as the Comprehensive Economic Trade
6	Agreement;
6	AND calls on the Federal and Provincial government to work
6	collaboratively and urgently to remove trade barriers between provinces
6	and territories, understanding that language requirements should continue
6	to be respected for Quebec;
7	AND calls on City of Waterloo staff to explore options for limiting the
7	impact of the potential United States’ sanctioned tariffs on City Services
7	and review current procurement processes for opportunities to increase
7	purchases from Canadian sources;
7	AND further that a copy of this motion be forwarded to the Right
7	Honourable Justin Trudeau, Prime Minister of Canada, The Honourable
7	Doug Ford, Premier of Ontario, local and area MP’s, MPP’s and Heads of
7	Council, The Association of Municipalities of Ontario and the Federation of
7	Canadian Municipalities.

7	great to see the different ways the City offers support.
7	Councillor Vasic also attended the exhibition opening for Miles Rufelds’ Salvage
7	Archives on February 8, in discussion with Angel Callander, at the Kitchener-Waterloo
7	Art Gallery. It was a talk on late stage capitalism and the alienation it brings. It
7	discussed conspiracy theories, and the idea of Cognitive Mapping, where people map
7	themselves into the places they are, finding in their own minds the places they fit and
7	don’t. It hammered home how imperative it is to make this City a place where people
7	can map themselves cognitively as a tool to prevent alienation.

7	together with the Neighbourhoods Team to run active bystander training at the Waterloo
7	Memorial Recreation Complex last week. It was really well attended, and it was great to
7	see neighbourhood leadership coming together to make Waterloo a more welcoming
7	place and challenge hateful expressions.
