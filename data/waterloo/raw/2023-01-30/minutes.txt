1	2. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
1	Mayor McCabe provided Council with a moment of reflection.
1	January 29 is a solemn day; this year it marked the sixth anniversary of the
1	National Day of Remembrance of the Quebec City Mosque Attack and Action
1	against Islamophobia. Yesterday, the Waterluminate LED lights in Uptown
1	Waterloo were green in recognition of this day.
1	On January 29, 2017, 6 Muslims - Ibrahima Barry, Mamadou Tanou Barry,
1	Khaled Belkacemi, Abdelkrim Hassane, Azzedine Soufiane, and Aboubaker
1	Thabti were killed and 19 others injured in a hateful act of terror when a person
1	wielding a gun entered the Centre culturel islamique de Québec in Sainte-Foy
1	following evening prayers. This senseless killing was a result of Islamophobia,
1	racism and bigotry.
2	In memory of the victims, in solidarity with the survivors and those who were
2	injured, the families and friends who lost a loved one, and in recognition of those
2	who put themselves in harm’s way to protect others, we pause, reflect and take
2	action.
2	This remembrance serves as a critical reminder of the work that must be done to
2	eliminate Islamophobia, hate, racism and all forms of discrimination and prejudice
2	locally and across Canada. A starting point is for residents to join the ongoing
2	Green Square Campaign. The green square represents the green carpets of the
2	Quebec City mosque, where the victims last prayed. It symbolizes that the
2	deceased are believed to spiritually be in a green garden, a better place than
2	their experience the night of the attack.
2	As a Council we will continue to respond and address all forms of hate and
2	racism in our community.
2	Mayor McCabe also offered some brief words on Black History Month:
2	I would like to briefly acknowledge that we are a few days from Black History
2	Month. Events and activities and reflections about Black History Month will start
2	at our next Council Meeting on February 6th, and carry on throughout the month
2	of February.

6	c) Title: 2023-2026 Strategic Plan – Council
6	Workshop #1
6	Report No.: CAO2023-010
6	Prepared By: Sandy Little
6	Sarah Lewis, Manager, Deloitte, facilitated the “Hot Off the Campaign Trail” workshop
6	and asked each Councillor to share three main themes heard from constituents on the
6	campaign trail.
6	Councillor Wright wanted to pass her thanks to the residents of Ward 7 who offered
6	their time to share their thoughts. The main themes she heard on the campaign trail
6	were about whether quality of life is eroding and whether we were making the right
6	decisions as a City, access to affordable housing and a mismatch in the supply, and the
6	election process and access to print materials, including meaningful engagement with
6	residents.
7	Councillor Roe echoed some of Councillor Wright’s comments regarding the election
7	process, and offered the themes she heard on the campaign trail including: affordable
7	housing and homelessness; traffic calming, pedestrian safety and connected systems,
7	such as bike lanes, GRT connections, and trails; and community connectedness and
7	excellence of service from the City, including winter maintenance and leaf collection.
7	Mayor McCabe shared Councillor Vasic’s three themes on her behalf: traffic calming,
7	safety and ease of mobility, including winter accessibility concerns; housing and
7	homelessness; and covid-19 recovery, including supporting small businesses, a general
7	lack of connectedness, and an enhanced arts and culture sector.
7	Councillor Freeman shared the main themes heard on her campaign: traffic and traffic
7	speeds, specifically that they are not supportive of 30km/h speed limits; climate change
7	and continuing to move forward with important efforts; and diversity, equity and
7	inclusion, including how individuals are recognized in programs and intake forms, the
7	opportunity to build a Reconciliation Contribution fund, and the role of the city within
7	housing and homelessness.
7	Councillor Roach also echoed Councillor Roe and Councillor Wright as far as the
7	election went, mentioning that there was a lot of election fatigue. The three main themes
7	heard on his campaign were: affordable housing, including the price of rent; traffic
7	concerns, with specific mention to the roundabouts in Conservation Meadows; and city
7	services such as leaf collection and snow clearing.
7	Councillor Bodaly shared some of the different concerns he heard while campaigning,
7	and offered the main themes: safe streets, including how to make streets safer for
7	pedestrians, cyclists, seniors, and youth; what the City is doing surrounding climate
7	change; affordable housing, including homelessness and affordable units; and equity
7	and inclusion, including a sense of belonging by the community.
7	Councillor Hanmer shared the main themes from her campaign: affordable housing,
7	including appropriate rents and the “missing middle”; traffic calming measures, including
7	30km/h speed limits around school zones and issues with the roundabout at Ira Needles
7	and Erb; active transportation and the connections across the City; and park
7	maintenance, which may have fallen to the wayside during the pandemic.
7	Mayor McCabe shared some of the concerns she heard on the campaign trail, as well
7	as the main themes: affordable and attainable housing; traffic and road safety, including
7	that many residents don’t want the speed reduced; climate change, including the need
7	for climate friendly new builds; and community connectedness, and finding a sense of
7	purpose within the community.
7	Sarah Lewis, Manager, Deloitte, continued her presentation and led Council through the
7	next steps of the Strategic Planning stages. Sandy Little shared information on the
7	Strategic Plan preparation steps.
