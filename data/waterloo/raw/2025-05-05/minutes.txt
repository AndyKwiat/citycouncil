7	11. NOTICE OF MOTION
7	Mayor McCabe – Federal and Provincial Stimulus Funding to Fast-Track
7	Investments in Infrastructure to Protect Local Economies Motion
7	Moved by Mayor McCabe, Seconded by Councillor Hanmer:
7	WHEREAS the United States has imposed tariffs on key Canadian
7	industries, and Canada has implemented counter-tariffs in response; and
7	WHEREAS tariffs are already disrupting local economies across Ontario,
7	jeopardizing jobs, investment, and economic stability in communities of all
7	sizes; and
7	WHEREAS we must protect our most vulnerable residents whose housing
7	supports and services may be negatively impacted by an economic
7	downturn; and
7	WHEREAS infrastructure investments in public transit, Light Rail Transit
7	and improving the electricity grid will simultaneously address the economic
7	uncertainty created by the US imposed tariffs by creating good, local jobs
7	while also addressing the climate crisis; and
7	WHEREAS in times of economic uncertainty, federal and provincial
7	governments have historically stepped up with stimulus investments to
7	support municipalities, create jobs, and maintain economic momentum;
7	and
7	WHEREAS past stimulus initiatives, such as the 2009 Economic Action
7	Plan, which funded 23,000 infrastructure projects across the country in
7	response to the global financial crisis, and the 2020 COVID-19 stimulus
7	plan, which provided critical economic relief during the pandemic, played a
7	critical role in stabilizing local economies and ensuring communities could
7	withstand economic downturns; and
8	WHEREAS municipal governments are uniquely positioned to drive
8	economic recovery by facilitating major infrastructure projects that create
8	well-paying jobs, attract investment, and improve public services for
8	residents; and
8	WHEREAS fast-tracking significant municipal projects as stimulus
8	initiatives would help mitigate the negative economic impacts of US tariffs
8	and Canadian counter-tariffs while building critical infrastructure that
8	benefits Ontarians for generations; and
8	WHEREAS projects such as housing enabling infrastructure, asset
8	management mitigation and adaptation, transit and transportation
8	infrastructure, affordable housing and supportive housing projects, and
8	other key infrastructure investments that support economic growth, job
8	creation, and sustainable urban development, are ready to serve as
8	engines of economic resilience and community capacity building,
8	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED:
8	THAT the City of Waterloo Council calls on the federal and provincial
8	governments to introduce comprehensive municipal stimulus strategies to
8	fast-track large-scale investments in infrastructure, housing, transit, state-
8	of-good-repair projects and other critical projects that will help safeguard
8	local economies against the destabilizing effects of US tariffs and
8	Canadian counter-tariffs; and
8	THAT these stimulus strategies also allow for funding of social housing
8	programs including wrap around services for our most vulnerable
8	residents; and
8	THAT Council urges the federal and provincial governments to work in
8	partnership with municipal associations such as the Association of
8	Municipalities of Ontario (AMO), Ontario Big City Mayors (OBCM), Mayors
8	and Regional Chairs of Ontario (MARCO), and the Federation of Canadian
8	Municipalities (FCM) to identify and accelerate key projects to serve as
8	stimulus initiatives, ensuring that economic growth remains centered on
8	protecting the livelihoods and well-being of the people within our Waterloo
8	and across Waterloo Region:
8	• City of Waterloo Beaver Creek Road and Conservation Drive
8	Pumping Stations - construction of 2 new pumping stations
8	required to enable the creation of over 4,500 new homes in
8	Waterloo.
9	• City of Waterloo Albert McCormick Community Centre
9	Renovation and Expansion - a significant redevelopment to
9	enhance its energy efficiency, accessibility, and overall functionality.
9	• City of Waterloo RIM Park GHG and Accessibility Retrofit
9	Project – a major renovation of a key community asset that
9	represents approximately 47% of the GHG savings required to meet
9	the City’s 2030 GHG emission target (in 2030).
9	AND THAT this resolution be sent to all municipalities in the Regional
9	Municipality of Waterloo, Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO),
9	local MPPs, local MPs, Ontario Big City Mayors Caucus (OBCM), Mayors
9	and Regional Chairs of Ontario (MARCO), Minister of Economic
9	Development, Job Creation and Trade, Minister of Infrastructure, Minister
9	of Finance, President of the Treasury Board, Premier Doug Ford and the
9	federal ministerial counterparts when the new federal cabinet is
9	established.
9	Carried Unanimously
9	12. NEW BUSINESS
9	Mayor McCabe shared that last Wednesday, April 30, she had the opportunity to attend
9	the Mississaugas of the Credit First Nation’s Historical gathering, which spanned three
9	days. It was a really great learning opportunity to sit and listen to the presentations, in
9	particular one about the Moccasin Identifier Project by the founder, Carolyn King. She
9	really appreciated the invite from Chief Sault.
9	Mayor McCabe hosted a Community Conversation on Saturday, May 3 with a number
9	of volunteers who lead neighbourhood and home associations, Councillor Roe, and a
9	number of staff helping to facilitate conversations. She offered her thanks to everyone
9	for the volunteering they do across the community. Volunteers do so much to build
9	community and to build connections.
9	Mayor McCabe gave a final shoutout to Council for a Day, which took place on
9	Thursday, May 1. Eight Grade 5 students attended State of the City, and then toured a
9	number of City facilities with Mayor and a few staff. City staff at the facilities were
9	fantastic and answered their very intelligent and thoughtful questions, and the students
9	learned about the different programs and services that the City offers. She offered
9	thanks to the students for their ideas, and to all the kids that submitted ideas.
10	Councillor Wright spoke about the weekend of celebrations in Ward 7. St. John’s
10	Christian Nursery School celebrated their 40th Anniversary and the adjacent Luthor
10	Manor Apartments for Seniors celebrated their 50th Anniversary. It’s really great to see
10	that Luthor Manor is still able to offer affordable housing for seniors 50 years later, and
10	to see the benefits of churches as third spaces in the community. Seven Shores, in its
10	current configuration, celebrated its 10th Anniversary, which is another great gathering
10	space for Ward 7.
10	Councillor Wright wanted to highlight the many volunteers, especially at the end of
10	Volunteer Week, that stepped up to run Jane’s Walks throughout the City. She joined
10	one on Sunday afternoon, where they explored the different sustainable efforts in the
10	Mary Allen neighbourhood.
