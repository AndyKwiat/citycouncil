2	6. PRESENTATIONS/DELEGATIONS
3	a) Six Nations Education and Training Workshop
3	Phil Monture, Land Rights Consultant, Six Nations spoke about his experience as a
3	Land Rights Consultant over the last 50 years. He explained the significance of the
3	different treaties entered into by the Five Nations with the settlers.
3	He spoke about the treaty that governs the Haldimand Tract and the six miles of land on
3	either side of the Grand River, and how the Six Nations have less than 5% of the land
3	promised to them. He specifically described Block 2, where Waterloo is located, and the
3	history surrounding the dissecting of the Haldimand Tract.
3	Daylon Gee, Land Use Technician, Lands and Resources Office, Six Nations spoke
3	about the legal duty to consult. He described the treaty rights more fully, including the
3	rights to land, rights to occupy, government rights, cultural and societal rights. He spoke
3	about the rules for consultation under the Planning Act, and stated that new Bills being
3	passed by the province to get homes built faster often undermine the rights of the First
3	Nations who live in these areas.
3	Daylon Gee discussed the conditions on the Six Nations reserves, such as water
3	contamination and poor road conditions, and stressed the importance of Indigenous
3	consultations. Consultations ensure the protection of treaty rights and the Indigenous
3	way of life, build relationships and trust with the communities, and it’s the law. Some of
3	the ways that communities can be accommodated include financial settlements, equity
3	partnerships, joint land stewardship, job opportunities, and education opportunities. He
3	mentioned a consultation package for working with the Region that is being developed.
3	Lauren Jones, Wildlife and Stewardship Manager, Six Nations, spoke about her role
3	within the Consultation and Accommodation Process (CAP) Team providing
3	environmentally specific comments. She described processes and frameworks they
3	have to follow as stagnated and not made with the intentions of including Indigenous
3	viewpoints. She spoke about the connection between the natural environment and the
3	identities of the Indigenous people within Ontario, and how it differs from Western,
3	settler perspectives.
3	She continued to say that disruptions to the natural environment directly affect
3	Indigenous communities on multiple levels, with pollution and degradation disrupting
3	their way of life and leading to food insecurity. The Wildlife and Stewardship Office
3	tends to push for going above and beyond the minimum that is set out in frameworks,
3	for improvements to the environment, not just replacing what was taken, and advocating
3	for the protection of species important to Indigenous cultures, which may not be on any
3	endangered species lists.
3	Phil Monture added that he wants to work with Council and the municipalities as allies,
3	and work through differences. He expressed that he wants to build on relationships with
3	open communication.
4	Ron Ormson and Joel Cotter responded to questions of Council. Daylon Gee also
4	responded to questions of Council.

11	12. NOTICE OF MOTION
11	Councillor Bodaly – Bill 17 Motion
11	Motion to be tabled May 26, 2025 for Council consideration May 26, 2025.
11	Matthew Day, Executive Director, WR Community Energy gave a presentation in
11	support of the motion, speaking about affordability, the high-performance development
11	standards, and the infrastructure needed to support population growth.
12	Councillor Roe joined the meeting. (Time: 10:41 p.m.)
12	Sean Campbell, Executive Director, Union Co-operative spoke in support of the motion,
12	mentioning the tools used by other municipalities in the US to help manage greenhouse
12	gas, and how affordable housing can be sustainable.
12	Moved by Councillor Bodaly, Seconded by Councillor Hanmer:
12	WHEREAS the Province of Ontario’s Bill 17 – “Protect Ontario by Building
12	Faster and Smarter” Act recently passed Second Reading and is now
12	open for comment; and
12	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo broadly supports provincial efforts to
12	standardize and streamline development approvals to boost housing and
12	economic growth; and
12	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo was recently recognized as a top
12	performing community under the Housing Accelerator Fund, having
12	implemented numerous initiatives to increase the amount of affordable and
12	attainable housing including; allowing four units and four storeys as of right
12	city wide, increasing height in Major Transit Station Areas, expanding
12	corridors, utilizing city owned land as part of a development at 2025
12	University Avenue among many other initiatives; and
12	WHEREAS Bill 17, in part, aims to standardize the application of the
12	Ontario Building Code by removing municipalities’ ability to set higher
12	green building standards; and
12	WHEREAS approximately 45% of all Waterloo Region community
12	greenhouse gas emissions are from building emissions; and
12	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo has a goal of reducing our community
12	greenhouse gas emissions 50% by 2030 and to be net-zero by 2050; and
12	WHEREAS WR Community Energy has been working on the creation and
12	harmonization of High-Performance Development Standards since 2021,
12	with the intention of implementation in 2026; and
12	WHEREAS the implementation of High-Performance Development
12	Standards is critical to achieve our community greenhouse gas emissions
12	reduction goals; and
13	WHEREAS High-Performance Development Standards are a planning tool
13	for municipalities that respect the Ontario Building Code without sacrificing
13	the important safety elements therein, do not require technical
13	requirements over and above the Ontario Building Code, and are intended
13	to complement the Ontario Building Code, not override it; and
13	WHEREAS High-Performance Development Standards help ensure the
13	long-term affordability of living in new homes by accounting for the long-
13	term operating costs of energy, incentivizing lower carbon and lower cost
13	sources of energy; and
13	WHEREAS Waterloo Region is a leader in green building technologies in
13	the construction industry and in related sectors; and
13	WHEREAS High-Performance Development Standards create demand for
13	specialized skills in green building technologies, leading to the creation of
13	skilled job opportunities, and contributing to local economic prosperity; and
13	WHEREAS High-Performance Development Standards are integral to
13	ensure smart growth in Waterloo Region, affording predictability for local
13	utilities, and ensuring long-term affordability by ensuring homes are built
13	with higher efficiency of water usage, preserving our groundwater supply;
13	and
13	WHEREAS fifteen municipalities in Ontario have already implemented
13	equivalent High-Performance Development Standards; and
13	WHEREAS to meet local, provincial, and federal climate targets, all
13	buildings that are built today in a GHG intensive manner are buildings that
13	will need to be retrofitted in a costly manner in the near future to meet
13	those goals; and
13	WHEREAS the current Ontario Building Code falls short of the National
13	Building Code when it comes to energy efficiency, necessitating municipal
13	leadership to achieve local community goals.
13	NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED:
13	THAT the City of Waterloo advocates for elimination of provisions within
13	Bill 17 that seek restricting local planning authority, particularly as pertains
13	to High-Performance Development Standards; and
14	THAT the City of Waterloo encourages the Province of Ontario to at
14	minimum align the forthcoming Ontario Building Code with energy Tier 3 of
14	the new National Building Code and/or adopt more stringent energy
14	performance standards such as has been implemented in British Columbia
14	and is contemplated in other Provinces such as Quebec; and
14	THAT Council directs staff to provide holistic commentary on Bill 17 to the
14	Environmental Registry of Ontario and in particular, related to concerns of
14	restricting local planning authority for High-Performance Development
14	Standards; and
14	THAT Council directs staff to continue the work on community High-
14	Performance Development Standards to guide future alternative pathways
14	to strengthen and maintain High-Performance Development Standards in
14	new home construction;
14	AND THAT a copy of this resolution be sent to the Premier of Ontario, the
14	Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing, to local and area MPPs, the
14	Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO), the Ontario Big City
14	Mayors (OBCM), the Association of Municipal Managers, Clerks and
14	Treasurers of Ontario (AMCTO), and to the Region of Waterloo and local
14	area municipalities.
14	Carried Unanimously
