2	5. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
2	Councillor Vasic provided Council with a moment of reflection.
2	a) The ‘holiday season’ is language with a shared understanding of dominant
2	cultures; it generally means the celebration of American Thanksgiving,
2	Christmas, and New Year’s between November and January. If we change
2	our perspective and acknowledge that there are additional holidays
2	celebrated by friends, colleagues, and neighbours during that period, we
2	arrive at the recognition of a season of many holidays.
2	Alongside Christmas and New Year’s in Canada, which are steeped in
2	meaningful personal traditions of gatherings, food, and sentiment for those
2	that celebrate, are equally significant celebrations:
2	• Diwali was celebrated on November 12
2	• Hanukkah occurs from December 7 to 15
2	• Bodhi Day, a Buddhist celebration, is December 8, 2023
2	• Winter Solstice is December 21
2	• Dongzhi Festival, celebrated in Japan, China and Taiwan is from
2	December 21 to 23
2	• Christmas is December 25
2	• Kwanzaa is from December 26 to January 1
2	• New Year’s Day is January 1
2	• Orthodox Christmas is January 7
2	• Maghi, a Sikh observance, will take place on January 13
3	• Lohri, the Punjab Winter Festival, is January 13
3	• Haudenosaunee Mid-winter Ceremonies will be celebrated during
3	the new moon in January and lasts for approximately eight days
3	• Lunar New Year will take place on February 10
3	• Vasant Panchami, a Hindu observance, is February 14
3	• Magha, a Buddhist observance, will take place on February 24
3	Despite the notable differences such as religion, date and appeal, together
3	these holidays remind us of human desires to be connected to each other.
3	For many years, traditional Christmas decorations have been put up in
3	municipal facilities and spaces across Waterloo. This décor is intended to
3	recognize the joy of the holiday season with the community. To create a
3	more inclusive and welcoming environment, banners recognizing the
3	season of many celebrations will be displayed in City of Waterloo facilities
3	until February.
3	b) During the 16 Days of Activism, Canadians reflect on the presence of
3	gender based violence in our own country. On December 6, many in our
3	community and across Canada will pause to remember the 14 young
3	women murdered at Polytechnique Montreal and all women who have
3	suffered gender-based violence.
3	These days of remembrance and activism push us to consider the links
3	between sexism, transphobia and homophobia, and the greater risks faced
3	by women, trans people, non-binary people and gender-diverse people.
3	The additional impact of racism and colonialism put Indigenous women,
3	girls and Two Spirit people at even greater risk, similarly the impact of
3	ableism on women with disabilities.
3	As a City, we recognize the importance of taking action on the devastating
3	impacts of gender-based violence. We are also mindful that challenging
3	sexist and transphobic systems must not be limited to a few observances
3	throughout the year but an ongoing action.
3	At the beginning of this Council meeting, we pause to think about the needs of our
3	community. May we show wisdom and compassion in all our decisions.

6	8. NOTICE OF MOTION
6	a) Mayor McCabe – Protection of Waterloo’s Natural Heritage Areas and
6	Green Space for consideration December 4, 2023
6	Moved by Mayor McCabe, Seconded by Councillor Roe:
6	WHEREAS in November 2022, the Government of Ontario passed Bill 23,
6	More Homes Built Faster Act, as part of Ontario's Housing Supply Action
6	Plan with the goal of building 1.5 million new homes in Ontario by 2031,
6	and
6	WHEREAS Bill 23 mandates that, upon legislative proclamation, all land
6	use planning be transitioned from regional governments – the Region of
6	Waterloo – to local municipalities such as the City of Waterloo, and
6	WHEREAS Waterloo City Council has and continues to value and take
6	measures to conserve significant natural features and systems through
6	adoption of environmental policies and municipal decision-making, and
6	understands the importance of conserving significant green spaces in a
6	natural state and to minimize disturbances to such spaces, and
6	WHEREAS Waterloo City Council’s Official Plan, Strategic Plan and
6	Economic Development Strategy outline a long term commitment to
6	building a community that is vibrant, diverse and sustainable as the city
6	continues to intensify and urbanize, and
6	WHEREAS Waterloo City Council acknowledges and understands the
6	importance of rural communities and their contributions particularly with
6	food security, preservation of sensitive natural heritage areas/systems and
6	other green spaces, and economic development, and
7	WHEREAS Waterloo City Council understands and appreciates that the
7	Countryside Line is a boundary that protects the cultural, economic, and
7	environmental heritage of our neighbouring municipalities’ rural lands from
7	the pressures of urban sprawl and that the Countryside Line protects the
7	integrity of rural communities from suburban expansion, and
7	WHEREAS the City of Waterloo has, for decades, actively planned to
7	grow inward through a comprehensive growth management
7	(intensification) strategy, and accordingly has sufficient land currently and
7	until 2050 to accommodate the growing population within Waterloo’s
7	existing municipal boundaries, and
7	WHEREAS past and present Waterloo City Councils have been leaders in
7	environmental protection and understand that it is our responsibility to
7	protect our environment, and that sound urban planning helps protect the
7	environment while enabling the city to grow,
7	THEREFORE be it resolved that Waterloo City Council reaffirm its
7	longstanding practice of acquiring and protecting sensitive natural
7	heritage areas/systems and green spaces from development, and
7	managing growth in a sustainable manner.
7	Carried Unanimously
