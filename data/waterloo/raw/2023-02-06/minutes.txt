7	8. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
7	Councillor Bodaly provided Council with a moment of reflection.
7	Black History Month has been recognized in Canada since 1978 when advocates
7	from the Ontario Black History Society emphasized the importance of this
7	celebration for Canadians of all races. Forty years of celebration of Black history
7	and Black excellence are still underlined by the struggles that come with the
7	Black experience.
7	Black communities are still underrepresented in workplaces and decision-making
7	structures, more negatively affected in health outcomes, lacking appropriate
7	ongoing investments for entrepreneurs and more. In communities across Canada
7	and locally, we have also seen that racialized individuals face extreme backlash
7	when they speak out against injustices.
7	We all have a role in creating a city that brings an end to racial discrimination,
7	and to build a more inclusive community. This starts with the basics of how we
7	relate to each other as humans -- we must be aware of our individual prejudice
7	and learn to treat each other with dignity, respect and compassion. Learning
7	about the experiences of others is an essential part of understanding how we can
7	effectively join in the fight against all forms of racism and discrimination, including
7	anti-Black racism.
8	Throughout February, we encourage you to support local Black-owned
8	businesses, read the work of Black writers and connect with your community at
8	one of the many events being hosted by local organizations.
8	As a Council, we reaffirm our commitment to addressing anti-Black racism and
8	rededicate ourselves to making the City of Waterloo a place where the
8	contributions of Black community members are recognized and celebrated.
