1	2. MOMENT OF REFLECTION
1	Mayor McCabe provided Council with a moment of reflection.
1	a) Today we recognize the International Day of Persons with Disabilities,
1	which takes place each year on December 3. We also recognize that
1	Indigenous Disability Awareness Month comes to a close at the end of
1	November. These observances promote the rights and wellbeing of
2	persons with disabilities, as well as to celebrate the contributions of
2	disabled people to political, social, economic and cultural life.
2	Disability is a broad term: there are many different types of disabilities,
2	they can be visible or invisible, present from birth or acquired later in life.
2	Each person’s experience of disability is unique, and impacted by other
2	aspects of identity. It is all too universal though the ongoing experience of
2	barriers that keep people with disabilities from participating fully at work,
2	school, play and in civic life.
2	As policy makers and service providers, we must acknowledge, address
2	and remove these barriers. Including accessible features in our buildings,
2	creating websites that work well with screen readers, and using plain
2	language to describe our services are just a few of the things we can do to
2	make our city more accessible.
2	We all have a role to play in creating a city that brings an end to ableism
2	and increases inclusivity. We encourage you to take in the resources and
2	upcoming events offered by the Waterloo Public Library, follow disabled
2	leaders and creators on social media and join the calls for change when
2	needed. The imperative of incorporating accessible design and
2	approaches into our day-to-day lives is clear when we consider that we are
2	all temporarily able-bodied.
2	As a City, we re-commit ourselves to the long-term work of confronting
2	ableism and removing barriers in order to create a community where
2	everyone can participate, contribute, belong and thrive.
2	b) It is with great sadness I learned about the passing of former city
2	Councillor Morty Taylor. A dedicated community leader, he served as City
2	Councillor for Ward 4 in Waterloo from 1991 to 1997 and 2000 to 2003. As
2	a resident of Waterloo for over 60 years, Morty was also very well known
2	in our city as the friendly, respected business person who founded Morty's
2	Pub at the corner of King and University in 1981.
2	His commitment to our city and the Ward 4 student community extended to
2	serving on the boards of various local organizations, including Wilfrid
2	Laurier University, Waterloo Library, Oktoberfest Advisory Council, Project
2	Lift, the Kitchener Waterloo Symphony, Recreation and Leisure 55 Plus,
2	The Waterloo Student Housing Task Force and The Affordable Housing
2	Task Force.
2	As a businessman and a member of council, Morty made a significant
2	contribution to the City of Waterloo and will be missed. On behalf of my
2	council colleagues and residents of the city, I extend my condolences to
2	Morty’s family and friends.
3	Flags at City of Waterloo facilities will be at half-mast in memory of former
3	Councillor Taylor until sunset on November 27, 2023.
